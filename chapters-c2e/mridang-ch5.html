Chapter 5: Seeing without Seeing

Senses Beyond Sight, Empathy, Touch, Sound & Braille | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Understanding how people with visual impairment perceive the world through other senses.
  • Recognizing objects by touch (texture, shape, temperature), smell, and hearing.
  • The blindfold classroom game: identifying fruits, flowers, and friends without looking.
  • Empathy, kindness, and inclusion for differently-abled individuals.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (17 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Textbook Activity • Blindfold Game
In the game 'Seeing without Seeing', what happens when a child is blindfolded?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Explanation:
• The child cannot use their eyes to see.
• They must rely on their hands to feel, their ears to listen, and their nose to smell to identify people and objects.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Absence of sight stated 1 M
Touch, sound, and smell usage explained 1 M
Q2 Senses Discrimination • Touch
How can you tell the difference between a rough stone and a smooth glass marble with your eyes closed?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Explanation:
• By using your fingertips to feel the surface texture.
• The stone feels bumpy, uneven, and rough, while the glass marble feels completely sleek, slippery, and smooth.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Fingertip touch method explained 1 M
Rough vs smooth texture contrasted 1 M
Q3 Senses Discrimination • Hearing
Can you recognize your best friend in the dark without seeing their face? How?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
• Yes, by listening to their voice.
• Every human voice has a unique pitch and tone that our ears can easily recognize.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Affirmative answer (Yes) 1 M
Unique voice pitch/tone explained 1 M
Q4 Senses Discrimination • Smell
Name two food items you can instantly identify just by their smell from the other room.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Sample Answers:
1. Fried samosas / Halwa (strong ghee and spice aroma).
2. A ripe mango / Peeling an orange (sweet citrus fragrance).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Two aromatic food items named 2 M
Q5 Inclusion & Empathy • Assisting Others
How can you help a visually challenged person who wants to cross a busy road?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Answer:
1. Gently ask politely: 'May I help you cross the road?'
2. Offer your arm for them to hold comfortably.
3. Look carefully in both directions for traffic, and guide them safely across the zebra crossing.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Polite permission asked 1 M
Offering arm guidance 1 M
Safe road crossing vigilance 1 M
Q6 Knowledge of Braille Script
What is Braille, and how do visually impaired people read books using it?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Explanation:
• Braille is a special writing system invented by Louis Braille.
• It uses patterns of raised dots embossed on thick paper.
• Blind people glide their fingertips over the dots to feel letters and read words fluently.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Braille definition 1 M
Raised dots system explained 1 M
Fingertip reading mechanism described 1 M

Q8 Walking Cane Purpose
Why do visually impaired people carry a white folding cane when walking outside?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Explanation:
• The white cane acts as an extension of their hand.
• Tapping it lightly on the ground ahead alerts them to obstacles, steps, puddles, or curbs, keeping them safe from tripping.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Detecting ground obstacles explained 1 M
Safety and mobility independence explained 1 M
Q9 Sound Identification in Nature
Name two sounds in nature that tell you rain is coming even before you see raindrops.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
1. The loud rumbling of thunder in the clouds.
2. The rushing whoosh of cool gusty wind rustling tree leaves.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Thunder named 1 M
Wind rustling leaves named 1 M
Q10 Vocabulary • Five Senses
Complete the sentence: Sight is to eyes as hearing is to _______.
[1 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
• Sight is to eyes as hearing is to ears.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Ears correct 1 M
Q11 Empathy & Friendship
How should we treat classmates who cannot see or hear well?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
• We should treat them with warmth, respect, and equality.
• We should include them in our classroom games, share stories, and never tease or exclude them.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Respect and equality 1 M
Inclusive play and kindness 1 M

Q13 Safety Sense • Danger Smells
How does our sense of smell protect us from danger in the kitchen?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Explanation:
• If a cooking gas cylinder leaks or something burns on the stove, our nose immediately detects the sharp smell.
• This alerts adults to turn off the gas and prevent fires.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Detecting gas leak / burning smell 1 M
Preventing fire accidents explained 1 M

Q15 True or False
State True or False:
(a) Blind people cannot enjoy listening to music.
(b) Our tongue helps us know if food is sweet or salty.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answers:
(a) False. Blind people have excellent hearing and enjoy beautiful songs and instrumental music.
(b) True. Taste buds on the tongue detect sweet, salty, sour, and bitter tastes.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
(a) False with reason 1 M
(b) True 1 M
Q16 Chapter Reflection
What is the most important lesson you learned in 'Seeing without Seeing'?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Summary:
• We learned that seeing is not the only way to experience our beautiful world.
• Touch, hearing, smell, and taste provide deep understanding, and we must appreciate our senses while respecting and assisting people with disabilities.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Multisensory understanding explained 1 M
Respect and assistance emphasized 1 M

[1 Marks]

Answer:
• Our skin covers our whole body and gives us the sense of touch.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Skin identified 1 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Classroom Sensory Mystery Box

The teacher placed a soft velvet handkerchief over Onu's eyes. She put three objects in front of him: a chilled steel spoon, a fragrant jasmine flower, and a ringing brass bell. Onu identified every single item correctly within five seconds.

Analytical Task: Which sense did Onu use to identify the chilled spoon? Which for the jasmine flower? Which for the bell? What does this show about our body?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Chilled spoon: Sense of Touch (cold and hard smooth metal).
2. Jasmine flower: Sense of Smell (sweet floral fragrance).
3. Brass bell: Sense of Hearing (ringing sound).
4. Learning: When we cannot see, our other senses become sharp and help us understand the world around us.
Rubric: 1 M for touch + 1 M for smell & hearing + 1 M for sensory power conclusion
