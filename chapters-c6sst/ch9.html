Chapter 9: Family and Community

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond (Class 6) — 100% Complete Textbook Questions, The Big Questions, Equal Domestic Chores, Community Support & CBSE Rubrics | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Civic & Social Concepts
  • Family as the Basic Unit: The family is our primary social institution providing love, security, emotional nurturing, and values.
    • Nuclear Family: Consists of parents and their children living together.
    • Joint / Extended Family: Multiple generations living together, sharing meals, resources, and caring for elders and youngsters collectively.
  • Equal Division of Domestic Chores: Modern families break gender stereotypes. Domestic chores (cooking, cleaning, laundry, grocery shopping) and caregiving are the shared responsibility of all members regardless of gender.
  • Community Living: A community consists of people living together in a neighborhood or village who share common resources (parks, water, streetlights, security) and support one another.
  • Civic Sense: Social etiquette and civic responsibility toward public spaces—proper garbage segregation, conserving public water and electricity, observing queue discipline, and avoiding noise pollution.

The Big Questions (Chapter Inquiry & Openers)
Big Q1. Why Is the Family the Foundational Unit of Society? Concept ▼

Question: Why is the family considered the foundational unit of human society? What essential roles does it perform for children and elders?

1. Primary Socialization & Love:
The family is the first social space where a child learns language, moral values, cultural manners, empathy, and emotional security.

2. Comprehensive Care & Support System:
Family members care for each other across all stages of life—nurturing newborn babies, educating growing children, and providing medical care, dignity, and companionship to elderly grandparents.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for primary socialization and moral foundation + 1.5 Marks for lifelong caregiving system across generations.

Big Q2. Why Do Communities Need Shared Rules and Civic Sense? Explain ▼

Question: How do communities support families, and why do human societies require shared rules and civic sense to function peacefully?

1. Community Support to Families:
No single family can build its own roads, power stations, hospitals, schools, or water supply. Communities combine resources to create shared public infrastructure and provide safety during crises.

2. Need for Shared Rules & Civic Sense:
Without rules, individuals would act solely out of self-interest, causing chaos, traffic jams, pollution, and conflict. Civic sense ensures that every resident respects public spaces, disposes of garbage properly, and lives in peace with their neighbours.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for community infrastructure & support + 1.5 Marks for shared rules preventing conflict and ensuring harmony.

Feature Nuclear Family Joint / Extended Family
Composition Consists of parents and their unmarried children living together. Consists of multiple generations (grandparents, parents, uncles, aunts, cousins) living under one roof.

Q2. Rules in the Family and Neighborhood: Need & Reform Think & Answer ▼

Question: Why are rules necessary in a family and in a neighborhood? Can rules sometimes be unfair or restrictive? Give examples of how unfair rules can be discussed and reformed. [4 Marks]

1. Why Rules Are Essential:
• In a family, rules (like screen-time limits, bedtime, shared chore schedules) maintain health, study habits, and harmony.
• In a neighborhood, rules (parking designated spots, no loud music late at night, pet waste disposal) prevent daily disputes.

2. Unfair or Restrictive Rules:
Rules can be unfair when they discriminate based on gender (e.g., asking only girls to wash dishes and serve meals while boys play, or allowing boys to stay out late while restricting girls).

3. Reforming Unfair Rules:
Unfair rules should be questioned respectfully through open family discussions. For instance, children and parents can sit together to create a rotating chore chart where all family members take turns cooking, cleaning, and doing laundry regardless of gender.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for necessity of rules + 1 Mark for identifying gender-biased unfair rules + 1.5 Marks for democratic reform via dialogue.

Q3. Community Support in Real-Life Scenarios Short ▼

Question: Describe how community support makes a critical difference in each of the following situations:
(a) During festivals and weddings
(b) During emergencies like floods or the COVID-19 pandemic
(c) Through collective initiatives like community kitchens (*Langar*) and cleanliness drives [3 Marks]

(a) Festivals and Weddings:
Neighbours volunteer to decorate streets, arrange food, welcome guests, and lend extra furniture, transforming family celebrations into joyful collective events.

(b) Emergencies and Disasters:
During floods or the COVID-19 lockdown, community groups organized food delivery to elderly patients, arranged emergency oxygen and medicines, and distributed masks, saving lives through immediate localized solidarity.

(c) Collective Welfare Initiatives:
Community kitchens (like Sikh Langars) feed thousands of hungry people daily without discrimination. Citizen-led cleanliness drives (*Swachhata Abhiyan*) and tree-planting drives keep neighborhoods healthy and green.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark each for celebrations, emergency disaster response, and collective public welfare initiatives.

Q4. Equal Sharing of Domestic Chores at Home Explain ▼

Question: Why is it vital that household chores and caregiving are shared equally among all family members regardless of gender? [3 Marks]

1. Eradicating Unfair Burden on Women:
Historically, women and girls have shouldered nearly all unpaid cooking, sweeping, washing, and care work, causing physical exhaustion and preventing them from pursuing education or careers.

2. Teaching Responsibility and Life Skills:
Cooking, cleaning, and managing a household are essential life skills that every human being (boy or girl) needs to live independently.

3. Fostering Mutual Respect and Domestic Joy:
When fathers, mothers, sons, and daughters share household chores as a united team, it builds deep affection, respect, and gender equality right at home.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for reducing unfair burden on women + 1 Mark for essential life skills + 1 Mark for family teamwork and gender equality.

Q5. Civic Sense and Protecting Public Spaces Short ▼

Question: What is civic sense? Give three examples of how practicing civic sense shows respect for public property and other citizens. [3 Marks]

1. Definition:
Civic sense is the social awareness and consideration for the welfare, rights, and comfort of fellow citizens in public spaces.

2. Three Concrete Examples:
• Waste Disposal: Never littering on roads or throwing wrappers out of car windows; segregating wet and dry garbage into public bins.
• Respecting Public Transport: Standing in queue without pushing, offering seats to the elderly, pregnant women, or persons with disabilities on buses and metro trains.
• Protecting Public Monuments and Parks: Never scribbling graffiti on historic monuments, plucking flowers in parks, or playing loudspeakers late at night.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for definition of civic sense + 2 Marks for three concrete examples of respectful civic behaviour.

Q6. Resolving Neighborhood Disputes Through Dialogue Think & Answer ▼

Question: Imagine two neighbours are arguing heatedly over parking space outside their houses. How can such a dispute be resolved peacefully through community dialogue? [3 Marks]

1. Calming Down and Active Listening:
Both parties should step back, avoid shouting or name-calling, and listen patiently to understand each other's genuine parking concerns and timings.

2. Finding a Mutually Agreed Solution:
They can agree on a shared parking schedule (e.g., parking one car slightly ahead, marking designated slots), or park in a nearby designated common community parking lot.

3. Involving Resident Welfare Associations (RWA):
If direct conversation stalls, they can request the local RWA committee or respected village/neighborhood elders to mediate fairly and set clear parking guidelines for the entire street.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for active listening/calm communication + 1 Mark for mutual compromise/parking schedule + 1 Mark for community mediation via RWA.

Q7. Project: Three-Generation Family Tree & Oral History Project ▼

Question: Describe how you would prepare a 3-generation family tree and conduct an oral history interview comparing life across generations. [3 Marks]

1. Constructing the Family Tree:
Draw a chart starting with Grandparents (Generation 1) → Parents, Uncles, Aunts (Generation 2) → Siblings and Cousins (Generation 3), writing their names and birth years.

2. Conducting the Interview:
Ask grandparents and parents questions about:
• Schooling: Did they walk to village schools, use slates, or have digital boards?
• Transportation: Did they use bullock carts, bicycles, or buses compared to modern metro/cars?
• Occupations: Has the family shifted from traditional agriculture to service/technology jobs?

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for 3-generation family tree structure + 1.5 Marks for oral interview questions on changes in schooling, transport, and jobs.
