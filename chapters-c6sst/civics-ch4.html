Chapter 20: Key Elements of a Democratic Government

NCERT Social and Political Life - I (Class 6) — South Africa's struggle against Apartheid (Nelson Mandela & ANC), Participation through regular elections, Other forms of participation (protests, rallies, strikes), Resolving conflicts peacefully, Rivers as a source of conflict (Cauvery river water dispute), Equality and Justice. | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Concepts & Formulas
  • Apartheid: System of racial segregation in South Africa where white minority ruled and black, Indian, and colored races were denied citizenship rights and separate hospitals, buses, and schools.
  • Nelson Mandela: Leader of the African National Congress (ANC); imprisoned for 27 years on Robben Island; triumphed in 1994 when South Africa became a democratic multiracial nation.
  • Citizen Participation: Regular voting in elections (every 5 years in India) holds governments accountable. Other ways: Dharnas, rallies, strikes, signature campaigns, public debates, and media scrutiny.
  • Resolving Conflicts: The Police and Judiciary resolve conflicts peacefully according to law.
  • Inter-State River Disputes: The Cauvery River dispute between Karnataka (Krishnarajasagara dam) and Tamil Nadu (Mettur dam) requires central tribunal adjudication.
  • Equality and Justice are Inseparable: A society cannot be truly just without equality (e.g., banning female infanticide, waiving tuition fees for girls).
NCERT Textbook Exercise & Practice Questions
Q1
How did Nelson Mandela fight against the Apartheid system in South Africa? What are the key elements of a democratic government?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Anti-Apartheid Struggle & Democracy:
• Nelson Mandela's Struggle: Mandela led the African National Congress (ANC) in a relentless multi-decade struggle against the brutal racial segregation of Apartheid. Despite enduring 27 years of solitary imprisonment, his moral resolve never broke, leading to the collapse of the white supremacist regime in 1994 and the birth of a multiracial democracy.
• Key Elements of Democracy:
1. People's participation through free and fair periodic elections.
2. Peaceful resolution of conflicts through constitutional courts and dialogue.
3. Uncompromising commitment to Equality and Justice for all citizens.

Q2
Why do rivers become a source of conflict between states? Explain using the Cauvery river dispute.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Inter-State Water Conflicts:
• Rivers flow across multiple states. When upstream states construct massive storage reservoirs to irrigate their own farmlands and cities, the downstream flow to neighboring states is drastically curtailed, creating severe water crises.
• Cauvery River Case: Karnataka constructed the Krishnarajasagara dam upstream to supply water to Bengaluru and irrigate Mandya district. Downstream in Tamil Nadu, the Mettur dam irrigates the fertile Cauvery delta. The Mettur dam can only fill if Karnataka releases water from upstream, leading to acute inter-state friction requiring central Supreme Court and tribunal adjudication.

CBSE Competency-Based Case Study (NEP 2020)
Environmental Federalism: Inter-State River Basin Management:
Climate change is altering monsoon precipitation, exacerbating river disputes across India. (a) Why can one state not claim absolute sovereign ownership over river water flowing through its territory? (b) What constitutional mechanism resolves inter-state river disputes in India?
Detailed Analytical Solution & Rubric
(a) Riparian Rights: Under international and constitutional riparian law, flowing water is a shared natural commons; an upstream state cannot deprive downstream ecosystems and populations of historical equitable flow.
(b) Constitutional Mechanism: Article 262 of the Indian Constitution empowers Parliament to enact the *Inter-State River Water Disputes Act*, establishing judicial tribunals whose awards have the force of Supreme Court decrees.
