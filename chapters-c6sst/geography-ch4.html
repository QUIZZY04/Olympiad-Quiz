Chapter 14: Maps

NCERT The Earth: Our Habitat (Class 6) — Maps vs Globes, Types of Maps (Physical, Political, Thematic), Three Components of Maps (Distance/Scale, Direction/Cardinals, Symbols/Conventional colours), Sketch and Plan. | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Concepts & Formulas
  • Map: A representation or a drawing of the earth's surface or a part of it drawn on a flat surface according to a scale.
  • Types: Physical (mountains, rivers, plateaus), Political (cities, states, countries, boundaries), Thematic (rainfall, road networks, industries).
  • Three Components: Distance (Scale: small scale vs large scale), Direction (Cardinals: N, S, E, W and Intermediate: NE, SE, SW, NW), Symbols (Conventional signs).
  • Sketch (rough drawing without scale) vs Plan (drawing of a small area on a large scale, like dimensions of rooms in a building).
NCERT Textbook Exercise & Practice Questions
Q1
What are the three components of a map? Differentiate between a small scale map and a large scale map.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Cartographic Fundamentals:
• Three Components: Distance (Scale), Direction, and Symbols.
• Small Scale Map: Used to show large land areas like continents or countries on paper using a small representative ratio (e.g., 5 cm on map = 500 km on ground). Gives less detailed information (e.g., Atlas maps).
• Large Scale Map: Used to show small geographic areas like a village or town in high detail (e.g., 5 cm on map = 500 metres on ground). Shows individual streets, ponds, and buildings.

Q2
Distinguish between a map and a plan. Why are conventional symbols used in maps?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Map vs Plan & Symbology:
• Map: A drawing of the Earth's surface or part of it drawn to scale on a flat sheet, typically depicting natural and political landscapes.
• Plan: A detailed technical drawing of a small area on a very large scale, revealing precise dimensions that cannot be shown on a map (such as length and breadth of individual rooms in an apartment).
• Conventional Symbols: Physical features like bridges, railway lines, trees, dams, and telegraph poles cannot be drawn to their actual shape and size on a sheet of paper. Standard universally recognized symbols, letters, colors, and line types allow rich geographical data to be packed into minimal space.

CBSE Competency-Based Case Study (NEP 2020)
GIS & Digital Navigation: Satellite GPS vs Cadastral Maps:
A land revenue officer uses a cadastral plan, while a tourist uses Google Maps navigation. (a) What type of map is Google Maps when showing highway traffic congestion? (b) Why must property deed boundaries rely on large-scale cadastral plans rather than small-scale regional maps?
Detailed Analytical Solution & Rubric
(a) Thematic Map: Real-time dynamic thematic map showing variable vehicular density and speed metrics.
(b) Cadastral Precision: Property ownership disputes require millimeter-level legal precision of plot boundaries, survey numbers, and fencing lines, which only large-scale cadastral plans provide.
