Chapter 5: India, That Is Bharat

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond (Class 6) — 100% Complete Textbook Questions, The Big Questions, True/False, Derivations & CBSE Rubrics | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Historical & Geographical Concepts
  • Multiple Historical Names:
    • Bharat / Bharatvarsha: Rooted in the Rigveda, named after the prominent Vedic tribe/clan of the Bharatas led by King Sudas. Later literature (Puranas, Mahabharata) defined Bharatvarsha as the land situated north of the oceans and south of the snowy Himalayas (Uttaram yat samudrasya...).
    • Jambudvipa: In ancient Buddhist, Jain, and Hindu texts, as well as Ashokan inscriptions, the central continent containing India was designated Jambudvipa ("Island of the Jamun / Rose-Apple tree").
    • Sapta Sindhu: The land of seven sacred rivers mentioned in the Rigveda (Indus, Sarasvati, Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej).
    • Hind / Hindustan: Derived from the Old Persian word Hindu (pronunciation of the Sanskrit river Sindhu). In 3rd century CE Sasanian Persian inscriptions of Shapur I, the land is called Hindustan.
    • India: The Greeks adapted the Persian Hindu into Indos, and the land beyond as Indike. Latin and modern European languages adopted this as India.
  • Geographical Identity of Bharatvarsha: Natural boundaries created a distinct subcontinental identity: the towering Himalayas in the north, and the vast expanse of the Indian Ocean, Arabian Sea, and Bay of Bengal surrounding the south.
  • Constitutional Synthesis: Article 1(1) of the Constitution of India formally unifies this timeless heritage: "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States."
  • Civilisational Continuity: Unlike ancient civilisations that vanished entirely (like Mesopotamia or ancient Egypt), India demonstrates unbroken cultural, philosophical, and social continuity spanning over 5,000 years.

The Big Questions (Chapter Inquiry & Openers)
Big Q1. What Were the Ancient Names of Our Subcontinent? Concept ▼

Question: What were the various names by which our country was known in ancient literature and foreign records, and what are their linguistic origins?

1. Indigenous Sanskrit Names:
• Bharata / Bharatvarsha: Named after the Bharata clan in the Rigveda and defined in the Puranas as the entire subcontinent between the Himalayas and the southern seas.
• Jambudvipa: Meaning "Island of the Jamun tree," found in Buddhist texts, Puranas, and Emperor Ashoka’s 3rd century BCE rock edicts.
• Sapta Sindhu: The Vedic land of seven sacred rivers in northwestern India.

2. External Names Derived from River Sindhu:
• Hindu / Hindush: Ancient Persian pronunciation of the Sanskrit river Sindhu.
• Hindustan: Persian term meaning "Land of the Hindus/Sindhu region."
• Indos / India: Greek and Roman adaptations of the Persian name.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for indigenous names (Bharat, Jambudvipa, Sapta Sindhu) + 1.5 Marks for external names (Hindu, Hindustan, India).

Big Q2. How Was India's Geographical and Cultural Identity Conceived? Explain ▼

Question: How did ancient Indian texts view the geographical and civilisational unity of the subcontinent? Did they see it as many separate countries or one unified homeland?

1. Clear Geographical Boundaries:
Ancient texts conceived India not as scattered isolated fragments, but as a single vast geographical and civilisational entity bounded by natural frontiers—the Himalayas in the north, and the ocean in the south, east, and west.

2. Cultural and Sacred Geography:
• The Vishnu Purana explicitly states that all people living south of the Himalayas and north of the ocean belong to the same land: "Varsham tad bharatam nama bharati yatra santatih."
• The epics Mahabharata and Ramayana enumerate kingdoms from Gandhara and Kashmir in the north to Kerala and Pandya in the south, demonstrating an organic civilisational consciousness.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for natural boundaries concept + 1.5 Marks for cultural unity evidenced in Puranas and Epics.

Let's Explore & In-Text Practical Activities
Activity 1. Why Did Ancient Travelers and Scholars Travel to India? Explain ▼

Question: In-Text "Let’s Explore" Activity:
Why did ancient scholars, pilgrims, and travelers like Xuanzang, Megasthenes, and Al-Biruni undertake perilous journeys across deserts and mountain passes to visit India?

1. Seeking Knowledge and Sacred Texts:
Chinese Buddhist monks like Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang) and Faxian traveled across Central Asia to study at world-renowned Indian universities like Nalanda, collect authentic Buddhist manuscripts, and visit sacred pilgrimage sites associated with the Buddha.

2. Trade and Economic Wealth:
Traders from Rome, Greece, Arabia, and Persia came to purchase India's exquisite spices (black pepper, cardamom), fine cotton textiles (muslin), silk, pearls, and ivory.

3. Cultural Curiosity and Philosophy:
Scholars like Megasthenes (Greek ambassador) and Al-Biruni (Persian scholar) came to observe India's advanced astronomy, mathematics, medicine (Ayurveda), and philosophical systems.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for study at Nalanda/sacred Buddhist texts + 1 Mark for lucrative spice & textile trade + 1 Mark for scientific & philosophical curiosity.

Questions, activities and projects (Official NCERT Textbook Exercises)
Q1. Sri Aurobindo Quotation on India's Cultural Unity Explain ▼

Question: Discuss the significance of Sri Aurobindo's quotation regarding the spiritual and cultural unity of India: How did ancient India achieve deep unity despite having diverse languages, customs, and rulers? [3 Marks]

1. Core Essence of Sri Aurobindo's Thought:
Sri Aurobindo observed that India's unity was never imposed through military conquest or uniform political tyranny; rather, it was built on a deep spiritual and cultural foundation.

2. Mechanisms of Ancient Unity:
• Shared Sacred Epics & Literature: The Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Puranas were translated and dramatized in every regional language across the subcontinent.
• Network of Pilgrimage (Tīrtha Yātrā): Sacred centers located in the four corners of India (Badrinath in the north, Rameshwaram in the south, Puri in the east, and Dwarka in the west) encouraged people to travel across regions, fostering mutual respect.
• Philosophy of Oneness (Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam): The civilisational belief that diverse paths lead to the same ultimate truth allowed multiple languages and local customs to flourish harmoniously within one umbrella.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for understanding spiritual/cultural unity + 2 Marks for explaining pilgrimage networks, shared epics, and philosophical pluralism.

Q2. True / False Statements with Historical Reasons Short ▼

Question: State whether the following statements are True or False. Give valid historical reasons for each:
(i) The Rigveda describes the geography of the entire Indian subcontinent.
(ii) The Vishnu Purana provides a clear geographical boundary of the entire subcontinent from the Himalayas to the ocean.
(iii) The name 'Jambudvipa' was used in Emperor Ashoka's rock edicts.
(iv) The Mahabharata lists regions like Kashmir, Kutch, and Kerala, showing an awareness of the whole land.
(v) The term 'Hindustan' was first introduced in an ancient Greek inscription.

  • (i) False: The Rigveda primarily describes the northwestern region of the subcontinent known as Sapta Sindhu (land of seven rivers), not the entire southern peninsula or northeastern regions.
  • (ii) True: The famous verse in the Vishnu Purana defines Bharatvarsha as the land situated north of the ocean and south of the snowy Himalayas.
  • (iii) True: Emperor Ashoka explicitly inscribed the name Jambudvipa on his Major Rock Edicts to describe his vast realm.
  • (iv) True: The Sabha Parva of the Mahabharata details royal expeditions and lists kingdoms stretching across Kashmir, Kutch, Assam (Kamarupa), and Kerala.
  • (v) False: The term 'Hindustan' was first used in a 3rd century CE Sasanian Persian rock inscription of King Shapur I, not in a Greek inscription (the Greeks used Indos / Indike).
🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 0.5 Mark for each True/False answer + 0.5 Mark for valid historical reason (Total 5 Marks).

Q3. Linguistic Derivation: Sindhu to India vs. Bharat Explain ▼

Question: Trace the linguistic evolution from the Sanskrit word "Sindhu" to the modern name "India". How does the origin of the name "Bharat" differ from this?

1. Evolution of "India":

  • Sanskrit Root: Sindhu (the great northwestern river Indus).
  • Old Persian: Persians replaced the "S" sound with "H", pronouncing the river as Hindu. In King Darius I's inscriptions (c. 518 BCE), the region was called Hindush.
  • Greek: Greeks dropped the "H" aspirate, referring to the river as Indos and the country as Indike.
  • Latin & English: Romans adapted this into India, which became the standard international name.

2. Contrast with "Bharat":
While "India" is an external geographical name given by foreign traders and travelers based on a border river, "Bharat" is an indigenous, cultural name rooted in the Vedic Bharata clan, celebrating internal civilisational heritage and righteous governance.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for tracing Sindhu → Hindu → Indos → India + 1.5 Marks for contrasting with Vedic indigenous origin of Bharat.

Q4. Vishnu Purana Shloka and Subcontinental Borders Describe ▼

Question: Quote or explain the famous shloka from Vishnu Purana defining Bharatvarsha. What does it prove about ancient Indian geographical awareness? [3 Marks]

The Sanskrit Shloka (Vishnu Purana 2.3.1):
"Uttaram yat samudrasya himadreschaiva dakshinam |
Varsham tad bharatam nama bharati yatra santatih ||"

Meaning:
"The country that lies north of the ocean and south of the snowy mountains is called Bharat; there dwell the descendants of Bharata (the Bharati)."

Significance:
This shloka demonstrates that ancient Indian scholars had a remarkably precise geographical map of the subcontinent thousands of years ago, perceiving it as a single natural, bounded homeland.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for quoting or explaining the verse + 1 Mark for exact boundaries (Himalayas in north, oceans in south) + 1 Mark for historical significance.

Q5. Role of Natural Boundaries & Mountain Passes Long Answer ▼

Question: How did the natural boundaries of the Indian subcontinent (the Himalayas and oceans) provide security, while mountain passes enabled cultural and economic exchange? [4 Marks]

1. Protective Barriers:
• The Himalayas in the North: Formidable physical wall that stopped freezing polar winds from Central Asia and provided a natural defence barrier against northern military invasions.
• The Seas in the South, East, and West: The Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal, and Indian Ocean surrounded the peninsula, offering natural protection and fish resources.

2. Mountain Passes as Bridges:
• The high northwestern mountains (Hindu Kush, Sulaiman) are pierced by natural mountain passes such as the Khyber Pass and Bolan Pass.
• These passes served as vital trade and cultural highways, allowing traders, pilgrims, scholars, and monks to carry Buddhism, mathematics, spices, and silk across Asia along the Silk Route.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 2 Marks for Himalayan and ocean natural barriers + 2 Marks for northwestern passes as economic and cultural bridges.

Q6. Article 1(1) of the Indian Constitution: "India, that is Bharat" Short ▼

Question: What does Article 1(1) of the Constitution of India state? Why did the framers of the Constitution decide to include both "India" and "Bharat"? [3 Marks]

1. Constitutional Text:
Article 1(1) states: "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States."

2. Rationale of the Constituent Assembly (1949):
• Bharat: Celebrates our millennia-old civilisational identity, cultural roots, and emotional heritage.
• India: Was the well-established international name recognized across treaties, the United Nations, and global diplomatic relations.
• By adopting "India, that is Bharat", the framers harmoniously bridged ancient civilisational continuity with modern democratic nationhood.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for quoting Article 1(1) + 2 Marks for explaining the dual significance of ancient cultural heritage (Bharat) and international diplomacy (India).
