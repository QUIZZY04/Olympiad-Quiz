Chapter 10: Grassroots Democracy — Part 1: Governance

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond (Class 6) — 100% Complete Textbook Questions, The Big Questions, Levels & Organs of Government, Case Study & CBSE Rubrics | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Political Science Concepts: State, Democracy & Government
  • What is Government? The governing body of a nation, state, or community that makes decisions, implements laws, manages public services, maintains law and order, and protects territorial borders.
  • Three Tiers / Levels of Government in India:
    • Local Level: Governs villages (Panchayati Raj) and towns/cities (Municipalities). Handles local drinking water, streetlights, sanitation, and primary health.
    • State Level: Governs an entire state (e.g., Uttar Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra). Led by the Chief Minister and Governor; handles state police, highways, agriculture, and schools.
    • National / Union Level: Governs the entire country from New Delhi. Led by the Prime Minister and President; handles national defence, foreign affairs, railways, and currency.
  • Three Organs of Government:
    • Legislature: The law-making body (Parliament at Union level; Vidhan Sabha at State level).
    • Executive: Implements and enforces laws (President, Prime Minister, Ministers, and Civil Servants/Police).
    • Judiciary: Interprets laws, protects fundamental rights, and resolves disputes (Supreme Court, High Courts, District Courts).
  • Universal Adult Franchise: Every Indian citizen who is 18 years of age or older has the right to vote without discrimination based on caste, religion, wealth, or gender (One person, One vote, One value).

The Big Questions (Chapter Inquiry & Openers)
Big Q1. What Is Governance, and Why Do People Need Governments? Concept ▼

Question: What is governance? Why cannot a society function peacefully without an established government?

1. Definition of Governance:
Governance is the organized process of making and implementing collective decisions, maintaining public order, delivering public goods, and upholding laws for the welfare of all citizens.

2. Why Society Needs Government:
Without a government, there would be no common laws, no police to protect people from crime, no courts to resolve disputes fairly, and no public funding to build highways, hospitals, schools, or military defence.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for definition of governance + 1.5 Marks for essential functions (order, justice, public goods, security).

Big Q2. What Is Democracy and What Are Its Key Principles? Explain ▼

Question: What is democracy? What essential principles make democratic governance superior to other systems?

1. Definition:
Democracy is government "of the people, by the people, and for the people." Ultimate political authority rests with the citizens.

2. Core Principles:
• Popular Consent & Regular Elections: Citizens freely choose their leaders at regular intervals through secret ballot.
• Rule of Law: Every citizen, including the Prime Minister, is subject to the same laws.
• Protection of Fundamental Rights: Guaranteed freedom of speech, religion, and equality before the law.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for definition + 2 Marks for regular elections, rule of law, and protection of rights.

Questions, activities and projects (Official NCERT Textbook Exercises)
Q1. What Is Government? Four Major Responsibilities Short ▼

Question: What is a government? List four major responsibilities of a government in a democratic society. [3 Marks]

1. Definition:
A government is the constitutional political authority empowered to make, administer, and enforce laws for a nation or state.

2. Four Major Responsibilities:
• Maintaining Law and Order: Running the police and courts to protect citizens from crime and injustice.
• Providing Public Infrastructure: Building highways, railways, public hospitals, schools, and drinking water facilities.
• National Defence: Protecting territorial borders from foreign invasion through the armed forces.
• Disaster Relief & Welfare: Organizing emergency rescue, financial relief, and food supply during floods, droughts, or epidemics.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for definition + 2 Marks for four clear responsibilities (0.5 Mark each).

Q2. Three Levels of Government in India: Roles & Examples Explain ▼

Question: Explain the three levels of government in India with their jurisdiction and practical examples. [3 Marks]

1. Local Level (Grassroots):
Governs a village, town, or city. Handles local sanitation, streetlights, parks, and primary healthcare centers (e.g., a Gram Panchayat building a village well or Delhi Municipal Corporation managing garbage).

2. State Level:
Governs an entire state (e.g., Karnataka, Rajasthan, Punjab). Handles law and order (State Police), state highways, agriculture, and high school education boards.

3. Union / National Level:
Governs the entire country from New Delhi. Responsible for issues of national importance like defence (Army, Navy, Air Force), foreign affairs, atomic energy, Indian Railways, and printing national currency.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for each level explained with jurisdiction and clear examples (Total 3 Marks).

Q3. Three Organs of Government: Legislature, Executive & Judiciary Compare ▼

Question: Describe the three organs of government and their specific functions under the Indian Constitution. [3 Marks]

Organ Primary Constitutional Function Key Institutions & Heads
Legislature Drafts, debates, and passes new laws and approves the national budget. Parliament (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha); State Legislative Assemblies (Vidhan Sabha).
Executive Implements and enforces laws passed by the legislature. President, Prime Minister, Union Council of Ministers, Civil Servants (IAS/IPS), and Police.
Judiciary Interprets laws, protects citizens' fundamental rights, and settles legal disputes. Supreme Court of India (Chief Justice), High Courts, and District/Session Courts.
🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark each for describing Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary accurately.

Q4. Direct Democracy vs. Representative Democracy Compare ▼

Question: Differentiate between Direct Democracy and Representative Democracy. Why does India follow a representative democracy? [3 Marks]

1. Direct Democracy:
All adult citizens gather together directly in a public assembly to vote on laws, policies, and decisions (e.g., ancient Athens, or today's Gram Sabha at the village level in India).

2. Representative Democracy:
Citizens elect representatives (Members of Parliament - MPs, Members of Legislative Assembly - MLAs) through periodic elections to make laws and govern on their behalf.

3. Why India Follows Representative Democracy:
India has a population of over 1.4 billion people spread across 3.28 million square kilometers. It is physically impossible for hundreds of millions of citizens to sit in one room every week to debate and vote on thousands of complex national laws.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for Direct Democracy + 1 Mark for Representative Democracy + 1 Mark for India's large population and geographic size.

Q5. Universal Adult Franchise: Meaning and Importance Short ▼

Question: What is Universal Adult Franchise? Why is it considered a cornerstone of Indian democracy? [3 Marks]

1. Definition:
Universal Adult Franchise means that every citizen who has attained the age of 18 years has the right to vote in elections, without any discrimination based on religion, race, caste, gender, or wealth.

2. Core Importance:
• Equal Political Voice: It enshrines the principle of "One person, one vote, one value." The vote of a billionaire industrialist carries the exact same mathematical weight as the vote of a poor daily-wage labourer.
• Democratic Accountability: It forces politicians and ministers to serve all sections of the public, knowing they can be voted out of power if they ignore ordinary citizens.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for definition (age 18 without discrimination) + 2 Marks for equal political voice and democratic accountability.

Q6. Case Study: Governance During the COVID-19 Pandemic Project ▼

Question: Analyze how the three levels (Union, State, Local) and three organs (Legislature, Executive, Judiciary) of government cooperated during the COVID-19 pandemic crisis. [4 Marks]

1. Action by the Three Levels:
• Union Level: Procured and distributed COVID vaccines, imposed national travel restrictions, and provided emergency grains under PMGKAY.
• State Level: Built dedicated COVID hospitals, mobilized state medical staff, and enforced containment zone rules.
• Local Level (Panchayats & Municipalities): Sanitized residential streets, distributed ration packets, and conducted door-to-door temperature and symptom surveys.

2. Action by the Three Organs:
• Legislature: Passed relief budgets and emergency healthcare funds.
• Executive: Doctors, police officers, and district magistrates worked on the frontlines 24/7 enforcing safety protocols.
• Judiciary: High Courts and Supreme Court monitored oxygen supply, vaccine pricing, and protected the rights of migrant workers.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 2 Marks for actions across the 3 levels + 2 Marks for actions across the 3 organs.

Q7. Democracy vs. Monarchy vs. Dictatorship Compare ▼

Question: Compare Democracy, Monarchy, and Dictatorship across source of authority, citizen freedoms, and accountability to the public. [3 Marks]

System Source of Authority Citizen Freedoms Accountability
Democracy Elected by the citizens through free and fair votes. Guaranteed fundamental freedoms (speech, protest, equality). Fully accountable; can be removed in the next election.
Monarchy Hereditary succession (King/Queen inherits the throne). Limited; depends entirely on the king's personal benevolence. Not accountable to citizens; power held for life.
Dictatorship Seized through military force or coup by a single tyrant. Severely suppressed; opposition jailed or silenced. Zero accountability; citizens have no legal power to change rulers.
🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for each system compared accurately.
