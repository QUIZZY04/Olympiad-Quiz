Chapter 12: Grassroots Democracy — Part 3: Local Government in Urban Areas

NCERT Exploring Society: India and Beyond (Class 6) — 100% Complete Textbook Questions, Municipal Corporations, Interview Project, Waste Segregation & CBSE Rubrics | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Urban Governance Concepts: Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)
  • Types of Urban Local Bodies:
    • Municipal Corporation (*Nagar Nigam*): Administers large cities with populations exceeding 10 lakhs (e.g., Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru, Chennai). Headed politically by the Mayor.
    • Municipal Council (*Nagar Palika*): Administers medium and smaller towns with populations between 20,000 and 10 lakhs.
    • Nagar Panchayat: Governs transitional areas transforming from rural villages to small towns.
  • Elected Councillors vs. Municipal Commissioner:
    • Ward Councillors (*Parshad*): Elected by citizens from their respective neighbourhood wards for a 5-year term. They debate policies and represent citizens' grievances.
    • Municipal Commissioner: An administrative officer (usually an IAS officer) appointed by the State Government who executes decisions and heads the city bureaucracy.
  • Municipal Services: Potable water supply, solid waste management, street lighting, public health, primary schools, parks, road repair, and issuing birth/death certificates.
  • Revenue Sources: Property tax, water/sewerage cess, trade license fees, entertainment tax, and state financial grants.

The Big Questions (Chapter Inquiry & Openers)
Big Q1. Who Runs Our Towns and Cities? How Do Urban Bodies Function? Concept ▼

Question: Who is responsible for running our complex towns and cities? How do urban local bodies organize and deliver civic services?

1. Urban Local Bodies (Municipalities):
Cities are governed by Municipal Corporations and smaller towns by Municipal Councils. Cities are divided into territorial segments called wards.

2. Operational Division:
Elected Ward Councillors form committees (Water, Sanitation, Public Works) to decide policies and allocate budget, while the Municipal Commissioner and specialized departments (Health, Engineering, Horticulture) execute the daily municipal operations.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for defining Municipalities/Corporations + 1.5 Marks for ward divisions and committee execution.

Big Q2. How Do Citizens Hold Municipal Authorities Accountable? Explain ▼

Question: How can urban citizens interact with and hold municipal authorities accountable for poor services?

1. Approaching the Ward Councillor:
Residents take issues like garbage accumulation, leaking water pipes, or broken streetlights directly to their elected Councillor.

2. Collective Resident Action:
Through Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs), community petitions, digital grievance portals (e.g., municipal mobile apps), and right-to-information queries, citizens hold officials accountable.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for approaching Ward Councillors + 1.5 Marks for RWAs and grievance portals.

Questions, activities and projects (Official NCERT Textbook Exercises)
Q1. Municipal Corporation vs. Municipal Council Compare ▼

Question: Differentiate between a Municipal Corporation (*Nagar Nigam*) and a Municipal Council (*Nagar Palika*). [3 Marks]

Feature Municipal Corporation (*Nagar Nigam*) Municipal Council (*Nagar Palika*)
City Size & Population Set up in large metropolitan cities with populations exceeding 10 lakhs (1 million). Set up in medium and smaller towns with populations between 20,000 and 10 lakhs.
Political Head Headed by the Mayor. Headed by the President / Chairperson.
Administrative Head Headed by a senior IAS officer designated as the Municipal Commissioner. Headed by an executive officer designated as the Chief Municipal Officer (CMO).
🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for population criteria + 1 Mark for political head + 1 Mark for administrative officer.

Q2. Ward Councillor vs. Municipal Commissioner Compare ▼

Question: Explain the differences between a Ward Councillor and the Municipal Commissioner. [3 Marks]

1. Ward Councillor (*Parshad*):
• Elected representative: Elected directly by voters of a specific municipal ward for a 5-year term.
• Role: Debates policies, raises neighborhood complaints, and forms committees to approve municipal budgets.

2. Municipal Commissioner:
• Appointed government official: Senior civil servant (IAS) appointed by the State Government.
• Role: Administrative head who implements council decisions, supervises thousands of city employees, and controls administrative expenditure.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1.5 Marks for Ward Councillor (elected, policymaking) + 1.5 Marks for Commissioner (appointed, execution).

Q3. Essential Civic Services Provided by Municipalities Short ▼

Question: List six essential public services provided by a Municipal Corporation to city residents. [3 Marks]

  • 1. Potable Drinking Water Supply: Laying water pipelines, filtration plants, and maintaining overhead water reservoirs.
  • 2. Solid Waste Management & Sanitation: Daily door-to-door garbage collection, street sweeping, and desilting open drains.
  • 3. Street Lighting & Road Maintenance: Installing and maintaining LED streetlights, repairing potholes, and paving pedestrian sidewalks.
  • 4. Public Health & Disease Prevention: Running primary municipal clinics, organizing mosquito fogging against dengue/malaria, and childhood vaccination drives.
  • 5. Public Parks and Green Spaces: Developing and maintaining neighborhood parks, children’s play areas, and planting roadside trees.
  • 6. Registration of Vital Statistics: Issuing official Birth Certificates and Death Certificates.
🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 0.5 Mark for each civic service correctly listed (Total 3 Marks).

Q4. Sources of Revenue of a Municipal Corporation Short ▼

Question: How does a Municipal Corporation generate funds to maintain city services? List its four major revenue sources. [3 Marks]

1. Property Tax (House Tax):
Levied on all residential houses, commercial offices, and shops located within municipal city limits (generates 25–30% of revenue).

2. User Charges and Cesses:
Water bills, sewerage connection cess, and parking fees.

3. Commercial and Trade License Fees:
Fees charged for licensing restaurants, retail shops, street vendors, and cinema halls.

4. State Government Grants:
Financial allocations and project grants devolved by the State Finance Commission for large infrastructure projects.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 0.75 Mark for each correctly explained revenue source (Total 3 Marks).

Q5. Citizens' Grievance Redressal for Civic Problems Short ▼

Question: What steps can residents take when municipal services fail (e.g., garbage is not collected for days or streetlights remain broken)? [3 Marks]

1. Contacting the Ward Councillor:
Residents can meet their elected Ward Councillor at their local office and demand prompt action with municipal sanitary inspectors.

2. Logging Complaints on Municipal Portals / Helplines:
Most cities now run 24/7 toll-free helpline numbers and citizen mobile apps (like the *Swachhata App*), generating a tracking complaint number.

3. Collective Resident Petitions via RWA:
The Resident Welfare Association can submit a joint signed petition directly to the Zonal Municipal Commissioner, warning of peaceful public protests if inaction continues.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark each for meeting Councillor, digital complaint app/helpline, and RWA collective petitions.

Q6. Project: Frame 6 Questions for a Ward Councillor Interview Project ▼

Question: Prepare a set of six constructive interview questions to ask your local Ward Councillor regarding civic amenities and children's welfare in your neighborhood. [3 Marks]

  1. 1. Election & Priorities: What were your top three civic promises during your election campaign, and which ones have been completed so far?
  2. 2. Solid Waste Management: How does the municipal corporation ensure that garbage is collected daily from all houses, and where is our neighborhood's waste processed?
  3. 3. Children's Recreation: Are there plans to upgrade our local park with safe swings, walking tracks, and adequate lighting for children?
  4. 4. Safe Drinking Water: What measures are taken during monsoon months to test drinking water pipelines and prevent contamination?
  5. 5. Monsoon Preparedness: How frequently are the stormwater drains desilted in our ward to prevent waterlogging during heavy rains?
  6. 6. Youth & Citizen Participation: How can schoolchildren and resident welfare groups participate in keeping our ward clean and green?
🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 0.5 Mark for each well-framed, relevant interview question (Total 3 Marks).

Q7. Solid Waste Segregation: Green vs. Blue Bins Short ▼

Question: Why is segregation of waste at source critical in urban areas? Differentiate between biodegradable wet waste and recyclable dry waste. [3 Marks]

1. Why Segregation at Source Is Critical:
When wet and dry waste are dumped together, organic food rots into plastic, creating toxic leachate and methane gas in dumping grounds. Segregating at home allows organic waste to become rich compost while dry materials can be recycled, drastically reducing landfill burden.

2. Green Bin — Wet Biodegradable Waste:
Vegetable peels, fruit rinds, leftover food, tea leaves, eggshells, and dry leaves. These naturally decompose into organic compost.

3. Blue Bin — Dry Recyclable Waste:
Plastic bottles, paper, cardboard, glass, metal cans, and rubber. These are sorted and sent to recycling factories.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

CBSE Marking Scheme: 1 Mark for importance of segregation + 1 Mark for Green bin wet waste + 1 Mark for Blue bin dry waste.
