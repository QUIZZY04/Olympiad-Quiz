Chapter 11: The Winner

NCERT Poorvi (Class 6) — Unit 4: Sports and Wellness | Poetic Appreciation, The Anatomy of True Victory, Grit, Resilience & Sportsmanship | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Poetic Overview, Central Idea & Values
  • Central Message: "The Winner" redefines success beyond medals, ribbons, and applause. The poem highlights that a genuine winner is not someone who never faces defeat; rather, it is the person who has the grit to stand up one more time after being knocked down.
  • Key Poetic Contrasts:
    • The Quitter vs The Winner: A quitter looks at obstacles, makes excuses, and gives up when the race gets exhausting. A winner looks at obstacles as stepping stones, perseveres through tired muscles, and finishes the race with dignity.
    • Humble in Victory, Gracious in Defeat: A true winner does not gloat, mock, or show arrogance when triumphant, nor do they blame referees, wind, or luck when they lose. They congratulate competitors warmly.
  • Poetic Devices & Tone: An energetic, uplifting motivational rhythm with vivid active verbs (strive, endure, rise, conquer), encouraging children to embrace effort over anxiety of outcome.

NCERT Textbook Comprehension & Poetic Exercises
Q1. Qualities of a Winner Short ▼

Question: According to the poem, what are the primary qualities that distinguish a winner from an ordinary player?

Key Distinguishing Qualities:

  • A winner does not surrender when the path turns steep or when fatigue sets in.
  • They view failures not as final endings, but as valuable lessons to improve their technique.
  • They display grace in defeat and humility in triumph, showing constant respect to opponents and referees.
🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

2 marks: 1 mark for persistence through fatigue/failure + 1 mark for humility and respect.

Q2. Explanation of the Line: "It is the courage to continue that counts" Poetic Analysis ▼

Question: Explain the meaning of the poetic thought: "Success is not final, failure is not fatal: it is the courage to continue that counts."

Explanation and Poetic Significance:

  • Success is Not Final: Achieving a victory today does not mean one can stop practicing; staying at the peak requires continuous dedication.
  • Failure is Not Fatal: Losing a match does not end life or talent; it is merely temporary.
  • Courage to Continue: The only true measure of greatness is possessing the internal grit to lace up one’s running shoes and run again tomorrow morning, regardless of yesterday’s outcome.
🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

3 marks: 1 mark for success not final + 1 mark for failure not fatal + 1 mark for endurance as the real standard.

Q3. Poetic Interpretation: "The Resilient Spirit" Poetry ▼

Question: Explain the significance of the inspirational stanza: "The winner is not the one who never fails, but the one who never quits."

Meaning & Philosophy:

Failure is an inevitable stepping stone in any human undertaking. True champions are not born without stumbling; their greatness lies in their stubborn resolve to rise every single time they fall. Giving up transforms a temporary stumble into a permanent defeat, whereas persevering turns every mistake into a valuable lesson of strength.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

3 marks: 1.5 marks interpretation + 1.5 marks resilience philosophy.

Q4. Grammar: Degrees of Comparison Grammar ▼

Question: Provide the comparative and superlative degrees for the following adjectives from the poem:
(a) Brave    (b) Strong    (c) Resilient    (d) Tough

Degrees of Comparison:

  • (a) Brave $\to$ Braver $\to$ Bravest
  • (b) Strong $\to$ Stronger $\to$ Strongest
  • (c) Resilient $\to$ More resilient $\to$ Most resilient
  • (d) Tough $\to$ Tougher $\to$ Toughest
🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

2 marks: 0.5 mark each.

Q5. Poetic Devices: Alliteration & Metaphor Poetry ▼

Question: Locate examples of alliteration in "The Winner" and explain their musical effect.

Examples like "battle of belief" and "stumble and stand" use repetition of initial consonant sounds (/b/ and /s/). This creates a driving, rhythmic momentum that inspires determination and mirrors the heartbeat of a runner pushing through fatigue.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

2 marks: 1 mark citation + 1 mark auditory effect.

Q6. Creative Writing: Personal Resilience Reflection Writing ▼

Question: Describe a moment in your school life when you faced failure and decided not to quit (50 words).

When I failed to qualify for the district debate team in grade 5, I felt utterly devastated. Instead of giving up, I spent months reading newspapers aloud and practicing speech delivery in front of the mirror. The following year, I won the first prize in the inter-school championship.

🎯 CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Exam Guide):

2 marks: personal connection.
