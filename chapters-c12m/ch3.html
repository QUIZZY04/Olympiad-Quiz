Q1
Exercise 3.1 (Verbatim)
In the matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 5 & 19 & -7 \\ 35 & -2 & \frac{5}{2} & 12 \\ \sqrt{3} & 1 & -5 & 17 \end{bmatrix}$, write:
(i) The order of the matrix,
(ii) The number of elements,
(iii) Write the elements $a_{13}, a_{21}, a_{33}, a_{24}, a_{23}$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Step-by-Step Solutions:

  1. (i) The Order of the Matrix:
    The matrix $A$ has 3 horizontal rows and 4 vertical columns.
    Therefore, the order of matrix $A$ is $3 \times 4$.
  2. (ii) The Number of Elements:
    Number of elements $= 3 \times 4 = \mathbf{12}$.
  3. (iii) Specific Element Positions ($a_{ij}$ denotes element in $i$-th row and $j$-th column):
    • $a_{13}$ (Row 1, Column 3) $= \mathbf{19}$
    • $a_{21}$ (Row 2, Column 1) $= \mathbf{35}$
    • $a_{33}$ (Row 3, Column 3) $= \mathbf{-5}$
    • $a_{24}$ (Row 2, Column 4) $= \mathbf{12}$
    • $a_{23}$ (Row 2, Column 3) $= \mathbf{\frac{5}{2}}$

Q2
Exercise 3.1 (Verbatim)
Construct a $2 \times 2$ matrix, $A = [a_{ij}]$, whose elements are given by:
(i) $a_{ij} = \frac{(i + j)^2}{2}$
(ii) $a_{ij} = \frac{i}{j}$
(iii) $a_{ij} = \frac{(i + 2j)^2}{2}$
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

A general $2 \times 2$ matrix is represented as: $$A = \begin{bmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} \end{bmatrix}$$

(i) $a_{ij} = \frac{(i + j)^2}{2}$:

  • $a_{11} = \frac{(1 + 1)^2}{2} = \frac{4}{2} = 2$
  • $a_{12} = \frac{(1 + 2)^2}{2} = \frac{9}{2}$
  • $a_{21} = \frac{(2 + 1)^2}{2} = \frac{9}{2}$
  • $a_{22} = \frac{(2 + 2)^2}{2} = \frac{16}{2} = 8$
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & \frac{9}{2} \\[6pt] \frac{9}{2} & 8 \end{bmatrix}$$

(ii) $a_{ij} = \frac{i}{j}$:

  • $a_{11} = \frac{1}{1} = 1$,   $a_{12} = \frac{1}{2}$
  • $a_{21} = \frac{2}{1} = 2$,   $a_{22} = \frac{2}{2} = 1$
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \frac{1}{2} \\[6pt] 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

(iii) $a_{ij} = \frac{(i + 2j)^2}{2}$:

  • $a_{11} = \frac{(1 + 2(1))^2}{2} = \frac{9}{2}$
  • $a_{12} = \frac{(1 + 2(2))^2}{2} = \frac{25}{2}$
  • $a_{21} = \frac{(2 + 2(1))^2}{2} = \frac{16}{2} = 8$
  • $a_{22} = \frac{(2 + 2(2))^2}{2} = \frac{36}{2} = 18$
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{9}{2} & \frac{25}{2} \\[6pt] 8 & 18 \end{bmatrix}$$

Q3
Exercise 3.1 (Verbatim)
Find the value of $a, b, c$ and $d$ from the equation: $$\begin{bmatrix} a - b & 2a + c \\ 2a - b & 3c + d \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 5 \\ 0 & 13 \end{bmatrix}$$
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Solution:

Two matrices are equal if and only if their corresponding elements are equal. Equating corresponding elements gives four simultaneous equations:

  1. $a - b = -1$   … (1)
  2. $2a + c = 5$   … (2)
  3. $2a - b = 0$   … (3)
  4. $3c + d = 13$   … (4)

Solving for $a$ and $b$:

  • From equation (3): $b = 2a$.
  • Substitute into equation (1): $$a - 2a = -1 \implies -a = -1 \implies \mathbf{a = 1}$$
  • Then: $$b = 2(1) \implies \mathbf{b = 2}$$

Solving for $c$ and $d$:

  • Substitute $a = 1$ into equation (2): $$2(1) + c = 5 \implies 2 + c = 5 \implies \mathbf{c = 3}$$
  • Substitute $c = 3$ into equation (4): $$3(3) + d = 13 \implies 9 + d = 13 \implies \mathbf{d = 4}$$

Final Answer: $\mathbf{a = 1, \quad b = 2, \quad c = 3, \quad d = 4}$.

Q4
Exercise 3.2 (Verbatim)
Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 3 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ -2 & 5 \end{bmatrix}, C = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 5 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$. Find each of the following:
(i) $A + B$
(ii) $A - B$
(iii) $3A - C$
(iv) $AB$
(v) $BA$
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution
(i) $A + B$: $$A + B = \begin{bmatrix} 2 + 1 & 4 + 3 \\ 3 + (-2) & 2 + 5 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 7 \\ 1 & 7 \end{bmatrix}}$$
(ii) $A - B$: $$A - B = \begin{bmatrix} 2 - 1 & 4 - 3 \\ 3 - (-2) & 2 - 5 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 5 & -3 \end{bmatrix}}$$
(iii) $3A - C$: $$3A = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 12 \\ 9 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$3A - C = \begin{bmatrix} 6 - (-2) & 12 - 5 \\ 9 - 3 & 6 - 4 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} 8 & 7 \\ 6 & 2 \end{bmatrix}}$$
(iv) $AB$: $$AB = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 3 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ -2 & 5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} (2)(1) + (4)(-2) & (2)(3) + (4)(5) \\ (3)(1) + (2)(-2) & (3)(3) + (2)(5) \end{bmatrix}$$ $$AB = \begin{bmatrix} 2 - 8 & 6 + 20 \\ 3 - 4 & 9 + 10 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} -6 & 26 \\ -1 & 19 \end{bmatrix}}$$
(v) $BA$: $$BA = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ -2 & 5 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 3 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} (1)(2) + (3)(3) & (1)(4) + (3)(2) \\ (-2)(2) + (5)(3) & (-2)(4) + (5)(2) \end{bmatrix}$$ $$BA = \begin{bmatrix} 2 + 9 & 4 + 6 \\ -4 + 15 & -8 + 10 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} 11 & 10 \\ 11 & 2 \end{bmatrix}}$$

Note: Notice that $AB \neq BA$, demonstrating that matrix multiplication is generally not commutative.

Q5
Exercise 3.2 (Verbatim)
Find $X$ and $Y$, if:
(i) $X + Y = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 0 \\ 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$ and $X - Y = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$
(ii) $2X + 3Y = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ and $3X + 2Y = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ -1 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution
(i) Solving for $X$ and $Y$:

Adding the two equations:

$$(X + Y) + (X - Y) = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 0 \\ 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$2X = \begin{bmatrix} 10 & 0 \\ 2 & 8 \end{bmatrix} \implies X = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} 10 & 0 \\ 2 & 8 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} 5 & 0 \\ 1 & 4 \end{bmatrix}}$$

Subtracting the two equations:

$$(X + Y) - (X - Y) = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 0 \\ 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$2Y = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 2 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \implies Y = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 2 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}$$
(ii) Solving $2X + 3Y = A$ and $3X + 2Y = B$:

Multiply equation (1) by 3 and equation (2) by 2:

$$6X + 9Y = 3\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 9 \\ 12 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \quad \dots (3)$$ $$6X + 4Y = 2\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ -1 & 5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & -4 \\ -2 & 10 \end{bmatrix} \quad \dots (4)$$

Subtracting (4) from (3):

$$5Y = \begin{bmatrix} 6 - 4 & 9 - (-4) \\ 12 - (-2) & 0 - 10 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 13 \\ 14 & -10 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\mathbf{Y = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{2}{5} & \frac{13}{5} \\[6pt] \frac{14}{5} & -2 \end{bmatrix}}$$

Now multiply (1) by 2 and (2) by 3:

$$4X + 6Y = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 6 \\ 8 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad 9X + 6Y = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & -6 \\ -3 & 15 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$5X = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & -6 \\ -3 & 15 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 6 \\ 8 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -12 \\ -11 & 15 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\mathbf{X = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{2}{5} & -\frac{12}{5} \\[6pt] -\frac{11}{5} & 3 \end{bmatrix}}$$

Q6
Exercise 3.2 (Verbatim)
If $F(x) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos x & -\sin x & 0 \\ \sin x & \cos x & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$, show that $F(x)F(y) = F(x + y)$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Proof:

Given $F(x) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos x & -\sin x & 0 \\ \sin x & \cos x & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$, we have:

$$F(y) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos y & -\sin y & 0 \\ \sin y & \cos y & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Compute the matrix product $F(x)F(y)$:

$$F(x)F(y) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos x & -\sin x & 0 \\ \sin x & \cos x & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \cos y & -\sin y & 0 \\ \sin y & \cos y & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Performing row-by-column multiplication:

Row 1:

  • $R_1 C_1 = \cos x \cos y - \sin x \sin y + 0 = \cos(x + y)$
  • $R_1 C_2 = -\cos x \sin y - \sin x \cos y + 0 = -(\sin x \cos y + \cos x \sin y) = -\sin(x + y)$
  • $R_1 C_3 = 0 + 0 + 0 = 0$

Row 2:

  • $R_2 C_1 = \sin x \cos y + \cos x \sin y + 0 = \sin(x + y)$
  • $R_2 C_2 = -\sin x \sin y + \cos x \cos y + 0 = \cos x \cos y - \sin x \sin y = \cos(x + y)$
  • $R_2 C_3 = 0 + 0 + 0 = 0$

Row 3:

  • $R_3 C_1 = 0 + 0 + 0 = 0$
  • $R_3 C_2 = 0 + 0 + 0 = 0$
  • $R_3 C_3 = 0 + 0 + 1 = 1$

Assembling the resulting matrix:

$$F(x)F(y) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos(x + y) & -\sin(x + y) & 0 \\ \sin(x + y) & \cos(x + y) & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = F(x + y)$$

Hence Proved.

Q7
Exercise 3.2 (Verbatim)
If $A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -\tan\frac{\alpha}{2} \\ \tan\frac{\alpha}{2} & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ and $I$ is the identity matrix of order 2, show that: $$I + A = (I - A)\begin{bmatrix} \cos\alpha & -\sin\alpha \\ \sin\alpha & \cos\alpha \end{bmatrix}$$
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Proof:

Let $t = \tan\frac{\alpha}{2}$. Then:

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -t \\ t & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad I = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

1. Left Hand Side (LHS):

$$I + A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -t \\ t & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -t \\ t & 1 \end{bmatrix} \quad \dots (1)$$

2. Right Hand Side (RHS):

$$I - A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -t \\ t & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & t \\ -t & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Express $\cos\alpha$ and $\sin\alpha$ in terms of $t = \tan\frac{\alpha}{2}$:

$$\cos\alpha = \frac{1 - t^2}{1 + t^2}, \quad \sin\alpha = \frac{2t}{1 + t^2}$$

Now evaluate the product $(I - A)M$:

$$(I - A)\begin{bmatrix} \cos\alpha & -\sin\alpha \\ \sin\alpha & \cos\alpha \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & t \\ -t & 1 \end{bmatrix} \frac{1}{1 + t^2}\begin{bmatrix} 1 - t^2 & -2t \\ 2t & 1 - t^2 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$= \frac{1}{1 + t^2} \begin{bmatrix} 1(1 - t^2) + t(2t) & 1(-2t) + t(1 - t^2) \\ -t(1 - t^2) + 1(2t) & -t(-2t) + 1(1 - t^2) \end{bmatrix}$$ $$= \frac{1}{1 + t^2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 - t^2 + 2t^2 & -2t + t - t^3 \\ -t + t^3 + 2t & 2t^2 + 1 - t^2 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$= \frac{1}{1 + t^2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 + t^2 & -t(1 + t^2) \\ t(1 + t^2) & 1 + t^2 \end{bmatrix}$$

Dividing every element by $(1 + t^2)$:

$$= \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -t \\ t & 1 \end{bmatrix} \quad \dots (2)$$

From (1) and (2), $\mathbf{LHS = RHS}$. Hence Proved.

Q8
Exercise 3.3 (Verbatim)
If $A = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 & 3 \\ 5 & 7 & 9 \\ -2 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{bmatrix} -4 & 1 & -5 \\ 1 & 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$, then verify that:
(i) $(A + B)' = A' + B'$
(ii) $(A - B)' = A' - B'$
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Verification:

We first find the transposes $A'$ and $B'$:

$$A' = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 5 & -2 \\ 2 & 7 & 1 \\ 3 & 9 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B' = \begin{bmatrix} -4 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \\ -5 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

(i) Verify $(A + B)' = A' + B'$:

$$A + B = \begin{bmatrix} -1-4 & 2+1 & 3-5 \\ 5+1 & 7+2 & 9+0 \\ -2+1 & 1+3 & 1+1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -5 & 3 & -2 \\ 6 & 9 & 9 \\ -1 & 4 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$(A + B)' = \begin{bmatrix} -5 & 6 & -1 \\ 3 & 9 & 4 \\ -2 & 9 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

Now compute $A' + B'$:

$$A' + B' = \begin{bmatrix} -1-4 & 5+1 & -2+1 \\ 2+1 & 7+2 & 1+3 \\ 3-5 & 9+0 & 1+1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -5 & 6 & -1 \\ 3 & 9 & 4 \\ -2 & 9 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

Hence, $(A + B)' = A' + B'$ verified.

(ii) Verify $(A - B)' = A' - B'$:

$$A - B = \begin{bmatrix} -1-(-4) & 2-1 & 3-(-5) \\ 5-1 & 7-2 & 9-0 \\ -2-1 & 1-3 & 1-1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 & 8 \\ 4 & 5 & 9 \\ -3 & -2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$(A - B)' = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 & -3 \\ 1 & 5 & -2 \\ 8 & 9 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Now compute $A' - B'$:

$$A' - B' = \begin{bmatrix} -1-(-4) & 5-1 & -2-1 \\ 2-1 & 7-2 & 1-3 \\ 3-(-5) & 9-0 & 1-1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 & -3 \\ 1 & 5 & -2 \\ 8 & 9 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Hence, $(A - B)' = A' - B'$ verified.

Q9
Exercise 3.3 (Verbatim)
For the matrices $A$ and $B$, verify that $(AB)' = B'A'$, where:
(i) $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -4 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}, B = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$
(ii) $A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}, B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 5 & 7 \end{bmatrix}$
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution
(i) $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -4 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}_{3 \times 1}, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}_{1 \times 3}$:

Compute $AB$ ($3 \times 3$ matrix):

$$AB = \begin{bmatrix} (1)(-1) & (1)(2) & (1)(1) \\ (-4)(-1) & (-4)(2) & (-4)(1) \\ (3)(-1) & (3)(2) & (3)(1) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 2 & 1 \\ 4 & -8 & -4 \\ -3 & 6 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$(AB)' = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 4 & -3 \\ 2 & -8 & 6 \\ 1 & -4 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$

Now compute $B'A'$: Here $B' = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}_{3 \times 1}$ and $A' = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -4 & 3 \end{bmatrix}_{1 \times 3}$:

$$B'A' = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -4 & 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 4 & -3 \\ 2 & -8 & 6 \\ 1 & -4 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$

Hence, $(AB)' = B'A'$ is verified.

(ii) $A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 5 & 7 \end{bmatrix}$:
$$AB = \begin{bmatrix} (0)(1) & (0)(5) & (0)(7) \\ (1)(1) & (1)(5) & (1)(7) \\ (2)(1) & (2)(5) & (2)(7) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 5 & 7 \\ 2 & 10 & 14 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$(AB)' = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 5 & 10 \\ 0 & 7 & 14 \end{bmatrix}$$

Now $B' = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 5 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix}$ and $A' = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$:

$$B'A' = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 5 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 5 & 10 \\ 0 & 7 & 14 \end{bmatrix}$$

Hence, $(AB)' = B'A'$ is verified.

Q10
Exercise 3.3 (Verbatim)
Express the following matrices as the sum of a symmetric and a skew symmetric matrix:
(i) $\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 5 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$
(ii) $\begin{bmatrix} 6 & -2 & 2 \\ -2 & 3 & -1 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Theoretical Basis:

Any square matrix $A$ can be uniquely expressed as $A = P + Q$, where: $$P = \frac{1}{2}(A + A') \quad \text{(Symmetric matrix, since } P' = P)$$ $$Q = \frac{1}{2}(A - A') \quad \text{(Skew-symmetric matrix, since } Q' = -Q)$$

(i) Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 5 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$:

  • $A' = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 5 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$
  • $A + A' = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 6 \\ 6 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \implies P = \frac{1}{2}(A + A') = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 3 \\ 3 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$
  • Check $P'$: $P' = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 3 \\ 3 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = P$. → Symmetric.
  • $A - A' = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 4 \\ -4 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \implies Q = \frac{1}{2}(A - A') = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
  • Check $Q'$: $Q' = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -2 \\ 2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = -Q$. → Skew-symmetric.
  • Check sum: $P + Q = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 3 \\ 3 & -1 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 5 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} = A$.
$$\mathbf{A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 3 \\ 3 & -1 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}}$$

(ii) Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & -2 & 2 \\ -2 & 3 & -1 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$:

  • Observe that $A' = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & -2 & 2 \\ -2 & 3 & -1 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} = A$.
  • Since $A' = A$, the matrix $A$ is itself already symmetric!
  • $P = \frac{1}{2}(A + A') = A = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & -2 & 2 \\ -2 & 3 & -1 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$ (Symmetric).
  • $Q = \frac{1}{2}(A - A') = \mathbf{0} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ (Skew-symmetric).
$$\mathbf{A = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & -2 & 2 \\ -2 & 3 & -1 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}}$$
