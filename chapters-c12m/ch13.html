Q1
Exercise 13.1 (Verbatim)
(i) Given that $E$ and $F$ are events such that $P(E) = 0.6$, $P(F) = 0.3$ and $P(E \cap F) = 0.2$, find $P(E|F)$ and $P(F|E)$.
(ii) Compute $P(A|B)$, if $P(B) = 0.5$ and $P(A \cap B) = 0.32$.
(iii) If $P(A) = 0.8$, $P(B) = 0.5$ and $P(B|A) = 0.4$, find: (a) $P(A \cap B)$, (b) $P(A|B)$, (c) $P(A \cup B)$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) $P(E|F)$ and $P(F|E)$:

  • $P(E|F) = \frac{P(E \cap F)}{P(F)} = \frac{0.2}{0.3} = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3}}$
  • $P(F|E) = \frac{P(E \cap F)}{P(E)} = \frac{0.2}{0.6} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{3}}$

(ii) $P(A|B)$:

$$P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} = \frac{0.32}{0.5} = \mathbf{0.64 = \frac{16}{25}}$$

(iii) Given $P(A) = 0.8, P(B) = 0.5, P(B|A) = 0.4$:

  1. $P(B|A) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(A)} \implies P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B|A) = 0.8 \times 0.4 = \mathbf{0.32}$.
  2. $P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} = \frac{0.32}{0.5} = \mathbf{0.64}$.
  3. Using addition theorem of probability: $$P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B) = 0.8 + 0.5 - 0.32 = 1.3 - 0.32 = \mathbf{0.98}$$

Q2
Exercise 13.1 (Verbatim)
(i) A coin is tossed three times. Find $P(E|F)$ where:
    $E$: Head on third toss
    $F$: Heads on first two tosses

(ii) Mother, father and son line up at random for a family picture. Find $P(E|F)$ where:
    $E$: Son on one end
    $F$: Father in middle

(iii) Assume that each born child is equally likely to be a boy or a girl. If a family has two children, what is the conditional probability that both are girls given that: (a) the youngest is a girl, (b) at least one is a girl?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) Coin tossed 3 times:

Sample space $S = \{HHH, HHT, HTH, HTT, THH, THT, TTH, TTT\}$, $n(S) = 8$.
  • $E = \{HHH, HTH, THH, TTH\} \implies n(E) = 4$.
  • $F = \{HHH, HHT\} \implies n(F) = 2$.
  • $E \cap F = \{HHH\} \implies n(E \cap F) = 1$.
  • $$P(E|F) = \frac{n(E \cap F)}{n(F)} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}}$$

(ii) Family picture line up ($M, F, S$):

Sample space: $S = \{MFS, MSF, FMS, FSM, SMF, SFM\}$, $n(S) = 6$.
  • $E$ (Son on one end): $\{MFS, FMS, SMF, SFM\} \implies n(E) = 4$.
  • $F$ (Father in middle): $\{MFS, SFM\} \implies n(F) = 2$.
  • $E \cap F = \{MFS, SFM\} \implies n(E \cap F) = 2$.
  • $$P(E|F) = \frac{n(E \cap F)}{n(F)} = \frac{2}{2} = \mathbf{1}$$

(iii) Two children family ($b = \text{boy}, g = \text{girl}$):

$S = \{(b, b), (b, g), (g, b), (g, g)\}$, where order is $(\text{elder}, \text{younger})$. $n(S) = 4$.
Event $A$: Both are girls $= \{(g, g)\}$.
  1. (a) Given youngest is a girl:
    $F_1 = \{(b, g), (g, g)\} \implies n(F_1) = 2$.
    $A \cap F_1 = \{(g, g)\} \implies n(A \cap F_1) = 1$.
    $$P(A|F_1) = \frac{n(A \cap F_1)}{n(F_1)} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}}$$
  2. (b) Given at least one is a girl:
    $F_2 = \{(b, g), (g, b), (g, g)\} \implies n(F_2) = 3$.
    $A \cap F_2 = \{(g, g)\} \implies n(A \cap F_2) = 1$.
    $$P(A|F_2) = \frac{n(A \cap F_2)}{n(F_2)} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{3}}$$

Q3
Exercise 13.2 (Verbatim)
(i) If $P(A) = \frac{3}{5}$ and $P(B) = \frac{1}{5}$, find $P(A \cap B)$ if $A$ and $B$ are independent events.
(ii) Two cards are drawn at random and without replacement from a pack of $52$ playing cards. Find the probability that both the cards are black.
(iii) A fair coin and an unbiased die are tossed. Let $A$ be the event 'head appears on the coin' and $B$ be the event '3 on the die'. Check whether $A$ and $B$ are independent events and not.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) Independent Events $A$ and $B$:

By definition of independent events: $$P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B) = \left(\frac{3}{5}\right) \left(\frac{1}{5}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{3}{25}}$$

(ii) Drawing two black cards without replacement:

A standard deck of 52 cards contains 26 black cards (13 spades + 13 clubs).
  • Probability of first card being black: $P(B_1) = \frac{26}{52} = \frac{1}{2}$.
  • Since drawing is without replacement, 25 black cards remain out of 51 total cards.
  • Probability of second card being black given first was black: $P(B_2|B_1) = \frac{25}{51}$.
By the multiplication theorem of probability: $$P(B_1 \cap B_2) = P(B_1) \cdot P(B_2|B_1) = \frac{26}{52} \times \frac{25}{51} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{25}{51} = \mathbf{\frac{25}{102}}$$

(iii) Coin and Die Experiment:

Sample space: $S = \{(H, 1), (H, 2), \dots, (H, 6), (T, 1), \dots, (T, 6)\}$, $n(S) = 12$.
  • Event $A$ (Head on coin): $A = \{(H, 1), (H, 2), (H, 3), (H, 4), (H, 5), (H, 6)\} \implies P(A) = \frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2}$.
  • Event $B$ (3 on die): $B = \{(H, 3), (T, 3)\} \implies P(B) = \frac{2}{12} = \frac{1}{6}$.
  • $A \cap B = \{(H, 3)\} \implies P(A \cap B) = \frac{1}{12}$.
Now compute $P(A) \cdot P(B)$: $$P(A) \cdot P(B) = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{12}$$ Since $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B) = \frac{1}{12}$, the events $A$ and $B$ are independent events.

Exercise 13.2 (Verbatim)
Given that the events $A$ and $B$ are such that $P(A) = \frac{1}{2}$, $P(A \cup B) = \frac{3}{5}$ and $P(B) = p$. Find $p$ if they are:
(i) mutually exclusive
(ii) independent

Also, if $A$ and $B$ are independent events with $P(A) = 0.3, \; P(B) = 0.4$, find: (a) $P(A \cap B)$, (b) $P(A \cup B)$, (c) $P(A|B)$, (d) $P(B|A)$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Part 1: Finding $p$:

We know that $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$:
  1. (i) Mutually Exclusive Events:
    Mutually exclusive means $A \cap B = \emptyset \implies P(A \cap B) = 0$. $$P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B)$$ $$\frac{3}{5} = \frac{1}{2} + p \implies p = \frac{3}{5} - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{6 - 5}{10} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{10}}$$
  2. (ii) Independent Events:
    Independent means $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B) = \frac{1}{2} p$. $$P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A)P(B)$$ $$\frac{3}{5} = \frac{1}{2} + p - \frac{1}{2}p = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}p$$ $$\frac{1}{2}p = \frac{3}{5} - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{10} \implies p = 2 \times \frac{1}{10} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{5}}$$

Part 2: $A$ and $B$ independent with $P(A) = 0.3, P(B) = 0.4$:

  • (a) $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B) = 0.3 \times 0.4 = \mathbf{0.12}$.
  • (b) $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B) = 0.3 + 0.4 - 0.12 = 0.7 - 0.12 = \mathbf{0.58}$.
  • (c) $P(A|B) = P(A) = \mathbf{0.3}$ (by independence).
  • (d) $P(B|A) = P(B) = \mathbf{0.4}$ (by independence).

Exercise 13.2 (Verbatim)
Probability of solving specific problem independently by $A$ and $B$ are $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{1}{3}$ respectively. If both try to solve the problem independently, find the probability that:
(i) the problem is solved
(ii) exactly one of them solves the problem
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Let $A$ be the event that person $A$ solves the problem, and $B$ the event that $B$ solves it.

Given: $P(A) = \frac{1}{2} \implies P(A') = 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}$.
$P(B) = \frac{1}{3} \implies P(B') = 1 - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}$.

Since $A$ and $B$ work independently, the events $A, B$ as well as their complements $A', B'$ are independent.

(i) Probability that the problem is solved:

The problem is solved if at least one of them solves it: $$P(\text{Problem is solved}) = 1 - P(\text{Neither solves the problem}) = 1 - P(A' \cap B')$$ Since $A'$ and $B'$ are independent: $$P(A' \cap B') = P(A') \cdot P(B') = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right) \left(\frac{2}{3}\right) = \frac{1}{3}$$ $$P(\text{Problem is solved}) = 1 - \frac{1}{3} = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3}}$$ (Alternative Method: $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{3 + 2 - 1}{6} = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}$).

(ii) Probability that exactly one of them solves the problem:

$$\text{Event} = (A \cap B') \cup (A' \cap B)$$ Since these two events are mutually exclusive: $$P(\text{Exactly one solves}) = P(A \cap B') + P(A' \cap B)$$ $$= P(A) \cdot P(B') + P(A') \cdot P(B)$$ $$= \left(\frac{1}{2}\right) \left(\frac{2}{3}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2}\right) \left(\frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{2}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{3}{6} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}}$$

Exercise 13.3 (Verbatim)
An urn contains $5$ red and $5$ black balls. A ball is drawn at random, its colour is noted and is returned to the urn. Moreover, $2$ additional balls of the colour drawn are put in the urn and then a ball is drawn at random. What is the probability that the second ball is red?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Let:

  • $E_1$: Event that the first ball drawn is red.
  • $E_2$: Event that the first ball drawn is black.
  • $A$: Event that the second ball drawn is red.

Step 1: Probabilities of the first draw:

Initial contents: 5 red, 5 black (total 10 balls). $$P(E_1) = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2}, \quad P(E_2) = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2}$$

Step 2: Conditional Probabilities of second draw being red:

  1. If first ball was red ($E_1$):
    The red ball is returned, and 2 additional red balls are added.
    New urn contents: $5 + 2 = 7$ red balls, $5$ black balls (total 12 balls). $$P(A|E_1) = \frac{7}{12}$$
  2. If first ball was black ($E_2$):
    The black ball is returned, and 2 additional black balls are added.
    New urn contents: $5$ red balls, $5 + 2 = 7$ black balls (total 12 balls). $$P(A|E_2) = \frac{5}{12}$$

Step 3: Law of Total Probability:

$$P(A) = P(E_1) \cdot P(A|E_1) + P(E_2) \cdot P(A|E_2)$$ $$P(A) = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right) \left(\frac{7}{12}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2}\right) \left(\frac{5}{12}\right) = \frac{7}{24} + \frac{5}{24} = \frac{12}{24} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}}$$

A bag contains $4$ red and $4$ black balls, another bag contains $2$ red and $6$ black balls. One of the two bags is selected at random and a ball is drawn from the bag which is found to be red. Find the probability that the ball is drawn from the first bag.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Let:

  • $E_1$: Event that Bag I is chosen.
  • $E_2$: Event that Bag II is chosen.
  • $A$: Event that the drawn ball is red.

Step 1: Prior Probabilities:

Since the bag is chosen at random: $$P(E_1) = \frac{1}{2}, \quad P(E_2) = \frac{1}{2}$$

Step 2: Likelihoods (Conditional Probabilities):

  • Bag I has 4 red, 4 black (8 balls total): $$P(A|E_1) = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}$$
  • Bag II has 2 red, 6 black (8 balls total): $$P(A|E_2) = \frac{2}{8} = \frac{1}{4}$$

Step 3: Bayes' Theorem Formula:

We need to find $P(E_1|A)$: $$P(E_1|A) = \frac{P(E_1) \cdot P(A|E_1)}{P(E_1) \cdot P(A|E_1) + P(E_2) \cdot P(A|E_2)}$$ Substitute the values: $$P(E_1|A) = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{4}} = \frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{8}} = \frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\frac{2 + 1}{8}} = \frac{1/4}{3/8} = \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{8}{3} = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3}}$$

Exercise 13.3 (Verbatim)
In answering a question on a multiple choice test, a student either knows the answer or guesses. Let $\frac{3}{4}$ be the probability that he knows the answer and $\frac{1}{4}$ be the probability that he guesses. Assuming that a student who guesses at the answer will be correct with probability $\frac{1}{4}$. What is the probability that the student knows the answer given that he answered it correctly?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Let:

  • $E_1$: Event that the student knows the answer.
  • $E_2$: Event that the student guesses the answer.
  • $A$: Event that the student answers the question correctly.

Step 1: Given Probabilities:

  • Prior: $P(E_1) = \frac{3}{4}, \quad P(E_2) = \frac{1}{4}$.
  • If the student knows the answer, he is guaranteed to answer correctly: $$P(A|E_1) = 1$$
  • If the student guesses, he is correct with probability $\frac{1}{4}$: $$P(A|E_2) = \frac{1}{4}$$

Step 2: Apply Bayes' Theorem:

We want to find $P(E_1|A)$: $$P(E_1|A) = \frac{P(E_1) P(A|E_1)}{P(E_1) P(A|E_1) + P(E_2) P(A|E_2)}$$ $$P(E_1|A) = \frac{\frac{3}{4} \times 1}{\left(\frac{3}{4} \times 1\right) + \left(\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{1}{4}\right)} = \frac{\frac{3}{4}}{\frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{16}} = \frac{\frac{12}{16}}{\frac{12 + 1}{16}} = \frac{12/16}{13/16} = \mathbf{\frac{12}{13}}$$

Exercise 13.3 (Verbatim)
A laboratory blood test is $99\%$ effective in detecting a certain disease when it is in fact present. However, the test also yields a 'false positive' result for $0.5\%$ of the healthy persons tested (i.e. if a healthy person is tested, then, with probability $0.005$, the test will imply he has the disease). If $0.1$ percent of the population actually has the disease, what is the probability that a person has the disease given that his test result is positive?
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Let:

  • $E_1$: Event that the person actually has the disease.
  • $E_2$: Event that the person does not have the disease (healthy).
  • $A$: Event that the blood test result is positive.

Step 1: Prior Probabilities:

Given: $0.1\%$ of the population has the disease: $$P(E_1) = \frac{0.1}{100} = 0.001$$ $$P(E_2) = 1 - P(E_1) = 1 - 0.001 = 0.999$$

Step 2: Likelihoods:

  • Test is $99\%$ effective when disease is present: $$P(A|E_1) = \frac{99}{100} = 0.99$$
  • Test yields false positive for $0.5\%$ of healthy individuals: $$P(A|E_2) = \frac{0.5}{100} = 0.005$$

Step 3: Apply Bayes' Theorem:

We seek $P(E_1|A)$: $$P(E_1|A) = \frac{P(E_1) P(A|E_1)}{P(E_1) P(A|E_1) + P(E_2) P(A|E_2)}$$ $$P(E_1|A) = \frac{(0.001)(0.99)}{(0.001)(0.99) + (0.999)(0.005)}$$ Multiply numerator and denominator by $100{,}000$: $$= \frac{100 \times 0.99}{100 \times 0.99 + 99.9 \times 5} = \frac{99}{99 + 4.995} = \frac{99}{99 + 4.995} = \frac{0.00099}{0.00099 + 0.004995}$$ $$= \frac{0.00099}{0.005985} = \frac{990}{5985}$$ Divide numerator and denominator by $45$: $$\frac{990 \div 45}{5985 \div 45} = \mathbf{\frac{22}{133} \approx 0.1654 \quad (16.54\%)}$$

A man is known to speak truth $3$ out of $4$ times. He throws a die and reports that it is a six. Find the probability that it is actually a six.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Let:

  • $E_1$: Event that a six actually occurs on the die.
  • $E_2$: Event that a six does not occur on the die (i.e., any number from $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ turns up).
  • $A$: Event that the man reports that it is a six.

Step 1: Prior Probabilities of Die:

$$P(E_1) = \frac{1}{6}, \quad P(E_2) = 1 - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$$

Step 2: Likelihoods of Reporting:

  • If six occurred ($E_1$), the man will report six if he speaks the truth: $$P(A|E_1) = P(\text{truth}) = \frac{3}{4}$$
  • If six did not occur ($E_2$), the man will report six if he lies: $$P(A|E_2) = P(\text{lie}) = 1 - \frac{3}{4} = \frac{1}{4}$$

Step 3: Apply Bayes' Theorem:

$$P(E_1|A) = \frac{P(E_1) P(A|E_1)}{P(E_1) P(A|E_1) + P(E_2) P(A|E_2)}$$ $$P(E_1|A) = \frac{\frac{1}{6} \times \frac{3}{4}}{\left(\frac{1}{6} \times \frac{3}{4}\right) + \left(\frac{5}{6} \times \frac{1}{4}\right)} = \frac{\frac{3}{24}}{\frac{3}{24} + \frac{5}{24}} = \frac{3/24}{8/24} = \mathbf{\frac{3}{8}}$$

✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

  • $E_1$: Insured person is a scooter driver.
  • $E_2$: Insured person is a car driver.
  • $E_3$: Insured person is a truck driver.

Step 2: Accident Probabilities:

$$P(A|E_1) = 0.01 = \frac{1}{100}, \quad P(A|E_2) = 0.03 = \frac{3}{100}, \quad P(A|E_3) = 0.15 = \frac{15}{100}$$

$$P(E_1|A) = \frac{P(E_1) P(A|E_1)}{P(E_1) P(A|E_1) + P(E_2) P(A|E_2) + P(E_3) P(A|E_3)}$$ $$P(E_1|A) = \frac{\frac{1}{6} \times \frac{1}{100}}{\left(\frac{1}{6} \times \frac{1}{100}\right) + \left(\frac{2}{6} \times \frac{3}{100}\right) + \left(\frac{3}{6} \times \frac{15}{100}\right)}$$ Multiply numerator and denominator by $600$: $$P(E_1|A) = \frac{1}{1 + 2(3) + 3(15)} = \frac{1}{1 + 6 + 45} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{52}}$$

Q12
Exercise 13.4 (Verbatim)
(i) State which of the following are not probability distributions of a random variable. Give reasons for your answer:
    (a) $X: 0, 1, 2$ with $P(X): 0.4, 0.4, 0.2$
    (b) $X: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$ with $P(X): 0.1, 0.5, 0.2, -0.1, 0.3$
    (c) $Y: -1, 0, 1$ with $P(Y): 0.6, 0.1, 0.2$

(ii) Find the probability distribution of number of heads in two tosses of a coin.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) Validity of Probability Distributions:

Criteria: For a valid probability distribution, (1) $p_i \ge 0$ for all $i$, and (2) $\sum p_i = 1$.
  1. (a):
    $p_i \ge 0$ for all $i$.
    $\sum p_i = 0.4 + 0.4 + 0.2 = 1.0$.
    → Valid probability distribution.
  2. (b):
    For $X = 3$, $P(X) = -0.1 < 0$.
    Since probability can never be negative, this is NOT a valid probability distribution.
  3. (c):
    $\sum P(Y) = 0.6 + 0.1 + 0.2 = 0.9 \neq 1$.
    Since the sum of probabilities is not equal to $1$, this is NOT a valid probability distribution.

(ii) Probability distribution of number of heads in two coin tosses:

Sample space: $S = \{HH, HT, TH, TT\}$, $n(S) = 4$.
Let $X$ denote the number of heads. The possible values of $X$ are $0, 1, 2$.
  • $P(X = 0) = P(\{TT\}) = \frac{1}{4}$
  • $P(X = 1) = P(\{HT, TH\}) = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$
  • $P(X = 2) = P(\{HH\}) = \frac{1}{4}$
$X$ $0$ $1$ $2$
$P(X)$ $\frac{1}{4}$ $\frac{1}{2}$ $\frac{1}{4}$

Q13
Exercise 13.4 (Verbatim)
A random variable $X$ has the following probability distribution:
$$\begin{array}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|} \hline X & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 \\ \hline P(X) & 0 & k & 2k & 2k & 3k & k^2 & 2k^2 & 7k^2 + k \\ \hline \end{array}$$ Determine:
(i) $k$
(ii) $P(X < 3)$
(iii) $P(X \ge 6)$
(iv) $P(0 < X < 3)$
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) Finding $k$:

We know that for any probability distribution: $$\sum_{i=0}^7 P(X_i) = 1$$ $$0 + k + 2k + 2k + 3k + k^2 + 2k^2 + (7k^2 + k) = 1$$ $$(k^2 + 2k^2 + 7k^2) + (k + 2k + 2k + 3k + k) = 1$$ $$10k^2 + 9k = 1$$ $$10k^2 + 9k - 1 = 0$$ $$10k^2 + 10k - k - 1 = 0 \implies 10k(k + 1) - 1(k + 1) = 0$$ $$(10k - 1)(k + 1) = 0 \implies k = \frac{1}{10} \quad \text{or} \quad k = -1$$ Since probability cannot be negative, $k \neq -1$.
Therefore: $$\mathbf{k = \frac{1}{10} = 0.1}$$

(ii) $P(X < 3)$:

$$P(X < 3) = P(X = 0) + P(X = 1) + P(X = 2) = 0 + k + 2k = 3k = 3(0.1) = \mathbf{0.3 = \frac{3}{10}}$$

(iii) $P(X \ge 6)$:

$$P(X \ge 6) = P(X = 6) + P(X = 7) = 2k^2 + (7k^2 + k) = 9k^2 + k$$ $$= 9\left(\frac{1}{10}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{10} = \frac{9}{100} + \frac{10}{100} = \mathbf{\frac{19}{100} = 0.19}$$

(iv) $P(0 < X < 3)$:

$$P(0 < X < 3) = P(X = 1) + P(X = 2) = k + 2k = 3k = \mathbf{\frac{3}{10} = 0.3}$$

Q14
Exercise 13.4 (Verbatim)
Two cards are drawn successively with replacement from a well-shuffled deck of $52$ cards. Find the probability distribution of the number of aces. Also find the mean of the number of aces.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Let $X$ denote the number of aces drawn in $2$ successive draws with replacement.

In a standard deck of $52$ cards, there are $4$ aces and $48$ non-aces.

  • Probability of drawing an ace on any single draw: $$p = \frac{4}{52} = \frac{1}{13}$$
  • Probability of drawing a non-ace on any single draw: $$q = 1 - p = 1 - \frac{1}{13} = \frac{12}{13}$$

Since drawings are with replacement, trials are independent.

The possible values of $X$ are $0, 1, 2$.

Step 1: Compute Probabilities:

  1. $P(X = 0) = q \times q = \left(\frac{12}{13}\right) \left(\frac{12}{13}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{144}{169}}$
  2. $P(X = 1) = pq + qp = 2pq = 2\left(\frac{1}{13}\right)\left(\frac{12}{13}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{24}{169}}$
  3. $P(X = 2) = p \times p = \left(\frac{1}{13}\right) \left(\frac{1}{13}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{1}{169}}$

Check: $\sum P(X) = \frac{144 + 24 + 1}{169} = \frac{169}{169} = 1$.

Probability Distribution Table:

$X$ $0$ $1$ $2$
$P(X)$ $\frac{144}{169}$ $\frac{24}{169}$ $\frac{1}{169}$

Step 2: Mean (Expectation) of $X$:

$$\mu = E(X) = \sum_{i=1}^3 x_i P(x_i)$$ $$\mu = 0 \left(\frac{144}{169}\right) + 1 \left(\frac{24}{169}\right) + 2 \left(\frac{1}{169}\right) = 0 + \frac{24}{169} + \frac{2}{169} = \mathbf{\frac{26}{169} = \frac{2}{13}}$$

(Check with formula for binomial expectation: $\mu = n p = 2 \times \frac{1}{13} = \frac{2}{13}$. Verified.)
