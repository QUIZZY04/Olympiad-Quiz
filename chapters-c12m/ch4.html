Q1
Exercise 4.1 (Verbatim)
Evaluate the determinants:
(i) $\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ -5 & -1 \end{vmatrix}$
(ii) $\begin{vmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{vmatrix}$
(iii) $\begin{vmatrix} x^2 - x + 1 & x - 1 \\ x + 1 & x + 1 \end{vmatrix}$
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) $\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ -5 & -1 \end{vmatrix}$:

$$\Delta = (2)(-1) - (4)(-5) = -2 - (-20) = -2 + 20 = 18$$

(ii) $\begin{vmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{vmatrix}$:

$$\Delta = (\cos\theta)(\cos\theta) - (-\sin\theta)(\sin\theta) = \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$$

(iii) $\begin{vmatrix} x^2 - x + 1 & x - 1 \\ x + 1 & x + 1 \end{vmatrix}$:

$$\Delta = (x^2 - x + 1)(x + 1) - (x - 1)(x + 1)$$ Using the standard identity $(x + 1)(x^2 - x + 1) = x^3 + 1$ and $(x - 1)(x + 1) = x^2 - 1$: $$\Delta = (x^3 + 1) - (x^2 - 1) = x^3 - x^2 + 2$$

Q2
Exercise 4.1 (Verbatim)
If $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 4 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$, then show that $|2A| = 4|A|$.
Also, if $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$, show that $|3A| = 27|A|$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Part 1: Matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 4 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$:

  1. $|A| = (1)(2) - (2)(4) = 2 - 8 = -6$.
    Hence, $4|A| = 4(-6) = -24$.
  2. Now, $2A = 2\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 4 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 8 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$.
  3. $|2A| = (2)(4) - (4)(8) = 8 - 32 = -24$.

Thus, $|2A| = 4|A| = -24$. (Verified, in accordance with the general rule $|kA| = k^n|A|$ where $n=2$).

Part 2: Matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$:

  1. Expanding $|A|$ along $C_1$: $$|A| = 1 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 4 \end{vmatrix} - 0 + 0 = 1(4 - 0) = 4$$ Thus, $27|A| = 27 \times 4 = 108$.
  2. Now scalar multiple $3A$: $$3A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 6 \\ 0 & 0 & 12 \end{bmatrix}$$
  3. Expanding $|3A|$ along $C_1$: $$|3A| = 3 \cdot \begin{vmatrix} 3 & 6 \\ 0 & 12 \end{vmatrix} = 3(36 - 0) = 3 \times 36 = 108$$

Thus, $|3A| = 27|A| = 108$. (Verified since for order $n=3$, $|kA| = k^3|A| = 3^3|A| = 27|A|$).

Q3
Exercise 4.1 (Verbatim)
Find values of $x$, if:
(i) $\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 5 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} 2x & 4 \\ 6 & x \end{vmatrix}$
(ii) $\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} x & 3 \\ 2x & 5 \end{vmatrix}$
(iii) If $\begin{vmatrix} x & 2 \\ 18 & x \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} 6 & 2 \\ 18 & 6 \end{vmatrix}$, then find $x$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Crucial Note: A determinant is a single scalar value, NOT a matrix. Elements cannot be equated directly; we must evaluate each determinant.

(i) $\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 5 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} 2x & 4 \\ 6 & x \end{vmatrix}$:

$$\text{LHS} = (2)(1) - (4)(5) = 2 - 20 = -18$$ $$\text{RHS} = (2x)(x) - (4)(6) = 2x^2 - 24$$ $$2x^2 - 24 = -18 \implies 2x^2 = 6 \implies x^2 = 3 \implies x = \pm \sqrt{3}$$

(ii) $\begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} x & 3 \\ 2x & 5 \end{vmatrix}$:

$$\text{LHS} = (2)(5) - (3)(4) = 10 - 12 = -2$$ $$\text{RHS} = (x)(5) - (3)(2x) = 5x - 6x = -x$$ $$-x = -2 \implies x = 2$$

(iii) $\begin{vmatrix} x & 2 \\ 18 & x \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} 6 & 2 \\ 18 & 6 \end{vmatrix}$:

$$x^2 - 36 = 36 - 36 = 0$$ $$x^2 = 36 \implies x = \pm 6$$

Q4
Exercise 4.2 (Verbatim)
(i) Find the area of the triangle whose vertices are $(1, 0)$, $(6, 0)$, and $(4, 3)$.
(ii) Show that points $A(a, b+c)$, $B(b, c+a)$, and $C(c, a+b)$ are collinear.
(iii) Find values of $k$ if area of triangle is $4\text{ sq. units}$ and vertices are $(k, 0)$, $(4, 0)$, $(0, 2)$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) Area of triangle with vertices $(1, 0), (6, 0), (4, 3)$:

$$\Delta = \frac{1}{2} \begin{vmatrix} x_1 & y_1 & 1 \\ x_2 & y_2 & 1 \\ x_3 & y_3 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = \frac{1}{2} \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 6 & 0 & 1 \\ 4 & 3 & 1 \end{vmatrix}$$ Expanding along $C_2$ (which has two zeros): $$\Delta = \frac{1}{2} \left[ -3(1 - 6) \right] = \frac{1}{2} [-3(-5)] = \frac{15}{2} = 7.5\text{ sq. units}$$

(ii) Points $A(a, b+c)$, $B(b, c+a)$, $C(c, a+b)$ are collinear:

$$\Delta = \frac{1}{2} \begin{vmatrix} a & b+c & 1 \\ b & c+a & 1 \\ c & a+b & 1 \end{vmatrix}$$ Applying column operation $C_2 \to C_2 + C_1$: $$\Delta = \frac{1}{2} \begin{vmatrix} a & a+b+c & 1 \\ b & a+b+c & 1 \\ c & a+b+c & 1 \end{vmatrix}$$ Taking $(a+b+c)$ common from $C_2$: $$\Delta = \frac{a+b+c}{2} \begin{vmatrix} a & 1 & 1 \\ b & 1 & 1 \\ c & 1 & 1 \end{vmatrix}$$ Since columns $C_2$ and $C_3$ are identical, the value of the determinant is $0$: $$\Delta = \frac{a+b+c}{2} \times 0 = 0$$ Since the area of $\Delta ABC = 0$, the points $A, B, C$ are collinear.

(iii) Vertices $(k, 0), (4, 0), (0, 2)$ with Area $= 4$:

$$\frac{1}{2} \begin{vmatrix} k & 0 & 1 \\ 4 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = \pm 4$$ Expanding along $C_2$: $$\frac{1}{2} \left[ -2(k - 4) \right] = \pm 4 \implies -(k - 4) = \pm 4$$
  • Case 1: $-(k - 4) = 4 \implies k - 4 = -4 \implies k = 0$
  • Case 2: $-(k - 4) = -4 \implies k - 4 = 4 \implies k = 8$
Thus, $k = 0$ or $k = 8$.

Q5
Exercise 4.3 (Verbatim)
Write Minors and Cofactors of the elements of the following determinants:
(i) $\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -4 \\ 0 & 3 \end{vmatrix}$
(ii) $\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{vmatrix}$
(iii) Using Cofactors of elements of second row, evaluate $\Delta = \begin{vmatrix} 5 & 3 & 8 \\ 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \end{vmatrix}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) $\begin{vmatrix} 2 & -4 \\ 0 & 3 \end{vmatrix}$:

  • $M_{11} = 3 \implies A_{11} = (-1)^{1+1}(3) = 3$
  • $M_{12} = 0 \implies A_{12} = (-1)^{1+2}(0) = 0$
  • $M_{21} = -4 \implies A_{21} = (-1)^{2+1}(-4) = 4$
  • $M_{22} = 2 \implies A_{22} = (-1)^{2+2}(2) = 2$

(ii) Identity matrix determinant:

  • $M_{11} = 1(1) - 0 = 1 \implies A_{11} = 1$
  • $M_{12} = 0 \implies A_{12} = 0$
  • $M_{13} = 0 \implies A_{13} = 0$
  • Similarly, $M_{22} = 1, A_{22} = 1$ and $M_{33} = 1, A_{33} = 1$. All other minors and cofactors are $0$.

(iii) Using Cofactors of elements of second row:

$$\Delta = a_{21} A_{21} + a_{22} A_{22} + a_{23} A_{23}$$ Here, the second row elements are $a_{21} = 2, a_{22} = 0, a_{23} = 1$.
Calculate cofactors:
  • $A_{21} = (-1)^{2+1} M_{21} = -\begin{vmatrix} 3 & 8 \\ 2 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = -(9 - 16) = -(-7) = 7$
  • $A_{22} = (-1)^{2+2} M_{22} = +\begin{vmatrix} 5 & 8 \\ 1 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = +(15 - 8) = 7$
  • $A_{23} = (-1)^{2+3} M_{23} = -\begin{vmatrix} 5 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = -(10 - 3) = -7$
Now evaluate $\Delta$: $$\Delta = 2(7) + 0(7) + 1(-7) = 14 + 0 - 7 = 7$$

Q6
Exercise 4.4 (Verbatim)
Find adjoint of each of the matrices:
(i) $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$
(ii) $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 & 5 \\ -2 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$
(iii) Verify $A(\operatorname{adj} A) = (\operatorname{adj} A)A = |A|I$ for $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ -4 & -6 \end{bmatrix}$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$:

Cofactors:
  • $A_{11} = 4, \quad A_{12} = -3$
  • $A_{21} = -2, \quad A_{22} = 1$
$$\operatorname{adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} A_{11} & A_{21} \\ A_{12} & A_{22} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & -2 \\ -3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$ Shortcut for 2x2: Interchange diagonal elements ($1 \leftrightarrow 4$) and change signs of off-diagonal elements ($2 \to -2, 3 \to -3$).

(ii) $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 2 \\ 2 & 3 & 5 \\ -2 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$:

Calculate cofactors:
  • $A_{11} = +(3(1) - 0) = 3$
  • $A_{12} = -(2(1) - (-10)) = -12$
  • $A_{13} = +(0 - (-6)) = 6$
  • $A_{21} = -((-1)(1) - 0) = 1$
  • $A_{22} = +(1(1) - (-4)) = 5$
  • $A_{23} = -(0 - 2) = 2$
  • $A_{31} = +((-5) - 6) = -11$
  • $A_{32} = -(5 - 4) = -1$
  • $A_{33} = +(3 - (-2)) = 5$
Transpose of cofactor matrix: $$\operatorname{adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} A_{11} & A_{21} & A_{31} \\ A_{12} & A_{22} & A_{32} \\ A_{13} & A_{23} & A_{33} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 & -11 \\ -12 & 5 & -1 \\ 6 & 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$$

(iii) Verification for $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ -4 & -6 \end{bmatrix}$:

  1. $|A| = (2)(-6) - (3)(-4) = -12 + 12 = 0$.
    Thus, $|A|I = 0 \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = O$.
  2. $\operatorname{adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} -6 & -3 \\ 4 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$.
  3. Product $A(\operatorname{adj} A)$: $$A(\operatorname{adj} A) = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ -4 & -6 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -6 & -3 \\ 4 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -12 + 12 & -6 + 6 \\ 24 - 24 & 12 - 12 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
  4. Product $(\operatorname{adj} A)A$: $$(\operatorname{adj} A)A = \begin{bmatrix} -6 & -3 \\ 4 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ -4 & -6 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -12 + 12 & -18 + 18 \\ 8 - 8 & 12 - 12 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
Hence, $A(\operatorname{adj} A) = (\operatorname{adj} A)A = |A|I = O$ is verified.

Exercise 4.4 (Verbatim)
Find the inverse of each of the matrices (if it exists):
(i) $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -2 \\ 4 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$

(ii) $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 2 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$:

  1. Since $A$ is an upper triangular matrix, its determinant is the product of diagonal elements: $$|A| = 1 \times 2 \times 5 = 10 \neq 0$$ Since $|A| \neq 0$, $A^{-1}$ exists.
  2. Calculate all 9 cofactors:
    • $A_{11} = +(10 - 0) = 10$
    • $A_{12} = -(0 - 0) = 0$
    • $A_{13} = +(0 - 0) = 0$
    • $A_{21} = -(10 - 0) = -10$
    • $A_{22} = +(5 - 0) = 5$
    • $A_{23} = -(0 - 0) = 0$
    • $A_{31} = +(8 - 6) = 2$
    • $A_{32} = -(4 - 0) = -4$
    • $A_{33} = +(2 - 0) = 2$

  4. Inverse matrix: $$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|} \operatorname{adj} A = \frac{1}{10} \begin{bmatrix} 10 & -10 & 2 \\ 0 & 5 & -4 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & \frac{1}{5} \\[6pt] 0 & \frac{1}{2} & -\frac{2}{5} \\[6pt] 0 & 0 & \frac{1}{5} \end{bmatrix}$$

Q8
Exercise 4.4 (Verbatim)
Let $A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 7 \\ 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 8 \\ 7 & 9 \end{bmatrix}$. Verify that $(AB)^{-1} = B^{-1} A^{-1}$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Step 1: Compute product $AB$ and its inverse $(AB)^{-1}$:

$$AB = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 7 \\ 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 8 \\ 7 & 9 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 18 + 49 & 24 + 63 \\ 12 + 35 & 16 + 45 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 67 & 87 \\ 47 & 61 \end{bmatrix}$$
Determinant of $AB$: $$|AB| = (67)(61) - (87)(47) = 4087 - 4089 = -2 \neq 0$$
Adjoint of $AB$: $$\operatorname{adj}(AB) = \begin{bmatrix} 61 & -87 \\ -47 & 67 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$(AB)^{-1} = \frac{1}{|AB|} \operatorname{adj}(AB) = -\frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 61 & -87 \\ -47 & 67 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -\frac{61}{2} & \frac{87}{2} \\[6pt] \frac{47}{2} & -\frac{67}{2} \end{bmatrix}$$

Step 2: Compute $B^{-1}$ and $A^{-1}$:

  • For $A$: $|A| = 15 - 14 = 1$.
    $\operatorname{adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & -7 \\ -2 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \implies A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & -7 \\ -2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$.
  • For $B$: $|B| = 54 - 56 = -2$.
    $\operatorname{adj} B = \begin{bmatrix} 9 & -8 \\ -7 & 6 \end{bmatrix} \implies B^{-1} = -\frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 9 & -8 \\ -7 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$.

Step 3: Compute product $B^{-1} A^{-1}$:

$$B^{-1} A^{-1} = -\frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 9 & -8 \\ -7 & 6 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 5 & -7 \\ -2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$= -\frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 45 + 16 & -63 - 24 \\ -35 - 12 & 49 + 18 \end{bmatrix} = -\frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 61 & -87 \\ -47 & 67 \end{bmatrix}$$

Comparing Step 1 and Step 3, we see: $$(AB)^{-1} = B^{-1} A^{-1}$$ Hence, verified.

Q9
Exercise 4.4 (Verbatim)
If $A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$, show that $A^2 - 5A + 7I = O$. Hence find $A^{-1}$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Part 1: Verification of $A^2 - 5A + 7I = O$:

  1. $A^2 = A \cdot A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 9 - 1 & 3 + 2 \\ -3 - 2 & -1 + 4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 8 & 5 \\ -5 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$.
  2. $5A = 5\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 15 & 5 \\ -5 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$.
  3. $7I = 7\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 0 \\ 0 & 7 \end{bmatrix}$.
  4. Evaluate LHS: $$A^2 - 5A + 7I = \begin{bmatrix} 8 & 5 \\ -5 & 3 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 15 & 5 \\ -5 & 10 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 0 \\ 0 & 7 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$= \begin{bmatrix} 8 - 15 + 7 & 5 - 5 + 0 \\ -5 - (-5) + 0 & 3 - 10 + 7 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = O$$

Hence, $A^2 - 5A + 7I = O$ is proven.

Part 2: Finding $A^{-1}$ using the matrix equation:

(CBSE Rule: Marks will be deducted if student uses direct adjoint method when the question says "Hence find $A^{-1}$")

$$A^2 - 5A + 7I = O$$ Post-multiplying both sides by $A^{-1}$: $$(A^2) A^{-1} - 5(A) A^{-1} + 7(I) A^{-1} = O \cdot A^{-1}$$ $$A(A A^{-1}) - 5I + 7A^{-1} = O$$ $$A(I) - 5I + 7A^{-1} = O$$ $$A - 5I + 7A^{-1} = O$$ $$7A^{-1} = 5I - A$$ $$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{7} (5I - A)$$ Now substitute $I$ and $A$: $$5I - A = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{7} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$

Q10
Exercise 4.4 (Verbatim)
(i) Let $A$ be a non-singular square matrix of order $3 \times 3$. Then $|\operatorname{adj} A|$ is equal to:
    (A) $|A|$      (B) $|A|^2$      (C) $|A|^3$      (D) $3|A|$

(ii) If $A$ is an invertible matrix of order $2$, then $\det(A^{-1})$ is equal to:
    (A) $\det(A)$      (B) $\frac{1}{\det(A)}$      (C) $1$      (D) $0$

(iii) If $A$ is a square matrix of order $3$ with $|A| = 5$, find $|2\operatorname{adj} A|$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) Value of $|\operatorname{adj} A|$:

We know from the fundamental theorem of adjoint matrices: $$A(\operatorname{adj} A) = |A|I_n$$ Taking determinant on both sides: $$|A(\operatorname{adj} A)| = ||A|I_n|$$ $$|A| \cdot |\operatorname{adj} A| = (|A|)^n |I_n| = |A|^n (1) = |A|^n$$ Dividing both sides by $|A|$ (since $A$ is non-singular, $|A| \neq 0$): $$|\operatorname{adj} A| = |A|^{n-1}$$ For order $n = 3$: $$|\operatorname{adj} A| = |A|^{3-1} = |A|^2$$ Correct Option: (B) $|A|^2$

(ii) Value of $\det(A^{-1})$:

By definition of inverse: $$A \cdot A^{-1} = I$$ Taking determinant on both sides: $$\det(A \cdot A^{-1}) = \det(I)$$ $$\det(A) \cdot \det(A^{-1}) = 1$$ $$\det(A^{-1}) = \frac{1}{\det(A)}$$ Correct Option: (B) $\frac{1}{\det(A)}$

(iii) Value of $|2\operatorname{adj} A|$ for order $n=3$, $|A| = 5$:

Using property of scalar multiplication $|kB| = k^n |B|$ where $B = \operatorname{adj} A$ (order $3 \times 3$): $$|2\operatorname{adj} A| = 2^3 |\operatorname{adj} A| = 8 |\operatorname{adj} A|$$ Since $|\operatorname{adj} A| = |A|^{3-1} = |A|^2$: $$|2\operatorname{adj} A| = 8 |A|^2 = 8(5^2) = 8 \times 25 = 200$$

Q11
Exercise 4.5 (Verbatim)
Solve system of linear equations using matrix method:
$$\begin{aligned} 2x + 5y &= 1 \\ 3x + 2y &= 7 \end{aligned}$$ Also examine the consistency of the system:
$$\begin{aligned} x + 3y &= 5 \\ 2x + 6y &= 8 \end{aligned}$$
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Part 1: Solving $2x + 5y = 1$ and $3x + 2y = 7$ by Matrix Method:

The system can be written in matrix form $AX = B$ where:

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad X = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix}$$
  1. Determinant of coefficient matrix: $$|A| = (2)(2) - (5)(3) = 4 - 15 = -11 \neq 0$$ Since $|A| \neq 0$, $A$ is non-singular and the system has a unique solution given by: $$X = A^{-1} B$$
  2. Adjoint of $A$: $$\operatorname{adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -5 \\ -3 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{|A|} \operatorname{adj} A = -\frac{1}{11} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -5 \\ -3 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$
  3. Calculate $X = A^{-1} B$: $$X = -\frac{1}{11} \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -5 \\ -3 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix} = -\frac{1}{11} \begin{bmatrix} 2(1) - 5(7) \\ -3(1) + 2(7) \end{bmatrix}$$ $$= -\frac{1}{11} \begin{bmatrix} 2 - 35 \\ -3 + 14 \end{bmatrix} = -\frac{1}{11} \begin{bmatrix} -33 \\ 11 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Therefore, $\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} \implies \mathbf{x = 3, \; y = -1}$.

Check: $2(3) + 5(-1) = 6 - 5 = 1$ (Correct); $3(3) + 2(-1) = 9 - 2 = 7$ (Correct).

Part 2: Consistency of $x + 3y = 5$ and $2x + 6y = 8$:

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 6 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$|A| = (1)(6) - (3)(2) = 6 - 6 = 0$$ Since $|A| = 0$, we evaluate $(\operatorname{adj} A) B$: $$\operatorname{adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & -3 \\ -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$(\operatorname{adj} A) B = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & -3 \\ -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 30 - 24 \\ -10 + 8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} \neq O$$ Since $|A| = 0$ and $(\operatorname{adj} A) B \neq O$, the system is inconsistent and has no solution.

Q12
Exercise 4.5 (Verbatim)
Solve the following system of equations by matrix method:
$$\begin{aligned} x - y + 2z &= 7 \\ 3x + 4y - 5z &= -5 \\ 2x - y + 3z &= 12 \end{aligned}$$ Also, the cost of $4\text{ kg}$ onion, $3\text{ kg}$ wheat and $2\text{ kg}$ rice is ₹$60$. The cost of $2\text{ kg}$ onion, $4\text{ kg}$ wheat and $6\text{ kg}$ rice is ₹$90$. The cost of $6\text{ kg}$ onion, $2\text{ kg}$ wheat and $3\text{ kg}$ rice is ₹$70$. Find cost of each item per kg by matrix method.
[6 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Part 1: 3x3 System of Equations:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 & -5 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ -5 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix} \implies AX = B$$

Step 1: Compute $|A|$:

$$|A| = 1(12 - 5) - (-1)(9 - (-10)) + 2(-3 - 8)$$ $$= 1(7) + 1(19) + 2(-11) = 7 + 19 - 22 = 4 \neq 0$$ Since $|A| \neq 0$, the system has a unique solution $X = A^{-1} B$.

Step 2: Cofactors of $A$:

  • $A_{11} = +(12 - 5) = 7$
  • $A_{12} = -(9 - (-10)) = -19$
  • $A_{13} = +(-3 - 8) = -11$
  • $A_{21} = -(-3 - (-2)) = -(-1) = 1$
  • $A_{22} = +(3 - 4) = -1$
  • $A_{23} = -(-1 - (-2)) = -(1) = -1$
  • $A_{31} = +(5 - 8) = -3$
  • $A_{32} = -(-5 - 6) = 11$
  • $A_{33} = +(4 - (-3)) = 7$
$$\operatorname{adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 1 & -3 \\ -19 & -1 & 11 \\ -11 & -1 & 7 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{4} \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 1 & -3 \\ -19 & -1 & 11 \\ -11 & -1 & 7 \end{bmatrix}$$

Step 3: Compute $X = A^{-1} B$:

$$X = \frac{1}{4} \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 1 & -3 \\ -19 & -1 & 11 \\ -11 & -1 & 7 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ -5 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{4} \begin{bmatrix} 7(7) + 1(-5) - 3(12) \\ -19(7) - 1(-5) + 11(12) \\ -11(7) - 1(-5) + 7(12) \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{4} \begin{bmatrix} 49 - 5 - 36 \\ -133 + 5 + 132 \\ -77 + 5 + 84 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{4} \begin{bmatrix} 8 \\ 4 \\ 12 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$$

Therefore, $\mathbf{x = 2, \; y = 1, \; z = 3}$.

Part 2: Real-World Commercial Word Problem (Ex 4.5 Q16):

Let the cost per kg of onion, wheat, and rice be ₹$x$, ₹$y$, and ₹$z$ respectively. $$\begin{aligned} 4x + 3y + 2z &= 60 \\ 2x + 4y + 6z &= 90 \\ 6x + 2y + 3z &= 70 \end{aligned}$$ Matrix representation $AX = B$: $$A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \\ 6 & 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad X = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 60 \\ 90 \\ 70 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$|A| = 4(12 - 12) - 3(6 - 36) + 2(4 - 24) = 4(0) - 3(-30) + 2(-20) = 90 - 40 = 50 \neq 0$$ Computing cofactors: $$\operatorname{adj} A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -5 & 10 \\ 30 & 0 & -20 \\ -20 & 10 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$X = \frac{1}{50} \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -5 & 10 \\ 30 & 0 & -20 \\ -20 & 10 & 10 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 60 \\ 90 \\ 70 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{50} \begin{bmatrix} 0 - 450 + 700 \\ 1800 + 0 - 1400 \\ -1200 + 900 + 700 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{50} \begin{bmatrix} 250 \\ 400 \\ 400 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 8 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix}$$

Final Answer:
Cost of Onion = ₹5 per kg
Cost of Wheat = ₹8 per kg
Cost of Rice = ₹8 per kg
