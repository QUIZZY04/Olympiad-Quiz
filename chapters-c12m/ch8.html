Q1
Exercise 8.1 (Verbatim)
Find the area of the region bounded by the curve $y^2 = x$ and the lines $x = 1, \; x = 4$ and the x-axis in the first quadrant.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Step 1: Curve Analysis & Sketch:

  • The curve $y^2 = x$ is a rightward-opening parabola with vertex at the origin $(0, 0)$, symmetric about the x-axis.
  • In the first quadrant, $y = +\sqrt{x}$.
  • The region is bounded by the vertical lines $x = 1$ and $x = 4$, the curve $y = \sqrt{x}$, and the x-axis ($y = 0$).

Step 2: Integral Formulation:

Taking a vertical approximating strip of width $dx$ and height $y$: $$\text{Area } A = \int_1^4 y \, dx = \int_1^4 \sqrt{x} \, dx = \int_1^4 x^{1/2} \, dx$$

Step 3: Integration & Limits:

$$A = \left[ \frac{x^{3/2}}{3/2} \right]_1^4 = \frac{2}{3} \left[ x^{3/2} \right]_1^4$$ $$A = \frac{2}{3} \left[ 4^{3/2} - 1^{3/2} \right] = \frac{2}{3} \left[ (2^2)^{3/2} - 1 \right] = \frac{2}{3} [2^3 - 1] = \frac{2}{3} [8 - 1] = \frac{2}{3} (7) = \mathbf{\frac{14}{3}\text{ sq. units}}$$

Q2
Exercise 8.1 (Verbatim)
(i) Find the area of the region bounded by $y^2 = 9x, \; x = 2, \; x = 4$ and the x-axis in the first quadrant.
(ii) Find the area of the region bounded by $x^2 = 4y, \; y = 2, \; y = 4$ and the y-axis in the first quadrant.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) $y^2 = 9x$ between $x = 2$ and $x = 4$:

In the first quadrant, $y = \sqrt{9x} = 3\sqrt{x}$. $$A = \int_2^4 3\sqrt{x} \, dx = 3 \left[ \frac{x^{3/2}}{3/2} \right]_2^4 = 3 \cdot \frac{2}{3} \left[ x^{3/2} \right]_2^4 = 2 [4^{3/2} - 2^{3/2}]$$ Since $4^{3/2} = 8$ and $2^{3/2} = 2\sqrt{2}$: $$\mathbf{A = 2(8 - 2\sqrt{2}) = (16 - 4\sqrt{2})\text{ sq. units}}$$

(ii) $x^2 = 4y$ between $y = 2$ and $y = 4$ along y-axis:

Here it is most convenient to take horizontal approximating strips of thickness $dy$.
In the first quadrant, $x = \sqrt{4y} = 2\sqrt{y}$. $$A = \int_2^4 x \, dy = \int_2^4 2\sqrt{y} \, dy = 2 \left[ \frac{y^{3/2}}{3/2} \right]_2^4 = 2 \cdot \frac{2}{3} \left[ y^{3/2} \right]_2^4$$ $$A = \frac{4}{3} [4^{3/2} - 2^{3/2}] = \frac{4}{3} (8 - 2\sqrt{2}) = \mathbf{\frac{32 - 8\sqrt{2}}{3}\text{ sq. units}}$$

Q3
Exercise 8.1 (Verbatim)
Find the area of the region bounded by the ellipse: $$\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$$ Also find the area of the region bounded by the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Part 1: Area of $\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$:

  1. The ellipse is symmetric about both the x-axis and y-axis.
    Semi-major axis $a = \sqrt{16} = 4$, semi-minor axis $b = \sqrt{9} = 3$.
    Total Area $= 4 \times (\text{Area in first quadrant})$.
  2. In the first quadrant: $$\frac{y^2}{9} = 1 - \frac{x^2}{16} = \frac{16 - x^2}{16} \implies y = \frac{3}{4}\sqrt{16 - x^2}$$
  3. Total Area: $$A = 4 \int_0^4 y \, dx = 4 \int_0^4 \frac{3}{4}\sqrt{16 - x^2} \, dx = 3 \int_0^4 \sqrt{4^2 - x^2} \, dx$$
  4. Using standard formula $\int \sqrt{a^2 - x^2} \, dx = \frac{x}{2}\sqrt{a^2 - x^2} + \frac{a^2}{2}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)$: $$A = 3 \left[ \frac{x}{2}\sqrt{16 - x^2} + \frac{16}{2}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{4}\right) \right]_0^4$$ $$= 3 \left[ \left(0 + 8\sin^{-1}(1)\right) - \left(0 + 8\sin^{-1}(0)\right) \right] = 3 \left[ 8 \left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) - 0 \right] = 3(4\pi) = \mathbf{12\pi\text{ sq. units}}$$

(Check with formula $\pi a b = \pi(4)(3) = 12\pi\text{ sq. units}$. Verified.)

Part 2: Area of $\frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$:

Here $a = 2, b = 3$. In the first quadrant, $y = \frac{3}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^2}$. $$A = 4 \int_0^2 \frac{3}{2}\sqrt{2^2 - x^2} \, dx = 6 \left[ \frac{x}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^2} + \frac{4}{2}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) \right]_0^2$$ $$= 6 \left[ 0 + 2\sin^{-1}(1) - 0 \right] = 6 \left[ 2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) \right] = 6\pi = \mathbf{6\pi\text{ sq. units}}$$

(Check: $\pi a b = \pi(2)(3) = 6\pi\text{ sq. units}$. Verified.)

Q4
Exercise 8.1 (Verbatim)
Find the area of the region in the first quadrant enclosed by x-axis, line $x = \sqrt{3}y$ and the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Step 1: Point of Intersection:

Substitute $x = \sqrt{3}y$ into $x^2 + y^2 = 4$: $$(\sqrt{3}y)^2 + y^2 = 4 \implies 3y^2 + y^2 = 4 \implies 4y^2 = 4 \implies y^2 = 1$$ Since we are in the first quadrant, $y = 1$.
Then $x = \sqrt{3}(1) = \sqrt{3}$.
The line intersects the circle at $P(\sqrt{3}, 1)$.

Step 2: Region Partition:

The area consists of two parts along the x-axis:
  1. From $x = 0$ to $x = \sqrt{3}$: under the line $y = \frac{x}{\sqrt{3}}$.
  2. From $x = \sqrt{3}$ to $x = 2$ (the radius of the circle): under the circle $y = \sqrt{4 - x^2}$.
$$\text{Total Area } A = \int_0^{\sqrt{3}} \frac{x}{\sqrt{3}} \, dx + \int_{\sqrt{3}}^2 \sqrt{4 - x^2} \, dx = A_1 + A_2$$

Step 3: Evaluate Integrals:

  1. $$A_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_0^{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \left( \frac{3}{2} - 0 \right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$
  2. $$A_2 = \left[ \frac{x}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^2} + \frac{4}{2}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) \right]_{\sqrt{3}}^2$$ $$= \left[ 0 + 2\sin^{-1}(1) \right] - \left[ \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sqrt{4 - 3} + 2\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) \right]$$ $$= 2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) - \left[ \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(1) + 2\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) \right] = \pi - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - \frac{2\pi}{3} = \frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

Step 4: Combine:

$$A = A_1 + A_2 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \left( \frac{\pi}{3} - \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) = \mathbf{\frac{\pi}{3}\text{ sq. units}}$$

Q5
Exercise 8.1 (Verbatim)
Find the area of the smaller part of the circle $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$ cut off by the line $x = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Step 1: Sketch & Symmetry:

The circle $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$ has radius $a$. The vertical line $x = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}$ cuts the circle into a larger piece (to the left) and a smaller piece (to the right, from $x = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}$ to $x = a$).
The region is symmetric about the x-axis, so: $$\text{Total Area } A = 2 \int_{a/\sqrt{2}}^a y \, dx = 2 \int_{a/\sqrt{2}}^a \sqrt{a^2 - x^2} \, dx$$

Step 2: Integration:

$$A = 2 \left[ \frac{x}{2}\sqrt{a^2 - x^2} + \frac{a^2}{2}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{a}\right) \right]_{a/\sqrt{2}}^a$$ Substitute upper limit $x = a$: $$\text{Upper} = 0 + \frac{a^2}{2}\sin^{-1}(1) = \frac{a^2}{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi a^2}{4}$$ Substitute lower limit $x = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}$: $$\text{Lower} = \frac{a}{2\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{a^2 - \frac{a^2}{2}} + \frac{a^2}{2}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \frac{a}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}\right) + \frac{a^2}{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = \frac{a^2}{4} + \frac{\pi a^2}{8}$$

Step 3: Difference:

$$A = 2 \left[ \frac{\pi a^2}{4} - \left(\frac{a^2}{4} + \frac{\pi a^2}{8}\right) \right] = 2 \left[ \frac{\pi a^2}{8} - \frac{a^2}{4} \right] = \mathbf{\frac{a^2}{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2} - 1\right)\text{ sq. units}}$$

Q6
Exercise 8.1 (Verbatim)
The area between $x = y^2$ and $x = 4$ is divided into two equal parts by the line $x = a$, find the value of $a$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

The curve $x = y^2$ is symmetric about the x-axis. The total area from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$ is:

$$A_{\text{total}} = 2 \int_0^4 \sqrt{x} \, dx = 2 \left[ \frac{2}{3} x^{3/2} \right]_0^4 = \frac{4}{3} [4^{3/2} - 0] = \frac{4}{3} (8) = \frac{32}{3}$$

The line $x = a$ ($0 < a < 4$) divides this area into two equal parts, so the area from $x = 0$ to $x = a$ must equal half of the total area:

$$A_1 = 2 \int_0^a \sqrt{x} \, dx = \frac{1}{2} A_{\text{total}} = \frac{16}{3}$$ $$2 \left[ \frac{2}{3} x^{3/2} \right]_0^a = \frac{16}{3}$$ $$\frac{4}{3} a^{3/2} = \frac{16}{3}$$ $$a^{3/2} = \frac{16}{4} = 4$$ $$a = 4^{2/3} = (2^2)^{2/3} = \mathbf{4^{2/3} = 2^{4/3} = 2\sqrt[3]{2}}$$

Q7
Exercise 8.1 (Verbatim)
Find the area of the region bounded by the parabola $y = x^2$ and $y = |x|$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Step 1: Symmetry & Intersection:

Both $y = x^2$ and $y = |x|$ are symmetric about the y-axis.
In the first quadrant ($x \ge 0$), $y = x$.
Intersection points: $x^2 = x \implies x(x - 1) = 0 \implies x = 0, \; x = 1$.
For $x \in [0, 1]$, the line $y = x$ lies above the parabola $y = x^2$.

Step 2: Total Area by Symmetry:

$$\text{Total Area } A = 2 \int_0^1 (y_{\text{upper}} - y_{\text{lower}}) \, dx = 2 \int_0^1 (x - x^2) \, dx$$

Step 3: Integration:

$$A = 2 \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_0^1 = 2 \left[ \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} \right] = 2 \left( \frac{1}{6} \right) = \mathbf{\frac{1}{3}\text{ sq. units}}$$

Q8
Exercise 8.1 (Verbatim)
Find the area bounded by the curve $x^2 = 4y$ and the line $x = 4y - 2$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Step 1: Points of Intersection:

From the line, $4y = x + 2$. Substitute into parabola equation: $$x^2 = x + 2 \implies x^2 - x - 2 = 0 \implies (x - 2)(x + 1) = 0$$ $$x = -1, \quad x = 2$$ The line and curve intersect at $x = -1$ and $x = 2$.
Corresponding y-values:
  • At $x = -1$: $y = \frac{-1 + 2}{4} = \frac{1}{4}$
  • At $x = 2$: $y = \frac{2 + 2}{4} = 1$

Step 2: Upper and Lower Curves:

In the interval $[-1, 2]$, the line lies above the parabola: $$y_{\text{upper}} = \frac{x + 2}{4}, \quad y_{\text{lower}} = \frac{x^2}{4}$$

Step 3: Area Integral:

$$A = \int_{-1}^2 (y_{\text{upper}} - y_{\text{lower}}) \, dx = \frac{1}{4} \int_{-1}^2 (x + 2 - x^2) \, dx$$ $$A = \frac{1}{4} \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} + 2x - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{-1}^2$$ Evaluate at $x = 2$: $$\text{Upper} = \frac{4}{2} + 4 - \frac{8}{3} = 6 - \frac{8}{3} = \frac{10}{3}$$ Evaluate at $x = -1$: $$\text{Lower} = \frac{1}{2} - 2 - \left(-\frac{1}{3}\right) = -\frac{3}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = -\frac{7}{6}$$ Difference: $$A = \frac{1}{4} \left[ \frac{10}{3} - \left(-\frac{7}{6}\right) \right] = \frac{1}{4} \left[ \frac{20 + 7}{6} \right] = \frac{1}{4} \left(\frac{27}{6}\right) = \frac{1}{4} \left(\frac{9}{2}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{9}{8}\text{ sq. units}}$$

Q9
Exercise 8.1 (Verbatim)
Find the area of the region bounded by the curve $y^2 = 4x$ and the line $x = 3$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Step 1: Geometric Symmetry:

The parabola $y^2 = 4x$ is symmetric about the x-axis.
The vertical line $x = 3$ bounds the region from $x = 0$ to $x = 3$.
The region spans both above and below the x-axis: $$\text{Total Area } A = 2 \int_0^3 y \, dx = 2 \int_0^3 2\sqrt{x} \, dx = 4 \int_0^3 x^{1/2} \, dx$$

Step 2: Integration:

$$A = 4 \left[ \frac{x^{3/2}}{3/2} \right]_0^3 = 4 \cdot \frac{2}{3} \left[ x^{3/2} \right]_0^3 = \frac{8}{3} [3^{3/2} - 0]$$ Since $3^{3/2} = 3\sqrt{3}$: $$A = \frac{8}{3} (3\sqrt{3}) = \mathbf{8\sqrt{3}\text{ sq. units}}$$

Q10
Exercise 8.1 (Verbatim)
(i) Area lying in the first quadrant and bounded by the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ and the lines $x = 0$ and $x = 2$ is:
    (A) $\pi$      (B) $\frac{\pi}{2}$      (C) $\frac{\pi}{3}$      (D) $\frac{\pi}{4}$

(ii) Area of the region bounded by the curve $y^2 = 4x$, y-axis and the line $y = 3$ is:
    (A) $2$      (B) $\frac{9}{4}$      (C) $\frac{9}{3}$      (D) $\frac{9}{2}$
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) Circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ in first quadrant:

The lines $x = 0$ (y-axis) and $x = 2$ (the right endpoint of the circle) bound exactly one quadrant of the circle. $$A = \frac{1}{4}(\text{Area of circle of radius } 2) = \frac{1}{4}(\pi \cdot 2^2) = \frac{4\pi}{4} = \mathbf{\pi}$$ By integration: $$A = \int_0^2 \sqrt{4 - x^2} \, dx = \left[ \frac{x}{2}\sqrt{4 - x^2} + 2\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) \right]_0^2 = 2\sin^{-1}(1) = 2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \pi$$ Correct Option: (A) $\pi$

(ii) Curve $y^2 = 4x$, y-axis and $y = 3$:

Express $x$ as a function of $y$: $$x = \frac{y^2}{4}$$ Using horizontal strips from $y = 0$ to $y = 3$: $$A = \int_0^3 x \, dy = \int_0^3 \frac{y^2}{4} \, dy = \frac{1}{4} \left[ \frac{y^3}{3} \right]_0^3 = \frac{1}{12} [3^3 - 0] = \frac{27}{12} = \mathbf{\frac{9}{4}}$$ Correct Option: (B) $\frac{9}{4}$
