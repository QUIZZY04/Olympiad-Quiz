Q1
Exercise 6.1 (Verbatim)
(i) Find the rate of change of the area of a circle with respect to its radius $r$ when $r = 3\text{ cm}$ and $r = 4\text{ cm}$.
(ii) The volume of a cube is increasing at the rate of $8\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$. How fast is the surface area increasing when the length of an edge is $12\text{ cm}$?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) Rate of change of area of circle:

Area of circle $A = \pi r^2$. $$\frac{dA}{dr} = \frac{d}{dr}(\pi r^2) = 2\pi r$$
  • When $r = 3\text{ cm}$: $$\frac{dA}{dr} = 2\pi(3) = \mathbf{6\pi\text{ cm}^2/\text{cm}}$$
  • When $r = 4\text{ cm}$: $$\frac{dA}{dr} = 2\pi(4) = \mathbf{8\pi\text{ cm}^2/\text{cm}}$$

(ii) Rate of increase of surface area of cube:

Let $x$ be the edge length, $V$ the volume, and $S$ the surface area of the cube. $$V = x^3 \quad \text{and} \quad S = 6x^2$$ Given: $\frac{dV}{dt} = 8\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$.
  1. Differentiate $V$ with respect to time $t$: $$\frac{dV}{dt} = 3x^2 \frac{dx}{dt}$$ $$8 = 3x^2 \frac{dx}{dt} \implies \frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{8}{3x^2}$$
  2. Differentiate surface area $S = 6x^2$ with respect to $t$: $$\frac{dS}{dt} = 12x \frac{dx}{dt}$$
  3. Substitute $\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{8}{3x^2}$: $$\frac{dS}{dt} = 12x \left(\frac{8}{3x^2}\right) = \frac{32}{x}$$
  4. When edge $x = 12\text{ cm}$: $$\frac{dS}{dt} = \frac{32}{12} = \frac{8}{3}\text{ cm}^2/\text{s}$$

Final Answer: The surface area is increasing at $\frac{8}{3}\text{ cm}^2/\text{s}$.

Q2
Exercise 6.1 (Verbatim)
(i) A stone is dropped into a quiet lake and waves move in circles at the speed of $5\text{ cm/s}$. At the instant when the radius of the circular wave is $8\text{ cm}$, how fast is the enclosed area increasing?
(ii) The length $x$ of a rectangle is decreasing at the rate of $5\text{ cm/minute}$ and the width $y$ is increasing at the rate of $4\text{ cm/minute}$. When $x = 8\text{ cm}$ and $y = 6\text{ cm}$, find the rates of change of: (a) the perimeter, and (b) the area of the rectangle.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) Circular Waves:

Given: $\frac{dr}{dt} = 5\text{ cm/s}$ and $r = 8\text{ cm}$.
Area of circular wave $A = \pi r^2$. $$\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}(\pi r^2) = 2\pi r \frac{dr}{dt}$$ Substitute $r = 8$ and $\frac{dr}{dt} = 5$: $$\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi (8)(5) = \mathbf{80\pi\text{ cm}^2/\text{s}}$$

(ii) Rectangle Dimensions:

Given: $\frac{dx}{dt} = -5\text{ cm/min}$ (decreasing), $\frac{dy}{dt} = +4\text{ cm/min}$ (increasing), $x = 8\text{ cm}$, $y = 6\text{ cm}$.
  1. (a) Rate of change of Perimeter $P = 2(x + y)$: $$\frac{dP}{dt} = 2\left( \frac{dx}{dt} + \frac{dy}{dt} \right) = 2(-5 + 4) = 2(-1) = \mathbf{-2\text{ cm/min}}$$ The perimeter is decreasing at the rate of $2\text{ cm/minute}$.
  2. (b) Rate of change of Area $A = xy$: $$\frac{dA}{dt} = x\frac{dy}{dt} + y\frac{dx}{dt}$$ Substitute given values: $$\frac{dA}{dt} = (8)(4) + (6)(-5) = 32 - 30 = \mathbf{2\text{ cm}^2/\text{min}}$$ The area is increasing at the rate of $2\text{ cm}^2/\text{minute}$.

Q3
Exercise 6.1 (Verbatim)
(i) A ladder $5\text{ m}$ long is leaning against a wall. The bottom of the ladder is pulled along the ground, away from the wall, at the rate of $2\text{ cm/s}$. How fast is its height on the wall decreasing when the foot of the ladder is $4\text{ m}$ away from the wall?
(ii) Sand is pouring from a pipe at the rate of $12\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$. The falling sand forms a cone on the ground in such a way that the height of the cone is always one-sixth of the radius of the base. How fast is the height of the sand cone increasing when the height is $4\text{ cm}$?
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution
Figure 6.1: (a) Ladder $5\text{ m}$ sliding against wall and (b) Sand pouring cone

(i) Ladder Problem:

Let the foot of the ladder be at distance $x$ from the wall, and height on the wall be $y$.
Length of ladder $= 5\text{ m}$.
By Pythagoras theorem: $$x^2 + y^2 = 5^2 = 25$$ Given: $\frac{dx}{dt} = 2\text{ cm/s} = 0.02\text{ m/s}$.
When $x = 4\text{ m}$: $$4^2 + y^2 = 25 \implies y^2 = 9 \implies y = 3\text{ m}$$ Differentiating $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ with respect to $t$: $$2x \frac{dx}{dt} + 2y \frac{dy}{dt} = 0 \implies x \frac{dx}{dt} + y \frac{dy}{dt} = 0$$ $$\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{x}{y} \frac{dx}{dt}$$ Substitute $x = 4\text{ m}, y = 3\text{ m}, \frac{dx}{dt} = 2\text{ cm/s}$: $$\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{4}{3}(2) = -\frac{8}{3}\text{ cm/s}$$

Answer: The height on the wall is decreasing at the rate of $\frac{8}{3}\text{ cm/s}$.

(ii) Sand Pouring Cone:

Given: $\frac{dV}{dt} = 12\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$ and $h = \frac{1}{6}r \implies r = 6h$.
Volume of cone: $$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3}\pi (6h)^2 h = \frac{1}{3}\pi (36h^2) h = 12\pi h^3$$ Differentiating with respect to $t$: $$\frac{dV}{dt} = 12\pi (3h^2) \frac{dh}{dt} = 36\pi h^2 \frac{dh}{dt}$$ Substitute $\frac{dV}{dt} = 12$ and $h = 4\text{ cm}$: $$12 = 36\pi (4)^2 \frac{dh}{dt} = 36\pi (16) \frac{dh}{dt} = 576\pi \frac{dh}{dt}$$ $$\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{12}{576\pi} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{48\pi}\text{ cm/s}}$$

Answer: The height is increasing at $\frac{1}{48\pi}\text{ cm/s}$.

Q4
Exercise 6.2 (Verbatim)
(i) Show that the function given by $f(x) = e^{2x}$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$.
(ii) Show that the function given by $f(x) = \sin x$ is:
    (a) strictly increasing in $\left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$
    (b) strictly decreasing in $\left(\frac{\pi}{2}, \pi\right)$
    (c) neither increasing nor decreasing in $(0, \pi)$
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) $f(x) = e^{2x}$:

Differentiating with respect to $x$: $$f'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}(e^{2x}) = 2e^{2x}$$ We know that the exponential function $e^z > 0$ for all real numbers $z \in \mathbb{R}$.
Therefore, for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$: $$f'(x) = 2e^{2x} > 0$$ Hence, $f(x)$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$.

(ii) $f(x) = \sin x$:

$$f'(x) = \cos x$$
  1. In interval $\left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ (First Quadrant):
    Cosine is positive in the first quadrant: $\cos x > 0$.
    Thus, $f'(x) > 0 \implies f$ is strictly increasing in $\left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$.
  2. In interval $\left(\frac{\pi}{2}, \pi\right)$ (Second Quadrant):
    Cosine is negative in the second quadrant: $\cos x < 0$.
    Thus, $f'(x) < 0 \implies f$ is strictly decreasing in $\left(\frac{\pi}{2}, \pi\right)$.
  3. In interval $(0, \pi)$:
    Since $f'(x) > 0$ on $\left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ and $f'(x) < 0$ on $\left(\frac{\pi}{2}, \pi\right)$, $f'(x)$ does not maintain a constant sign across the entire interval $(0, \pi)$.
    Hence, $f$ is neither increasing nor decreasing in $(0, \pi)$.

(ii)(b) Second quadrant cos x < 0 => strictly decreasing1 Mark

Exercise 6.2 (Verbatim)
Find the intervals in which the function $f$ given by $f(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 36x + 7$ is:
(a) strictly increasing
(b) strictly decreasing
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

Given: $f(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 36x + 7$.

Step 1: Compute $f'(x)$:

$$f'(x) = 6x^2 - 6x - 36 = 6(x^2 - x - 6) = 6(x - 3)(x + 2)$$

Step 2: Find critical points ($f'(x) = 0$):

$$6(x - 3)(x + 2) = 0 \implies x = 3, \quad x = -2$$ These critical points partition the real line $(-\infty, \infty)$ into three disjoint intervals: $$(-\infty, -2), \quad (-2, 3), \quad (3, \infty)$$

Step 3: Test signs of $f'(x)$ in each interval:

Interval Sign of $(x - 3)$ Sign of $(x + 2)$ Sign of $f'(x)$ Nature of function $f$
$(-\infty, -2)$ (e.g. $x=-3$) $-$ $-$ $+$ ($> 0$) Strictly Increasing
$(-2, 3)$ (e.g. $x=0$) $-$ $+$ $-$ ($< 0$) Strictly Decreasing
$(3, \infty)$ (e.g. $x=4$) $+$ $+$ $+$ ($> 0$) Strictly Increasing

Conclusion:

  • (a) $f(x)$ is strictly increasing in $(-\infty, -2) \cup (3, \infty)$.
  • (b) $f(x)$ is strictly decreasing in $(-2, 3)$.

Exercise 6.2 (Verbatim)
(i) Find the intervals in which the function $f$ given by $f(x) = \sin 3x, \; x \in \left[0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right]$ is:
    (a) strictly increasing
    (b) strictly decreasing
(ii) Which of the following functions are strictly decreasing on $\left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$?
    (A) $\cos x$      (B) $\cos 2x$      (C) $\cos 3x$      (D) $\tan x$
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) $f(x) = \sin 3x$ on $\left[0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right]$:

$$f'(x) = 3\cos 3x$$ For critical points, set $f'(x) = 0$: $$3\cos 3x = 0 \implies \cos 3x = 0$$ Since $x \in \left[0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right]$, we have $3x \in \left[0, \frac{3\pi}{2}\right]$. $$\cos 3x = 0 \implies 3x = \frac{\pi}{2} \implies x = \frac{\pi}{6}$$ This divides the interval into $\left(0, \frac{\pi}{6}\right)$ and $\left(\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$.
  1. For $x \in \left(0, \frac{\pi}{6}\right) \implies 3x \in \left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ (Quadrant I):
    $\cos 3x > 0 \implies f'(x) > 0$.
    Hence, $f(x)$ is strictly increasing in $\left[0, \frac{\pi}{6}\right]$.
  2. For $x \in \left(\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{\pi}{2}\right) \implies 3x \in \left(\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2}\right)$ (Quadrants II & III):
    $\cos 3x < 0 \implies f'(x) < 0$.
    Hence, $f(x)$ is strictly decreasing in $\left[\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{\pi}{2}\right]$.

(ii) Testing decreasing nature on $\left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$:

  • (A) $f_1(x) = \cos x \implies f_1'(x) = -\sin x < 0$ on $(0, \pi/2)$. → Strictly decreasing.
  • (B) $f_2(x) = \cos 2x \implies f_2'(x) = -2\sin 2x$.
    For $x \in (0, \pi/2) \implies 2x \in (0, \pi) \implies \sin 2x > 0 \implies f_2'(x) < 0$. → Strictly decreasing.
  • (C) $f_3(x) = \cos 3x \implies f_3'(x) = -3\sin 3x$.
    For $x \in (0, \pi/2) \implies 3x \in (0, 3\pi/2)$. $\sin 3x$ changes sign at $3x = \pi \implies x = \pi/3$. → Neither.
  • (D) $f_4(x) = \tan x \implies f_4'(x) = \sec^2 x > 0$. → Strictly increasing.

Answer: Both (A) $\cos x$ and (B) $\cos 2x$ are strictly decreasing on $\left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$.

Exercise 6.3 (Verbatim)
Find the maximum and minimum values, if any, of the following functions given by:
(i) $f(x) = (2x - 1)^2 + 3$
(ii) $f(x) = 9x^2 + 12x + 2$
(iii) $f(x) = -|x + 1| + 3$
(iv) $g(x) = x^3 + 1$
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) $f(x) = (2x - 1)^2 + 3$:

Since the square of any real number is non-negative: $$(2x - 1)^2 \ge 0 \quad \forall x \in \mathbb{R}$$ $$(2x - 1)^2 + 3 \ge 3 \implies f(x) \ge 3$$ The minimum value occurs when $2x - 1 = 0 \implies x = \frac{1}{2}$, giving $f(1/2) = 3$.
Since $(2x - 1)^2$ can be made arbitrarily large as $x \to \pm\infty$, there is no maximum value.
Verdict: Minimum value $= 3$ (at $x = 1/2$); No maximum value.

(ii) $f(x) = 9x^2 + 12x + 2$:

Completing the square: $$f(x) = (3x)^2 + 2(3x)(2) + 4 - 2 = (3x + 2)^2 - 2$$ Since $(3x + 2)^2 \ge 0$, we have $f(x) \ge -2$.
Minimum value occurs when $3x + 2 = 0 \implies x = -\frac{2}{3}$, giving $f(-2/3) = -2$.
Verdict: Minimum value $= -2$ (at $x = -2/3$); No maximum value.

(iii) $f(x) = -|x + 1| + 3$:

Since $|x + 1| \ge 0 \implies -|x + 1| \le 0$. $$-|x + 1| + 3 \le 3 \implies f(x) \le 3$$ Maximum value occurs when $x + 1 = 0 \implies x = -1$, giving $f(-1) = 3$.
Verdict: Maximum value $= 3$ (at $x = -1$); No minimum value.

(iv) $g(x) = x^3 + 1$:

$g'(x) = 3x^2$. Critical point: $3x^2 = 0 \implies x = 0$.
For $x < 0$, $g'(x) > 0$. For $x > 0$, $g'(x) > 0$.
$g'(x)$ does not change sign as $x$ increases through $0$. Hence, $x = 0$ is an inflection point.
As $x \to \infty$, $g(x) \to \infty$; as $x \to -\infty$, $g(x) \to -\infty$.
Verdict: Neither maximum value nor minimum value exists.

Exercise 6.3 (Verbatim)
(i) Find all points of local maxima and local minima of the function $f$ given by $f(x) = 2x^3 - 6x^2 + 6x + 5$.
(ii) Find the absolute maximum value and the absolute minimum value of the function $f$ given by $f(x) = 2x^3 - 15x^2 + 36x + 1$ on the interval $[1, 5]$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) Local Extrema of $f(x) = 2x^3 - 6x^2 + 6x + 5$:

  1. $f'(x) = 6x^2 - 12x + 6 = 6(x^2 - 2x + 1) = 6(x - 1)^2$.
  2. Critical points: $f'(x) = 0 \implies 6(x - 1)^2 = 0 \implies x = 1$.
  3. First derivative test:
    For $x < 1$: $(x - 1)^2 > 0 \implies f'(x) > 0$.
    For $x > 1$: $(x - 1)^2 > 0 \implies f'(x) > 0$.
    Since $f'(x)$ does not change sign as $x$ increases through $1$, $x = 1$ is neither a point of local maxima nor a point of local minima.

Conclusion: $x = 1$ is a point of inflection. The function has no local maxima and no local minima.

(ii) Absolute Extrema of $f(x) = 2x^3 - 15x^2 + 36x + 1$ on $[1, 5]$:

  1. Find critical points in $(1, 5)$: $$f'(x) = 6x^2 - 30x + 36 = 6(x^2 - 5x + 6) = 6(x - 2)(x - 3)$$ $$f'(x) = 0 \implies x = 2, \quad x = 3 \quad (\text{both lie in } [1, 5])$$
  2. Evaluate $f(x)$ at critical points ($x = 2, 3$) and end points ($x = 1, 5$):
    • $f(1) = 2(1)^3 - 15(1)^2 + 36(1) + 1 = 2 - 15 + 36 + 1 = 24$
    • $f(2) = 2(8) - 15(4) + 36(2) + 1 = 16 - 60 + 72 + 1 = 29$
    • $f(3) = 2(27) - 15(9) + 36(3) + 1 = 54 - 135 + 108 + 1 = 28$
    • $f(5) = 2(125) - 15(25) + 36(5) + 1 = 250 - 375 + 180 + 1 = 56$

Comparing the values:

  • Absolute Maximum Value: $\mathbf{56}$ (at $x = 5$)
  • Absolute Minimum Value: $\mathbf{24}$ (at $x = 1$)

Q9
Exercise 6.3 (Verbatim)
(i) Find two numbers whose sum is $24$ and whose product is as large as possible.
(ii) Find two positive numbers $x$ and $y$ such that $x + y = 60$ and $xy^3$ is maximum.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution

(i) Sum is 24, maximum product:

Let the two numbers be $x$ and $y$. Given $x + y = 24 \implies y = 24 - x$.
Product function: $$P(x) = x y = x(24 - x) = 24x - x^2$$
  1. Differentiate with respect to $x$: $$P'(x) = 24 - 2x$$
  2. Critical points: $P'(x) = 0 \implies 24 - 2x = 0 \implies x = 12$.
  3. Second derivative test: $$P''(x) = -2 < 0$$ Since $P''(12) = -2 < 0$, $P(x)$ has a local maximum at $x = 12$.
  4. Value of $y$: $y = 24 - 12 = 12$.

Answer: The two numbers are $12$ and $12$ (Maximum product $= 144$).

(ii) $x + y = 60$ and $xy^3$ is maximum ($x, y > 0$):

Given $x = 60 - y$.
Function to maximize: $$F(y) = (60 - y) y^3 = 60y^3 - y^4$$
  1. Differentiate with respect to $y$: $$F'(y) = 180y^2 - 4y^3 = 4y^2(45 - y)$$
  2. Critical points ($F'(y) = 0$):
    Since $y > 0$, $4y^2 \neq 0 \implies 45 - y = 0 \implies y = 45$.
  3. Second derivative test: $$F''(y) = 360y - 12y^2$$ At $y = 45$: $$F''(45) = 360(45) - 12(45)^2 = 45[360 - 540] = 45(-180) = -8100 < 0$$ Since $F''(45) < 0$, $F(y)$ attains a maximum at $y = 45$.
  4. Value of $x$: $x = 60 - 45 = 15$.

Answer: The required positive numbers are $x = 15$ and $y = 45$.

Q10
Exercise 6.3 (Verbatim)
A square piece of tin of side $18\text{ cm}$ is to be made into a box without top, by cutting a square from each corner and folding up the flaps to form the box. What should be the side of the square to be cut off so that the volume of the box is the maximum possible?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution
Figure 6.2: Flat sheet with corner squares $x \times x$ cut out & folded open box

Let the side of the square cut from each corner be $x\text{ cm}$.

When the flaps are folded up:

  • Length of box $l = 18 - 2x$
  • Breadth of box $b = 18 - 2x$
  • Height of box $h = x$
  • Constraint: Since dimensions must be positive, $x > 0$ and $18 - 2x > 0 \implies 0 < x < 9$.

Volume of the box:

$$V(x) = l \cdot b \cdot h = (18 - 2x)^2 x = (324 - 72x + 4x^2)x = 4x^3 - 72x^2 + 324x$$

Step 1: First derivative:

$$V'(x) = 12x^2 - 144x + 324 = 12(x^2 - 12x + 27) = 12(x - 3)(x - 9)$$

Step 2: Critical points:

$$V'(x) = 0 \implies x = 3 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 9$$ Since $x < 9$ (if $x = 9$, side length $= 18 - 18 = 0$, volume $= 0$), the only feasible critical point is: $$x = 3$$

Step 3: Second derivative test:

$$V''(x) = \frac{d}{dx}(12x^2 - 144x + 324) = 24x - 144$$ At $x = 3$: $$V''(3) = 24(3) - 144 = 72 - 144 = -72 < 0$$ Since $V''(3) < 0$, the volume $V$ is maximum when $x = 3$.

Step 4: Maximum Volume:

$$V_{\text{max}} = (18 - 2(3))^2 (3) = (12)^2 (3) = 144 \times 3 = \mathbf{432\text{ cm}^3}$$

Final Answer: The side of the square to be cut off is $3\text{ cm}$, yielding a maximum volume of $432\text{ cm}^3$.

Q11
Exercise 6.3 (Verbatim)
Prove that the volume of the largest cone that can be inscribed in a sphere of radius $R$ is $\frac{8}{27}$ of the volume of the sphere.
[6 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution
Figure 6.3: Right circular cone of height $h = R + x$ inscribed in sphere of radius $R$

Let $R$ be the radius of the sphere with center $O$.

Let a cone of base radius $r$ and height $h$ be inscribed in this sphere.

Let $x$ be the distance from the center $O$ of the sphere to the base of the cone.
Then: $$h = R + x \quad (-R < x < R)$$ In the right triangle formed by radius of sphere $R$, distance $x$, and base radius $r$: $$r^2 + x^2 = R^2 \implies r^2 = R^2 - x^2$$

Volume of the cone:

$$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3}\pi (R^2 - x^2)(R + x)$$ $$V(x) = \frac{\pi}{3} (R^3 + R^2 x - R x^2 - x^3)$$

Step 1: First derivative with respect to $x$:

$$V'(x) = \frac{\pi}{3} (R^2 - 2Rx - 3x^2) = \frac{\pi}{3} (R - 3x)(R + x)$$ Setting $V'(x) = 0$: $$(R - 3x)(R + x) = 0 \implies x = \frac{R}{3} \quad (\text{since } x = -R \implies h = 0)$$

Step 2: Second derivative test:

$$V''(x) = \frac{\pi}{3} (-2R - 6x)$$ At $x = \frac{R}{3}$: $$V''\left(\frac{R}{3}\right) = \frac{\pi}{3} \left(-2R - 6\left(\frac{R}{3}\right)\right) = \frac{\pi}{3} (-2R - 2R) = -\frac{4\pi R}{3} < 0$$ Since $V''\left(\frac{R}{3}\right) < 0$, the volume of the cone is maximum at $x = \frac{R}{3}$.

Step 3: Calculating Maximum Volume:

At $x = \frac{R}{3}$: $$h = R + \frac{R}{3} = \frac{4R}{3}$$ $$r^2 = R^2 - \left(\frac{R}{3}\right)^2 = R^2 - \frac{R^2}{9} = \frac{8R^2}{9}$$ Substitute into cone volume: $$V_{\text{max}} = \frac{1}{3}\pi \left(\frac{8R^2}{9}\right) \left(\frac{4R}{3}\right) = \frac{1}{3}\pi \frac{32R^3}{27} = \frac{8}{27} \left( \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 \right)$$ Since the volume of the sphere is $V_{\text{sphere}} = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$: $$\mathbf{V_{\text{max}} = \frac{8}{27} V_{\text{sphere}}}$$ Hence proved.

Q12
Exercise 6.3 (Verbatim)
Show that the semi-vertical angle of the cone of the maximum volume and of given slant height is $\tan^{-1}\sqrt{2}$.
Also, show that semi-vertical angle of right circular cone of given surface area and maximum volume is $\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)$.
[6 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution
Figure 6.4: Cone geometry with semi-vertical angle $\alpha$, slant height $l$, radius $r$, and height $h$

Part 1: Given Slant Height $l$, Maximum Volume:

Let $l$ be the given slant height, and $\alpha$ be the semi-vertical angle of the cone.
Radius $r = l \sin\alpha$, Height $h = l \cos\alpha$.
Volume of the cone: $$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3}\pi (l\sin\alpha)^2 (l\cos\alpha) = \frac{\pi l^3}{3} \sin^2\alpha \cos\alpha$$ Differentiating with respect to $\alpha$: $$\frac{dV}{d\alpha} = \frac{\pi l^3}{3} [2\sin\alpha \cos\alpha (\cos\alpha) + \sin^2\alpha(-\sin\alpha)] = \frac{\pi l^3}{3} \sin\alpha [2\cos^2\alpha - \sin^2\alpha]$$ Setting $\frac{dV}{d\alpha} = 0$: $$2\cos^2\alpha - \sin^2\alpha = 0 \implies \sin^2\alpha = 2\cos^2\alpha \implies \tan^2\alpha = 2 \implies \mathbf{\tan\alpha = \sqrt{2}}$$ $$\mathbf{\alpha = \tan^{-1}\sqrt{2}}$$ Checking second derivative: $\frac{d^2V}{d\alpha^2} < 0$ at $\tan\alpha = \sqrt{2}$, confirming maximum volume.

Part 2: Given Surface Area $S$, Maximum Volume:

Total surface area of cone: $$S = \pi r^2 + \pi r l \implies l = \frac{S - \pi r^2}{\pi r}$$ Height $h^2 = l^2 - r^2 = \left( \frac{S - \pi r^2}{\pi r} \right)^2 - r^2 = \frac{(S - \pi r^2)^2 - \pi^2 r^4}{\pi^2 r^2} = \frac{S^2 - 2\pi r^2 S}{\pi^2 r^2}$.
Square of Volume: $$V^2 = \frac{1}{9}\pi^2 r^4 h^2 = \frac{1}{9}\pi^2 r^4 \left( \frac{S^2 - 2\pi r^2 S}{\pi^2 r^2} \right) = \frac{S}{9} (S r^2 - 2\pi r^4) = Z$$ Differentiating $Z$ with respect to $r$: $$\frac{dZ}{dr} = \frac{S}{9} (2Sr - 8\pi r^3)$$ Setting $\frac{dZ}{dr} = 0$: $$2Sr - 8\pi r^3 = 0 \implies S = 4\pi r^2$$ Substitute $S = \pi r^2 + \pi r l$: $$4\pi r^2 = \pi r^2 + \pi r l \implies 3\pi r^2 = \pi r l \implies 3r = l \implies \frac{r}{l} = \frac{1}{3}$$ If $\alpha$ is the semi-vertical angle: $$\sin\alpha = \frac{r}{l} = \frac{1}{3} \implies \mathbf{\alpha = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)}$$ Since $\frac{d^2Z}{dr^2} = \frac{S}{9}(2S - 24\pi r^2) = \frac{S}{9}(8\pi r^2 - 24\pi r^2) = -\frac{16\pi r^2 S}{9} < 0$, volume is maximum.
Hence proved.
