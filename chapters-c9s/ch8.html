Which of the following has more inertia:
(a) a rubber ball and a stone of the same size?
(b) a bicycle and a train?
(c) a five-rupees coin and a one-rupee coin?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Scientific Principle:
Inertia is the inherent natural property of an object to resist any change in its state of rest or uniform motion. The inertia of an object is directly proportional to its mass (Inertia ∝ Mass). A heavier mass possesses greater inertia.
Answers:
  • (a) A stone of the same size: A stone has much higher density and significantly greater mass than a rubber ball of identical volume. Hence, the stone has more inertia.
  • (b) A train: A train has an enormously larger mass than a bicycle. Hence, the train has far more inertia.
  • (c) A five-rupees coin: A five-rupee coin is thicker, heavier, and has greater mass than a one-rupee coin. Hence, the five-rupees coin has more inertia.

Page 118 — Q3
Explain why some of the leaves may get detached from a tree if we vigorously shake its branch.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Scientific Explanation based on Inertia of Rest:
1. Initially, both the tree branch and its leaves are at rest.
2. When we vigorously shake the branch, the branch is suddenly brought into a state of rapid oscillating motion.
3. However, due to inertia of rest (Newton's First Law of Motion), the leaves tend to maintain their original stationary position.
4. This creates a severe mechanical shear stress at the junction (petiole) connecting the leaves to the branch.
5. The fragile stalks break under this unbalanced inertial stress, causing the leaves to detach and fall under gravity.

Page 118 — Q4
Why do you fall in the forward direction when a moving bus brakes to a stop and fall backwards when it accelerates from rest?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Falling Forward when Bus Brakes to a Stop (Inertia of Motion):
• While the bus is moving, our entire body shares the forward velocity of the bus.
• When brakes are suddenly applied, the lower part of our body in direct contact with the bus floor comes to rest immediately due to friction.
• However, the upper part of our body tends to continue moving forward due to inertia of motion, causing us to lurch forward.
2. Falling Backward when Bus Accelerates from Rest (Inertia of Rest):
• When the bus is at rest, our entire body is stationary.
• When the bus suddenly accelerates forward, our feet move forward with the floor.
• But the upper body tends to remain at rest due to inertia of rest. The bus floor moves forward underneath us, making us fall backward.

NCERT Intext Questions (Page 126)
Page 126 — Q1
If action is always equal to the reaction, explain how a horse can pull a cart.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Resolution of Action and Reaction on Different Bodies:
1. According to Newton's Third Law, action and reaction are always equal and opposite, but they act on two completely different bodies, so they never cancel each other out.
2. To pull the cart forward, the horse bends forward and pushes the ground backward with its hooves with a muscular force (F_{push}, Action).
3. According to Newton's Third Law, the ground exerts an equal and opposite forward reaction force on the horse (R_{ground}, Reaction).
4. The forward component of this reaction force exceeds the backward friction force between the cart wheels and the ground (R_{forward} > F_{friction}).
5. This net unbalanced forward force accelerates the horse and cart forward.

Action and reaction act on different bodies (horse and ground)1 Mark
Horse pushes ground backward (action); ground pushes horse forward (reaction)1 Mark
Net forward reaction force overcomes wheel friction to accelerate cart1 Mark
Page 126 — Q2
Explain, why is it difficult for a fireman to hold a hose, which ejects large amounts of water at a high velocity.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Newton's Third Law & Momentum Conservation:
1. A firefighting hose ejects a massive stream of water (dm/dt) forward with tremendous velocity (v), carrying a very high forward momentum.
2. According to Newton's Third Law of Motion, for this powerful forward action of water, an exactly equal and opposite backward reaction force is exerted onto the nozzle of the hose (F_{reaction} = - v(dm/dt)).
3. This strong backward thrust pushes the hose vigorously backward, making it extremely difficult for a single firefighter to hold it steady without being thrown off balance.

Page 126 — Q3
From a rifle of mass 4 kg, a bullet of mass 50 g is fired with an initial velocity of 35 m s⁻¹. Calculate the initial recoil velocity of the rifle.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given Data:
• Mass of rifle (m₁) = 4 kg
• Mass of bullet (m₂) = 50 g = 50 / 1000 = 0.05 kg
• Initial velocity of rifle and bullet before firing: u₁ = 0, u₂ = 0
• Muzzle velocity of bullet (v₂) = +35 m s⁻¹
• Recoil velocity of rifle = v₁
Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum:
Total Momentum before firing = Total Momentum after firing
0 = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂
4 × v₁ + 0.05 × 35 = 0
4 v₁ + 1.75 = 0 ⟹ 4 v₁ = -1.75
v₁ = -1.75 / 4 = -0.4375 m s⁻¹.

Final Answer: The recoil velocity of the rifle is -0.4375 m s⁻¹ (the negative sign indicates the rifle recoils backward in the direction opposite to the bullet's flight).

Two objects of masses 100 g and 200 g are moving along the same line and direction with velocities of 2 m s⁻¹ and 1 m s⁻¹, respectively. They collide and after the collision, the first object moves at a velocity of 1.67 m s⁻¹. Determine the velocity of the second object.

Given:
• m₁ = 100 g = 0.1 kg, u₁ = +2 m s⁻¹
• m₂ = 200 g = 0.2 kg, u₂ = +1 m s⁻¹
• After collision: v₁ = +1.67 m s⁻¹, v₂ = ?

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

0.4 = 0.167 + 0.2 v₂
0.2 v₂ = 0.4 - 0.167 = 0.233
v₂ = 0.233 / 0.2 = 1.165 m s⁻¹.

Final Answer: The velocity of the second object after collision is +1.165 m s⁻¹ (moving forward in the same direction).

Exercise — Q3
Why is it advised to tie any luggage kept on the roof of a bus with a rope?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Reasons Based on Inertia:
Luggage placed on the roof of a bus is not rigidly attached to the vehicle. It is subject to sudden changes in motion:
  1. When bus starts suddenly: Due to inertia of rest, the luggage tends to remain stationary while the bus moves forward, causing luggage to slide off the rear.
  2. When bus brakes suddenly: Due to inertia of motion, the luggage continues its forward motion, potentially flying off the front.
  3. When bus takes a sharp turn: Due to inertia of direction, the luggage tends to move in a straight line, sliding sideways off the roof.
Tying it firmly with a rope provides the necessary restraining force against all these inertial forces.

Exercise — Q11
According to the third law of motion, action and reaction are equal and opposite. How then can a car move forward when the wheels push against the ground?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Newton's Third Law Acting on Distinct Bodies:
1. Action and reaction forces never act on the same body; they act simultaneously on two different bodies.
2. The rotating wheels of the car push backward against the ground via tire friction (Action on the road).
3. Simultaneously, the ground exerts an equal and opposite forward reaction force on the car tires (Reaction on the car).

Exercise — Q12
Explain why it is easier to stop a tennis ball than a cricket ball moving with the same velocity.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Concept of Linear Momentum (p = mv):
1. Momentum depends on both mass and velocity (p = mv).
2. A cricket ball has significantly greater mass than a tennis ball (m_{cricket} \gg m_{tennis}).
3. When both travel with the same velocity (v), the cricket ball has a much larger momentum than the tennis ball.
4. By Newton's Second Law (F = Δp / Δt), bringing the cricket ball to rest requires a substantially greater stopping force or greater impulse, making it much harder to stop.

📝 Case Study Question
Cricketer Catching a Fast Ball: A cricket fielder pulls his hands backward while catching a fast cricket ball of mass 0.15 kg travelling at 30 m s⁻¹.
(a) Why does the fielder pull his hands backward?
(b) What is the impulse delivered to the hands?
(c) What would happen if the hands were held stiff and stationary?
(a) Pulling hands backward increases the time duration (Δt) over which the ball's momentum is reduced to zero. Since F = Δp / Δt, increasing time drastically reduces the impact force on the player's palms.
(b) Impulse = Δp = m(v - u) = 0.15 × (0 - 30) = -4.5 N s (magnitude 4.5 N s).
(c) Stopping the ball in an extremely short time interval would exert a massive impact force, causing severe pain and potential bone fracture.
🧠 HOTS
HOTS Question: Why does a gun always recoil backward when fired, and why is the recoil velocity of the gun much smaller than the muzzle velocity of the bullet?
By conservation of momentum, total momentum before firing is zero, so after firing: m_{gun} v_{gun} + m_{bullet} v_{bullet} = 0 ⟹ v_{gun} = - (m_{bullet} / m_{gun}) v_{bullet}. The negative sign represents backward recoil. Because the mass of the gun is hundreds of times greater than the mass of the bullet (m_{gun} \gg m_{bullet}), the recoil velocity is correspondingly much smaller.
