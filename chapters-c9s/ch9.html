NCERT Intext Questions (Page 134)
Page 134 — Q1
State the universal law of gravitation.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Statement of Universal Law of Gravitation:
Sir Isaac Newton stated that:
"Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force which is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres."

Mathematical Formula:
F = G × (m₁ × m₂) / r²
Where G is the Universal Gravitational Constant (G ≈ 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²), m₁, m₂ are the masses, and r is the distance separating them.
CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Statement: Proportional to product of masses and inversely to square of distance1 Mark

Page 134 — Q2
Write the formula to find the magnitude of the gravitational force between the earth and an object on the surface of the earth.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Formula:
Let: • Mass of the Earth = M
• Mass of the object on the surface = m
• Radius of the Earth = R (distance from center to surface)

The magnitude of the gravitational force is given by:
F = G × (M × m) / R²
(This force is also equal to the weight of the object: W = mg, where g = GM / R²).

Page 138 — Q1
What do you mean by free fall?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Scientific Definition:
When an object falls towards the center of the Earth under the influence of Earth's gravitational force alone (with all other forces such as air resistance and buoyancy being absent or neglected), the object is said to be in a state of free fall.
During free fall, the object experiences no net reaction force and exhibits apparent weightlessness.

Page 138 — Q2
What do you mean by acceleration due to gravity?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Definition of Acceleration Due to Gravity (g):
• When a body falls freely under gravity, its velocity increases uniformly with time.
• The uniform acceleration produced in a freely falling body due to the gravitational pull of the Earth is called the acceleration due to gravity, denoted by the symbol g.
• Its standard value on Earth's surface at sea level is approximately g = 9.8 m s⁻² (or 9.8 N kg⁻¹).
• Formula: g = GM / R², which is independent of the mass of the falling object.

Page 141 — Q2
Why is the weight of an object on the moon 1/6th its weight on the earth?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Derivation from Newton's Gravitation Law:
Weight is proportional to surface acceleration due to gravity: W = mg, where g = GM / R².

• Mass of the Moon (M_m) is about 1/100 of Earth's mass (M_e).
• Radius of the Moon (R_m) is about 1/4 of Earth's radius (R_e).

g_{moon} / g_{earth} = (M_m / M_e) × (R_e / R_m)²
g_{moon} / g_{earth} = (1 / 100) × (4 / 1)² = 16 / 100 ≈ 1/6.

Because the Moon has significantly smaller mass and radius, its gravitational pull is only one-sixth as strong as Earth's (g_{moon} ≈ 1.63 m s⁻² ≈ g_e / 6).
Therefore, an object's weight on the moon is exactly 1/6th of its weight on Earth.

NCERT Intext Questions (Page 142)
Page 142 — Q1
Why is it difficult to hold a school bag having a strap made of a thin and strong string?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Pressure Formula Analysis:
Pressure = Force / Area = Thrust / Area
1. The downward force (thrust) exerted on the shoulder is equal to the weight of the loaded school bag.
2. A thin string has an extremely small contact surface area (A).
3. Since pressure is inversely proportional to surface area (P ∝ 1/A), the small contact area results in a very large pressure concentrated on the shoulder skin.
4. This intense localized pressure cuts painfully into the shoulder tissues. (Hence, wide straps are provided to distribute the load over a larger area, reducing pressure).

Formula Pressure = Thrust / Area (P ∝ 1/A)1 Mark

Page 142 — Q2
What do you mean by buoyancy?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Scientific Definition:
Buoyancy is the natural upward force exerted by any fluid (liquid or gas) on an object wholly or partially immersed in it.
• This upward force is known as the buoyant force or upthrust (F_B).
• According to Archimedes' Principle, the magnitude of buoyant force equals the weight of the fluid displaced by the immersed body: F_B = V \cdot ho_{fluid} \cdot g.

Page 142 — Q3
Why does an object float or sink when placed on the surface of water?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Principle of Flotation based on Density & Forces:
Whether an object floats or sinks depends on the relative density of the object compared to water ( ho_{water} = 1 g cm⁻³ = 1000 kg m⁻³):
  1. Sinking: If the density of the object is greater than the density of water ( ho_{object} > ho_{water}), the downward gravitational pull (weight) of the object exceeds the maximum buoyant force. The net downward force pulls the object down, causing it to sink (e.g. an iron nail).
  2. Floating: If the density of the object is less than or equal to the density of water ( ho_{object} < ho_{water}), the buoyant force equals or exceeds the object's weight. The object remains on the surface and floats (e.g. a piece of cork or wood).
CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Object sinks if its density is greater than liquid density (Weight > Upthrust)1 Mark
Object floats if its density is less than liquid density (Upthrust ≥ Weight)1 Mark

Exercise — Q1
How does the force of gravitation between two objects change when the distance between them is reduced to half?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Inverse Square Law:
F = G (m₁ m₂) / r².
When distance is reduced to half, new distance r' = r / 2.
F' = G (m₁ m₂) / (r / 2)² = G (m₁ m₂) / (r² / 4) = 4 × [G (m₁ m₂) / r²] = 4 F.

Conclusion: The gravitational force between them becomes four times (quadrupled) its original value.

Exercise — Q2
Gravitational force acts on all objects in proportion to their masses. Why then, a heavy object does not fall faster than a light object?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Independence of Acceleration from Mass:
1. The gravitational force acting on an object of mass m is: F = G (M m) / R².
2. By Newton's Second Law, the acceleration produced is: a = F / m.
3. Substituting F: a = [G (M m) / R²] / m = GM / R² = g.
4. The mass of the falling object (m) completely cancels out.
5. While a heavier object experiences a larger force, it also has proportionately greater inertia (m) resisting acceleration. Therefore, all objects (heavy or light) accelerate at the exact same rate (g ≈ 9.8 m s⁻²) in vacuum and fall at the same speed.
CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Derivation showing a = g = GM/R² independent of mass m1 Mark
Greater force on heavier object balanced by greater inertia1 Mark
Exercise — Q3
What is the magnitude of the gravitational force between the earth and a 1 kg object on its surface? (Mass of the earth is 6 × 10²⁴ kg and radius of the earth is 6.4 × 10⁶ m).
3 Marks

Given:
• M = 6 × 10²⁴ kg, m = 1 kg
• R = 6.4 × 10⁶ m
• G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²
Calculation:
F = G (M m) / R² = (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 6 × 10²⁴ × 1) / (6.4 × 10⁶)²
F = (40.02 × 10¹³) / (40.96 × 10¹²) = 400.2 / 40.96 ≈ 9.77 N ≈ 9.8 N.

Final Answer: The magnitude of the gravitational force is 9.8 N (which equals the weight of a 1 kg mass on Earth).

Exercise — Q4
The earth and the moon are attracted to each other by gravitational force. Does the earth attract the moon with a force that is greater or smaller or the same as the force with which the moon attracts the earth? Why?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Answer: The Earth attracts the Moon with the EXACT SAME force with which the Moon attracts the Earth.

Reason:
• According to Newton's Third Law of Motion, every action has an equal and opposite reaction. Gravitational attraction is a mutual interaction between two bodies.
• In Newton's law of gravitation, F = G (M_e M_m) / r²; the formula is symmetric with respect to both masses.
• Therefore, the gravitational force exerted by Earth on Moon and the force exerted by Moon on Earth form an action-reaction pair of strictly equal magnitude.

Exercise — Q5
If the moon attracts the earth, why does the earth not move towards the moon?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Comparison of Accelerations:
1. By Newton's Third Law, the force on Earth is equal in magnitude to the force on the Moon (F_{earth} = F_{moon}).
2. By Newton's Second Law, acceleration is inversely proportional to mass: a = F / M.
3. The mass of the Earth is enormously large (approximately 81 times greater than the Moon, M_e ≈ 81 M_m).
4. Consequently, the acceleration produced in the Earth by the Moon's pull is extremely tiny (a_{earth} ≈ 1/81 a_{moon}), so small that it is completely undetectable in daily life. (Instead, Earth and Moon orbit their common centre of mass, called the barycentre).
CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Formula a = F/M showing acceleration inversely proportional to mass1 Mark
Enormous mass of Earth produces imperceptibly small acceleration1 Mark
Exercise — Q6
What happens to the force between two objects, if:
(i) the mass of one object is doubled?
(ii) the distance between the objects is doubled and tripled?
(iii) the masses of both objects are doubled?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Formula: F = G (m₁ m₂) / r²

• (i) Mass of one object is doubled (m₁' = 2m₁):
F' = 2F ⟹ The force is doubled.

• (ii) Distance is doubled (r' = 2r):
F' = F / 2² = F / 4 ⟹ The force becomes one-fourth (1/4th).
Distance is tripled (r' = 3r):
F' = F / 3² = F / 9 ⟹ The force becomes one-ninth (1/9th).

• (iii) Masses of both objects are doubled (m₁' = 2m₁, m₂' = 2m₂):
F' = (2 × 2) F = 4F ⟹ The force becomes four times.

(i) Force is doubled1 Mark
(ii) Doubled distance ⟹ 1/4th; Tripled distance ⟹ 1/9th1 Mark

What is the importance of universal law of gravitation?

Cosmological Importance of Universal Law of Gravitation:
Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation successfully unified terrestrial and celestial mechanics by explaining phenomena previously thought unrelated:
  1. The gravitational force that binds human beings and all objects firmly to the surface of the Earth.
  2. The orbital motion of the Moon around the Earth.
  3. The motion of all planets in elliptical orbits around the Sun.
  4. The periodic occurrence of ocean tides caused by the combined gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun on ocean waters.

Exercise — Q10
Amit buys few grams of gold at the poles as per the instruction of one of his friends. He hands over the same when he meets him at the equator. Will the friend agree with the weight of gold bought? If not, why?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Answer: No, the friend will NOT agree with the weight of the gold bought. The gold will weigh less at the equator.

Scientific Explanation:
1. The Earth is not a perfect sphere; it is an oblate spheroid flattened at the poles and bulging at the equator.
2. The polar radius is smaller than the equatorial radius (R_{pole} < R_{equator} by approx 21 km).
3. Since acceleration due to gravity is inversely proportional to the square of the radius (g = GM / R²), the value of g is maximum at the poles and minimum at the equator (g_{pole} > g_{equator}).
4. Weight is given by W = mg. Since g at the equator is smaller, the spring balance reading of weight at the equator will be less than what was recorded at the poles.

Negative answer (friend will not agree; weight decreases)1 Mark
Oblate Earth shape: R_equator > R_pole1 Mark

Exercise — Q11
Why will a sheet of paper fall slower than one that is crumpled into a ball?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Role of Air Resistance:
1. A flat sheet of paper has an expansive surface area, so it encounters a large upward air resistance (viscous drag) as it falls.
2. When the same sheet is crumpled tightly into a small ball, its surface area is reduced dramatically, experiencing minimal air resistance.
3. Consequently, the net downward acceleration of the crumpled ball (g - a_{drag}) is much higher, so it reaches terminal velocity later and hits the ground much faster than the flat sheet. (In vacuum where air is absent, both fall at the identical speed).
CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Flat sheet has large surface area experiencing high air resistance1 Mark
Crumpled ball has small surface area with negligible drag, falling faster1 Mark

Exercise — Q13
A ball is thrown vertically upwards with a velocity of 49 m s⁻¹. Calculate (i) the maximum height to which it rises, (ii) the total time it takes to return to the surface of the earth.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: u = 49 m s⁻¹, g = -9.8 m s⁻², at maximum height v = 0.

• (i) Maximum Height (h):
v² = u² + 2gh ⟹ 0² = (49)² + 2(-9.8)h
19.6 h = 2401 ⟹ h = 2401 / 19.6 = 122.5 m.

• (ii) Total Time of Flight (T):
Time of ascent (t): v = u + gt ⟹ 0 = 49 - 9.8 t ⟹ t = 49 / 9.8 = 5 s.
Time of descent equals time of ascent (t_{down} = 5 s).
Total Time = 2 × t = 2 × 5 = 10 s.

Final Answer: (i) Maximum height = 122.5 m, (ii) Total time = 10 seconds.

Exercise — Q17
A stone is allowed to fall from the top of a tower 100 m high and at the same time another stone is projected vertically upwards from the ground with a velocity of 25 m s⁻¹. Calculate when and where the two stones will meet. (Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² or 10 m s⁻²).
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Let the stones meet after time t seconds at a height x from the ground.
• Height fallen by Stone 1 (from top): s₁ = 100 - x
s₁ = ½ g t² ⟹ 100 - x = ½ g t²   ...(Equation 1)

• Height ascended by Stone 2 (from bottom): s₂ = x
s₂ = u t - ½ g t² ⟹ x = 25 t - ½ g t²   ...(Equation 2)
Adding Equation 1 and Equation 2:
(100 - x) + x = (½ g t²) + (25 t - ½ g t²)
100 = 25 t ⟹ t = 100 / 25 = 4 seconds.
Find Position of Meeting Point:
Using g = 9.8 m s⁻²:
x = 25(4) - ½ × 9.8 × (4)² = 100 - 4.9 × 16 = 100 - 78.4 = 21.6 m.
(Distance from top of tower = 100 - 21.6 = 78.4 m).

Final Answer: The stones will meet after 4 seconds at a height of 21.6 metres from the ground (or 78.4 m below the top).

Exercise — Q20
Why does a block of plastic released under water come up to the surface of water?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Comparison of Forces:
1. The density of plastic is lower than the density of water ( ho_{plastic} < ho_{water}).
2. When fully submerged under water, the weight of the water displaced by the plastic block is greater than the actual gravitational weight of the block itself.
3. Consequently, the upward buoyant force (upthrust) is greater than the downward gravitational force (F_{buoyant} > W).
4. This net upward unbalanced force accelerates the plastic block upward until it surfaces and floats with part of its volume out of the water.

Exercise — Q22
The volume of a 500 g sealed packet is 350 cm³. Will the packet float or sink in water if the density of water is 1 g cm⁻³? What will be the mass of the water displaced by this packet?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: Mass (m) = 500 g, Volume (V) = 350 cm³, ho_w = 1 g cm⁻³.
Step 1: Determine Density of Packet:
Density = Mass / Volume = 500 / 350 ≈ 1.428 g cm⁻³.
Since density (1.43 g cm⁻³) > density of water (1 g cm⁻³), the packet will sink to the bottom.
Step 2: Mass of Displaced Water:
Since the packet sinks completely, it displaces a volume of water equal to its own entire volume (350 cm³).
Mass of displaced water = Volume × Density of water = 350 cm³ × 1 g cm⁻³ = 350 g.

Final Answer: The packet will sink; the mass of displaced water is 350 grams.

📝 Case Study Question
Lactometer & Hydrometer Applications: A dairy technician uses a lactometer to test the purity of milk samples. The instrument sinks to a certain depth in pure milk, but when tested in watered-down milk, it sinks much deeper.
(a) On which fundamental scientific principle does a lactometer operate?
(b) Why does the lactometer sink deeper in adulterated (watered-down) milk?
(c) State Archimedes' principle.
(a) It operates on Archimedes' Principle (and Principle of Flotation).
(b) Pure milk has a higher density (~1.03 g cm⁻³) than water (1.00 g cm⁻³). Adding water reduces the average density of milk. To displace a sufficient weight of liquid to balance its fixed weight, the hydrometer must submerge a larger volume, sinking deeper.
(c) Archimedes' Principle states that when a body is fully or partially immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by it.
🧠 HOTS
HOTS Question: An iron needle sinks in water, but a gigantic ship made of thousands of tons of iron and steel floats easily on the ocean. Explain the scientific reason.
An iron needle is compact and solid; its density (~7.8 g cm⁻³) is far greater than water, so its weight exceeds the buoyant force of the tiny volume displaced, causing it to sink. A ship, although built of heavy steel, is hollow and encloses enormous volumes of trapped air. This vast hollow shape yields a very large overall volume and a low average density (mass/volume) that is much lower than seawater. Hence, the buoyant force easily balances the total ship weight, allowing it to float.
