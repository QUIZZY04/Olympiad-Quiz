NCERT Intext Questions (Page 100)
Page 100 — Q1
An object has moved through a distance. Can it have zero displacement? If yes, support your answer with an example.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Answer: Yes, an object that has moved through a finite distance can have zero net displacement.

Explanation:
• Distance is the total actual path length traversed by an object, which is a scalar quantity and always positive for any moving body.
• Displacement is the shortest straight-line vector distance between the initial position and final position of the object.
• If a moving object returns to its starting point, its initial position and final position coincide. Hence, its net displacement is zero.

Example: An athlete runs around a circular athletic track of radius r and completes one full round. The distance covered is 2πr, but the displacement is 0 because the runner finishes exactly where they started.

Affirmative answer (Yes) with definition of displacement1 Mark

Page 100 — Q2
A farmer moves along the boundary of a square field of side 10 m in 40 s. What will be the magnitude of displacement of the farmer at the end of 2 minutes 20 seconds from his initial position?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given Data:
• Side of square field (s) = 10 m
• Perimeter of field = 4 × 10 = 40 m
• Time to complete 1 round = 40 s
• Total time of motion = 2 min 20 s = 2 × 60 + 20 = 140 s
Step 1: Calculate Number of Rounds Completed:
Number of rounds = Total time / Time for 1 round = 140 / 40 = 3.5 rounds.
After 3 complete rounds, the farmer is back at the initial corner (say point A).
In the remaining 0.5 round (half round = 20 s), the farmer covers two sides (from A to B to C) and ends up at the diagonally opposite corner C.
Step 2: Magnitude of Displacement:
The displacement is the straight line diagonal AC of the square ABCD:
By Pythagoras Theorem in right triangle ABC:
AC = √(AB² + BC²) = √(10² + 10²) = √(100 + 100) = √200 = 10√2 m.
Taking √2 ≈ 1.414:
Displacement = 10 × 1.414 = 14.14 m.

Final Answer: The magnitude of displacement of the farmer is 10√2 m (or 14.14 m).

CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Converting time to 140 s and finding 3.5 rounds1 Mark
Identifying final position at diagonally opposite corner1 Mark
Using Pythagoras theorem to find displacement = 10√2 m ≈ 14.14 m1 Mark
Page 100 — Q3
Which of the following is true for displacement?
(a) It cannot be zero.
(b) Its magnitude is greater than the distance travelled by the object.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Evaluation:
• (a) "It cannot be zero": FALSE. Displacement becomes zero whenever the object returns to its original starting position (or does not move).

• (b) "Its magnitude is greater than the distance travelled": FALSE. Displacement is the shortest straight-line distance between two points. Therefore, the magnitude of displacement is always less than or equal to the distance travelled (|Displacement| ≤ Distance). It can never be strictly greater than distance.

Conclusion: Neither (a) nor (b) is true. Both statements are false.

CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Statement (a) is False with reason (displacement can be zero)1 Mark
Statement (b) is False with reason (|displacement| ≤ distance)1 Mark

NCERT Intext Questions (Page 103)
Page 103 — Q1
When will you say a body is in (i) uniform acceleration? (ii) non-uniform acceleration?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) Uniform Acceleration:
A body is said to have uniform acceleration if it travels in a straight line and its velocity increases (or decreases) by equal amounts in equal intervals of time, no matter how small these time intervals may be.
Example: A body falling freely under gravity in a vacuum (g ≈ 9.8 m s⁻²).

(ii) Non-Uniform Acceleration:
A body has non-uniform acceleration if its velocity changes by unequal amounts in equal intervals of time (or by equal amounts in unequal intervals).
Example: A car driving through a crowded city street with fluctuating traffic.
CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

Page 103 — Q2
A bus decreases its speed from 80 km h⁻¹ to 60 km h⁻¹ in 5 s. Find the acceleration of the bus.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Step 1: Convert Speeds into SI Units (m s⁻¹):
• Initial velocity (u) = 80 × (5/18) = 400 / 18 = 22.22 m s⁻¹
• Final velocity (v) = 60 × (5/18) = 300 / 18 = 16.67 m s⁻¹
• Time interval (t) = 5 s
Step 2: Calculate Acceleration:
a = (v - u) / t = (16.67 - 22.22) / 5 = -5.55 / 5 = -1.11 m s⁻².

Final Answer: The acceleration is -1.11 m s⁻² (or a retardation of 1.11 m s⁻²).

NCERT Intext Questions (Page 107)
Page 107 — Q1
What is the nature of the distance-time graphs for uniform and non-uniform motion of an object?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Nature of Distance-Time Graphs:
• For Uniform Motion: The distance-time graph is a straight line with a constant non-zero slope passing through the origin. The slope of the line represents constant speed.

• For Non-Uniform Motion: The distance-time graph is a curved line (non-linear graph), indicating that the rate of change of distance varies with time (accelerated or decelerated motion).

Page 109 — Q1
A bus starting from rest moves with a uniform acceleration of 0.1 m s⁻² for 2 minutes. Find (a) the speed acquired, (b) the distance travelled.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given Data:
• Starts from rest ⟹ u = 0 m s⁻¹
• Acceleration (a) = 0.1 m s⁻²
• Time (t) = 2 minutes = 2 × 60 = 120 s
(a) Speed Acquired (v):
v = u + at = 0 + (0.1 × 120) = 12 m s⁻¹.
(b) Distance Travelled (s):
s = ut + ½ at² = (0 × 120) + ½ × 0.1 × (120)² = 0.05 × 14400 = 720 m.

Final Answer: (a) Speed = 12 m s⁻¹, (b) Distance = 720 m.

Page 109 — Q2
A train is travelling at a speed of 90 km h⁻¹. Brakes are applied so as to produce a uniform acceleration of -0.5 m s⁻². Find how far the train will go before it is brought to rest.

• Acceleration (a) = -0.5 m s⁻²

0 = 625 - 1.0 s ⟹ s = 625 m.

Page 109 — Q5
A stone is thrown in a vertically upward direction with a velocity of 5 m s⁻¹. If the acceleration of the stone during its motion is 10 m s⁻² in the downward direction, what will be the height attained by the stone and how much time will it take to reach there?

• Initial velocity (u) = +5 m s⁻¹ (upward)
• Acceleration (a) = -10 m s⁻² (downward retardation)

0 = 5 + (-10)t ⟹ 10t = 5 ⟹ t = 5 / 10 = 0.5 s.

Final Answer: Maximum height = 1.25 m, Time taken = 0.5 s.

Exercise — Q1
An athlete completes one round of a circular track of diameter 200 m in 40 s. What will be the distance covered and the displacement at the end of 2 minutes 20 s?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given:
• Diameter (D) = 200 m ⟹ Radius (r) = 100 m
• Time for 1 round = 40 s
• Total time = 2 min 20 s = 140 s
Step 1: Number of Rounds:
Rounds = 140 / 40 = 3.5 rounds.
Step 2: Total Distance Covered:
Distance = 3.5 × Circumference = 3.5 × (2πr) = 3.5 × 2 × (22/7) × 100
Distance = 7 × (22/7) × 100 = 22 × 100 = 2200 m.
Step 3: Magnitude of Displacement:
After 3.5 rounds, the athlete is at the point exactly opposite to the starting point across the circle.
Displacement = Diameter of circular track = 200 m.

Final Answer: Distance = 2200 m, Displacement = 200 m.

Exercise — Q2
Joseph jogs from one end A to the other end B of a straight 300 m road in 2 min 30 s and then turns around and jogs 100 m back to point C in another 1 min. What are Joseph’s average speeds and velocities in jogging (a) from A to B and (b) from A to C?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) From A to B:
• Distance = 300 m; Displacement = 300 m (straight line).
• Time = 2 min 30 s = 150 s.
Average Speed = 300 / 150 = 2.0 m s⁻¹.
Average Velocity = 300 / 150 = 2.0 m s⁻¹ (in direction A to B).
(b) From A to C (via B):
• Total distance = AB + BC = 300 + 100 = 400 m.
• Total time = 150 s + 60 s = 210 s.
Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time = 400 / 210 ≈ 1.90 m s⁻¹.

• Net Displacement = AB - BC = 300 - 100 = 200 m (from A to C).
Average Velocity = Net Displacement / Total Time = 200 / 210 ≈ 0.95 m s⁻¹ (in direction A to C).

Exercise — Q3
Abdul, while driving to school, computes the average speed for his trip to be 20 km h⁻¹. On his return trip along the same route, there is less traffic and the average speed is 30 km h⁻¹. What is the average speed for Abdul’s trip?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Let the one-way distance to school be d km.
• Time for forward trip (t₁) = d / 20 hours.
• Time for return trip (t₂) = d / 30 hours.
• Total distance = d + d = 2d km.
• Total time (T) = t₁ + t₂ = d/20 + d/30 = (3d + 2d)/60 = 5d/60 = d/12 hours.
Average Speed:
Average Speed = Total Distance / Total Time = (2d) / (d / 12) = 2 × 12 = 24 km h⁻¹.

Final Answer: The average speed for Abdul's entire round trip is 24 km h⁻¹ (Harmonic mean of speeds).

Exercise — Q5
A driver of a car travelling at 52 km h⁻¹ applies the brakes and accelerates uniformly in the opposite direction. The car stops in 5 s. Another driver going at 3 km h⁻¹ in another car applies his brakes slowly and stops in 10 s. On the same graph paper, plot the speed versus time graphs for the two cars. Exercise — Q6
Figure shows the distance-time graph of three objects A, B and C. Study the graph and answer the following questions:
(a) Which of the three is travelling fastest?
(b) Are all three ever at the same point on the road?
(c) How far has C travelled when B passes A?

4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Analysis from Slope & Intersections of Distance-Time Graph:
• (a) Fastest Object: The speed is directly given by the slope of the distance-time graph (Speed = Δs / Δt). Object B has the steepest slope (largest angle with the time axis). Hence, Object B is travelling the fastest.

• (b) Common Point: For all three to be at the same point on the road at the same time, all three lines must intersect at a single common intersection point. Since there is no point where all three lines intersect simultaneously, no, all three are never at the same point on the road.

• (c) Distance C travelled when B passes A: B passes A at the intersection of line B and line A (at approx t = 1.2 h). Projecting this time vertically onto the graph of C gives distance of C from origin as approx 8 km. Since C started at 2 km, distance travelled by C = 8 - 2 = 6 km (approx 6 to 6.5 km).

• (d) Distance B travelled when it passes C: B passes C at the intersection of line B and line C (at approx distance of 5.14 km on the y-axis). Since B started from the origin (0 km), B has travelled approx 5.14 km.

Exercise — Q9
State which of the following situations are possible and give an example for each:
(a) An object with a constant acceleration but with zero velocity.
(b) An object moving with an acceleration but with uniform speed.
(c) An object moving in a certain direction with an acceleration in the perpendicular direction.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) Constant acceleration with zero velocity:
• Possible.
• Example: When a ball is thrown vertically upward, at the highest point of its trajectory, its instantaneous velocity becomes momentarily zero (v = 0), while it is still experiencing constant downward gravitational acceleration (g = 9.8 m s⁻²).
(b) Moving with acceleration but with uniform speed:
• Possible.
• Example: An object moving in a uniform circular motion at a constant speed. Because the direction of motion continuously changes at every instant, velocity is constantly changing, generating a non-zero centripetal acceleration directed towards the centre.
(c) Moving in a certain direction with acceleration in perpendicular direction:
• Possible.
• Example: An aeroplane flying horizontally while gravity pulls it downward, or a body in circular motion where instantaneous velocity is tangential while centripetal acceleration is directed radially inward (perpendicular at 90°).

Final Answer: The satellite's speed is 3.07 km s⁻¹ (or 3072 m s⁻¹).

(b) What is the braking distance after the pedal is pressed?
(c) What is the total stopping distance?
(a) Speed = 72 × (5/18) = 20 m s⁻¹. Reaction distance = Speed × reaction time = 20 × 0.5 = 10 m.
(b) Using v² = u² + 2as: 0 = 20² + 2(-5)s ⟹ 10s = 400 ⟹ Braking distance = 40 m.
(c) Total stopping distance = Reaction distance + Braking distance = 10 + 40 = 50 m.

HOTS Question: Why is uniform circular motion called an accelerated motion even if the speed of the object is strictly constant?
Velocity is a vector quantity having both magnitude (speed) and direction. In circular motion, although speed is constant, the direction of motion is changing continuously at every point along the tangent. Any change in direction produces a change in velocity vector (Δv ≠ 0). Since acceleration is the time rate of change of velocity (a = Δv/Δt), a non-zero centripetal acceleration (a_c = v²/r) acts radially inward, making it an accelerated motion.
