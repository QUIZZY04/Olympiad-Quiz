How do biotic and abiotic factors affect crop production?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Impact of Environmental Stress Factors on Crops:
• 1. Biotic Factors (Living Stresses):
- Infestation by insect pests (chewing roots/leaves, boring into stems/fruits).
- Pathogenic infections by fungi, bacteria, and viruses causing blights, rusts, and wilts.
- Nematodes and rodents destroying standing crops and stored grain.
- Weeds competing aggressively for sunlight, moisture, space, and soil nutrients.

• 2. Abiotic Factors (Non-living Environmental Stresses):
- Drought (water scarcity) and waterlogging / flooding (root suffocation).
- High soil salinity inhibiting osmotic water absorption.
- Extreme temperatures (heat waves, frost, cold injury) causing flower drop, poor pollination, and enzyme denaturation.

Consequences: Loss of vegetative vigour, stunted growth, premature seed drop, discoloration of grain, poor germinability, and severe reduction in total crop yield and market value.

What are macro-nutrients and why are they called macro-nutrients?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Definition & Significance:
Plants require 16 essential mineral nutrients derived from air, water, and soil. Nutrients absorbed from the soil are divided into two categories:

• Macro-nutrients: Essential elements that are required by plants in relatively large quantities (concentrations > 1 mg per gram of dry matter) for structural building and primary metabolic pathways.
• Why they are called Macro-nutrients: Because the plant's physiological demand for these elements is high (e.g. building proteins, nucleic acids, cell membranes, and chlorophyll).

• The Six Soil Macro-nutrients are: Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K), Calcium (Ca), Magnesium (Mg), and Sulphur (S).

How do plants get nutrients?
2 Marks

Sources of Nutrients for Plants (Total 16 Essential Nutrients):
  1. From Air (2 Nutrients): Carbon (C) and Oxygen (O) are absorbed as carbon dioxide (CO₂) through leaf stomata during photosynthesis and respiration.
  2. From Water (1 Nutrient): Hydrogen (H) is absorbed through roots from soil moisture (H₂O).

    • Micro-nutrients (7): Iron, Manganese, Boron, Zinc, Copper, Molybdenum, Chlorine.

Page 209 — Q1
Which of the following conditions will give the most benefits? Why?
(a) Farmers use high quality seeds, do not adopt irrigation or use fertilizers.
(b) Farmers use ordinary seeds, adopt irrigation and use fertilizer.
(c) Farmers use quality seeds, adopt irrigation, use fertilizer and use crop protection measures.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Answer: Condition (c) will give the most benefits.

Detailed Justification:
Agricultural productivity is governed by the synergistic interaction of multiple coordinated management practices:
  1. Quality Seeds (High-Yielding Varieties): Genetically superior seeds possess inherent potential for high yield, disease resistance, and uniform maturity. But this genetic potential can only be expressed if provided with optimal nutrition and water.
  2. Irrigation: Ensures timely, regulated moisture supply during critical phenological growth stages (tillering, flowering, grain-filling), avoiding drought stress.
  3. Fertilizers: Supply required macro-nutrients (N, P, K) in readily absorbable form at peak growth periods.
  4. Crop Protection Measures: Without weed, pest, and disease control, up to 30–50% of the standing crop could be decimated, negating all financial investments made in seeds and fertilizers.
Condition (a) fails due to drought and nutrient starvation. Condition (b) is limited by the poor genetic ceiling of ordinary seeds. Only condition (c) achieves complete, synergistic agricultural optimization.

Page 211 — Q1
Why should preventive measures and biological control methods be preferred for protecting crops?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Advantages of Preventive & Biological Control Methods:
Chemical pesticides (insecticides, fungicides, herbicides) pose severe ecological and human health hazards:
  1. Eco-friendly & Non-toxic: Biological methods (using natural predators, parasitoids, or biopesticides like Bacillus thuringiensis or neem extracts) target only specific pests without harming beneficial pollinating insects (bees), earthworms, or soil microbes.
  2. Prevents Biomagnification: Synthetic chemical pesticides are non-biodegradable and accumulate along trophic food chains (biomagnification), poisoning wildlife and contaminating human food/milk supplies.
  3. Prevents Pest Resistance: Pests quickly evolve biochemical resistance to repeated chemical sprays; biological control maintains a self-sustaining natural ecological predator-prey balance.
  4. Cost-effective & Sustainable: Preventive measures like proper seed bed preparation, timely sowing, crop rotation, and mixed cropping eradicate pests at the source with minimal financial expense.

What factors may be responsible for losses of grains during storage?

Factors Responsible for Post-Harvest Storage Grain Losses:

What management practices are common in dairy and poultry farming?

✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Common Management Practices in Dairy and Poultry Farming:
  1. Shelter & Housing Management: Providing clean, dry, well-ventilated, spacious, and well-lit animal sheds with sloped floors for easy drainage and waste removal, protecting animals from rain, heat, and cold.

  4. Disease Prevention & Vaccination: Timely vaccination schedules against infectious bacterial and viral diseases, alongside strict quarantine protocols for sick animals.

Page 213 — Q7
What are the advantages of composite fish culture?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Composite Fish Culture (Polyculture System):
A combination of five or six compatible fish species having different feeding habits is cultured simultaneously in a single fish pond. Typically:
• Catla: Surface feeder (zooplankton).
• Rohu: Column feeder (aquatic microplants/algae in middle depth).
• Mrigal & Common Carp: Bottom feeders (decaying organic matter and bottom detritus).
• Grass Carp: Feeds on submersed aquatic weeds.

Major Advantages:
  1. Zero Food Competition: Because each species occupies a different ecological niche and feeds at a different depth, they do not compete with each other for food.
  2. Complete Food Utilization: Food available in all vertical zones (surface, middle, bottom) and aquatic weeds are fully consumed without waste.
  3. Tremendously High Yield: Fish survival rate is maximized, yielding exceptionally high fish harvest per hectare of water area.

Exercise — Q1
Explain any one method of crop production which ensures high yield.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Method: Inter-Cropping System:
• Description: Inter-cropping is the practice of growing two or more crops simultaneously on the same piece of agricultural land in a definite row pattern (e.g. 1:1, 1:2, or 2:2 row proportions).
• Classic Examples: Soyabean + Maize; Finger millet (Bajra) + Cowpea (Lobia).

How It Ensures High Yield:
  1. Complementary Nutrient Uptake: The crops are selected such that their nutrient requirements, root depths, and growth periods differ. This prevents mutual competition and maximizes utilization of soil nutrients and sunlight.
  2. Insurance Against Total Crop Failure: If one crop is damaged by drought or a specific pest, the companion crop survives, ensuring guaranteed financial return to the farmer.
  3. Pest Containment: Prevents rapid spread of host-specific pests and plant diseases across the field.

Exercise — Q2
Why are manure and fertilizers used in fields?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Reasons for Applying Manure and Fertilizers:
With continuous intensive crop cultivation, soils become progressively depleted of essential mineral nutrients.
  1. Replenishing Essential Mineral Nutrients: Both manure and fertilizers replenish depleted nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and micronutrients necessary for vigorous vegetative growth (leaves, stems), timely flowering, and robust grain formation.
  2. Improving Soil Physical Structure (Role of Manure): Manure enriches soil with organic matter (humus), increasing aeration and water retention in sandy soils and preventing waterlogging in clayey soils.
  3. Ensuring Rapid High Yields (Role of Fertilizers): Chemical fertilizers deliver concentrated, highly soluble N-P-K nutrients immediately to root zones, maximizing short-term commercial crop yields.

Exercise — Q3
What are the advantages of inter-cropping and crop rotation?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Advantages of Inter-Cropping:
• Cultivating complementary crops in alternate rows maximizes spatial utilization of sunlight, moisture, and soil layers.
• Inhibits the geometric proliferation of host-specific pests and soil-borne pathogens.
• Provides agricultural risk insurance against adverse climatic events.

2. Advantages of Crop Rotation:
• Replenishes Soil Fertility Naturally: Alternating a cereal crop (e.g. wheat) with a leguminous pulse crop (e.g. gram/pea) enriches the soil with fixed nitrogen via symbiotic Rhizobium root nodules without chemical fertilizers.
• Breaks Pest & Weed Life Cycles: Changing the host crop species disrupts the continuous breeding cycles of insect pests, pathogens, and weed seeds.
• Allows two or three successful harvests per year with minimal soil exhaustion.

Exercise — Q5
How do storage grain losses occur?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Mechanisms of Storage Grain Losses:
Post-harvest storage losses are caused by a combination of biotic and abiotic factors:
  1. Biotic Factors: Insects (weevils, beetles), rodents (rats, mice), fungi (moulds), mites, and bacteria consume the germ and endosperm of grains, contaminate food stocks with hair and droppings, and secrete dangerous mycotoxins (e.g. aflatoxin).
  2. Abiotic Factors: High moisture content (> 12%) in freshly harvested grains coupled with warm warehouse temperatures stimulates cellular respiration of grains, generating moisture and heat.
Cumulative Damage: Leads to loss of total grain weight, severe discoloration, rancid odour, poor seed viability/germinability, and loss of commercial grade, rendering grain unfit for human consumption.

HOTS Question: Why is Italian bee (Apis mellifera) extensively preferred over our indigenous Indian bee (Apis cerana indica) in commercial apiculture?
Apis mellifera is commercially preferred because: (1) It has a substantially higher honey collection and storage capacity per colony, (2) It exhibits a gentle temperament with fewer stings, facilitating safer extraction, (3) It stays inside artificial wooden Langstroth hives for prolonged periods with minimal swarming or absconding, and (4) The queen bee has a much higher egg-laying prolificacy.
