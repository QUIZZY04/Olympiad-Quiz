What are canal rays?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Scientific Definition & Discovery:
• Canal rays (also known as anode rays) are streams of positively charged subatomic radiations discovered by German physicist E. Goldstein in 1886 in a modified gas discharge tube using a perforated cathode.
• These rays travel in a direction opposite to cathode rays (from anode toward the cathode) and pass through the perforations (canals) of the cathode.
• The investigation of canal rays led to the discovery of another subatomic particle carrying a positive charge equal in magnitude to the electron, which was later named the proton by Ernest Rutherford.

Page 47 — Q2
If an atom contains one electron and one proton, will it carry any charge or not?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Analysis:
• An electron carries a unit negative electrical charge (-1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C or -1).
• A proton carries an exactly equal magnitude of positive electrical charge (+1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C or +1).
• When an atom contains 1 proton and 1 electron, the total net charge is:
Net Charge = (+1) + (-1) = 0.

Conclusion: The atom will be electrically neutral and carry no net charge (this represents a normal protium hydrogen atom, ¹₁H).

Page 49 — Q1
On the basis of Thomson's model of an atom, explain how the atom is neutral as a whole.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Thomson's Plum Pudding / Watermelon Model:
1. J.J. Thomson proposed that an atom consists of a uniform sphere of positive charge in which negatively charged electrons are embedded like seeds in a watermelon or raisins in a pudding.
2. The negative and positive charges are equal in magnitude.
3. Because the total positive charge of the sphere exactly equals the sum of the negative charges of all embedded electrons, they cancel each other out completely, making the atom electrically neutral as a whole.

Page 49 — Q4
What do you think would be the observation if the α-particle scattering experiment is carried out using a foil of a metal other than gold?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Scientific Analysis:
1. If another heavy metal foil (like Platinum) is used: Observations would remain essentially identical, because platinum atoms also have a massive, highly positively charged nucleus that can deflect heavy α-particles (⁴₂He²⁺).
2. If a very light metal foil (like Aluminium or Lithium) is used:
• The nucleus of a light atom is much less massive and carries a much smaller positive nuclear charge.
• The electrostatic repulsion would be too weak to cause large-angle deflections or rebound fast-moving α-particles.
• Most α-particles would pass straight through with virtually no large deflections or back-scattering. Gold was uniquely chosen because it is the most malleable metal, capable of being hammered into an extremely thin sheet just ~1000 atoms thick.

If K and L shells of an atom are full, then what would be the total number of electrons in the atom?
2 Marks

• For K shell (n = 1): Maximum electrons = 2(1)² = 2.
• For L shell (n = 2): Maximum electrons = 2(2)² = 8.
Calculation:
Total number of electrons = Electrons in K shell + Electrons in L shell
Total electrons = 2 + 8 = 10 electrons.

Note: An atom with 10 electrons is the noble gas Neon (Ne).

Page 53 — Q3
How will you find the valency of chlorine, sulphur and magnesium?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Definition of Valency: Valency is the combining capacity of an atom to achieve a stable octet (8 valence electrons).
If valence electrons (v) ≤ 4, Valency = v.
If valence electrons (v) > 4, Valency = 8 - v.

• Chlorine (Cl, Z = 17):
Configuration = 2, 8, 7. Valence electrons = 7.
Valency = 8 - 7 = 1 (Gains 1 electron to complete octet).

• Sulphur (S, Z = 16):
Configuration = 2, 8, 6. Valence electrons = 6.
Valency = 8 - 6 = 2 (Gains 2 electrons to complete octet).

• Magnesium (Mg, Z = 12):
Configuration = 2, 8, 2. Valence electrons = 2.
Valency = 2 (Loses 2 valence electrons to achieve stable octet).

Exercise — Q3
What are the limitations of Rutherford’s model of the atom?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Major Limitation — Instability of the Atom:
1. According to classical electromagnetic theory (Maxwell's electrodynamics), any charged particle revolving in a circular orbit undergoes continuous centripetal acceleration.
2. An accelerating charged particle must continuously radiate energy in the form of electromagnetic waves.
3. Consequently, the revolving electron should continuously lose kinetic energy, its orbital radius would steadily shrink, and it should spiral inward into the nucleus in less than 10⁻⁸ seconds.
4. If this were true, all atoms would collapse, and matter would not exist in a stable form. But atoms are known to be completely stable. Rutherford's model could not explain this stability.

Exercise — Q4
Describe Bohr’s model of the atom.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Postulates of Neils Bohr's Atomic Model (1913):
  1. Discrete Orbits: Electrons revolve around the nucleus only in certain specific, non-radiating circular orbits known as discrete orbits or stationary energy states.
  2. Non-radiating Energy: While revolving in these discrete orbits, electrons do not radiate energy.
  3. Energy Shells: These orbits or shells are represented by letters K, L, M, N... or principal quantum numbers n = 1, 2, 3, 4...
  4. Energy Transitions: An electron absorbs energy when it jumps from a lower orbit to a higher orbit, and radiates energy when it drops from a higher orbit to a lower orbit (ΔE = E₂ - E₁ = hν).

Exercise — Q6
Summarize the rules for writing of distribution of electrons in various shells for the first eighteen elements.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Bohr-Bury Scheme Rules:
  1. Maximum Capacity Formula (2n²): The maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in any shell is given by 2n² (where n is the shell number):
    • K-shell (n = 1): 2(1)² = 2 electrons
    • L-shell (n = 2): 2(2)² = 8 electrons
    • M-shell (n = 3): 2(3)² = 18 electrons
  2. Octet Rule for Outermost Shell: The maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in the outermost shell is strictly 8, even if the shell has capacity for more.
  3. Stepwise Shell Filling: Electrons are not accommodated in a given shell unless the inner shells are completely filled (shells are filled in a step-wise, progressive manner).

Exercise — Q7
Define valency by taking examples of silicon and oxygen.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Definition of Valency:
Valency is the combining capacity of an atom of an element to achieve a completely filled, stable outermost octet (or duplet) electron shell.

• Example 1: Silicon (Si, Z = 14):
Electronic configuration = 2, 8, 4. It has 4 valence electrons in its outermost M-shell.
To attain a stable octet, it shares 4 electrons with other atoms.
Therefore, the valency of silicon is 4.

• Example 2: Oxygen (O, Z = 8):
Electronic configuration = 2, 6. It has 6 valence electrons in its outermost L-shell.
Instead of losing 6 electrons, it easily gains or shares 2 electrons to complete its octet (8 - 6 = 2).
Therefore, the valency of oxygen is 2.

Exercise — Q8
Explain with examples (i) Atomic number, (ii) Mass number, (iii) Isotopes and (iv) Isobars. Give any two uses of isotopes.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) Atomic Number (Z): The total number of protons present in the nucleus of an atom. Example: Carbon has 6 protons, so Z = 6.

(ii) Mass Number (A): The total sum of protons and neutrons (nucleons) present in the nucleus of an atom (A = p + n). Example: Aluminium has 13 protons and 14 neutrons, so A = 13 + 14 = 27.

(iii) Isotopes: Atoms of the same element having the same atomic number (Z) but different mass numbers (A) due to differing numbers of neutrons. Example: Hydrogen isotopes: Protium (¹₁H), Deuterium (²₁H), and Tritium (³₁H); Carbon isotopes: ¹²₆C and ¹⁴₆C.

(iv) Isobars: Atoms of different chemical elements having different atomic numbers (Z) but the same mass number (A). Example: Argon (⁴⁰₁₈Ar) and Calcium (⁴⁰₂₀Ca).

Two Important Uses of Isotopes:
  1. An isotope of Uranium (U-235) is used as fuel in nuclear reactors to generate electricity.
  2. An isotope of Cobalt (Co-60) is used in radiation therapy for the treatment of cancer. (Also, Iodine-131 is used in treating goitre).

Exercise — Q9
Na⁺ has completely filled K and L shells. Explain.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Step-by-Step Electronic Analysis:
1. A neutral Sodium atom (Na) has atomic number Z = 11, meaning it possesses 11 protons and 11 electrons.
2. Its electronic configuration is: K = 2, L = 8, M = 1.
3. When sodium forms a cation (Na⁺), it loses its single outermost valence electron from the M shell:
Na (2, 8, 1) ⟶ Na⁺ (2, 8) + e⁻.
4. Now, the Na⁺ ion possesses 10 electrons: • K shell has 2 electrons (maximum capacity = 2).
• L shell has 8 electrons (maximum capacity = 8).
Both K and L shells are completely filled to their maximum capacities, giving Na⁺ a highly stable noble gas configuration (isoelectronic with Neon).

📝 Case Study Question
Rutherford's Gold Foil Experiment: Rutherford bombarded a 100 nm thin gold sheet with fast-moving alpha particles (⁴₂He²⁺).
(a) What unexpected result led to the rejection of Thomson's plum pudding model?
(b) What conclusion was drawn from the observation that 1 in 20,000 particles rebounded backwards?
(c) Why did Rutherford select gold specifically?
(a) Thomson's model predicted slight deflections across the uniform sphere; however, 1 in 20,000 α-particles were deflected by more than 90° or rebounded back at 180°, which was impossible in a diffuse positive cloud.
(b) The entire positive charge and almost all the mass of the atom is concentrated in an unimaginably tiny, dense central volume called the nucleus.
(c) Gold is the most malleable metal known, allowing a sheet just ~1000 atoms thick to be produced.
🧠 HOTS
HOTS Question: Why do isotopes of an element show identical chemical properties but different physical properties?
Chemical properties are governed solely by the electronic configuration (specifically the number of valence electrons), which is identical in all isotopes of an element since their atomic number (Z) is the same. Physical properties (such as density, boiling point, and diffusion rate) depend directly on atomic mass, which differs among isotopes due to different numbers of neutrons.
