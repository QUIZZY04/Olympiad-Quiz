Page 32 — Q1
In a reaction, 5.3 g of sodium carbonate reacted with 6 g of ethanoic acid. The products were 2.2 g of carbon dioxide, 0.9 g water and 8.2 g of sodium ethanoate. Show that these observations are in agreement with the law of conservation of mass.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Chemical Reaction:
Sodium carbonate + Ethanoic acid ⟶ Sodium ethanoate + Carbon dioxide + Water
Step 1: Total Mass of Reactants:
• Mass of sodium carbonate = 5.3 g
• Mass of ethanoic acid = 6.0 g
Total mass of reactants = 5.3 g + 6.0 g = 11.3 g
Step 2: Total Mass of Products:
• Mass of sodium ethanoate = 8.2 g
• Mass of carbon dioxide = 2.2 g
• Mass of water = 0.9 g
Total mass of products = 8.2 g + 2.2 g + 0.9 g = 11.3 g
Conclusion:
Since Total mass of reactants (11.3 g) = Total mass of products (11.3 g), mass is neither created nor destroyed during the chemical change. Hence, these experimental observations completely agree with the Law of Conservation of Mass.

Hydrogen and oxygen combine in the ratio of 1:8 by mass to form water. What mass of oxygen gas would be required to react completely with 3 g of hydrogen gas?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given:
Mass ratio of Hydrogen to Oxygen in water (H₂O) = 1 : 8.
This means 1 g of hydrogen reacts completely with 8 g of oxygen.
Calculation:
Mass of oxygen required for 3 g of hydrogen:
Mass of Oxygen = 3 × 8 = 24 g.

Final Answer: Exactly 24 grams of oxygen gas is required to react completely with 3 g of hydrogen gas, in accordance with the Law of Definite Proportions.

Page 35 — Q1
Define the atomic mass unit.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Definition of Unified Atomic Mass Unit (u):
One atomic mass unit is defined as a mass exactly equal to one-twelfth (1/12th) of the mass of one atom of carbon-12 (¹²C) isotope at rest.
1 u = (1/12) × Mass of one ¹²C atom ≈ 1.6605 × 10⁻²⁴ g = 1.6605 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.

Page 35 — Q2
Why is it not possible to see an atom with naked eyes?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Scientific Reasons:
1. Extremely Submicroscopic Dimensions: Atoms are unimaginably tiny. The atomic radius of a typical atom (like hydrogen) is about 10⁻¹⁰ m (0.1 nanometre).
2. Limit of Resolution of Human Eye: The human eye cannot resolve any object smaller than approximately 0.1 mm (10⁻⁴ m).
3. Since the size of an atom is about a million times smaller than the resolving limit of the human retina, an individual atom cannot reflect visible light to be seen with naked eyes.

Page 39 — Q3
What is meant by the term chemical formula?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Definition:
A chemical formula is a symbolic representation of the composition of a compound.
It indicates:
  1. The types of elements present in the compound (represented by chemical symbols).
  2. The exact number of atoms of each element present in one molecule or formula unit of that compound (represented by numerical subscripts).
Example: H₂SO₄ represents one molecule of sulphuric acid consisting of 2 atoms of Hydrogen, 1 atom of Sulphur, and 4 atoms of Oxygen.

NCERT Intext Questions (Page 42)
Page 42 — Q1
If one mole of carbon atoms weighs 12 grams, what is the mass (in grams) of 1 atom of carbon?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given:
• Mass of 1 mole of carbon atoms = 12 g
• 1 mole contains Avogadro's number (N_A) of atoms = 6.022 × 10²³ atoms.
Calculation:
Mass of 1 atom = (Mass of 1 mole) / N_A
Mass of 1 atom = 12 / (6.022 × 10²³) ≈ 1.993 × 10⁻²³ g.

Final Answer: The mass of one atom of carbon is 1.993 × 10⁻²³ g.

CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Formula: Mass of 1 atom = Molar mass / N_A1 Mark
Substitution and calculation: 1.993 × 10⁻²³ g1 Mark
Page 42 — Q2
Which has more number of atoms, 100 grams of sodium or 100 grams of iron? (given, atomic mass of Na = 23 u, Fe = 56 u)
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Formula: Number of atoms = (Given Mass / Molar Mass) × N_A
1. For 100 g Sodium (Na):
Moles of Na = 100 / 23 ≈ 4.348 moles
Atoms of Na = 4.348 × N_A = 4.348 × 6.022 × 10²³ ≈ 2.618 × 10²⁴ atoms.
2. For 100 g Iron (Fe):
Moles of Fe = 100 / 56 ≈ 1.786 moles
Atoms of Fe = 1.786 × N_A = 1.786 × 6.022 × 10²³ ≈ 1.075 × 10²⁴ atoms.
Conclusion:
Since 4.348 moles > 1.786 moles, 100 grams of sodium has significantly more atoms than 100 grams of iron (approximately 2.44 times more).

Calculation of moles/atoms of Na = 4.348 N_A ≈ 2.618 × 10²⁴1 Mark
Calculation of moles/atoms of Fe = 1.786 N_A ≈ 1.075 × 10²⁴1 Mark

A 0.24 g sample of compound of oxygen and boron was found by analysis to contain 0.096 g of boron and 0.144 g of oxygen. Calculate the percentage composition of the compound by weight.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given:
• Mass of compound = 0.24 g
• Mass of boron = 0.096 g
• Mass of oxygen = 0.144 g
Percentage of Boron:
% Boron = (Mass of Boron / Mass of Compound) × 100
% Boron = (0.096 / 0.24) × 100 = 40%.
Percentage of Oxygen:
% Oxygen = (Mass of Oxygen / Mass of Compound) × 100
% Oxygen = (0.144 / 0.24) × 100 = 60%.

Final Answer: Boron = 40%, Oxygen = 60%.

Exercise — Q2
When 3.0 g of carbon is burnt in 8.00 g oxygen, 11.00 g of carbon dioxide is produced. What mass of carbon dioxide will be formed when 3.00 g of carbon is burnt in 50.00 g of oxygen? Which law of chemical combination will govern your answer?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Step 1: Determine Combining Ratio:
C + O₂ ⟶ CO₂
3 g C + 8 g O₂ ⟶ 11 g CO₂
The mass ratio of Carbon to Oxygen in CO₂ is strictly 3 : 8.
Step 2: Burning 3.00 g Carbon in 50.00 g Oxygen:
• 3.00 g of carbon can react with only 8.00 g of oxygen.
• Carbon is the limiting reactant.
• The remaining oxygen (50.00 g - 8.00 g = 42.00 g) will remain unreacted as excess.
• Therefore, the mass of carbon dioxide produced will still be: 3.00 g + 8.00 g = 11.00 g.
Governing Law:
This is governed by the Law of Definite Proportions (or Law of Constant Composition), which states that in a chemical substance, the elements are always present in definite proportions by mass.
CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Identifying mass of CO₂ formed = 11.00 g with 42 g excess O₂1½ Marks
Stating Law of Definite Proportions and its definition1½ Marks
Exercise — Q3
What are polyatomic ions? Give examples.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Definition:
A polyatomic ion is a charged group of covalently bonded atoms that behaves as a single unit with a net positive or negative electrical charge.

Examples:
  • Ammonium ion: NH₄⁺ (Valency = +1)
  • Hydroxide ion: OH⁻ (Valency = -1)
  • Nitrate ion: NO₃⁻ (Valency = -1)
  • Sulphate ion: SO₄²⁻ (Valency = -2)
  • Carbonate ion: CO₃²⁻ (Valency = -2)
  • Phosphate ion: PO₄³⁻ (Valency = -3)

Exercise — Q11
Calculate the number of aluminium ions present in 0.051 g of aluminium oxide. (Hint: The mass of an ion is the same as that of an atom of the same element. Atomic mass of Al = 27 u).
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Step 1: Molar Mass of Aluminium Oxide (Al₂O₃):
M = 2 × 27 + 3 × 16 = 54 + 48 = 102 g/mol.
Step 2: Number of Moles and Formula Units of Al₂O₃:
Moles = 0.051 / 102 = 0.0005 mole = 5 × 10⁻⁴ mole.
Number of Al₂O₃ formula units = 5 × 10⁻⁴ × 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.011 × 10²⁰ units.
Step 3: Number of Aluminium Ions (Al³⁺):
Each formula unit of Al₂O₃ contains 2 aluminium ions (2 Al³⁺).
Number of Al³⁺ ions = 2 × (3.011 × 10²⁰) = 6.022 × 10²⁰ ions.

Final Answer: There are 6.022 × 10²⁰ aluminium ions in 0.051 g of aluminium oxide.

📝 Case Study Question
Avogadro's Hypothesis and Stoichiometry: A sealed container holds 44 g of CO₂ gas, while another identical container holds 32 g of O₂ gas at identical temperature and pressure.
(a) Calculate the number of moles in each container.
(b) Compare the number of molecules present in both containers.
(c) Compare the total number of atoms in both containers.
(a) Moles of CO₂ = 44/44 = 1.0 mole; Moles of O₂ = 32/32 = 1.0 mole.
(b) Since both contain 1.0 mole, both containers have an identical number of molecules (6.022 × 10²³ molecules).
(c) Each CO₂ molecule has 3 atoms (1C + 2O), so total atoms in CO₂ = 3 × N_A = 1.8066 × 10²⁴. Each O₂ molecule has 2 atoms, so total atoms in O₂ = 2 × N_A = 1.2044 × 10²⁴. Therefore, the CO₂ container has 1.5 times more atoms.
🧠 HOTS
HOTS Question: Why is carbon-12 chosen as the international standard reference for atomic mass rather than hydrogen-1 or oxygen-16?
Carbon-12 was chosen by IUPAC in 1961 because: (1) It is a stable, non-radioactive solid at room temperature and very abundant, (2) It yields near whole-number atomic masses for almost all other chemical elements, and (3) Oxygen-16 was rejected because natural oxygen contains multiple isotopes (¹⁶O, ¹⁷O, ¹⁸O) leading to discrepancies between physical and chemical mass scales.
