When do we say that work is done?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Two Essential Conditions for Scientific Work:
In science, work is said to be done when and only when the following two simultaneous conditions are met:
  1. An external force must act on the object (F ≠ 0).
  2. The object must be displaced from its initial position along a direction having a non-zero component along the line of action of the force (s ≠ 0 and force is not perpendicular to displacement, θ ≠ 90°).

The kinetic energy of an object of mass, m moving with a velocity of 5 m s⁻¹ is 25 J. What will be its kinetic energy when its velocity is doubled? What will be its kinetic energy when its velocity is increased three times?

Exercise — Q6
The potential energy of a freely falling object decreases progressively. Does this violate the law of conservation of energy? Why?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Answer: No, it does NOT violate the law of conservation of energy.

Explanation:
• As an object falls freely from height h, its height decreases, causing its gravitational potential energy (E_p = mgh) to decrease progressively.
• However, as it accelerates downward under gravity, its velocity increases uniformly, causing a corresponding equal increase in its kinetic energy (E_k = ½ mv²).
• The loss in potential energy at any point along the descent is exactly equal to the gain in kinetic energy (ΔE_p = ΔE_k).
• The total mechanical energy (E = E_p + E_k = constant) remains strictly conserved throughout the motion.

Sequence of Energy Transformations:
1. Muscular (Chemical) Energy: The rider's muscles metabolize food glucose, releasing chemical energy that produces muscular force on the pedals.
2. Kinetic (Mechanical) Energy of Mechanism: Muscular energy is converted into kinetic energy of the rotating pedals, gears, and chain drive, propelling the bicycle wheels forward.

Can there be displacement of an object in the absence of any force acting on it? Think. Discuss this question with your friends and teacher.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Answer: Yes, an object can undergo displacement even in the total absence of any net external force.

Reasoning based on Newton's First Law:
• According to Newton's First Law of Motion, if an object is already moving with a constant velocity v and the net external force is zero (F_{net} = 0), its acceleration is zero (a = 0).
• The object will continue to move uniformly in a straight line forever, continuously covering non-zero displacement (s = v imes t ≠ 0).
• A force is required only to initiate motion or change velocity, not to sustain existing uniform motion (e.g. a space probe drifting through deep interstellar space).

Exercise — Q15
Illustrate the law of conservation of energy by discussing the energy changes which occur when we draw a pendulum bob to one side and allow it to oscillate. Why does the bob eventually come to rest? What happens to its energy eventually? Is it a violation of the law of conservation of energy?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Oscillations of Simple Pendulum:
  1. At Extreme Positions (A and B): When displaced to one side, the bob is raised through a vertical height h. Its velocity is zero (v = 0). The energy is 100% Potential Energy (mgh).
  2. At Mean Position (O): As it swings down, PE converts into Kinetic Energy. At lowest point O, h = 0, speed is maximum, and energy is 100% Kinetic Energy (½ mv²).
  3. Continuous Exchange: PE and KE continuously interchange, keeping total mechanical energy constant (PE + KE = constant).
Why the Bob Eventually Stops & Conservation:
• The bob encounters continuous air resistance and friction at the suspension pivot.
• Mechanical energy is gradually transformed into heat energy of air and suspension string, and sound energy.
• Once all mechanical energy is dissipated into the environment as heat and sound, the bob stops.
• No, this is NOT a violation of the law of conservation of energy; total energy of the universe (mechanical + heat + sound) remains strictly conserved.

Exercise — Q19
Soni says that the acceleration in an object could be zero even when several forces are acting on it. Do you agree with her? Why?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Answer: Yes, we completely agree with Soni.

Explanation:
• By Newton's Second Law, acceleration is proportional to the net resultant unbalanced force: a = F_{net} / m.
• If several individual forces act simultaneously on an object such that their vector sum is zero (F_{net} = \sum ec{F} = 0), the forces are called balanced forces.
• Under balanced forces, F_{net} = 0 ⟹ a = 0. The object will experience zero acceleration.
Example: A book resting on a table experiences downward gravitational force and upward normal contact reaction; the two forces cancel out, and acceleration is zero.

Exercise — Q21
A freely falling object eventually stops on reaching the ground. What happens to its kinetic energy?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Dissipation of Kinetic Energy:
When a freely falling object hits the ground and comes to an abrupt halt, its kinetic energy does not vanish; it is transformed into other non-mechanical forms of energy in accordance with the Law of Conservation of Energy:
  1. Heat Energy: The kinetic energy is converted into thermal energy, raising the temperature of both the object and the ground at the impact zone.
  2. Sound Energy: A fraction is converted into acoustic waves (the thud/crash sound heard upon impact).
  3. Mechanical Strain / Deformation: A portion goes into doing work to deform, crack, or fracture the object or create an indentation/crater in the ground.

🧠 HOTS
HOTS Question: A light body A and a heavy body B have the exact same kinetic energy. Which one has greater linear momentum?
Linear momentum is related to kinetic energy by p = √(2m E_k). Since kinetic energy E_k is the same for both bodies, momentum is directly proportional to the square root of mass: p ∝ √m. Because body B has a heavier mass (m_B > m_A), the heavy body B has a greater momentum.
