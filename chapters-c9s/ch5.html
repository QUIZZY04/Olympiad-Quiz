Page 59 — Q1
Who discovered cells, and how?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Discovery of Cells:
• Scientist: Cells were first discovered by the English scientist Robert Hooke in 1665.
• How: Hooke examined a very thin slice of bottle cork (a substance that comes from the bark of a tree) under a self-designed primitive compound microscope.
• Observation: He observed that the cork slice resembled the structure of a honeycomb consisting of numerous tiny compartments or walled boxes.
• Hooke coined the term 'cell' (derived from the Latin word cellula meaning 'a little room') to describe these individual box-like compartments. (Note: These were dead cell walls of plant tissue; Antonie van Leeuwenhoek in 1674 first observed free living cells).

Page 59 — Q2
Why is the cell called the structural and functional unit of life?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Structural Unit:
• All living organisms (from microscopic bacteria to gigantic blue whales and redwood trees) are composed of one or more cells.
• Cells assemble together to form tissues, tissues form organs, organs form organ systems, which ultimately build the entire physical anatomical architecture of an organism. Without cells, biological structure cannot exist.
2. Functional Unit:
• A single independent cell possesses all biochemical machinery to carry out all vital metabolic life processes independently (e.g. cellular respiration, nutrient intake, protein synthesis, excretion of waste, and reproduction).
• All functions of an organ or organism are the cumulative aggregate of the functions performed by its individual constituent cells.

NCERT Intext Questions (Page 61)
Page 61 — Q1
How do substances like CO₂ and water move in and out of the cell? Discuss.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Movement of Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) by Diffusion:
• As cellular respiration occurs inside the cell, CO₂ (a metabolic waste product) accumulates, creating a high concentration of CO₂ inside the cytoplasm.
• In the external environment, the concentration of CO₂ is comparatively low.
• Due to this concentration difference, CO₂ moves spontaneously across the cell membrane from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration by simple diffusion.
2. Movement of Water (H₂O) by Osmosis:
• Water moves across the selectively permeable plasma membrane following the law of diffusion, specifically termed osmosis.
• The net direction and rate of water movement depend on the water potential (solute concentration) difference between the inside and outside of the cell:
- In a hypotonic solution, water enters the cell (endosmosis), causing it to swell.
- In a hypertonic solution, water leaves the cell (exosmosis), causing it to shrink.
- In an isotonic solution, there is no net movement of water.

Mechanism of CO₂ transport via simple diffusion down concentration gradient1½ Marks

Page 61 — Q2
Why is the plasma membrane called a selectively permeable membrane?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Scientific Explanation:
The plasma membrane is termed selectively permeable because:
  1. It permits the selective passage and entry/exit of only specific substances (such as respiratory gases O₂ and CO₂, water, glucose, and essential ions) into and out of the cell.
  2. It simultaneously acts as an impenetrable barrier that prevents the movement of harmful foreign substances, large unwanted macromolecules, or the leakage of vital cellular cytoplasmic components out of the cell.

Semi-Autonomous Organelles:
The two cytoplasmic organelles that possess their own genetic material are:

Page 65 — Q2
If the organisation of a cell is destroyed due to some physical or chemical influence, what will happen?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Consequences of Structural Destruction:
1. The cell's compartmentalization, selective membrane permeability, and organelle coordination will break down completely.
2. The cell will be unable to carry out basic life-sustaining metabolic functions such as respiration, protein synthesis, active transport, and waste clearance.
3. The damaged membrane will cause lysosomes to rupture, releasing powerful hydrolytic digestive enzymes that will digest and self-destruct the dead cell components.
4. Ultimately, the cell will cease to live and die.
CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Loss of metabolic functions (respiration, protein synthesis)1 Mark

Page 65 — Q3
Why are lysosomes known as suicide bags?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Hydrolytic Action of Lysosomes:
1. Lysosomes are membrane-bound vesicular organelles containing powerful hydrolytic digestive enzymes (synthesized by the Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum) capable of breaking down all organic polymers.
2. When a cell gets infected, damaged, aged, or when its cellular metabolism is irreversibly disturbed, the lysosomal membrane ruptures.
3. The released active digestive enzymes pour into the cytoplasm and digest their own cell from within (autolysis).
4. Hence, lysosomes are famously termed the 'suicide bags' of a cell.

Contain powerful hydrolytic/digestive enzymes synthesized by RER1 Mark

Page 65 — Q4
Where are proteins synthesised inside the cell?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Site of Protein Synthesis:
• Proteins are synthesized on sub-microscopic non-membrane-bound granular organelles called Ribosomes.
• Ribosomes are found either attached to the outer surface of the Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER) (which gives it a rough, studded appearance under an electron microscope) or floating freely within the cytosol.
• The synthesized proteins are then transported by the ER to the Golgi apparatus for modification, packaging, and dispatch.

Exercise — Q3
What would happen if the plasma membrane ruptures or breaks down?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Consequences of Plasma Membrane Rupture:
1. The plasma membrane provides structural containment and maintains the homeostatic balance of the internal cytoplasmic environment.
2. If it ruptures or breaks down, the selective permeability barrier is permanently destroyed.
3. Cytoplasmic contents, organelles, and enzymes will spill out into the extracellular environment, while uncontrolled substances and water rush in.
4. Cellular metabolism immediately collapses, and the cell dies.

Exercise — Q4
What would happen to the life of a cell if there was no Golgi apparatus?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Crucial Roles of Golgi Apparatus:
The Golgi apparatus (discovered by Camillo Golgi) acts as the central shipping, packaging, and dispatch department of the cell. If it were absent:
  1. Packaging & Modification Failure: Proteins and lipids synthesized by the endoplasmic reticulum could not be modified (e.g. glycosylation into glycoproteins/glycolipids), concentrated, or packaged into secretory vesicles.
  2. Intracellular Transport Breakdown: Materials could not be dispatched to intracellular targets or secreted outside the cell membrane.
  3. No Lysosome Formation: Lysosomes are formed through the budding of Golgi cisternae. In its absence, no lysosomes would be produced, preventing cellular waste digestion and autophagy, poisoning the cell with accumulated debris.

Inability to modify, package, and dispatch proteins/lipids from ER1 Mark
Disruption of secretor transport to plasma membrane1 Mark
Failure to produce lysosomes leading to cell degeneration1 Mark
Exercise — Q5
Which organelle is known as the powerhouse of the cell? Why?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
The Powerhouse of the Cell: Mitochondria.

Reasons:
  1. Mitochondria are the primary sites of cellular aerobic respiration. They oxidize carbohydrate and lipid metabolic products (pyruvate, fatty acids) in the presence of oxygen.
  2. During this oxidation, energy is released and captured in the high-energy phosphoanhydride bonds of Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) molecules.
  3. ATP is universally recognized as the energy currency of the cell. The cell utilizes the energy stored in ATP to perform mechanical work (muscle contraction, locomotion) and synthesize chemical compounds (proteins, nucleic acids).
  4. Mitochondria feature a deeply folded inner membrane (forming cristae) that provides a massive surface area for ATP-generating electron transport enzyme complexes.

Exercise — Q7
How does an Amoeba obtain its food?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Endocytosis / Phagocytosis in Amoeba:
1. Amoeba is a unicellular, eukaryotic freshwater protozoan with a flexible, fluid plasma membrane.
2. When an Amoeba senses a microscopic food particle nearby, it extends finger-like temporary projections of its cytoplasm called pseudopodia (false feet) around the food particle.
3. The pseudopodia engulf and fuse around the food particle, forming a membrane-bound vesicle called a food vacuole. This process of engulfing extracellular food via membrane flexibility is called endocytosis (or phagocytosis).
4. Lysosomal digestive enzymes enter the food vacuole to hydrolyze complex nutrients into soluble forms, which diffuse into the cytoplasm.

Exercise — Q8
What is osmosis?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Scientific Definition:
Osmosis is the spontaneous net movement of solvent (water) molecules from a region of higher water concentration (dilute solution with lower solute concentration) to a region of lower water concentration (concentrated solution with higher solute concentration) across a selectively permeable membrane until dynamic equilibrium is established.

Exercise — Q9
Carry out the following potato osmoscope experiment:
Take four peeled potato halves and scoos each one out to make potato cups. One of these potato cups should be made from a boiled potato. Put each potato cup in a trough containing water. Now:
(a) Keep cup A empty
(b) Put one teaspoon sugar in cup B
(c) Put one teaspoon salt in cup C
(d) Put one teaspoon sugar in the boiled potato cup D.
Keep these for two hours. Then observe the four potato cups and answer the following:
(i) Explain why water gathers in the hollowed portion of B and C.
(ii) Why is potato A necessary for this experiment?
(iii) Explain why water does not gather in the hollowed out portions of A and D.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) Why water gathers in cups B and C:
In cups B (sugar) and C (salt), the high concentration of solute inside the cavity creates a hypertonic environment (low water concentration). The water in the trough represents a pure hypotonic medium (high water concentration). Water moves across the living, selectively permeable membranes of the potato tuber cells into the cavity by endosmosis, accumulating liquid inside.
(ii) Role of Potato A:
Potato cup A is empty; it acts as a control experiment. It proves that the accumulation of water in cups B and C is solely due to the presence of solute creating an osmotic gradient, and not simply due to physical suction or natural seeping through potato tissue.
(iii) Why water does not gather in A and D:
• In Cup A: There is no solute inside the cavity, so there is no concentration gradient or osmotic difference to drive water movement.
• In Cup D (Boiled Potato): Boiling denatures cellular proteins and permanently kills the potato cells. Dead cell membranes lose their semi-permeable / selectively permeable properties and become freely permeable. Without intact semi-permeable membranes, osmosis cannot take place.
CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
(i) Endosmosis in B and C due to hypertonic solute cavity1½ Marks
(ii) Cup A acts as control experiment1 Mark
(iii) Lack of gradient in A; boiling destroys cell membrane selectively in D1½ Marks
Exercise — Q10
Which type of cell division is required for growth and repair of body and which type is involved in formation of gametes?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Types of Cell Division:
1. For Growth and Tissue Repair: Mitosis (Equational division).
• A parent diploid cell divides into two genetically identical daughter cells, maintaining the exact chromosome number (2n ⟶ 2n), essential for somatic body growth, development, and tissue healing.

2. For Formation of Gametes: Meiosis (Reductional division).
• Specific diploid reproductive germ cells divide twice to produce four haploid gametes (sperm and ova), halving the chromosome number (2n ⟶ n). This ensures that upon fertilization, the original diploid chromosome number of the species is conserved.

📝 Case Study Question
Osmotic Responses in Blood and Plant Cells: Fresh RBCs and plant epidermal peel cells are placed in three beakers containing: (A) Pure distilled water, (B) 0.9% NaCl solution, and (C) Concentrated 10% NaCl solution.
(a) What will happen to RBCs in beaker A? How will plant cells behave differently?
(b) What term describes the state of plant cells in beaker C?
(c) Which structure provides mechanical strength to plant cells against bursting?
(a) In beaker A (hypotonic), RBCs will swell and burst (hemolysis) because animal cells lack a cell wall. Plant cells will swell and become turgid, but will not burst.
(b) The plant cells will undergo plasmolysis (shrinkage of protoplast away from the rigid cell wall due to exosmosis).
(c) The rigid, cellulose cell wall exerts turgor counter-pressure that prevents osmotic rupture.
🧠 HOTS
HOTS Question: Why are mitochondria and plastids called semi-autonomous organelles?
They are termed semi-autonomous because: (1) They contain their own extranuclear circular DNA, (2) They possess their own 70S ribosomes and RNA, enabling them to synthesize some of their own structural proteins and enzymes, and (3) They can self-replicate by binary fission independently within the host eukaryotic cell.
