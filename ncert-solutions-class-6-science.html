Chapter 1: The Wonderful World of Science

NCERT Curiosity (Class 6) — Nature of Scientific Inquiry, Observation, Asking Questions, Hypothesis, Experimentation & Laboratory Safety | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Scientific Concepts & Principles
  • Nature of Science: Science is an organized, systematic way of exploring, understanding, and explaining natural phenomena around us through careful observation, experimentation, and logical reasoning.
  • Steps of Scientific Method:
    • Observation: Noticing an interesting pattern or occurrence using our senses or measuring tools.
    • Formulating a Question: Asking "Why?", "How?", or "What happens if...?" regarding the observation.
    • Hypothesis: Proposing a tentative, testable explanation based on prior knowledge.
    • Experimentation: Designing controlled trials with variables to test the hypothesis.
    • Data Collection & Analysis: Recording measurements accurately in tables or graphs.
    • Conclusion: Deciding whether the evidence supports or refutes the original hypothesis.
  • Branches of Science: Biology (study of living organisms), Chemistry (study of substances and their transformations), Physics (study of matter, energy, and forces), and Earth & Space Science (study of our planet and the universe).
  • Laboratory Safety & Ethics: Always wear safety goggles and lab apron, handle glassware gently, avoid smelling or tasting unknown chemicals directly, and dispose of wastes in designated containers.

NCERT Exercise 1.1: Foundations of Scientific Inquiry
Q1
What is science? Explain why curiosity and observation are considered the twin pillars of scientific discovery with suitable examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Definition of Science:
Science is the systematic pursuit and application of knowledge and understanding of the natural and physical world following a verifiable methodology based on evidence.
Role of Curiosity and Observation:
  • Curiosity (The Starting Spark): Curiosity is the innate desire to learn and understand. It drives a person to question why the sky is blue, how birds fly, or why milk curdles. Without curiosity, no scientific questions would ever be formulated.
  • Observation (The Evidence Collector): Observation involves carefully watching, listening, measuring, and noting down details of objects and events using our senses and scientific instruments.
  • Historical Example: Sir Isaac Newton observed an apple falling from a tree to the ground. Driven by curiosity regarding why the fruit fell straight down rather than moving sideways or upwards, he formulated the Universal Law of Gravitation.

Clear definition of Science as a systematic body of knowledge1 Mark
Explanation of Curiosity and Observation as foundational steps1 Mark
Real-world or historical example (e.g., falling apple / gravitation)1 Mark
Q2
List the sequential steps of the scientific method. Why is a controlled experiment essential in testing a hypothesis?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Sequential Steps of the Scientific Method:
1. Making an accurate Observation.
2. Raising a well-defined Question.
3. Formulating an educated, testable Hypothesis.
4. Designing and executing a Controlled Experiment.
5. Recording and analyzing Data.
6. Drawing a logical Conclusion and communicating findings.
Importance of a Controlled Experiment:
In a controlled experiment, only one factor (the independent variable) is altered at a time while all other conditions are kept strictly constant. This ensures that any observed result is directly caused by the tested variable, eliminating accidental influences and bias.

Stating all 6 steps in correct sequence1.5 Marks

NCERT Exercise 1.2: Laboratory Safety & Scientific Tools
Q3
State four important laboratory safety rules that every science student must follow while conducting experiments.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Key Safety Rules:
1. Wear Protective Gear: Always wear safety goggles, cotton lab coats, and closed shoes to protect eyes and skin from chemical spills.
2. No Tasting or Direct Inhalation: Never taste any substance in the lab. When checking an odour, gently waft the vapors towards your nose with your hand from a safe distance.
3. Safe Handling of Heat & Glassware: Always point test tubes away from yourself and classmates while heating over a spirit burner. Inspect glassware for cracks before heating.
4. Immediate Reporting: Immediately inform the teacher in case of chemical spills, accidental glass breakage, or skin contact. Wash affected skin thoroughly with abundant cold tap water.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Listing four valid laboratory safety guidelines with scientific justifications0.5 Mark each (Total 2 Marks)
Q4
Differentiate between qualitative and quantitative observations with two examples of each. Which type of observation is preferred in scientific experiments, and why?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
1. Qualitative Observation:
Observations described using words, characteristics, or sensory qualities without numerical measurements.
Examples: "The solution turned dark blue", "The leaf has a rough surface."
2. Quantitative Observation:
Observations described using measurable numbers, quantities, and standard units.
Examples: "The liquid volume is 250 mL", "The temperature increased by 5 °C."
Scientific Preference:
Quantitative observations are strongly preferred in scientific research because they are objective, exact, reproducible by other scientists worldwide, and can be statistically verified without personal interpretation bias.

Accurate definition and distinction with examples2 Marks
Reason for preferring quantitative data (objectivity, precision)1 Mark
CBSE Competency-Based Question (Inquiry & Real-Life Case Study)
Case Study: Testing Seed Germination Conditions:
Aarav noticed that gram (chana) seeds kept in a dry container in the kitchen did not sprout, while seeds soaked in moist cloth germinated within two days. He decided to investigate the exact conditions needed for seed germination by setting up four test cups:
• Cup A: Dry seeds exposed to room temperature and sunlight.
• Cup B: Seeds completely submerged in water with oil layer on top (no air) at room temperature.
• Cup C: Seeds kept on moist cotton with access to air at room temperature.
• Cup D: Seeds kept on moist cotton placed inside a refrigerator at 4 °C.

(a) In which cup will the seeds successfully germinate? What was Aarav's hypothesis?
(b) What was the purpose of Cup B with the oil layer?
(c) Identify the independent variable and the controlled factors in this experiment.
Detailed Analytical Solution & Marking Scheme

(a) Successful Germination & Hypothesis:
Seeds in Cup C will successfully germinate. Aarav's hypothesis was: "Seeds require both moisture (water), oxygen (air), and suitable temperature to break dormancy and germinate."

(b) Purpose of Cup B:
The oil layer forms an impermeable barrier that prevents atmospheric oxygen (air) from dissolving into the water. This cup tests whether water alone is sufficient for germination in the absence of air.

(c) Variables Identified:
• Independent Variable: Environmental growth conditions (Moisture, Air, and Temperature availability).
• Controlled Factors (Constants): Same type and count of gram seeds, same type of container, and identical testing duration.
