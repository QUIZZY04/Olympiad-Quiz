📌 Key Concepts & Formulas Summary
  • Equation: A mathematical statement showing that two expressions have equal value (LHS = RHS), involving at least one variable.
  • Solution of an Equation: The value of the variable for which the equation is satisfied (LHS = RHS).
  • Rules for Balancing Equations:
    • Add or subtract the same number on both sides.
    • Multiply or divide both sides by the same non-zero number.
  • Transposition Method: Moving a term from one side of the equation to the other changes its sign:
    • + becomes -  |  - becomes +
    • × becomes ÷  |  ÷ becomes ×
Exercise 4.1
Q1
Check whether the value given in the brackets is a solution to the given equation or not:
(a) n + 5 = 19 (n = 1)
(b) 7n + 5 = 19 (n = −2)
(c) 7n + 5 = 19 (n = 2)
(d) 4p − 3 = 13 (p = 1)
(e) 4p − 3 = 13 (p = −4)
(f) 4p − 3 = 13 (p = 0)
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) n + 5 = 19 for n = 1:
LHS = 1 + 5 = 6 ≠ 19 (RHS) ⇒ No, not a solution.
(b) 7n + 5 = 19 for n = −2:
LHS = 7(−2) + 5 = −14 + 5 = −9 ≠ 19 (RHS) ⇒ No, not a solution.
(c) 7n + 5 = 19 for n = 2:
LHS = 7(2) + 5 = 14 + 5 = 19 = RHS ⇒ Yes, it is a solution.
(d) 4p − 3 = 13 for p = 1:
LHS = 4(1) − 3 = 4 − 3 = 1 ≠ 13 (RHS) ⇒ No, not a solution.
(e) 4p − 3 = 13 for p = −4:
LHS = 4(−4) − 3 = −16 − 3 = −19 ≠ 13 (RHS) ⇒ No, not a solution.
(f) 4p − 3 = 13 for p = 0:
LHS = 4(0) − 3 = −3 ≠ 13 (RHS) ⇒ No, not a solution.

Exercise 4.2 & 4.3 (Solving Equations)
Q1
Solve the following equations:
(a) 2y + 5/2 = 37/2
(b) 5t + 28 = 10
(c) a/5 + 3 = 2
(d) q/4 + 7 = 5
(e) 5/2 x = −10
(f) 5/2 x = 25/4
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) 2y + 5/2 = 37/2:
Transpose 5/2: 2y = 37/2 − 5/2 = 32/2 = 16 ⇒ y = 16/2 = 8.
(b) 5t + 28 = 10:
5t = 10 − 28 = −18 ⇒ t = -18/5.
(c) a/5 + 3 = 2:
a/5 = 2 − 3 = −1 ⇒ a = −1 × 5 = −5.
(d) q/4 + 7 = 5:
q/4 = 5 − 7 = −2 ⇒ q = −2 × 4 = −8.
(e) 5/2x = −10:
x = −10 × 2/5 = −2 × 2 = −4.
(f) 5/2x = 25/4:
x = 25/4 × 2/5 = 5/2 = 2 1/2.

Q2
Solve the following equations with brackets:
(a) 2(x + 4) = 12
(b) 3(n − 5) = 21
(c) 3(n − 5) = −21
(d) −4(2 + x) = 8
(e) 4(2 − x) = 8
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) 2(x + 4) = 12:
Divide both sides by 2: x + 4 = 6 ⇒ x = 6 − 4 = 2.
(b) 3(n − 5) = 21:
Divide both sides by 3: n − 5 = 7 ⇒ n = 7 + 5 = 12.
(c) 3(n − 5) = −21:
Divide both sides by 3: n − 5 = −7 ⇒ n = −7 + 5 = −2.
(d) −4(2 + x) = 8:
Divide by −4: 2 + x = 8/(−4) = −2 ⇒ x = −2 − 2 = −4.
(e) 4(2 − x) = 8:
Divide by 4: 2 − x = 2 ⇒ −x = 2 − 2 = 0 ⇒ x = 0.

Exercise 4.4 (Applications & Word Problems)
Q1
Set up equations and solve them to find the unknown numbers:
(a) Add 4 to eight times a number; you get 60.
(b) One-fifth of a number minus 4 gives 3.
(c) If I take three-fourths of a number and add 3 to it, I get 21.
(d) When I subtracted 11 from twice a number, the result was 15.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) Let the number be x.
Equation: 8x + 4 = 60 ⇒ 8x = 60 − 4 = 56 ⇒ x = 56/8 = 7.
(b) Let the number be y.
Equation: y/5 − 4 = 3 ⇒ y/5 = 7 ⇒ y = 7 × 5 = 35.
(c) Let the number be z.
Equation: 3/4z + 3 = 21 ⇒ 3/4z = 18 ⇒ z = 18 × 4/3 = 6 × 4 = 24.
(d) Let the number be n.
Equation: 2n − 11 = 15 ⇒ 2n = 15 + 11 = 26 ⇒ n = 26/2 = 13.

Q2
Laxmi’s father is 49 years old. He is 4 years older than three times Laxmi’s age. What is Laxmi’s age?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Step 1: Define variable:
Let Laxmi's present age be y years.
Step 2: Set up equation:
Three times Laxmi's age = 3y.
Father is 4 years older than 3y: 3y + 4.
Given father's age is 49 years:
3y + 4 = 49.
Step 3: Solve for y:
3y = 49 − 4 = 45 ⇒ y = 45/3 = 15.

Conclusion: Laxmi’s age is 15 years.

Q3
Irfan says that he has 7 marbles more than five times the marbles Parmit has. Irfan has 37 marbles. How many marbles does Parmit have?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Step 1: Define variable:
Let the number of marbles Parmit has be m.
Step 2: Form equation:
Five times Parmit's marbles = 5m.
7 marbles more than 5m = 5m + 7.
Given Irfan has 37 marbles:
5m + 7 = 37.
Step 3: Solve equation:
5m = 37 − 7 = 30 ⇒ m = 30/5 = 6.

Conclusion: Parmit has 6 marbles.

Case Study: Cricket Partnership
Scenario: Rahul scored twice as many runs as Sachin. Together, their runs fell two short of a double century (200 runs). (i) Form an equation to represent this situation. (ii) How many runs did each one score?

(i) Equation Formulation:
Let Sachin's score = x runs.
Rahul's score = 2x runs.
Combined runs = x + 2x = 3x.
Two short of a double century = 200 − 2 = 198.
Equation: 3x = 198.

(ii) Solving for individual scores:
x = 198/3 = 66.
Sachin scored = 66 runs.
Rahul scored = 2 × 66 = 132 runs.
Check: 66 + 132 = 198 (verified).
