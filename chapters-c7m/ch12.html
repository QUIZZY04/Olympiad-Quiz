📌 Key Concepts & Symmetry Properties Summary
  • Line Symmetry: A figure has line symmetry if a line can be drawn dividing it into two identical halves that coincide exactly when folded along that line.
  • Lines of Symmetry for Regular Polygons: Each regular polygon of n sides has exactly n lines of symmetry:
    • Equilateral triangle: 3 lines
    • Square: 4 lines
    • Regular pentagon: 5 lines
    • Regular hexagon: 6 lines
    • Circle: Infinitely many lines (along any diameter)
  • Rotational Symmetry: When a figure rotated around a fixed point (centre of rotation) by an angle ≤ 360° looks exactly the same as in its original orientation.
  • Order of Rotational Symmetry: The number of times a figure fits onto itself in one full 360° rotation: Order = 360°/(Angle of rotation)
  • Dual Symmetry: Figures possessing both line symmetry and rotational symmetry of order > 1 (e.g. Equilateral triangle, square, rectangle, circle, and English letters H, I, O, X).
Exercise 12.1 (Line Symmetry)
Q1
State the number of lines of symmetry for the following figures:
(a) An equilateral triangle
(b) An isosceles triangle
(c) A scalene triangle
(d) A square
(e) A rectangle
(f) A rhombus
(g) A parallelogram
(h) A quadrilateral
(i) A regular hexagon
(j) A circle
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) Equilateral Triangle: 3 lines of symmetry (each altitude/median).
(b) Isosceles Triangle: 1 line of symmetry (bisector of vertex angle).
(c) Scalene Triangle: 0 lines of symmetry (none of the sides or angles are equal).
(d) Square: 4 lines of symmetry (2 diagonals + 2 perpendicular bisectors of opposite sides).
(e) Rectangle: 2 lines of symmetry (perpendicular bisectors of opposite sides).
(f) Rhombus: 2 lines of symmetry (along the two diagonals).
(g) Parallelogram: 0 lines of symmetry (in general, cannot be folded to coincide).
(h) Quadrilateral: 0 lines of symmetry (in general).
(i) Regular Hexagon: 6 lines of symmetry (3 joining opposite vertices + 3 joining midpoints of opposite sides).
(j) Circle: Infinitely many lines of symmetry (any straight line through the centre / diameter).

Exercise 12.2 (Rotational Symmetry)
Q1
Give the order of the rotational symmetry and the angle of rotation for each of the following figures:
(a) Square
(b) Rectangle
(c) Rhombus
(d) Equilateral triangle
(e) Regular hexagon
(f) Circle
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) Square:
• Centre of rotation: Intersection of diagonals.
• Angle of rotation: 360°/4 = 90°.
• Order of rotational symmetry = 4.
(b) Rectangle:
• Centre of rotation: Intersection of diagonals.
• Angle of rotation: 360°/2 = 180°.
• Order of rotational symmetry = 2.
(c) Rhombus:
• Centre of rotation: Intersection of diagonals.
• Angle of rotation: 360°/2 = 180°.
• Order of rotational symmetry = 2.
(d) Equilateral Triangle:
• Centre of rotation: Centroid (intersection of medians).
• Angle of rotation: 360°/3 = 120°.
• Order of rotational symmetry = 3.
(e) Regular Hexagon:
• Centre of rotation: Centre of hexagon.
• Angle of rotation: 360°/6 = 60°.
• Order of rotational symmetry = 6.
(f) Circle:
• Centre of rotation: Centre of circle.
• Angle of rotation: Any angle θ.
• Order of rotational symmetry = Infinitely many.

Q2
Can we have a rotational symmetry of order more than 1 whose angle of rotation is:
(i) 45°?
(ii) 17°?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Rule: A figure can have rotational symmetry if and only if the angle of rotation divides 360° completely without a remainder.

(i) Angle of rotation = 45°:
360°/45° = 8 (an exact integer).
Yes, we can have a rotational symmetry of order 8 (e.g. a regular octagon).
(ii) Angle of rotation = 17°:
360°/17° = 21.176... (not an integer).
No, since 360° is not divisible by 17°, a rotational symmetry of order more than 1 is not possible.

Exercise 12.3 (Dual Symmetry)
Q1
Name any two figures that have both line symmetry and rotational symmetry.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. A Square:
• Has 4 lines of symmetry.
• Has rotational symmetry of order 4 (angle of rotation = 90°).
2. An Equilateral Triangle:
• Has 3 lines of symmetry.
• Has rotational symmetry of order 3 (angle of rotation = 120°).
(A Circle or Regular Hexagon are also valid examples).

Alphabet Symmetry Analysis
Problem: Name any two letters of the English alphabet that possess: (i) Both line symmetry and rotational symmetry, (ii) Only rotational symmetry of order > 1 but no line symmetry.

(i) Both line and rotational symmetry:
Letters H, I, O, X.
• Letter H has 2 lines of symmetry (1 vertical, 1 horizontal) and rotational symmetry of order 2 (rotates by 180°).
• Letter O has infinitely many lines of symmetry and infinite rotational symmetry.

(ii) Only rotational symmetry (no line symmetry):
Letters S, Z, N.
• Letter S rotates by 180° onto itself (order 2), but cannot be folded along any line to coincide.
• Letter Z rotates by 180° (order 2), but has no line of symmetry.
