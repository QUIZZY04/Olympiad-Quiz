Chapter 6: The Triangle and its Properties

NCERT Exercise 6.1 to 6.5 — Complete Solutions as per CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

📌 Key Concepts & Theorems Summary
  • Median of a Triangle: The line segment connecting a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. Every triangle has 3 medians that concur at the Centroid.
  • Altitude of a Triangle: The perpendicular line segment from a vertex to the line containing the opposite side. A triangle has 3 altitudes that concur at the Orthocentre.
  • Exterior Angle Property: An exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of its two interior opposite angles: ∠ ext = ∠ A + ∠ B
  • Angle Sum Property: The sum of all three interior angles of a triangle is always 180°: ∠ A + ∠ B + ∠ C = 180°
  • Triangle Inequality Property: The sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle is always greater than the length of the third side: a + b > c, b + c > a, c + a > b
  • Pythagoras Theorem: In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides: Hypotenuse² = Base² + Perpendicular² ⇒ h² = b² + p²

Q1
In ΔPQR, D is the mid-point of QR. (i) Line segment PM is ..... (ii) Line segment PD is ..... (iii) Is QM = MR?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) Line segment PM:
Since PM ⊥ QR (makes a right angle 90° with QR), PM is an Altitude of ΔPQR.
(ii) Line segment PD:
Since D is the midpoint of QR, line segment joining vertex P to midpoint D is the Median of ΔPQR.
(iii) Is QM = MR?
No, QM ≠ MR. Because D is the midpoint (so QD = DR), whereas M is the foot of the perpendicular altitude, which is not necessarily the midpoint for a scalene triangle.

Exercise 6.3 (Angle Sum Property)
Q1
Find the value of the unknown x in the following triangles:
(i) Two angles are 50° and 60°
(ii) Right-angled triangle with one acute angle 30°
(iii) Two angles are 30° and 110°
(iv) Isosceles triangle with two equal angles x and vertex angle 50°
(v) Equilateral-like triangle with all angles equal to x
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

By Angle Sum Property of a triangle: Sum of all 3 angles = 180°.

(i) x + 50° + 60° = 180° ⇒ x + 110° = 180° ⇒ x = 70°.
(ii) x + 90° + 30° = 180° ⇒ x + 120° = 180° ⇒ x = 60°.
(iii) x + 30° + 110° = 180° ⇒ x + 140° = 180° ⇒ x = 40°.
(iv) x + x + 50° = 180° ⇒ 2x = 180° − 50° = 130° ⇒ x = 130°/2 = 65°.
(v) x + x + x = 180° ⇒ 3x = 180° ⇒ x = 180°/3 = 60°.

Exercise 6.4 (Triangle Inequality)
Q1
Is it possible to have a triangle with the following sides?
(i) 2 cm, 3 cm, 5 cm
(ii) 3 cm, 6 cm, 7 cm
(iii) 6 cm, 3 cm, 2 cm
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Property: In any triangle, the sum of lengths of any two sides must be strictly greater than the third side (a + b > c).

(i) 2 cm, 3 cm, 5 cm:
Check sum of smaller sides: 2 + 3 = 5 cm.
Since 5 is not greater than the third side 5 (5 = 5), a triangle is NOT possible.
(ii) 3 cm, 6 cm, 7 cm:
Check all three combinations:
• 3 + 6 = 9 > 7 (True)
• 6 + 7 = 13 > 3 (True)
• 3 + 7 = 10 > 6 (True)
Therefore, a triangle IS possible.
(iii) 6 cm, 3 cm, 2 cm:
Check sum of smaller sides: 3 + 2 = 5 cm.
Since 5 < 6 (less than third side), a triangle is NOT possible.

Q2
The lengths of two sides of a triangle are 12 cm and 15 cm. Between what two measures should the length of the third side fall?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Mathematical Principle:
For any triangle with sides a, b, c:
|a − b| < c < (a + b).
That is, the third side must be greater than the difference of the two sides and less than their sum.
Calculation:
Sum of two sides = 15 + 12 = 27 cm.
Difference of two sides = 15 − 12 = 3 cm.

Conclusion: The length of the third side must be greater than 3 cm and less than 27 cm (i.e. between 3 cm and 27 cm).

Exercise 6.5 (Pythagoras Theorem)
Q1
PQR is a triangle, right-angled at P. If PQ = 10 cm and PR = 24 cm, find QR.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Step 1: Identify sides of right-angled triangle:
Right angle is at vertex P ⇒ QR is the hypotenuse.
Given legs: PQ = 10 cm, PR = 24 cm.
Step 2: Apply Pythagoras Theorem:
QR² = PQ² + PR²
QR² = 10² + 24² = 100 + 576 = 676
QR = √(676) = 26 cm.

Conclusion: The length of QR is 26 cm.

Q3
A tree is broken at a height of 5 m from the ground and its top touches the ground at a distance of 12 m from the base of the tree. Find the original height of the tree.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Step 1: Right-angled triangle model:
Let BC = 5 m be the standing trunk (perpendicular).
Distance from trunk base to top AB = 12 m (base).
The broken fallen trunk AC is the hypotenuse.
Step 2: Calculate hypotenuse AC:
AC² = AB² + BC² = 12² + 5² = 144 + 25 = 169
AC = √(169) = 13 m.
Step 3: Calculate original tree height:
Original height = BC + AC = 5 m + 13 m = 18 m.

Conclusion: The original height of the tree was 18 metres.

Q3
AM is a median of a triangle ABC. Is AB + BC + CA > 2 AM? (Consider the sides of triangles ΔABM and ΔAMC).
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
In any triangle, the sum of any two sides is greater than the third side.
1. In ΔABM: AB + BM > AM ... (Equation 1)
2. In ΔAMC: AC + MC > AM ... (Equation 2)
Adding equations (1) and (2):
(AB + BM) + (AC + MC) > AM + AM
AB + (BM + MC) + AC > 2 AM.
Since M is on BC, BM + MC = BC.
Therefore, AB + BC + CA > 2 AM.
Yes, the statement is true!

In a rectangle, adjacent sides and diagonal form a right-angled triangle:

Q8
The diagonals of a rhombus measure 16 cm and 30 cm. Find its perimeter.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles (90°).
Half of diagonals = 16/2 = 8 cm and 30/2 = 15 cm.
In the right triangle formed by the half-diagonals and a side of the rhombus (s):
s² = 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289 ⇒ s = √289 = 17 cm.
Since all 4 sides of a rhombus are equal:
Perimeter = 4 × s = 4 × 17 = 68 cm.

Final Answer: The perimeter of the rhombus is 68 cm.

Q3
Verify by drawing a diagram if the median and altitude of an isosceles triangle can be same.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Draw an isosceles triangle ΔABC where AB = AC.
2. Draw median AD from vertex A to the midpoint D of the non-equal base BC (so BD = DC).
3. In ΔABD and ΔACD:
• AB = AC (given)
• BD = DC (AD is median)
• AD = AD (common side)
By SSS Congruence, \Delta ABD \cong \Delta ACD.
Hence, \angle ADB = \angle ADC (CPCTC).
Since they form a linear pair: \angle ADB + \angle ADC = 180° ⟹ \angle ADB = 90°.
Therefore, AD is perpendicular to BC, making AD an altitude as well.
Conclusion: In an isosceles triangle, the median drawn to the non-equal base is identical to its altitude.

Q5
A tree is broken at a height of 5 m from the ground and its top touches the ground at a distance of 12 m from the base of the tree. Find the original height of the tree.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Let the tree be broken at point B at height AB = 5\text{ m}.
Its top touches the ground at point C, so distance AC = 12\text{ m}.
The broken part BC forms the hypotenuse of right triangle ΔBAC.

By Pythagoras theorem:
BC^2 = AB^2 + AC^2
BC^2 = 5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169
BC = \sqrt{169} = 13\text{ m}.

Original height of tree = AB + BC = 5 + 13 = 18 m.
