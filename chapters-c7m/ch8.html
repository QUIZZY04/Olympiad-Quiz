📌 Key Concepts & Properties Summary
  • Rational Number: A number that can be written in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.
    • Positive: Both p and q have the same sign (e.g. 2/3, (-5)/(-7)).
    • Negative: p and q have opposite signs (e.g. (-3)/8, 5/(-9)).
    • 0 is a rational number since 0 = 0/1.
  • Standard Form: A rational number p/q is in standard form if q > 0 and p, q have no common factor other than 1.
  • Equivalent Rational Numbers: Obtained by multiplying or dividing numerator and denominator by the same non-zero integer: p/q = (p × m)/(q × m)
  • Density Property: Between any two rational numbers, there exist infinitely many rational numbers.
  • Operations on Rational Numbers:
    • Addition/Subtraction: Take LCM of denominators and adjust numerators accordingly.
    • Multiplication: a/b × c/d = (a × c)/(b × d).
    • Division: a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c (multiplication by reciprocal of divisor).
Exercise 8.1
Q1
List five rational numbers between:
(i) −1 and 0
(ii) −2 and −1
(iii) −4/5 and −2/3
(iv) −1/2 and 2/3
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) Between −1 and 0:
Multiply numerator and denominator by 5 + 1 = 6:
−1 = -6/6,   0 = 0/6.
Five rational numbers: -5/6, -4/6, -3/6, -2/6, -1/6
(Simplified: -5/6, -2/3, -1/2, -1/3, -1/6).
(ii) Between −2 and −1:
Multiply by 6:
−2 = -12/6,   −1 = -6/6.
Five rational numbers: -11/6, -10/6, -9/6, -8/6, -7/6.
(iii) Between −4/5 and −2/3:
LCM of 5 and 3 is 15. Make denominators equal to 15 × 3 = 45:
-4/5 = -36/45,   -2/3 = -30/45.
Five rational numbers: -35/45, -34/45, -33/45, -32/45, -31/45.
(iv) Between −1/2 and 2/3:
LCM of 2 and 3 is 6. Multiply by 2 ⇒ denominator 12:
-1/2 = -6/12,   2/3 = 8/12.
Five rational numbers: -5/12, -4/12, 0, 1/12, 5/12.

Q2
Rewrite the following rational numbers in the simplest (standard) form:
(i) −8/6
(ii) 25/45
(iii) −44/72
(iv) −8/10
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Divide numerator and denominator by their HCF:

(i) -8/6:
HCF of 8 and 6 is 2: -(8 ÷ 2)/(6 ÷ 2) = -4/3.
(ii) 25/45:
HCF of 25 and 45 is 5: (25 ÷ 5)/(45 ÷ 5) = 5/9.
(iii) -44/72:
HCF of 44 and 72 is 4: -(44 ÷ 4)/(72 ÷ 4) = -11/18.
(iv) -8/10:
HCF of 8 and 10 is 2: -(8 ÷ 2)/(10 ÷ 2) = -4/5.

Q3
Which is greater in each of the following?
(i) 2/3, 5/2
(ii) −5/6, −4/3
(iii) −3/4, 2/−3
(iv) −3 2/7, −3 4/5
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) 2/3 and 5/2:
LCM = 6: 2/3 = 4/6, 5/2 = 15/6.
Since 15 > 4 ⇒ 5/2 > 2/3. (Also 5/2 > 1 while 2/3 < 1).
(ii) −5/6 and −4/3:
LCM = 6: -5/6, -4/3 = -8/6.
On the number line, −5 > −8. Hence, -5/6 > -4/3.
(iii) −3/4 and 2/−3:
In standard form: -3/4 and -2/3.
LCM = 12: -3/4 = -9/12, -2/3 = -8/12.
Since −8 > −9 ⇒ 2/(−3) > -3/4.
(iv) −3 2/7 and −3 4/5:
-3 2/7 = -23/7 = -115/35.
-3 4/5 = -19/5 = -133/35.
Since −115 > −133 ⇒ -3 2/7 > -3 4/5.

Exercise 8.2 (Operations on Rational Numbers)
Q1
Find the sum:
(i) 5/4 + (−11/4)
(ii) 5/3 + 3/5
(iii) −9/10 + 22/15
(iv) −3/−11 + 5/9
(v) −8/19 + (−2/57)
(vi) −2⅓ + 4⅗
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) 5/4 + (-11/4) = (5 − 11)/4 = (−6)/4 = -3/2 = -1 1/2
(ii) 5/3 + 3/5: LCM = 15.
= (5 × 5 + 3 × 3)/15 = (25 + 9)/15 = 34/15 = 2 4/15.
(iii) -9/10 + 22/15: LCM = 30.
= (−9 × 3 + 22 × 2)/30 = (−27 + 44)/30 = 17/30.
(iv) (−3)/(−11) + 5/9 = 3/11 + 5/9: LCM = 99.
= (3 × 9 + 5 × 11)/99 = (27 + 55)/99 = 82/99.
(v) -8/19 + (-2/57): LCM = 57.
= (−8 × 3 − 2)/57 = (−24 − 2)/57 = -26/57.
(vi) -2 1/3 + 4 3/5 = -7/3 + 23/5: LCM = 15.
= (−7 × 5 + 23 × 3)/15 = (−35 + 69)/15 = 34/15 = 2 4/15.

Q3
Find the value of:
(i) (−4) ÷ 2/3
(ii) −3/5 ÷ 2
(iii) −4/5 ÷ (−3)
(iv) −1/8 ÷ 3/4
(v) −2/13 ÷ 1/7
(vi) −7/12 ÷ (−2/13)
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Multiply the first number by the reciprocal of the divisor:

(i) (−4) ÷ 2/3 = (−4) × 3/2 = −2 × 3 = −6
(ii) -3/5 ÷ 2 = -3/5 × 1/2 = -3/10
(iii) -4/5 ÷ (−3) = -4/5 × (-1/3) = 4/15
(iv) -1/8 ÷ 3/4 = -1/8 × 4/3 = -1/6
(v) -2/13 ÷ 1/7 = -2/13 × 7/1 = -14/13 = -1 1/13
(vi) -7/12 ÷ (-2/13) = -7/12 × (-13/2) = 91/24 = 3 19/24

HOTS: Insertion of Rationals
Problem: Is it true that there are only 5 rational numbers between −1 and 0? Explain mathematically how many rational numbers exist between any two distinct rational numbers.

Answer: No, it is not true.

Between any two distinct rational numbers, there are infinitely many rational numbers.

Reasoning: We can multiply the numerator and denominator by any arbitrarily large integer k. For example, choosing k = 100 gives -100/100 and 0/100, yielding 99 rational numbers. Choosing k = 10,000 yields 9,999 rational numbers. By choosing k arbitrarily large, we can insert as many rational numbers as we desire. This fundamental property is called the Density Property of rational numbers.
