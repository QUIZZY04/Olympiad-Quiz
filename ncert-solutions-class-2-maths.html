Chapter 1: A Day at the Beach

Counting in Groups, Pairs & Estimating Collections | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Counting objects by grouping into pairs (twos) and fives.
  • Estimating quantities visually before counting exact amounts.
  • Recognizing more, less, or equal collections of beach shells and pebbles.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (4 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Textbook Exercise • Group Counting
Look at the starfish on the sand. There are 4 groups with 2 starfish in each group. How many starfish are there in total?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
• Number of groups = 4
• Starfish in each group = 2
• Total starfish = 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8 starfish (or 4 × 2 = 8).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct addition/multiplication sentence 1 M
Final answer with unit (8 starfish) 1 M
Q2 Visual Estimation • Pairs
Rohit has 11 flip-flops (slippers). Can he make complete pairs without any slipper left over? Explain your answer.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
• A pair requires 2 items.
• 11 = 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1.
• Rohit makes 5 complete pairs and 1 slipper is left over. Therefore, 11 is an odd number and cannot form complete pairs.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Division into pairs (5 pairs) 1.5 M
Identification of 1 remainder / odd number 1.5 M
Q3 Word Problem • Shells on the Beach
Aarav found 15 white shells and 9 brown shells on the beach. How many shells did he find in all? Which shells were more and by how many?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Calculation:
1. White shells = 15
2. Brown shells = 9
3. Total shells = 15 + 9 = 24 shells.
4. Comparison: White shells (15) are more than Brown shells (9).
5. Difference = 15 - 9 = 6 more white shells.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Total shells (15 + 9 = 24) 1.5 M
Comparison and difference (15 - 9 = 6) 1.5 M
Q4 Mental Math • Skip Counting
Complete the skip-counting chain: 2, 4, 6, __, __, 12, __, 16.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
The rule is adding 2 each time:
2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Filled in 8, 10, 14 correctly 2 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Seashell Collection Project

Meena collects 14 seashells on the shore. Her brother collects 18 seashells. Meena groups her shells into pairs of 2.

Analytical Task: How many pairs does Meena form? How many total seashells did both children collect altogether?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Meena has 14 shells. Since 1 pair = 2 shells, 14 ÷ 2 = 7 pairs.
2. Total seashells = 14 + 18 = 32 seashells.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (3 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Shape Identification • 2D vs 3D
Classify the following items into 'Rolls', 'Slides', or 'Both Rolls and Slides': (a) Cricket Ball, (b) Eraser, (c) Coin, (d) Pencil.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification:
• (a) Cricket Ball: Rolls (it has only a curved round surface).
• (b) Eraser: Slides (it has flat faces).
• (c) Coin: Both rolls and slides (rolls on circular edge, slides on flat faces).
• (d) Round Pencil: Both rolls (along cylindrical body) and slides (on flat ends).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct classification for all 4 items 3 M
Q2 Corner & Edge Counting
How many corners and straight edges does a rectangular postcard have? Does a circle have any corners?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
1. A rectangular postcard has 4 straight edges and 4 corners (vertices).
2. A circle has 0 corners and 0 straight edges (it is formed by 1 continuous curved line).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Postcard: 4 edges, 4 corners 1 M
Circle: 0 corners, curved line 1 M
Q3 Creative Construction • Towers
Can you stack footballs on top of each other to make a tall tower? Why or why not? What shapes make stable towers?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
• No, we cannot stack round footballs because they have curved surfaces and roll off.
• Objects with flat surfaces like books, square wooden blocks, and shoe boxes make stable towers because flat faces sit securely on each other.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Reason why round balls cannot stack 1.5 M
Identification of flat surface objects for stacking 1.5 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Playground Ramp Experiment

During lunch break, Ananya slides her lunchbox down a slide and rolls an apple down the same slide.

Analytical Task: Why does the apple roll while the lunchbox slides? Name one everyday item that can do both.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. The apple has a curved surface without flat edges, allowing it to roll smoothly.
2. The lunchbox has flat surfaces, so it slides along the slide.
3. A coin or a carrom coin or a water bottle can both roll (on its curved side) and slide (on its flat face).
Rubric: 1.5 M for surface explanation + 1.5 M for coin/bottle example

Cricket Scoreboard Case Study

In a friendly gully cricket match, Priya scored 47 runs and Kabir scored 63 runs.

Analytical Task: Who scored more runs? How many more runs did the top scorer make? How many runs does Priya need to reach 50?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Kabir scored 63 runs and Priya scored 47 runs. Since 63 > 47, Kabir scored more runs.
2. Difference = 63 - 47 = 16 more runs.
3. Priya needs: 50 - 47 = 3 runs to reach 50.

Sundial Tree Experiment

Tanvi measures the shadow of a small school tree at 8:00 AM, 12:00 PM, and 4:30 PM.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (2 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Arithmetic Drill • Addition with Carryover
Solve the following additions:
(a) 38 + 25
(b) 47 + 36
(c) 59 + 18.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solutions:
• (a) 38 + 25: 8 + 5 = 13 (3 ones, carry 1 ten). 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 tens. Result = 63.
• (b) 47 + 36: 7 + 6 = 13 (carry 1). 4 + 3 + 1 = 8. Result = 83.
• (c) 59 + 18: 9 + 8 = 17 (carry 1). 5 + 1 + 1 = 7. Result = 77.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
1 Mark each with correct carryover working 3 M
Q2 Story Problem • Subtraction
A toy shop had 65 rubber balls. In the morning, children bought 32 balls. How many balls are left in the shop?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solution:
• Initial balls = 65
• Balls sold = 32
• Balls remaining = 65 - 32 = 33 balls.
• Verification: 33 + 32 = 65.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct subtraction statement (65 - 32) 1 M
Final answer: 33 balls 1 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Birthday Gift Distribution

On his birthday, Rana brought 54 crayons to share with his classmates. He gifted 28 crayons to his table group.

Analytical Task: How many crayons does Rana have left? Write the mathematical subtraction statement.

1. Total crayons = 54.
2. Crayons gifted = 28.
3. Subtraction: 54 - 28.
• Subtract ones: 4 cannot subtract 8, borrow 1 ten (14 - 8 = 6 ones).
• Subtract tens: 4 tens - 2 tens = 2 tens.
4. Crayons left = 26.
Rubric: 1.5 M for borrowing step + 1.5 M for final answer 26
