Chapter 1: Family and Friends

Understanding Diverse Family Structures, Daily Responsibilities, Care, and Joyful Relationships

Scientific Principles, Environmental Values & Key Inquiries
  • Diverse types of families: Nuclear families, joint families, and single-parent households.
  • Sharing household chores, caring for elderly grandparents, and supporting siblings.
  • Family traditions, cultural stories passed down across generations, and family albums.
  • Empathy, cooperation, and conflict resolution in everyday friendships at school.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions & Inquiry Tasks (100% Coverage)
Q1 What is the difference between a nuclear family and a joint family? Give examples of tasks shared in both. Understanding Family Dynamics 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
A nuclear family consists of parents and their children living together in a household. In contrast, a joint family includes grandparents, parents, aunts, uncles, and cousins living together under one roof.

Shared Tasks:
• In a joint family, grandparents often help take care of grandchildren and narrate moral stories, while uncles and aunts help cook or run family businesses.
• In a nuclear family, parents and children work as a tight team—parents cook and buy groceries while children help lay the dining table, water plants, and keep school bags tidy.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Accurate definitions of nuclear and joint families 1.5 Marks
Clear examples of shared responsibilities and mutual support 1.5 Marks
Q2 How can children show respect and help elderly family members in their daily lives? Empathy & Grandparent Care 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Children can care for elderly family members by:
1. Helping with simple chores: Bringing their spectacles, newspaper, medicines, or a glass of water promptly.
2. Spending quality time: Listening to their childhood memories and reading books or news headlines to them.
3. Assisting mobility: Holding their hands while walking in the park or climbing stairs to prevent falls.
4. Speaking politely: Being patient and never shouting when grandparents have trouble hearing.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Identification of 3-4 concrete caring behaviors 2 Marks
Emphasis on patience and emotional well-being 1 Mark
Q3 What should you do when you and your friend disagree about which game to play at recess? Friendship & Conflict Resolution 2 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
When friends disagree about a game, they should avoid fighting or leaving the playground. Instead, they can:
1. Take Turns: Play one game for the first 10 minutes and the other game for the next 10 minutes.
2. Toss a Coin / Vote: Use a fair choice method like a coin toss or stone-paper-scissors with good sportsmanship.
3. Find a Common Alternative: Pick a third game that both friends enjoy playing together.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (2 Marks):
Fair cooperative methods (turn-taking, consensus) 1.5 Marks
Positive conflict resolution attitude 0.5 Marks
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Promoting Inclusivity and Social Empathy

Real-Life Context: A new boy named Kabir has joined Class 3. He uses a wheelchair and speaks a different regional language at home. During lunch recess, Kabir sits alone near the classroom door while other children run outside to play tag.

Analytical Task: Drawing on the values in 'Family and Friends', what thoughtful actions should the classmates take to welcome Kabir and make him feel included?

Classmates should demonstrate the warmth of an extended family. They can invite Kabir to join their lunch circle, learn greeting words in his native language, and introduce games that everyone can play together from a seated position (such as chess, carrom, word building, or singing). They can also take turns keeping him company, pushing his wheelchair gently during playground walks, and ensuring wheelchair ramps are clear of bags and obstacles.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: Demonstration of empathy, peer inclusivity, accessibility awareness, and cooperative social values (4 Marks).

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Supporting Local Artisans and Economic Sustainability

Real-Life Context: At a district fair, modern imported plastic battery toys are sold right next to a traditional potter selling hand-moulded clay bird whistles. Many children rush to the plastic toys, while the elderly potter sits with few buyers.

Analytical Task: Why is it important to support traditional local artisans at fairs, and what can school communities do to promote eco-friendly indigenous crafts?

Supporting traditional artisans is vital because their crafts represent centuries of cultural heritage, utilize biodegradable natural materials (clay, wood, natural dyes), and provide honest livelihoods to rural families. Plastic toys generate non-recyclable electronic and microplastic waste. Schools can organize 'Heritage Craft Days', invite local potters to conduct workshops, and encourage children to purchase eco-friendly festive gifts directly from local craftspeople.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: Appreciation of indigenous crafts, environmental comparison (clay vs plastic), and actionable cultural promotion ideas (4 Marks).

Chapter 3: Celebrating Festivals

Cultural Diversity, Traditional Delicacies, Festive Music, and Unified Community Celebrations

Scientific Principles, Environmental Values & Key Inquiries
  • Festivals of India: Harvest festivals (Bihu, Pongal, Baisakhi), religious festivals (Diwali, Eid, Christmas, Gurpurab).
  • Special festive foods and sweets prepared with seasonal agricultural harvests.
  • Eco-friendly celebrations: Rangoli with flower petals, earthen diyas, and avoiding firecrackers.
  • The underlying spirit of festivals: Brotherhood, generosity, forgiveness, and community sharing.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions & Inquiry Tasks (100% Coverage)
Q1 What are harvest festivals? Name two harvest festivals celebrated in different parts of India and their special dishes. Festival Diversity & Significance 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Harvest festivals are joyous community celebrations held when standing agricultural crops ripen and farmers gather their hard-earned yield.

Examples:
1. Pongal (Tamil Nadu): Celebrated to honor the Sun God and cattle after harvesting paddy and sugarcane. The special dish is 'Sweet Pongal' made of newly harvested rice, jaggery, milk, and cashew nuts boiled in an earthen pot.
2. Bihu / Magh Bihu (Assam): Marks the end of the winter harvesting season. People construct thatched bamboo huts (Bhelaghar) and prepare traditional rice cakes called 'Pitha' and 'Laru' (sesame/coconut sweets).
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Precise definition of harvest festivals 1 Mark
Identification of two regional harvest festivals with authentic festive foods 2 Marks
Q2 Why should we avoid bursting chemical firecrackers during festivals? List three harmful effects. Eco-Friendly Celebrations 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Bursting chemical firecrackers causes severe harm to our living environment:
1. Dangerous Air Pollution: Firecrackers release toxic fumes (sulfur dioxide, lead, barium) that trigger asthma, coughs, and breathing distress in infants and elderly individuals.
2. Noise Pollution: The loud explosions terrify street animals, pet dogs, and nesting birds, causing severe trauma and hearing loss.
3. Toxic Chemical Litter: Burnt cracker residues leave hazardous chemical dust on streets that pollutes groundwater when washed by rain.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Air pollution and human health impact 1 Mark
Noise impact on birds and domestic animals 1 Mark
Hazardous solid waste generation 1 Mark
Q3 What is the noble message behind community feasts such as the 'Langar' organized on Gurpurab? Community Harmony 2 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
The message behind 'Langar' is absolute human equality, brotherhood, and selfless service. People of all castes, religions, rich and poor, sit together on the floor in a straight row (pangat) and partake of the same freshly cooked vegetarian food, teaching us that everyone is equal in the eyes of God.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (2 Marks):
Concept of equality and selfless community service 1.5 Marks
Inclusivity without discrimination 0.5 Marks
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Environmental Citizenship During Festive Celebrations

Real-Life Context: During Diwali and Ganesh Utsav, vast numbers of plaster-of-paris idols painted with chemical dyes and plastic decorations are submerged into local rivers and lakes, poisoning aquatic life and rendering water unfit for drinking.

Analytical Task: As responsible young environmental ambassadors, how can students encourage their neighborhoods to adopt 100% green and eco-friendly festive celebrations?

Students can launch school awareness campaigns to inspire green celebrations: (1) Advocate for idols made exclusively of unbaked local river clay (shadu mati) embedded with seeds, which dissolve harmlessly in home garden pots to sprout plants. (2) Replace synthetic chemical colors with natural flower petals (marigold, rose), turmeric, and spinach paste for rangolis. (3) Light traditional clay diyas filled with mustard or sesame oil instead of single-use plastic electric strings. (4) Celebrate with music, storytelling, and sharing sweets with underprivileged children rather than spending money on crackers.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: Clay seed-idols, natural bio-rangolis, water protection awareness, and empathetic community sharing (4 Marks).

Chapter 4: Getting to Know Plants

Observing Plant Anatomy, Root Systems, Leaf Venation, Photosynthesis, and Habitats

Scientific Principles, Environmental Values & Key Inquiries
  • Classification of plants based on stem structure: Herbs, shrubs, trees, climbers, and creepers.
  • Root functions: Anchoring the plant firmly in soil and absorbing water and minerals.
  • Leaves as food factories: Chlorophyll, sunlight, air, and water combining in photosynthesis.
  • Leaf patterns: Reticulate venation (net-like) and parallel venation with distinct leaf margins.
Reticulate: Net-like (Peepal, Mango) • Parallel: Straight lines (Grass, Banana) Chlorophyll traps sunlight for photosynthesis
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions & Inquiry Tasks (100% Coverage)
Q1 How do climbers and creepers differ from trees? Give two common examples of each. Plant Classification 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Plants differ based on the strength and thickness of their stems:
1. Trees: Have thick, hard, woody brown trunks that stand erect independently (e.g., Banyan tree, Neem tree).
2. Climbers: Have weak, green stems that cannot stand upright alone. They produce tendrils to climb up walls, fences, or other trees (e.g., Money plant, Pea plant, Grapevine).
3. Creepers: Have soft, fragile stems and heavy fruits that creep along the horizontal surface of the ground (e.g., Pumpkin, Watermelon).
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Contrast of stem characteristics (woody vs weak) 1.5 Marks
Accurate botanical examples for trees, climbers, and creepers 1.5 Marks
Q2 Why are green leaves known as the 'food factories' or 'kitchen' of a plant? Leaf Structure & Function 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Leaves are called the food factories because they prepare nourishment for the entire plant through the process of photosynthesis:
• Leaves contain a green pigment called chlorophyll that traps light energy from the sun.
• They absorb carbon dioxide gas from surrounding air through tiny pores called stomata.
• Roots absorb water and minerals from soil and send them up the stem into the leaves.
• Using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide, the leaf manufactures sugar (glucose) and releases life-giving oxygen into the air.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Explanation of photosynthesis and chlorophyll 1.5 Marks
Role of stomata, water, and oxygen release 1.5 Marks
Q3 Explain the difference between reticulate venation and parallel venation with leaf rubbings. Botanical Observation 2 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
• Reticulate Venation: The veins branch out to create a web-like or criss-cross network across the entire leaf blade (e.g., Rose, Peepal, China Rose).
• Parallel Venation: The veins run side-by-side in parallel straight lines from the base to the leaf tip (e.g., Grass, Wheat, Maize, Banana).
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (2 Marks):
Clear visual description of both vein patterns 1.5 Marks
Accurate leaf examples 0.5 Marks
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Investigative Botanical Inquiry: Sunlight and Plant Growth

Real-Life Context: Simran placed two identical potted mint plants on her balcony. She watered both with 100 ml of water daily, but placed an opaque cardboard box over Plant A while Plant B was exposed to normal sunlight. After 10 days, Plant A's leaves turned pale yellow and wilted, whereas Plant B was thriving and deep green.

Analytical Task: What scientific conclusion can Simran draw from this controlled observation, and how does chlorophyll depend on light?

Simran can conclude that sunlight is an indispensable requirement for photosynthesis and healthy plant life. Plant A could not produce chlorophyll in total darkness; without chlorophyll, it could not synthesize food from water and air, resulting in energy starvation, loss of green pigmentation (etiolation), and eventual wilting. Plant B, having regular sunlight, synthesized food continuously and grew vibrant leaves.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: Hypothesis testing, role of sunlight in chlorophyll formation, controlled experiment interpretation (4 Marks).

Symbiosis, Food Chains, Animal Habitats, Pollination, and Ecological Interdependence

Scientific Principles, Environmental Values & Key Inquiries
  • Ecological interdependence: How plants provide oxygen, food, and shelter to animals.
  • How animals assist plants: Pollination by bees and butterflies, seed dispersal by birds.

📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions & Inquiry Tasks (100% Coverage)
Q1 Explain two ways in which plants help animals, and two ways in which animals help plants. Mutual Interdependence 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Ways Plants Help Animals:
1. Oxygen & Nourishment: Green plants release oxygen during the daytime for animals to breathe and produce fruits, seeds, and nectar for food.
2. Shelter & Protection: Trees provide nesting places for birds, hollows for squirrels, and shade for grazing animals.

Ways Animals Help Plants:
1. Pollination: Insects like honeybees and butterflies carry pollen grains from flower to flower, allowing flowers to develop into fruits.
2. Seed Dispersal: Birds and monkeys eat fruits and drop seeds in distant places through their droppings, helping new trees germinate.

Q2 What is a food chain? Construct a simple 4-step land food chain starting with grass. Food Chains & Producers 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
A food chain is a sequential feeding relationship that demonstrates who eats whom in nature to transfer energy from producers to consumers.

4-Step Food Chain:
Grass (Producer) ➔ Grasshopper (Herbivore) ➔ Frog (Carnivore) ➔ Snake (Apex Predator).
• Grass captures solar energy.
• The grasshopper feeds on green leaves.
• The frog preys on the grasshopper.
• The snake consumes the frog.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Definition of food chain and energy flow 1.5 Marks
Correct 4-organism progression with appropriate ecological terms 1.5 Marks
Q3 Why are earthworms honored as 'the farmer's best friends'? Soil Ecosystems 2 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Earthworms burrow through underground soil, making it loose and aerated so plant roots can breathe and absorb rainwater easily. Furthermore, their waste castings enrich the soil with rich natural humus and organic nutrients.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (2 Marks):

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Ecosystem Fragility: Decline of Honeybees

Real-Life Context: A farmer sprayed heavy chemical pesticides across his mustard and apple orchards to kill aphids. Soon after, all local wild honeybees and bumblebees vanished from the farm. The following spring, the fruit yield dropped by more than 75% even though the trees bloomed with flowers.

Analytical Task: Apply your understanding of 'Plants and Animals Live Together' to explain why the farmer's harvest collapsed after the bees died.

The farmer's harvest failed because honeybees are the primary pollinators of apple and mustard blossoms. When the bees landed on blossoms to gather nectar, pollen grains stuck to their fuzzy bodies and were transferred to neighboring flowers, enabling fertilization and seed/fruit formation. When pesticides wiped out the pollinators, the flowers withered without setting fruit. This demonstrates that killing one vital link disrupts the entire food-producing ecosystem.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: Pollination mechanism, role of beneficial insects, ecological domino effect of chemical pesticides (4 Marks).

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Urban Wildlife Conflict: The Glass Window Hazard

Real-Life Context: A newly built glass office building in town reports that dozens of sunbirds and kingfishers crash into the clear glass facade every week because the glass reflects surrounding trees and blue sky like an open path.

Analytical Task: How can the building management and local children solve this hazard to live in harmony with urban birds?

The bird collision problem occurs because birds perceive the glass reflection as continuous sky or foliage. To eliminate this hazard: (1) Apply frosted, patterned, or silhouette stickers (such as falcon decals or dense dot arrays spaced 5 cm apart) on external window panes so birds recognize a barrier. (2) Install outdoor blinds or lightweight nylon netting. (3) Turn off unnecessary bright internal lights at night to prevent disorienting migratory birds.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: Understanding reflection hazards, practical non-invasive deterrents (window decals, netting), compassionate architectural ethics (4 Marks).

Chapter 7: Water — A Precious Gift

Water Cycle, Sources of Fresh Water, Scarcity, Conservation Techniques, and Rainwater Harvesting

Scientific Principles, Environmental Values & Key Inquiries
  • Natural freshwater sources: Rain, rivers, lakes, ponds, springs, and underground wells.
  • States of water: Solid (ice), liquid (water), and gas (water vapor) in the continuous water cycle.

📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions & Inquiry Tasks (100% Coverage)
Q1 Describe the three key stages of the water cycle: Evaporation, Condensation, and Precipitation. Water Science & The Water Cycle 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
The continuous movement of water on Earth is governed by three primary stages:
1. Evaporation: Solar heat warms up surface water in oceans, lakes, and rivers, converting liquid water into invisible water vapor that rises into the atmosphere.
2. Condensation: As water vapor climbs higher into cooler atmospheric regions, it cools down and condenses into microscopic water droplets that cluster together to form fluffy clouds.
3. Precipitation: When clouds grow heavy and cool, the accumulated water droplets fall back to the ground as rain, hail, or snow, replenishing water bodies.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Clear physical explanation of evaporation and condensation 2 Marks
Description of precipitation completing the hydrological cycle 1 Mark
Q2 Suggest four practical habits that can save liters of clean water in a school every day. Water Conservation Habits 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
1. Fix Leaking Taps: Report dripping taps to school maintenance immediately; a slow drip wastes over 20 liters of water daily.
2. Turn Off Taps While Soap-Washing: Shut the tap while lathering hands with soap or brushing teeth instead of letting water run continuously.
3. Repurpose Leftover Drinking Water: Empty unused water from student water bottles into flowerbeds or potted plants rather than dumping it down the sink drain.
4. Install Aerators / Push-Taps: Use push-button taps that automatically stop flow after 5 seconds in restrooms.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Four distinct, actionable water-saving habits 2 Marks
Practical applicability to school routines 1 Mark
Q3 What is rooftop rainwater harvesting and why is it essential in cities? Rainwater Harvesting 2 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Rooftop rainwater harvesting is the technique of capturing clean rainwater that falls on building roofs, passing it through sand-gravel filters, and diverting it into underground storage tanks or percolation recharge pits.
It is essential because it recharges declining groundwater levels, prevents city waterlogging during heavy monsoon downpours, and provides fresh water during drought seasons.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (2 Marks):
Definition of rooftop rainwater collection and filtration 1 Mark
Benefits of groundwater recharge and flood prevention 1 Mark
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Community Drought Mitigation: Reviving a Village Stepwell

Real-Life Context: A drought-prone village faced acute drinking water shortages during summers because tube wells had dried up. The village panchayat cleaned and restored an ancient 200-year-old stone stepwell (baoli) that had been abandoned and filled with plastic rubbish.

Analytical Task: Analyze why traditional water structures like stepwells (baolis) are scientifically superior to deep borewells in drought-prone areas.

Traditional stepwells (baolis) are architectural masterpieces of natural ecological engineering. Unlike deep borewells that recklessly extract fossil groundwater until aquifers collapse, stepwells rely on seasonal rainwater runoff and shallow seepage. The wide porous stone walls allow rainwater to infiltrate naturally, filter sediment through surrounding sandy soil layers, and maintain a perennial subterranean water pool without electricity or motors. Reviving baolis recharges local aquifers and provides community-controlled water resilience.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: Traditional water wisdom vs deep borehole depletion, ecological percolation, sustainable community water management (4 Marks).

Chapter 8: Food We Eat

Nutritional Food Groups, Balanced Diet, Seasonal Regional Foods, and Avoiding Food Waste

Scientific Principles, Environmental Values & Key Inquiries
  • The three primary food groups: Energy-giving foods, body-building foods, and protective foods.

Q1 What is meant by the 'Web of Life'? How are plants, animals, and non-living elements tied together? Web of Life Concept 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
The Web of Life describes the intricate, interdependent network of relationships connecting all living creatures (plants, animals, microorganisms, humans) with non-living environmental elements (sunlight, air, water, soil).
• The Sun powers plant photosynthesis.
• Plants absorb rainwater and soil minerals to synthesize food and oxygen.
• Herbivores eat plants, and carnivores prey on herbivores.
• When organisms die, decomposers return nutrients back to the soil, sustaining the eternal cycle.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Definition of the ecological web connecting biotic and abiotic elements 1.5 Marks
Cyclical flow: Sun ➔ Plants ➔ Animals ➔ Decomposers ➔ Soil 1.5 Marks
Q2 What would happen to the Web of Life if all green trees on Earth were cut down? Impact of Disruption 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
If all green trees were destroyed:
1. Atmospheric oxygen would plummet while heat-trapping carbon dioxide would soar, causing runaway global heating.
2. Herbivorous animals would starve to death, causing carnivores to die out soon after.
3. Soil would wash away without tree roots to bind it, causing desertification and the total collapse of human civilization.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Oxygen/carbon dioxide collapse 1 Mark
Food chain starvation 1 Mark
Soil erosion and collapse of life 1 Mark
Q3 Why are humans not 'owners' of the Earth, but rather 'caretakers' or 'trustees'? Human Role as Stewards 2 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Humans are simply one thread woven into the vast web of life, completely dependent on clean air, fresh water, and plants for our very survival. As caretakers (custodians), it is our sacred responsibility to protect all species and pass down a healthy, vibrant planet to future generations.
