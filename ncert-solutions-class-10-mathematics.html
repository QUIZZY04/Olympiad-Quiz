Q1
Express each number as a product of its prime factors: (i) 140, (ii) 156, (iii) 3825, (iv) 5005, (v) 7429.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Concept Statement

According to the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, every composite number can be expressed (factorised) as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique, apart from the order in which the prime factors occur.

Step 2: Prime Factorisation Calculations

  1. (i) Express 140 as a product of prime factors:
    $$140 = 2 imes 70 = 2 imes 2 imes 35 = 2 imes 2 imes 5 imes 7 = 2^2 imes 5 imes 7$$ Conclusion: $140 = 2^2 imes 5^1 imes 7^1$.
  2. (ii) Express 156 as a product of prime factors:
    $$156 = 2 imes 78 = 2 imes 2 imes 39 = 2 imes 2 imes 3 imes 13 = 2^2 imes 3 imes 13$$ Conclusion: $156 = 2^2 imes 3^1 imes 13^1$.
  3. (iii) Express 3825 as a product of prime factors:
    Sum of digits = $3 + 8 + 2 + 5 = 18$ (divisible by 3).
    $$3825 = 3 imes 1275 = 3 imes 3 imes 425 = 3^2 imes 5 imes 85 = 3^2 imes 5 imes 5 imes 17 = 3^2 imes 5^2 imes 17$$ Conclusion: $3825 = 3^2 imes 5^2 imes 17^1$.
  4. (iv) Express 5005 as a product of prime factors:
    $$5005 = 5 imes 1001 = 5 imes 7 imes 143 = 5 imes 7 imes 11 imes 13$$ Conclusion: $5005 = 5 imes 7 imes 11 imes 13$.
  5. (v) Express 7429 as a product of prime factors:
    $$7429 = 17 imes 437 = 17 imes 19 imes 23$$ Conclusion: $7429 = 17 imes 19 imes 23$.

Q2
Find the LCM and HCF of the following pairs of integers and verify that LCM × HCF = product of the two numbers: (i) 26 and 91, (ii) 510 and 92.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Formula and Relationship Statement

For any two positive integers $a$ and $b$:

  • $ ext{HCF}(a, b) = ext{Product of the smallest power of each common prime factor involved in the numbers.}$
  • $ ext{LCM}(a, b) = ext{Product of the greatest power of each prime factor involved in the numbers.}$
  • Verification Formula: $ ext{HCF}(a, b) imes ext{LCM}(a, b) = a imes b$.

Step 2: Calculations and Verification

(i) For 26 and 91:

  • Prime factorisation of $26 = 2 imes 13$
  • Prime factorisation of $91 = 7 imes 13$
  • $ ext{HCF}(26, 91) = 13$
  • $ ext{LCM}(26, 91) = 2 imes 7 imes 13 = 182$
  • Verification:
    $ ext{LHS} = ext{HCF} imes ext{LCM} = 13 imes 182 = 2366$
    $ ext{RHS} = ext{Product of numbers} = 26 imes 91 = 2366$
    Since $ ext{LHS} = ext{RHS} = 2366$, the relationship is verified.

(ii) For 510 and 92:

  • Prime factorisation of $510 = 2 imes 3 imes 5 imes 17$
  • Prime factorisation of $92 = 2^2 imes 23$
  • $ ext{HCF}(510, 92) = 2^1 = 2$
  • $ ext{LCM}(510, 92) = 2^2 imes 3 imes 5 imes 17 imes 23 = 4 imes 15 imes 17 imes 23 = 23460$
  • Verification:
    $ ext{LHS} = ext{HCF} imes ext{LCM} = 2 imes 23460 = 46920$
    $ ext{RHS} = 510 imes 92 = 46920$
    Since $ ext{LHS} = ext{RHS} = 46920$, verified.

Q3
Prove that √5 is irrational.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Assumption (Method of Contradiction)

Let us assume, on the contrary, that $\sqrt{5}$ is a rational number.

Therefore, we can find coprime integers $a$ and $b$ (where $b eq 0$) such that:

$$\sqrt{5} = rac{a}{b}$$

where $a$ and $b$ have no common factor other than 1.

Step 2: Squaring and Divisibility Analysis

Cross-multiplying, we obtain:

$$a = \sqrt{5}b$$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$$a^2 = 5b^2 \quad ext{--- (Equation 1)}$$

This implies that $5$ divides $a^2$.

Theorem: If $p$ is a prime number and $p$ divides $a^2$, then $p$ divides $a$, where $a$ is a positive integer.
Therefore, $5$ divides $a$.

Step 3: Substitution and Second Divisibility

Since $5$ divides $a$, we can write $a = 5c$ for some integer $c$.

Substituting $a = 5c$ into Equation 1:

$$(5c)^2 = 5b^2$$ $$25c^2 = 5b^2 \implies b^2 = 5c^2$$

This means that $5$ divides $b^2$, and hence by the same theorem, $5$ divides $b$.

Step 4: Conclusion of Contradiction

From above, $5$ divides both $a$ and $b$. Therefore, $a$ and $b$ have at least $5$ as a common factor.
But this contradicts our initial assumption that $a$ and $b$ are coprime (having no common factor other than 1).
This contradiction has arisen because of our incorrect assumption that $\sqrt{5}$ is rational.
Hence, we conclude that $\sqrt{5}$ is an irrational number.

Q4
Prove that 3 + 2√5 is irrational.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Assumption

Let us assume, on the contrary, that $3 + 2\sqrt{5}$ is a rational number.
Then there exist coprime integers $a$ and $b$ ($b eq 0$) such that:

$$3 + 2\sqrt{5} = rac{a}{b}$$

Step 2: Rearranging to Isolate the Radical

Subtracting $3$ from both sides:

$$2\sqrt{5} = rac{a}{b} - 3 = rac{a - 3b}{b}$$

Dividing both sides by $2$:

$$\sqrt{5} = rac{a - 3b}{2b}$$

Step 3: Analysing Rationality

Since $a$ and $b$ are integers, $a - 3b$ is an integer and $2b$ is a non-zero integer.
Therefore, the quotient $ rac{a - 3b}{2b}$ is a rational number.
This implies that $\sqrt{5}$ is a rational number.

Step 4: Contradiction and Conclusion

But this contradicts the known fact that $\sqrt{5}$ is irrational.
This contradiction has arisen because of our incorrect assumption that $3 + 2\sqrt{5}$ is rational.
Hence, $3 + 2\sqrt{5}$ is an irrational number.

Q5
Explain why 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 and 7 × 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 + 5 are composite numbers.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Factorising the Given Expression

(i) For the number $7 imes 11 imes 13 + 13$:

  • Taking out the common factor $13$:
    $$7 imes 11 imes 13 + 13 = 13 imes (7 imes 11 + 1) = 13 imes (77 + 1) = 13 imes 78$$
  • Factorising $78$: $78 = 2 imes 3 imes 13$
    Therefore, the number can be expressed as: $$2 imes 3 imes 13 imes 13 = 2 imes 3 imes 13^2$$
  • Reason: Since this number has factors other than 1 and the number itself (namely 2, 3, 13), by definition of a composite number, it is a composite number.

(ii) For the number $7 imes 6 imes 5 imes 4 imes 3 imes 2 imes 1 + 5$:

  • Taking out the common factor $5$:
    $$5 imes (7 imes 6 imes 4 imes 3 imes 2 imes 1 + 1) = 5 imes (1008 + 1) = 5 imes 1009$$
  • Here, $1009$ cannot be factorised further (it is a prime number).
  • Reason: The given number has $5$ and $1009$ as its prime factors, meaning it has more than two factors (1, 5, 1009, and the number itself). By the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, any number that can be expressed as a product of two or more prime numbers is a composite number.
    Hence, both are composite numbers.

Competency Real-Life Word Problem
Question: There is a circular path around a sports field. Sonia takes 18 minutes to drive one round of the field, while Ravi takes 12 minutes for the same. Suppose they both start at the same point and at the same time, and go in the same direction. After how many minutes will they meet again at the starting point?

Step-by-step Solution:

  • Mathematical Formulation: The time after which Sonia and Ravi will meet again at the starting point will be the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of the times taken by each to complete one round, i.e., $\text{LCM}(18, 12)$.
  • Prime Factorisation:
    $$18 = 2 \times 3^2$$
    $$12 = 2^2 \times 3$$
  • Finding LCM:
    $$\text{LCM}(18, 12) = 2^2 \times 3^2 = 4 \times 9 = 36$$
  • Conclusion: Hence, Sonia and Ravi will meet again at the starting point after 36 minutes.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): The HCF of two numbers is 5 and their product is 150, then their LCM is 30.
Reason (R): For any two positive integers $a$ and $b$, $\text{HCF}(a, b) \times \text{LCM}(a, b) = a \times b$.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Board Solution:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Calculation:
Using $\text{HCF} \times \text{LCM} = \text{Product of numbers}$:
$$5 \times \text{LCM} = 150 \implies \text{LCM} = \frac{150}{5} = 30.$$
Hence, Assertion (A) is completely true and directly follows from the formula stated in Reason (R).
