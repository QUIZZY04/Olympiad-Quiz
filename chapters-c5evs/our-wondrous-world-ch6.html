Chapter 6: Some Unique Places

Investigating extraordinary ecological habitats: Ladakh's cold desert, Meghalaya's living root bridges, the Great Rann of Kutch, and Loktak Lake | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Ladakh (High-Altitude Cold Desert): Located over 3,000 metres above sea level in the rain-shadow of the Himalayas; features freezing arid climate, double-humped Bactrian camels, and Changpa nomads rearing Pashmina goats.
  • Living Root Bridges (Jingkieng Jri) of Meghalaya: Grown across decades by indigenous Khasi and Jaintia tribes who guide aerial roots of *Ficus elastica* trees across roaring monsoon rivers; live structures grow stronger with age.
  • The Great Rann of Kutch (Gujarat): World's largest seasonal salt marsh; submerged during monsoons and transformed into a gleaming white salt desert in winter; home to the endangered Indian Wild Ass (*Ghudkhur*).
  • Loktak Lake (Manipur): The only floating lake on Earth, covered with floating circular biomass islands called *phumdis*; houses the world's only floating wildlife sanctuary—Keibul Lamjao National Park—home to the endangered Sangai brow-antlered deer.
  • Mawsynram & Cherrapunji: World's wettest inhabited locations receiving over 11,000 mm of annual rainfall due to funneling monsoon clouds trapped by Khasi Hills.
  • Ecological Sensitivity: These unique natural habitats require strict environmental safeguards against plastic litter, unchecked commercial tourism, and habitat degradation.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Comparative Geography
Why is Ladakh described as a 'Cold Desert'? How does its climate differ from the hot Thar Desert of Rajasthan?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Why Ladakh is a Cold Desert:
  1. High Altitude Rain Shadow: Ladakh lies in the Himalayan rain shadow at an average elevation exceeding 3,000 metres. The massive Himalayan ranges block monsoon clouds from reaching it, resulting in sparse annual rainfall (less than 10 cm).
  2. Freezing Temperatures: Winter temperatures drop below -30°C, and rivers freeze solid. Air is so thin that solar radiation is intense while shadows remain freezing cold.
Differences with Thar Desert (Rajasthan):
  • Thar Desert: Characterized by scorching summer temperatures exceeding 48°C, sweeping sand dunes, and xerophytic thorny shrubs.
  • Ladakh Cold Desert: Characterized by sub-zero freezing temperatures, snow-capped rocky mountains, glaciers, and sparse alpine vegetation along stream beds.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Explanation of high altitude and Himalayan rain shadow effect 2 Marks
Clear contrast between freezing rocky desert and scorching sandy Thar desert 2 Marks
Q2 Indigenous Bio-Engineering
Explain how the Khasi and Jaintia tribes of Meghalaya create 'Living Root Bridges'. Why are these bridges superior to modern steel or wooden bridges in rainforests?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Creation of Living Root Bridges (Jingkieng Jri):
  1. The Khasi and Jaintia tribes guide the flexible, aerial secondary roots of the Ficus elastica (Indian rubber fig tree) across mountain river chasms using hollowed betel nut tree trunks.
  2. Over 15 to 30 years, the aerial roots reach the opposite bank and take root in the soil.
  3. Villagers weave and intertwine the living roots together with rocks and timber, forming sturdy suspended pathways across the gorges.
Why They are Superior in Rainforests:
  • Living Structures Never Rot: In Meghalaya's extremely humid climate (receiving 11,000+ mm rain), steel bridges rust and timber bridges rot quickly. Living root bridges remain alive and absorb water.
  • Strengthen with Age: As the tree grows and root trunks thicken, the bridge becomes sturdier over decades, supporting over 50 people simultaneously and surviving for centuries.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Step-by-step description of guiding Ficus elastica aerial roots 2 Marks
Scientific explanation of why living roots do not rot and strengthen over time 2 Marks
Q3 Ecological Sanctuaries
What makes Loktak Lake in Manipur globally unique? What are 'phumdis' and which endangered animal lives on them?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Unique Global Features of Loktak Lake:
  1. Loktak Lake is the largest freshwater lake in Northeast India and the world's only floating lake.
  2. What are Phumdis? Phumdis are thick, floating circular islands formed from a heterogeneous mass of soil, organic vegetation, and decomposed plant roots. Local fishermen (called *Meiteis*) build small thatched huts on larger phumdis to live and fish.
  3. Keibul Lamjao National Park: The southern part of the lake houses the world's only floating national park (spanning 40 sq km).
  4. Endangered Inhabitant: It is the exclusive natural home of the Sangai (Brow-antlered deer), fondly called the Dancing Deer of Manipur because it treads delicately on the spongy, floating phumdi vegetation.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of Loktak Lake as world's only floating lake 1 Mark
Accurate description of floating phumdis (soil and organic mats) 1.5 Marks
Keibul Lamjao National Park and endangered Sangai deer 1.5 Marks
Q4 Geomorphology & Wildlife
Describe the Great Rann of Kutch in Gujarat. How does it transition from a submerged sea into a white salt desert?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The Great Rann of Kutch in Gujarat is the world's largest seasonal salt marsh:
  1. Monsoon Inundation: During the summer southwest monsoon (July to September), sea water from the Arabian Sea rushes in, flooding the vast low-lying basin into a shallow saline lake.
  2. Winter Transformation into White Desert: As the rains stop and the scorching winter sun evaporates the shallow water, billions of tons of crystalline salt remain deposited across thousands of square kilometers, creating a dazzling, snow-white salt plain.
  3. Unique Wildlife: The Little Rann of Kutch is the last global refuge of the endangered Indian Wild Ass (Khur), and its shallow saline waters host nesting colonies of thousands of migratory pink Greater Flamingos.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Monsoon flooding from Arabian Sea explained 1 Mark
Evaporation leading to sparkling white salt crust 1 Mark
Identification of Indian Wild Ass and Flamingos 1 Mark
Q5 Animal Adaptations & Livelihood
Why are Changpa nomads of Ladakh deeply connected with the Pashmina goat? How does cold weather influence Pashmina wool?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The Changpas are a nomadic pastoral tribe who live in high-altitude Changthang plateau at heights of 4,500 to 5,000 metres in portable cone-shaped yak-hair tents called Rebo.

Connection with Pashmina Goats:
  • At extreme sub-zero temperatures (-40°C), the goats grow an exceptionally fine, warm undercoat of ultra-soft hair (underfleece) to protect their bodies from freezing.
  • This hair is six times thinner than a human hair and cannot be spun by machine. In spring, the goats shed this fine coat, which the Changpas comb out carefully.
  • Weavers in Kashmir hand-spin and hand-weave this fleece into world-renowned Pashmina shawls, providing the nomads with essential livelihood.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of Changpa tribe and high-altitude nomadic life 1 Mark
Biological adaptation of goat growing fine underfleece in freezing cold 1 Mark
Handwoven Pashmina shawl production and cultural trade 1 Mark
Q6 Meteorology & Topography
Why do Mawsynram and Cherrapunji receive the highest rainfall in the world? Explain the role of the Khasi Hills.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Mawsynram and Cherrapunji in Meghalaya receive over 11,000 mm of rain annually due to their unique funnel-shaped topography:
  1. Moisture-laden monsoon winds traveling north from the warm Bay of Bengal hit the sudden, steep vertical wall of the Khasi Hills.
  2. The hills act like a gigantic funnel, trapping the rain clouds in a deep canyon.
  3. Forced to rise rapidly to over 1,400 metres, the air cools rapidly, causing intense, continuous condensation and torrential orographic rainfall over these two towns.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Bay of Bengal monsoon winds identified 1 Mark
Funnel effect and barrier role of the steep Khasi Hills 1 Mark
Rapid orographic cooling resulting in world-record rainfall 1 Mark

Q8 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles explored in 'Some Unique Places', classify living organisms or resources according to their ecological adaptations and state the scientific reasoning behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Natural Ecosystems / Renewable Cycles): Organisms and resources that actively contribute to natural nutrient replenishment, such as soil microorganisms, pollinators, and freshwater bodies.
• Category 2 (Anthropogenic Impact / High-Consumption Zones): Human-made systems that alter natural habitats, requiring sustainable management, recycling, and conservation laws.
• Scientific Rationale: Understanding ecological interdependence prevents habitat fragmentation and ensures equitable resource sharing across all living species.

Q9 Community Living & Traditional Knowledge • Social Harmony
How does 'Some Unique Places' showcase the value of traditional indigenous knowledge, community water/soil management, or collaborative living? Provide two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Traditional Wisdom & Community Interdependence:
1. Indigenous Conservation Practices: Traditional Indian communities developed remarkable systems like stepwells (Bawris), rainwater harvesting ponds (Johads), and sacred groves that protected local ecosystems for centuries.
2. Social Interdependence: Sustainable resource management relies on shared community responsibility, where farmers, forest dwellers, and urban citizens protect shared commons together.
3. Concrete Examples: From water sharing traditions in arid regions to collective seed conservation banks, indigenous wisdom offers practical blueprints for modern climate resilience.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of traditional knowledge and stewardship 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting collective community heritage 1.5 M
Q10 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 5 student can lead at school and home inspired by the concepts of 'Some Unique Places'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Stewardship: Conduct an audit of leaking taps on the school campus, collect condensation water from air coolers to water garden beds, and practice mindful bucket bathing.
2. Waste Reduction & Composting: Segregate organic cafeteria peelings into a vermicompost pit and establish a 'zero single-use plastic' pledge in the classroom.
3. Habitat Creation: Install safe bird feeding platforms and shallow earthen water dishes on shaded tree branches, especially during hot summer afternoons.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Practical water conservation measure explained 1.0 M
Actionable waste segregation and composting system 1.0 M
Micro-habitat protection step for birds and urban wildlife 1.0 M
Q11 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze an environmental problem from 'Some Unique Places': If excessive groundwater depletion or forest deforestation threatens a local community, what comprehensive 3-step action plan should local authorities and citizens adopt?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Comprehensive Restoration Plan:
1. Strict Conservation Policy: Enforce groundwater recharge mandates for all buildings and strictly prohibit commercial tree cutting in ecological buffer zones.
2. Afforestation & Soil Moisture Retention: Plant indigenous drought-resistant tree species along slopes and waterways to prevent topsoil erosion and enhance the water table.
3. Mass Awareness & Citizen Monitoring: Empower youth eco-clubs to monitor local water levels and organize tree care circles to ensure 100% sapling survival.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate regulatory policy and preventive step 1.5 M
Long-term afforestation and community monitoring strategy 1.5 M
Q12 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate active empathy towards stray animals, local ecosystems, and vulnerable community members as emphasized in 'Some Unique Places'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Active Empathy in Everyday Life:
1. Compassion for Animals: Never tease, displace, or harm neighborhood animals; report injured birds or puppies to registered wildlife rescue groups.
2. Dignity of Labor & Social Respect: Treat sanitation staff, gardeners, and recycling workers with courtesy, ensuring wet and dry waste is hygienically separated.
3. Core Principle: Every organism plays an irreplaceable role in Earth's delicate balance. Living with humility and kindness creates a resilient, joyful world.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Compassionate treatment of animals and urban fauna 1.5 M
Respectful support towards essential community workers 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Eco-Tourism Guidelines for Fragile High-Altitude and Wetland Ecosystems

Booming tourism in Ladakh and Loktak Lake has resulted in diesel-spewing tourist vehicles, discarded single-use plastic water bottles in mountain streams, and motorboats disturbing nesting birds and wildlife.

Analytical Task: Formulate a code of conduct for responsible tourists visiting unique ecological wonders like Ladakh and Loktak Lake to preserve their delicate biodiversity.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Responsible Eco-Tourist Code of Conduct:
  1. Zero Single-Use Plastic Mandate: Carry a refillable copper/steel water bottle; use community water-filtration refilling stations instead of buying commercial disposable plastic bottles.
  2. Leave No Trace (Pack It In, Pack It Out): Carry all personal snack wrappers and waste back to major towns with recycling facilities rather than littering remote mountain passes or lake shores.
  3. Silent & Clean Transport: Restrict motorboat speeds on Loktak Lake near the Keibul Lamjao floating sanctuary; encourage electric vehicles and bicycles in Leh town.
  4. Respect Indigenous Lifestyles & Wildlife: Keep a safe distance from wildlife (Pashmina goats, Sangai deer, wild asses); never feed wild animals human packaged food.
  5. Support Local Communities: Stay in traditional village homestays and purchase authentic handmade crafts (like local apricot jam and handwoven woolens) directly from indigenous artisans.
Rubric: 2 Marks for waste and plastic prevention rules + 3 Marks for wildlife respect, noise limits, and sustainable local community homestay support = 5 Marks
