Chapter 18: No Place for Us?

Dam Construction Displacement, Jatrya Bhai's Story, Village Harmony vs Urban Slum Struggles | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Jatrya Bhai lived happily in Khedi village surrounded by lush green forests and the river, where community members shared farm work, songs, and food.
  • A large river dam was constructed, submerging Khedi village and forcing residents to leave their ancestral land.
  • Relocation struggles in Sinduri village: infertile rocky land, costly medical care, lack of schooling, and mockery from local residents.
  • Life in Mumbai slums: cramped, expensive tin-roof rooms, dirty gutters, daily wage exploitation, and loneliness.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Textbook Exercise
Describe the harmonious community life of Khedi village before the dam was built. How did villagers support one another?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
In Khedi village, people lived in peaceful harmony with nature:
  • Children learned together to dance, sing, play flute, make clay pots, and recognize birds and medicinal forest herbs.
  • Villagers worked together as one big family: during harvests or weddings, everyone pitched in, and elders resolved disputes lovingly without police or courts.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Learning from nature and forest knowledge 1.5 Marks
Community solidarity and mutual assistance in farming and festivals 1.5 Marks
Q2 Textbook Exercise
What are the advantages and disadvantages of constructing large river dams for development?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Advantages of Dams:
  1. Generates clean, renewable hydroelectric power.
  2. Provides irrigation canals to supply water to drought-prone farms.
  3. Helps control seasonal river flooding and supplies drinking water to distant cities.
Disadvantages & Human Costs:
  1. Massive submergence of fertile agricultural lands and ancient forest ecosystems.
  2. Forced displacement of indigenous villagers who lose their homes, livelihood, and community identity without fair compensation.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Listing 2-3 key benefits (hydroelectricity, irrigation, flood control) 2 Marks
Listing 2 severe drawbacks (displacement, environmental destruction) 2 Marks
Q3 Textbook Exercise
Describe the traumatic emotional and physical impact of forced displacement on tribal communities when dams or factories are built.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Forced displacement shatters the entire social, economic, and cultural fabric of tribal families:
  • Loss of Ancestral Heritage: Villagers leave behind sacred riverbanks, cremation grounds of ancestors, and ancient forests where their families lived for generations.
  • Economic Ruin: Independent farmers become landless casual day-labourers in cities, struggling to pay for water, electricity, food, and rent.
  • Cultural Alienation: Folk songs, herbal medicinal knowledge, and community solidarity disappear in crowded urban slums where neighbours do not know or trust each other.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Severance from ancestral land and sacred natural sites 1.5 Marks
Economic descent into landless wage exploitation in slums 1.5 Marks
Loss of indigenous culture, songs, and communal trust 1 Mark
Q4 Textbook Exercise
What fundamental facilities should the government ensure are fully established before relocating displaced families?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Before any relocation takes place, the government must provide:
  1. Fertile, irrigable agricultural land equal in productivity to what was submerged.
  2. Permanent pucca houses equipped with free potable water taps, electricity connections, and sanitation drainage.
  3. Proper government schools, primary health clinics, and accessible all-weather roads.
  4. Guaranteed rehabilitation employment or livelihood training for youth.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Fertile agricultural land and permanent pucca housing 1.5 Marks
Schools, healthcare, sanitation, and livelihood rehabilitation 1.5 Marks
Q5 Textbook Exercise
Compare the environmental sounds and air quality of Khedi village with the urban slums of Mumbai.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
  • Khedi Village: Clean, fresh mountain air smelling of wet earth and pine trees. Sounds of rushing river water, leaves rustling in the breeze, birds singing melodiously, and children laughing.
  • Mumbai Slums: Air polluted with vehicular exhaust smoke, foul stench from open drains, burning plastic, and deafening traffic horns, train whistles, and factory sirens.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Sensory description of Khedi's pure air and natural sounds 1.5 Marks
Sensory description of urban slum pollution, stench, and noise 1.5 Marks
Q6 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'No Place for Us?', describe how students can conduct an experiential field study in their neighborhood or school eco-garden. What three primary ecological parameters should they document in their field log?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Field Observation Protocol:
1. Direct Inquiry: Students observe the local ecosystem, habitat structures, and environmental interactions linked to No Place for Us? during a guided outdoor trail.
2. Three Field Log Parameters:
• (a) Flora and fauna interactions (sensory adaptations, feeding habits, or nesting locations).
• (b) Environmental conditions (ambient temperature, soil moisture, sunlight exposure, and freshwater access).
• (c) Human footprint (waste disposal practices, water conservation structures, or tree cover density).
3. Conclusion: Maintaining a regular nature journal sharpens analytical thinking and nurtures an enduring respect for biodiversity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Observation methodology clearly outlined 1.5 M
Three tangible field parameters detailed 1.5 M
Q7 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles explored in 'No Place for Us?', classify living organisms or resources according to their ecological adaptations and state the scientific reasoning behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Natural Ecosystems / Renewable Cycles): Organisms and resources that actively contribute to natural nutrient replenishment, such as soil microorganisms, pollinators, and freshwater bodies.
• Category 2 (Anthropogenic Impact / High-Consumption Zones): Human-made systems that alter natural habitats, requiring sustainable management, recycling, and conservation laws.
• Scientific Rationale: Understanding ecological interdependence prevents habitat fragmentation and ensures equitable resource sharing across all living species.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q8 Community Living & Traditional Knowledge • Social Harmony
How does 'No Place for Us?' showcase the value of traditional indigenous knowledge, community water/soil management, or collaborative living? Provide two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Traditional Wisdom & Community Interdependence:
1. Indigenous Conservation Practices: Traditional Indian communities developed remarkable systems like stepwells (Bawris), rainwater harvesting ponds (Johads), and sacred groves that protected local ecosystems for centuries.
2. Social Interdependence: Sustainable resource management relies on shared community responsibility, where farmers, forest dwellers, and urban citizens protect shared commons together.
3. Concrete Examples: From water sharing traditions in arid regions to collective seed conservation banks, indigenous wisdom offers practical blueprints for modern climate resilience.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of traditional knowledge and stewardship 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting collective community heritage 1.5 M
Q9 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 5 student can lead at school and home inspired by the concepts of 'No Place for Us?'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Stewardship: Conduct an audit of leaking taps on the school campus, collect condensation water from air coolers to water garden beds, and practice mindful bucket bathing.
2. Waste Reduction & Composting: Segregate organic cafeteria peelings into a vermicompost pit and establish a 'zero single-use plastic' pledge in the classroom.
3. Habitat Creation: Install safe bird feeding platforms and shallow earthen water dishes on shaded tree branches, especially during hot summer afternoons.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Practical water conservation measure explained 1.0 M
Actionable waste segregation and composting system 1.0 M
Micro-habitat protection step for birds and urban wildlife 1.0 M
Q10 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze an environmental problem from 'No Place for Us?': If excessive groundwater depletion or forest deforestation threatens a local community, what comprehensive 3-step action plan should local authorities and citizens adopt?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Comprehensive Restoration Plan:
1. Strict Conservation Policy: Enforce groundwater recharge mandates for all buildings and strictly prohibit commercial tree cutting in ecological buffer zones.
2. Afforestation & Soil Moisture Retention: Plant indigenous drought-resistant tree species along slopes and waterways to prevent topsoil erosion and enhance the water table.
3. Mass Awareness & Citizen Monitoring: Empower youth eco-clubs to monitor local water levels and organize tree care circles to ensure 100% sapling survival.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate regulatory policy and preventive step 1.5 M
Long-term afforestation and community monitoring strategy 1.5 M
Q11 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate active empathy towards stray animals, local ecosystems, and vulnerable community members as emphasized in 'No Place for Us?'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Active Empathy in Everyday Life:
1. Compassion for Animals: Never tease, displace, or harm neighborhood animals; report injured birds or puppies to registered wildlife rescue groups.
2. Dignity of Labor & Social Respect: Treat sanitation staff, gardeners, and recycling workers with courtesy, ensuring wet and dry waste is hygienically separated.
3. Core Principle: Every organism plays an irreplaceable role in Earth's delicate balance. Living with humility and kindness creates a resilient, joyful world.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Compassionate treatment of animals and urban fauna 1.5 M
Respectful support towards essential community workers 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Tehri Dam Construction & The Displacement of Khedi Village

Jatrya Bhai's peaceful riverbank village of Khedi was submerged under a massive reservoir created by a hydroelectric dam. Although promised agricultural land and modern schools in Sinduri, the displaced villagers received barren stony plots, expensive electricity bills, and hostility in Mumbai.

Analytical Task: Analyze the ethical, social, and economic consequences of large development projects like dams on indigenous rural populations.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Consequences of Forced Displacement: 1. Loss of Culture and Identity: Villages like Khedi lived in harmony with rivers and forests for centuries. Uprooting them destroys traditional songs, folk knowledge, and community solidarity. 2. Flawed Rehabilitation Promises: Displaced families are often allotted unfertile rocky land without functioning schools, hospitals, or irrigation, sinking them into immediate poverty. 3. Urban Alienation and Poverty: When forced to migrate to mega-cities like Mumbai, skilled rural farmers become landless labourers living in cramped, unhygienic slums, struggling to afford basic drinking water and electricity. Policy Imperative: Development projects must obtain informed consent, provide genuine fertile land compensation, and ensure displaced communities receive direct shares in generated electricity and profits.
Rubric: 2 Marks for loss of community culture + 2 Marks for rehabilitation failures + 1 Mark for equitable policy recommendation = 5 Marks
