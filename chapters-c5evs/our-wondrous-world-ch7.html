Chapter 7: Energy — How Things Work

Understanding energy forms, renewable vs fossil sources, everyday mechanical transformations, and sustainable conservation | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Definition of Energy: Energy is the capacity or ability to do work; it exists in many forms including solar, thermal, mechanical, chemical, electrical, and kinetic energy.
  • The Sun as Primary Energy Source: Sunlight powers plant photosynthesis, drives wind circulation, and evaporates water in the hydrological cycle, making the Sun the ultimate driver of Earth's energy.
  • Renewable Energy: Inexhaustible natural sources that regenerate quickly and do not emit greenhouse gases—Solar (photovoltaic panels), Wind (turbines), Hydroelectric (flowing water), and Biogas.
  • Non-Renewable Fossil Fuels: Coal, petroleum (petrol, diesel), and natural gas formed over millions of years from buried prehistoric organisms; exhaustible and causes air pollution and global warming.
  • Energy Transformations in Everyday Machines: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed (e.g. Electric iron: electrical to thermal; Fan: electrical to kinetic; Battery: chemical to electrical).
  • Simple Machines: Force multipliers and directional tools that make mechanical work easier—inclined plane, lever, pulley, screw, wheel and axle, and wedge.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Energy Fundamentals
What is the key scientific difference between renewable and non-renewable energy sources? Give three examples of each.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Definitions and Differences:
  • Renewable Energy: Energy derived from inexhaustible natural cycles and resources that replenish naturally on a human timescale, producing negligible air pollution and greenhouse gases.
    Examples: Solar energy (Sun), Wind energy (Windmills), and Hydroelectric energy (Flowing river water).
  • Non-Renewable Energy: Energy derived from finite geological reserves formed over millions of years inside Earth's crust. Once consumed, they cannot be replaced in a human lifetime, and burning them releases toxic smoke and carbon dioxide.
    Examples: Coal, Petrol (Crude oil / Petroleum), and Natural Gas (CNG/LPG).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Renewable energy definition and 3 examples 2 Marks
Non-renewable energy definition and 3 examples 2 Marks
Q2 Scientific Reasoning
Why is the Sun recognized as the primary or ultimate source of almost all energy on planet Earth?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The Sun is the ultimate source of Earth's energy because:
  1. Powers Plant Photosynthesis: Sunlight enables green plants to synthesize glucose and oxygen. All herbivores, carnivores, and humans depend on this plant biomass for chemical food energy.
  2. Origin of Fossil Fuels: Coal and crude oil are the compressed, fossilized remains of ancient plants and prehistoric organisms that originally harnessed sunlight millions of years ago.
  3. Drives Natural Weather Cycles: Unequal solar heating of Earth's surface creates wind currents that power wind turbines, while solar evaporation drives the rain cycle that fills hydroelectric river reservoirs.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Photosynthesis and food web dependence 1 Mark
Solar origin of fossil fuels explained 1 Mark
Driver of wind and water cycles 1 Mark
Q3 Energy Transformations
Identify the energy transformation taking place in each of the following everyday devices: (a) Electric fan, (b) Toaster, (c) Solar street light, and (d) Torch battery.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Energy transformation in each device:
  1. Electric Fan: Electrical Energy → Kinetic (Mechanical) Energy of rotating blades, which moves air.
  2. Electric Toaster: Electrical Energy → Thermal (Heat) Energy and Light Energy that toasts bread slices.
  3. Solar Street Light: Solar (Light) Energy → Chemical Energy stored in lithium batteries during the day → Electrical Energy → Light Energy emitted by LED lamps at night.
  4. Torch Battery (Dry Cell): Chemical Energy stored inside battery pastes → Electrical Energy → Light and Heat Energy in the bulb.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Electric fan: electrical to kinetic 1 Mark
Electric toaster: electrical to thermal/heat 1 Mark
Solar street light: solar to chemical to electrical/light 1 Mark
Torch battery: chemical to electrical/light 1 Mark
Q4 Hands-on Activity
Hands-on Activity: Describe how to build a simple paper pinwheel (turbine) to demonstrate that wind carries kinetic energy.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Pinwheel (Wind Turbine) Demonstration:
  • Materials: A square sheet of colored craft paper (15 cm × 15 cm), scissors, a push-pin, a wooden pencil with an eraser top, and a small plastic bead.
  • Procedure:
    1. Draw two diagonal lines across the square sheet from opposite corners, meeting at the center point.
    2. Cut along the four diagonal lines with scissors, stopping about 1.5 cm before reaching the center point.
    3. Fold every alternating tip (every second corner) into the center without creasing the folds.
    4. Push the pin through the folded tips and the center, slide on the bead (to reduce friction), and push the pin firmly into the pencil eraser.
    5. Blow gently on the paper blades or run holding the pencil forward.
  • Scientific Observation: The pinwheel spins rapidly. The kinetic energy of moving air pushes against the angled paper vanes, creating rotational mechanical motion just like an industrial wind turbine.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
List of materials and clear construction steps 2 Marks
Explanation of moving air transferring kinetic energy to rotate blades 2 Marks
Q5 Mechanical Principles
What are 'Simple Machines'? Name four types of simple machines found in homes and explain how one of them makes work easier.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
A Simple Machine is a mechanical device with few or no moving parts that modifies the direction or magnitude of a force, making physical tasks easier.

Four Types Found in Homes:
  1. Inclined Plane: A sloping ramp used to roll wheelchairs or heavy gas cylinders into vehicles.
  2. Lever: A seesaw, bottle opener, scissors, or crowbar that pivots on a fixed fulcrum.
  3. Pulley: A grooved wheel with a rope used to draw water from wells or raise window blinds.
  4. Wheel and Axle: A door knob, screwdriver, or bicycle pedal.
How a Lever Makes Work Easier: Using a long lever (like a bottle opener), a small input effort applied over a longer distance creates a powerful upward force at the short end, easily popping open a tight metal bottle cap with minimal effort.

Definition of simple machines as force modifiers 1 Mark
Four everyday examples identified 1 Mark
Explanation of leverage reducing input effort 1 Mark
Q6 Energy Conservation
Suggest five practical habits that a family can adopt to conserve electricity and fuel in daily life.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Five Everyday Energy Conservation Habits:
  1. Switch to Energy-Efficient LED Lighting: Replace outdated incandescent bulbs with 5-star Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) rated LED bulbs that consume 80% less electricity.
  2. Turn Off Idling Vehicle Engines: Switch off scooter or car engines at traffic signals when red lights exceed 20 seconds to save fuel and reduce urban exhaust smoke.
  3. Maximize Natural Daylight: Open window curtains during the daytime to illuminate study desks with natural sunlight instead of turning on electric lamps.
  4. Unplug Standby Electronics: Switch off television sets, phone chargers, and computers at the wall switch when not in use, preventing 'phantom energy' drain.
  5. Carpool & Use Public Transit: Walk or ride bicycles for short neighborhood grocery trips, and use school buses or metro trains instead of individual private cars.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Electricity conservation habits (LEDs, daylight, wall switches) 1.5 Marks
Fuel conservation habits (engine idling, carpooling/cycling) 1.5 Marks

Q8 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles explored in 'Energy — How Things Work', classify living organisms or resources according to their ecological adaptations and state the scientific reasoning behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Natural Ecosystems / Renewable Cycles): Organisms and resources that actively contribute to natural nutrient replenishment, such as soil microorganisms, pollinators, and freshwater bodies.
• Category 2 (Anthropogenic Impact / High-Consumption Zones): Human-made systems that alter natural habitats, requiring sustainable management, recycling, and conservation laws.
• Scientific Rationale: Understanding ecological interdependence prevents habitat fragmentation and ensures equitable resource sharing across all living species.

Q9 Community Living & Traditional Knowledge • Social Harmony
How does 'Energy — How Things Work' showcase the value of traditional indigenous knowledge, community water/soil management, or collaborative living? Provide two concrete examples.

Q10 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 5 student can lead at school and home inspired by the concepts of 'Energy — How Things Work'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Stewardship: Conduct an audit of leaking taps on the school campus, collect condensation water from air coolers to water garden beds, and practice mindful bucket bathing.
2. Waste Reduction & Composting: Segregate organic cafeteria peelings into a vermicompost pit and establish a 'zero single-use plastic' pledge in the classroom.
3. Habitat Creation: Install safe bird feeding platforms and shallow earthen water dishes on shaded tree branches, especially during hot summer afternoons.

Practical water conservation measure explained 1.0 M
Actionable waste segregation and composting system 1.0 M
Micro-habitat protection step for birds and urban wildlife 1.0 M
Q11 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze an environmental problem from 'Energy — How Things Work': If excessive groundwater depletion or forest deforestation threatens a local community, what comprehensive 3-step action plan should local authorities and citizens adopt?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Comprehensive Restoration Plan:
1. Strict Conservation Policy: Enforce groundwater recharge mandates for all buildings and strictly prohibit commercial tree cutting in ecological buffer zones.
2. Afforestation & Soil Moisture Retention: Plant indigenous drought-resistant tree species along slopes and waterways to prevent topsoil erosion and enhance the water table.
3. Mass Awareness & Citizen Monitoring: Empower youth eco-clubs to monitor local water levels and organize tree care circles to ensure 100% sapling survival.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate regulatory policy and preventive step 1.5 M
Long-term afforestation and community monitoring strategy 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

School Energy Audit & Rooftop Solar Transition Blueprint

During a campus sustainability meeting, the electricity meter showed that the school consumed 1,800 units of grid electricity monthly, incurring high utility bills while coal-fired power plants generated this power 200 km away.

Analytical Task: Develop an energy-saving blueprint for the school, detailing immediate zero-cost behavioural changes and a proposal for installing a 10 kW grid-tied rooftop solar photovoltaic system.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
School Energy Transition & Solar Blueprint:
  1. Phase 1: Zero-Cost Behavioral Measures (Immediate 15% Reduction):
    • Designate a student 'Green Monitor' in every section responsible for turning off fans and lights during lunch breaks and outdoor games.
    • Clean dust off ceiling fan blades and window panes quarterly to maximize ventilation and natural illumination.
    • Set air conditioner thermostats in teacher staffrooms to a mandatory 24°C–25°C instead of power-hungry 18°C.
  2. Phase 2: 10 kW Rooftop Solar PV System:
    • Installation: Mount 25 polycrystalline solar panels on the unshaded cafeteria flat roof.
    • Inverter & Net Metering: Solar panels convert sunlight into DC electricity, which an inverter converts into AC power to run classroom lights and computers. A bidirectional 'Net Meter' sends excess electricity generated on Sundays back to the government grid for credit.
    • Environmental Payback: The 10 kW system generates ~40 units of clean electricity daily, offsetting approximately 12 tonnes of carbon dioxide emissions annually.
Rubric: 2 Marks for behavioral zero-cost conservation steps + 3 Marks for technical solar PV net-metering proposal and carbon reduction metrics = 5 Marks
