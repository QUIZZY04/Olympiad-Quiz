Chapter 9: Up You Go!

Mountaineering at NIM Uttarkashi, Group Leader Duties, Bachendri Pal & Mountain Equipment | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Nehru Institute of Mountaineering (NIM) in Uttarkashi trains mountaineers in rock climbing and survival under leader Brigadier Gyan Singh.
  • Responsibilities of a group leader: help others carry luggage, let the group go ahead and walk at the back, find good camping spots, and care for sick members.
  • Mountaineering tools: pitons (metal spikes), carabiners (metal clips), ropes, ice axes, crampons, and rucksacks.
  • Bachendri Pal became the first Indian woman (and 5th in the world) to climb Mount Everest (Sagarmatha) on 23 May 1984.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Textbook Exercise
What are the key responsibilities of a group leader in a mountaineering expedition?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
A group leader has several critical responsibilities:
  • Help group members carry their heavy rucksacks if they are struggling.
  • Let the entire team go ahead and walk last to ensure nobody is left behind.
  • Look for suitable places to rest and pitch tents.
  • Take care of anyone who falls sick and keep team spirits high even when tired.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Assisting team members and walking last 1.5 Marks
Camping site selection and caring for sick members 1.5 Marks
Q2 Textbook Exercise
Who is Bachendri Pal? Describe her historic climb to Mount Everest on 23 May 1984.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Bachendri Pal was born in Nakuri village, Garhwal (Uttarakhand). She trained at the Nehru Institute of Mountaineering and was selected for the 1984 Everest expedition:
  • On the night of 15 May, an avalanche buried her camp in snow; she was rescued by teammates.
  • On 23 May 1984 at 1:07 PM, Bachendri stepped onto the 8,848-metre-high summit of Mount Everest (known as Sagarmatha in Nepal).
  • She spent 43 minutes on the summit, planted the Indian National Tricolour flag, and bowed in prayer, becoming the first Indian woman to conquer Everest.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Background and avalanche survival 1.5 Marks
Summit timestamp (23 May 1984) and national flag hoisting 1.5 Marks
First Indian woman milestone achievement 1 Mark
Q3 Textbook Exercise
Why is high-calorie food (like vitamin C tablets, iron tablets, hot chocolate milk) provided to mountaineers every morning?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
At high altitudes in snowy mountains:
  • The body burns immense energy carrying heavy rucksacks against steep gravitational inclines and freezing temperatures.
  • Vitamin C boosts immunity against mountain colds, iron tablets stimulate red blood cell and haemoglobin production for better oxygen transport, and hot chocolate milk provides rapid carbohydrates and keeps the core body warm.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immense energy expenditure in freezing conditions 1.5 Marks
Specific nutritional role of Vitamin C, Iron, and hot chocolate 1.5 Marks
Q4 Textbook Exercise
What is a 'Tyrolean traverse' and what specialized gear (carabiners, pitons, slings) is required to cross an icy mountain river safely?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
A Tyrolean traverse is a mountaineering method used to cross rapid, icy mountain torrents without stepping into the freezing water:
  • A thick, high-tensile climbing rope is stretched tightly across the river bank and anchored securely to rock walls using forged steel spikes called pitons.
  • The climber wears a sturdy safety harness sling around their waist.
  • A metal spring-loaded clip ring called a carabiner hooks the climber's harness directly to the horizontal rope.
  • The climber pulls themselves hand-over-hand along the suspended rope to cross over to the opposite bank safely.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Definition of rope crossing over turbulent mountain river 1.5 Marks
Function of pitons (anchors), carabiner (locking clip), and harness 2.5 Marks
Q5 Textbook Exercise
What is mountain sickness (altitude sickness)? Why do climbers carry oxygen cylinders to high mountain peaks?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
As climbers ascend above 3,000 metres:
  • Atmospheric air pressure drops and the concentration of oxygen molecules decreases drastically (thin air).
  • Climbers experience altitude sickness: breathlessness, nausea, intense dizziness, headache, and extreme fatigue.
  • To survive in the 'Death Zone' (above 8,000 metres where oxygen is insufficient to sustain life), climbers carry portable pressurized oxygen cylinders fitted with breathing masks.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Explanation of low air pressure and thin oxygen 1.5 Marks
Symptoms and necessity of supplementary oxygen tanks 1.5 Marks
Q6 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'Up You Go!', describe how students can conduct an experiential field study in their neighborhood or school eco-garden. What three primary ecological parameters should they document in their field log?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Field Observation Protocol:
1. Direct Inquiry: Students observe the local ecosystem, habitat structures, and environmental interactions linked to Up You Go! during a guided outdoor trail.
2. Three Field Log Parameters:
• (a) Flora and fauna interactions (sensory adaptations, feeding habits, or nesting locations).
• (b) Environmental conditions (ambient temperature, soil moisture, sunlight exposure, and freshwater access).
• (c) Human footprint (waste disposal practices, water conservation structures, or tree cover density).
3. Conclusion: Maintaining a regular nature journal sharpens analytical thinking and nurtures an enduring respect for biodiversity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Observation methodology clearly outlined 1.5 M
Three tangible field parameters detailed 1.5 M
Q7 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles explored in 'Up You Go!', classify living organisms or resources according to their ecological adaptations and state the scientific reasoning behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Natural Ecosystems / Renewable Cycles): Organisms and resources that actively contribute to natural nutrient replenishment, such as soil microorganisms, pollinators, and freshwater bodies.
• Category 2 (Anthropogenic Impact / High-Consumption Zones): Human-made systems that alter natural habitats, requiring sustainable management, recycling, and conservation laws.
• Scientific Rationale: Understanding ecological interdependence prevents habitat fragmentation and ensures equitable resource sharing across all living species.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q8 Community Living & Traditional Knowledge • Social Harmony
How does 'Up You Go!' showcase the value of traditional indigenous knowledge, community water/soil management, or collaborative living? Provide two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Traditional Wisdom & Community Interdependence:
1. Indigenous Conservation Practices: Traditional Indian communities developed remarkable systems like stepwells (Bawris), rainwater harvesting ponds (Johads), and sacred groves that protected local ecosystems for centuries.
2. Social Interdependence: Sustainable resource management relies on shared community responsibility, where farmers, forest dwellers, and urban citizens protect shared commons together.
3. Concrete Examples: From water sharing traditions in arid regions to collective seed conservation banks, indigenous wisdom offers practical blueprints for modern climate resilience.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of traditional knowledge and stewardship 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting collective community heritage 1.5 M
Q9 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 5 student can lead at school and home inspired by the concepts of 'Up You Go!'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Stewardship: Conduct an audit of leaking taps on the school campus, collect condensation water from air coolers to water garden beds, and practice mindful bucket bathing.
2. Waste Reduction & Composting: Segregate organic cafeteria peelings into a vermicompost pit and establish a 'zero single-use plastic' pledge in the classroom.
3. Habitat Creation: Install safe bird feeding platforms and shallow earthen water dishes on shaded tree branches, especially during hot summer afternoons.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Practical water conservation measure explained 1.0 M
Actionable waste segregation and composting system 1.0 M
Micro-habitat protection step for birds and urban wildlife 1.0 M
Q10 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze an environmental problem from 'Up You Go!': If excessive groundwater depletion or forest deforestation threatens a local community, what comprehensive 3-step action plan should local authorities and citizens adopt?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Comprehensive Restoration Plan:
1. Strict Conservation Policy: Enforce groundwater recharge mandates for all buildings and strictly prohibit commercial tree cutting in ecological buffer zones.
2. Afforestation & Soil Moisture Retention: Plant indigenous drought-resistant tree species along slopes and waterways to prevent topsoil erosion and enhance the water table.
3. Mass Awareness & Citizen Monitoring: Empower youth eco-clubs to monitor local water levels and organize tree care circles to ensure 100% sapling survival.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate regulatory policy and preventive step 1.5 M
Long-term afforestation and community monitoring strategy 1.5 M
Q11 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate active empathy towards stray animals, local ecosystems, and vulnerable community members as emphasized in 'Up You Go!'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Active Empathy in Everyday Life:
1. Compassion for Animals: Never tease, displace, or harm neighborhood animals; report injured birds or puppies to registered wildlife rescue groups.
2. Dignity of Labor & Social Respect: Treat sanitation staff, gardeners, and recycling workers with courtesy, ensuring wet and dry waste is hygienically separated.
3. Core Principle: Every organism plays an irreplaceable role in Earth's delicate balance. Living with humility and kindness creates a resilient, joyful world.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Compassionate treatment of animals and urban fauna 1.5 M
Respectful support towards essential community workers 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Brigadier Gyan Singh's Camp & Dealing with Blizzards

During a harsh winter expedition in the Himalayas, a blizzard hit Camp 4 at 21,000 feet. Temperatures plummeted to -20°C, and two members developed severe hypothermia. As group leader, Sangeeta had to coordinate emergency shelters, warmth, and evacuation.

Analytical Task: What crucial leadership decisions should a mountaineering leader take during extreme weather emergencies to protect team lives?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
A mountaineering leader must prioritize human safety over conquering the summit: 1. Maintain Calm and Morale: Avoid panic, gather all members, and keep everyone talking to prevent hypothermia drowsiness. 2. Secure Immediate Shelter: Anchor two-layer nylon tents firmly using pitons and rocks to resist howling blizzard winds. 3. Immediate First Aid for Hypothermia: Remove wet clothing, wrap victims in dry sleeping bags, share body warmth, and provide hot energy drinks (tea with glucose/chocolate). 4. Timely Retreat Call: A courageous leader knows when to abort an ascent and safely guide members back down to lower base camp before oxygen supplies run out.
Rubric: 2 Marks for emergency shelter and hypothermia response + 3 Marks for leadership qualities and timely retreat decision = 5 Marks
