Chapter 2: Journey of a River

Following river courses from snowy glacial sources down fertile plains to ocean deltas, studying ecosystems and river conservation | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • River Course Stages: A river journeys across three distinct stages—Upper Course (steep mountain origin, rapid flow, V-shaped valleys, waterfalls), Middle Course (gentle gradient, meanders, wide valleys, human settlements), and Lower Course (slow flow, sediment deposition, delta formation, meeting the sea).
  • Perennial vs Seasonal Rivers: Perennial rivers (Ganga, Yamuna, Brahmaputra) are fed year-round by melting Himalayan glaciers and monsoons; seasonal rivers (Godavari, Krishna, Narmada) rely primarily on monsoon rainfall.
  • River Ecosystems: Rivers provide freshwater habitats for fish, freshwater turtles, aquatic plants, and endangered species like the Gangetic Dolphin (*Platanista gangetica*).
  • Historical & Agricultural Importance: River floodplains deposit nutrient-rich alluvial silt, creating India's most fertile agricultural belts that supported ancient civilizations.
  • River Pollution Drivers: Municipal untreated sewage, industrial chemical effluents, washing clothes with synthetic detergents, and plastic trash threaten river health.
  • Conservation Programs: Initiatives like the Namami Gange Project, constructing Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs), and community riverbank tree planting (riparian buffers) help restore river cleanliness.

Q1 Physical Geography
Trace the three distinct stages of a river from its mountain source to the sea. How does the river's speed and shape change?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
A river journeys through three distinct geographical stages:
  1. The Upper Course (Mountain Stage): The river originates high in mountains from melting glaciers or rain-fed springs. The terrain is steep, causing the river to flow rapidly with high erosive power, cutting steep V-shaped valleys and cascading down waterfalls.
  2. The Middle Course (Valley & Plains Stage): As the river enters flat plains, the land gradient decreases. The river widens, slows down, and swings from side to side in wide loops called meanders. Tributaries join it, increasing water volume.
  3. The Lower Course (Delta & Mouth Stage): Near its journey's end, the river flows very sluggishly. It deposits heavy sediments (silt and sand), splitting into multiple smaller channels (distributaries) to form a triangular delta before merging into the sea or ocean.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Upper course: steep gradient, rapid flow, V-shaped valleys 1.5 Marks
Middle course: flat terrain, meanders, human settlements 1.5 Marks
Lower course: sluggish flow, silt deposition, delta formation 1 Mark
Q2 Geographical Terminology
What is the difference between a tributary and a distributary? Give one Indian example of each.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Definitions and Differences:
  • Tributary: A smaller stream or river that flows into and joins a larger parent river, increasing its total volume.
    Example: River Yamuna is the largest tributary of River Ganga (joining at Prayagraj).
  • Distributary: A channel that branches off from the main river near its mouth in a delta and carries water away toward the sea without rejoining the main stream.
    Example: River Hooghly in West Bengal is a major distributary branching off from River Ganga.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate definition and role of a tributary + Indian example 1.5 Marks
Accurate definition and role of a distributary + Indian example 1.5 Marks
Q3 Historical & Environmental Insight
Why are river basins historically known as the 'cradles of human civilization'? State three reasons.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Ancient human civilizations—such as the Indus Valley Civilization along the Indus River and Mesopotamian civilization along the Tigris and Euphrates—developed around river basins because:
  1. Abundant Freshwater: Rivers provided a perennial source of freshwater for drinking, cooking, livestock, and domestic needs.
  2. Fertile Alluvial Soil: Seasonal river floods deposited fresh silt on surrounding plains, creating nutrient-rich farmland ideal for growing food crops.
  3. Natural Transportation & Trade: Boats on rivers served as the fastest and easiest trade routes to transport heavy goods before roads were built.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Explanation of freshwater access for human settlements 1 Mark
Alluvial soil deposition for agricultural productivity 1 Mark
Transport and trade route connectivity 1 Mark

Q5 Map & Spatial Skill
Map Skill Task: Name two major rivers flowing into the Bay of Bengal and two major rivers flowing into the Arabian Sea.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Based on India's physical geography and drainage patterns:
  1. Rivers Flowing Eastward into the Bay of Bengal:
    • River Ganga: Flows east through Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and West Bengal before emptying into the Bay of Bengal.
    • River Godavari: Originates in Maharashtra and flows across Telangana and Andhra Pradesh into the Bay of Bengal.
    • (Additional: Krishna, Mahanadi, Brahmaputra, Kaveri).
  2. Rivers Flowing Westward into the Arabian Sea:
    • River Narmada: Flows westward through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges into the Gulf of Khambhat (Arabian Sea).
    • River Tapi (Tapti): Flows westward parallel to Narmada through Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Gujarat into the Arabian Sea.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Two accurate east-flowing Bay of Bengal rivers 2 Marks
Two accurate west-flowing Arabian Sea rivers (Narmada & Tapi) 2 Marks
Q6 Biodiversity & Ecology
What is the Gangetic River Dolphin, and why is its presence considered an indicator of a healthy river ecosystem?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The Gangetic River Dolphin (Platanista gangetica) is India's National Aquatic Animal, living exclusively in freshwater river stretches of the Ganga and Brahmaputra basins.

Significance as an Ecosystem Indicator:
  1. These dolphins are almost completely blind and navigate and hunt fish using echolocation (sonar clicks).
  2. They can survive only in deep, clean, unpolluted freshwater with abundant fish and high dissolved oxygen.
  3. Therefore, their presence in a river stretch proves that the aquatic food chain is healthy and chemical pollution is low. When river water becomes polluted or silty, dolphins migrate away or die out.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of Gangetic dolphin as National Aquatic Animal 1 Mark
Explanation of reliance on clean water and echolocation 1 Mark
Role as biological indicator of river health 1 Mark

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Community Action Plan for Rejuvenating Polluted Urban Riverfronts

During a field trip to a city riverbank, Class 5 students noticed piles of plastic bottles, rotting floral offerings, and a municipal open storm drain pouring foamy grey wastewater directly into the river water where people were bathing.

Analytical Task: Propose a 4-point student-led action plan to protect and restore this urban riverfront, combining community awareness with scientific engineering solutions.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Student-Led Urban River Rejuvenation Action Plan:
  1. Installation of Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs): Petitions submitted to municipal authorities demanding that all urban storm drains pass through bio-filters and decentralized STPs before discharging treated effluent into the river.
  2. Riverfront Trash Traps & Booms: Installing floating mesh booms across drainage mouths to physically trap plastic bottles, polythene, and floating trash before they enter the main river current.
  3. Dedicated Eco-Friendly Immersion Ponds: Constructing designated artificial immersion tanks near riverbanks for religious ceremonies and flowers, preventing toxic chemical paints from contaminating the natural stream.
  4. Riparian Vegetative Bio-Shield: Mobilizing local schools and citizen groups to plant native riverside trees (like Neem, Peepal, Arjun, and Vetiver grass) along the embankments to prevent soil erosion and naturally absorb excess chemical nitrates.
Rubric: 2 Marks for engineering solutions (STPs & trash booms) + 3 Marks for community engagement, dedicated tanks, and riparian vegetative bio-shields = 5 Marks
