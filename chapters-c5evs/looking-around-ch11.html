Chapter 11: Sunita in Space

Space Travel, Zero Gravity, Earth from Space, Neil Armstrong & Moon Phases | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Astronaut Sunita Williams spent over 6 months aboard the International Space Station (ISS).
  • In space, there is microgravity (weightlessness): astronauts float everywhere, water forms floating bubbles, food floats, and hair stands upright.
  • Seen from space, Earth looks like a glowing blue-and-white marble; national borders do not exist in nature, only land, oceans, and clouds.
  • Neil Armstrong became the first human to walk on the Moon on 20 July 1969.
  • Gravity is the invisible force with which the Earth pulls everything towards its center.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Textbook Exercise
Describe what living in a zero-gravity spacecraft is like based on Sunita Williams' experiences.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Living in zero gravity is very different from living on Earth:
  1. Floating constantly: Astronauts cannot stand or walk on the floor; they float from one cabin to another and must strap themselves to their beds to sleep.
  2. Floating Water & Food: Water does not flow; it forms floating blobs that astronauts catch with tissues to wipe their faces. Food packets float in the cabin.
  3. Hair Stands Up: Without gravity pulling it down, hair stays standing upright all the time, eliminating the need for combs.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Continuous floating and bed straps 1.5 Marks
Floating water droplets and food packets 1.5 Marks
Upright hair phenomenon 1 Mark
Q2 Textbook Exercise
Why did Sunita Williams say: 'Division of countries is only on paper, Earth has no lines drawn on it'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
When looking down at Earth from space, one can only see continents, vast blue oceans, mountain ranges, and white swirl clouds. There are no lines, walls, or fences separating India, Pakistan, or America. Sunita pointed out that political borders are artificial divisions created in human minds and drawn on paper maps, whereas Earth is one interconnected home for all humanity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Natural features visible from orbit vs absence of physical borders 1.5 Marks
Philosophical concept of one united global planet 1.5 Marks
Q3 Textbook Exercise
What is gravity? Give three daily life examples showing the effect of Earth's gravitational force on our planet.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Gravity is the universal invisible force of attraction with which the Earth pulls all objects towards its center.
Three Examples:
  1. When a ball is thrown up into the sky, it slows down, stops, and falls back down to Earth.
  2. Ripe apples and mangoes fall downward from tree branches onto the ground rather than flying into the clouds.
  3. Water in rivers always flows downhill towards lakes and oceans, and stays inside our glasses without floating away.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Definition of Earth's gravitational attraction 1.5 Marks
3 clear everyday examples (falling ball, falling fruit, water flow) 1.5 Marks
Q4 Textbook Exercise
Who was Neil Armstrong? On what date did he achieve the historic milestone of landing on the Moon?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Neil Armstrong was an American astronaut who commanded the Apollo 11 lunar space mission. On 20 July 1969, he stepped out of the lunar module 'Eagle' and became the first human being in history to walk on the surface of the Moon, famously declaring: "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind."
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of Neil Armstrong and Apollo 11 mission 1.5 Marks
Exact historical date (20 July 1969) and first moonwalk milestone 1.5 Marks
Q5 Textbook Exercise
Why does the shape of the Moon appear to change every night in the sky? What is the difference between Purnima and Amavasya?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The Moon does not produce its own light; it reflects light from the Sun. As the Moon orbits the Earth, we see different portions of its illuminated sunlit side:
  • Phases of the Moon: Crescent Moon -> Half Moon (First Quarter) -> Gibbous Moon -> Full Moon -> Waning phases.
  • Purnima (Full Moon Day): The entire illuminated hemisphere of the Moon faces the Earth; the Moon appears as a complete, glowing circular disk.
  • Amavasya (New Moon Day): The sunlit side of the Moon faces completely away from Earth; the Moon is entirely invisible in the night sky.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Explanation of reflected sunlight during orbital motion 1.5 Marks
Definition of Purnima (full visible disk) 1.25 Marks
Definition of Amavasya (sunlit side facing away, dark sky) 1.25 Marks
Q6 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'Sunita in Space', describe how students can conduct an experiential field study in their neighborhood or school eco-garden. What three primary ecological parameters should they document in their field log?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Field Observation Protocol:
1. Direct Inquiry: Students observe the local ecosystem, habitat structures, and environmental interactions linked to Sunita in Space during a guided outdoor trail.
2. Three Field Log Parameters:
• (a) Flora and fauna interactions (sensory adaptations, feeding habits, or nesting locations).
• (b) Environmental conditions (ambient temperature, soil moisture, sunlight exposure, and freshwater access).
• (c) Human footprint (waste disposal practices, water conservation structures, or tree cover density).
3. Conclusion: Maintaining a regular nature journal sharpens analytical thinking and nurtures an enduring respect for biodiversity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Observation methodology clearly outlined 1.5 M
Three tangible field parameters detailed 1.5 M
Q7 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles explored in 'Sunita in Space', classify living organisms or resources according to their ecological adaptations and state the scientific reasoning behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Natural Ecosystems / Renewable Cycles): Organisms and resources that actively contribute to natural nutrient replenishment, such as soil microorganisms, pollinators, and freshwater bodies.
• Category 2 (Anthropogenic Impact / High-Consumption Zones): Human-made systems that alter natural habitats, requiring sustainable management, recycling, and conservation laws.
• Scientific Rationale: Understanding ecological interdependence prevents habitat fragmentation and ensures equitable resource sharing across all living species.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q8 Community Living & Traditional Knowledge • Social Harmony
How does 'Sunita in Space' showcase the value of traditional indigenous knowledge, community water/soil management, or collaborative living? Provide two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Traditional Wisdom & Community Interdependence:
1. Indigenous Conservation Practices: Traditional Indian communities developed remarkable systems like stepwells (Bawris), rainwater harvesting ponds (Johads), and sacred groves that protected local ecosystems for centuries.
2. Social Interdependence: Sustainable resource management relies on shared community responsibility, where farmers, forest dwellers, and urban citizens protect shared commons together.
3. Concrete Examples: From water sharing traditions in arid regions to collective seed conservation banks, indigenous wisdom offers practical blueprints for modern climate resilience.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of traditional knowledge and stewardship 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting collective community heritage 1.5 M
Q9 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 5 student can lead at school and home inspired by the concepts of 'Sunita in Space'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Stewardship: Conduct an audit of leaking taps on the school campus, collect condensation water from air coolers to water garden beds, and practice mindful bucket bathing.
2. Waste Reduction & Composting: Segregate organic cafeteria peelings into a vermicompost pit and establish a 'zero single-use plastic' pledge in the classroom.
3. Habitat Creation: Install safe bird feeding platforms and shallow earthen water dishes on shaded tree branches, especially during hot summer afternoons.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Practical water conservation measure explained 1.0 M
Actionable waste segregation and composting system 1.0 M
Micro-habitat protection step for birds and urban wildlife 1.0 M
Q10 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze an environmental problem from 'Sunita in Space': If excessive groundwater depletion or forest deforestation threatens a local community, what comprehensive 3-step action plan should local authorities and citizens adopt?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Comprehensive Restoration Plan:
1. Strict Conservation Policy: Enforce groundwater recharge mandates for all buildings and strictly prohibit commercial tree cutting in ecological buffer zones.
2. Afforestation & Soil Moisture Retention: Plant indigenous drought-resistant tree species along slopes and waterways to prevent topsoil erosion and enhance the water table.
3. Mass Awareness & Citizen Monitoring: Empower youth eco-clubs to monitor local water levels and organize tree care circles to ensure 100% sapling survival.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate regulatory policy and preventive step 1.5 M
Long-term afforestation and community monitoring strategy 1.5 M
Q11 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate active empathy towards stray animals, local ecosystems, and vulnerable community members as emphasized in 'Sunita in Space'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Active Empathy in Everyday Life:
1. Compassion for Animals: Never tease, displace, or harm neighborhood animals; report injured birds or puppies to registered wildlife rescue groups.
2. Dignity of Labor & Social Respect: Treat sanitation staff, gardeners, and recycling workers with courtesy, ensuring wet and dry waste is hygienically separated.
3. Core Principle: Every organism plays an irreplaceable role in Earth's delicate balance. Living with humility and kindness creates a resilient, joyful world.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Compassionate treatment of animals and urban fauna 1.5 M
Respectful support towards essential community workers 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Life Aboard the International Space Station (ISS)

Astronaut Sunita Williams spent more than 195 days in space aboard the International Space Station orbiting Earth at 28,000 km/h. She described that in space, water does not flow into a glass; it floats as wobbling blobs that astronauts catch with tissues.

Analytical Task: Explain why water floats in blobs in space and describe two physical exercises astronauts must perform daily to prevent muscle and bone loss in zero gravity.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Why water floats in spherical blobs: In orbit, the space station is in perpetual free-fall around Earth, producing a state of weightlessness (microgravity). Without gravity pulling liquid downward, water molecules are held together exclusively by their internal surface tension, naturally pulling the liquid into the shape with the minimum surface area—a sphere. Essential Daily Exercises in Space: 1. Stationary Treadmill with Harness: Astronauts strap themselves with bungee cords to a treadmill and run 1-2 hours daily to provide impact force to leg bones. 2. Resistance Exercise Device (ARED): Mimics weightlifting using vacuum cylinders to maintain bone density and prevent severe muscle atrophy in microgravity.
Rubric: 2.5 Marks for surface tension and weightlessness explanation + 2.5 Marks for treadmill and resistance exercise methods = 5 Marks
