Chapter 10: Earth — Our Shared Home

Exploring Earth's unique life-supporting conditions, global continents and oceans, transboundary wildlife migrations, and planetary stewardship | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • The 'Goldilocks' Planet: Earth is the only known planet harboring life because it sits at an optimal distance from the Sun—neither too hot like Venus nor too frozen like Mars—allowing liquid water, breathable oxygen, and a protective ozone shield.
  • Global Continents & Oceans: Earth's surface is 71% water and 29% land, divided into seven continents (Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe, Australia) and five major oceans (Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern, Arctic).
  • Wildlife Without Borders: Transboundary migratory species travel thousands of miles across national borders—such as Olive Ridley Sea Turtles swimming across the Indian Ocean to Odisha's beaches for mass nesting (*Arribada*), and migratory Rosy Starlings.
  • Global Interconnectedness: Environmental events are transboundary—burning fossil fuels in one nation accelerates Arctic ice melting, which in turn raises global sea levels and threatens low-lying island communities worldwide.
  • Planetary Environmental Threats: Plastic contamination in oceans, industrial deforestation, loss of wildlife corridors, and rising greenhouse gas emissions.
  • Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam & Global Stewardship: The timeless Indian philosophy 'The World is One Family' urges humanity to live in harmonious coexistence with all living species, safeguarding Earth for future generations.

Q1 Planetary Science
What unique planetary conditions make Earth the only known planet capable of supporting life?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Earth sustains living ecosystems because of four ideal conditions:
  1. Optimal Habitable Zone (Goldilocks Distance): Earth is situated at an ideal distance from the Sun, creating temperatures that allow water to remain in liquid form rather than boiling off or freezing completely.
  2. Breathable Atmosphere: Earth's atmosphere contains 21% Oxygen (vital for cellular respiration) and nitrogen, balanced with carbon dioxide needed by plants for photosynthesis.
  3. Protective Ozone Layer & Magnetic Shield: The stratospheric ozone layer filters out lethal solar ultraviolet (UV) radiation, while Earth's magnetic field deflects dangerous cosmic solar wind particles.
  4. Abundant Liquid Water: Oceans, rivers, and groundwater drive biological life and moderate planetary climates.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Optimal distance from Sun permitting liquid water 1 Mark
Oxygen-rich atmosphere and protective ozone/magnetic shields 1 Mark
Abundance of liquid water and climate moderation 1 Mark

Q3 Wildlife Migration & Marine Ecology
Describe the mass nesting (Arribada) of Olive Ridley sea turtles on the coast of Odisha. Why do they cross thousands of miles of ocean?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The annual arrival of Olive Ridley Sea Turtles (Lepidochelys olivacea) in Odisha is one of Earth's greatest natural spectacles:
  1. Long-Distance Oceanic Migration: Adult turtles migrate thousands of kilometres across the Indian Ocean to congregate offshore along the coastal waters of Gahirmatha and Rushikulya in Odisha every winter.
  2. The Phenomenon of 'Arribada' (Mass Arrival): Over a span of several nights, hundreds of thousands of female turtles crawl ashore simultaneously under moonlit tides. Using their hind flippers, they excavate deep flask-shaped sand pits and lay approximately 100 to 120 soft-shelled eggs each, carefully covering the nests with sand before returning to the sea.
  3. Biological Marvel: Hatchlings emerge after 45–50 days and scramble guided by the starlight reflection on ocean waves. Years later, grown females navigate back to the exact same beach where they hatched to lay their own eggs.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of Olive Ridley migration across the Indian Ocean 1.5 Marks
Explanation of Arribada mass nesting in Odisha 1.5 Marks
Hatchling emergence and navigational fidelity to natal beaches 1 Mark
Q4 Global Environmental Interconnectedness
How does single-use plastic discarded into city drainage canals eventually harm marine life in deep oceans thousands of miles away?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Earth's waterways are interconnected, carrying land-based pollution to the sea:
  1. Path from City to Ocean: Plastic bottles, wrappers, and bags thrown onto roads are washed into city storm drains by rains, entering local rivers and flowing downstream into the ocean.
  2. Ingestion and Entanglement: Marine creatures like sea turtles mistake floating plastic bags for jellyfish (their favorite food), choking their digestive tracts. Fish, seals, and seabirds become entangled in discarded nylon fishing nets.
  3. Breakdown into Microplastics: Over decades, sunlight and wave action fragment plastic into toxic microscopic particles (microplastics). Plankton and small fish ingest these microplastics, transferring toxins up the food chain to large fish and eventually back to humans who eat seafood.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Drain-to-river-to-sea transport pathway 1 Mark
Direct wildlife impact (mistaken for food, choking, entanglement) 1 Mark
Microplastic bioaccumulation in marine and human food webs 1 Mark
Q5 Map & Globe Interpretation
Map Skill Task: On a world map or globe, identify and define: (a) The Equator, (b) The Northern and Southern Hemispheres, and (c) The Prime Meridian.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Key Planetary Lines of Reference:
  1. The Equator (0° Latitude): An imaginary horizontal circle drawn around the exact middle of the Earth, equidistant from the North and South Poles. It divides the Earth into the Northern Hemisphere and Southern Hemisphere.
  2. Northern & Southern Hemispheres:
    • Northern Hemisphere: The half of Earth lying north of the Equator (contains India, Asia, Europe, and North America).
    • Southern Hemisphere: The half of Earth lying south of the Equator (contains Australia, Antarctica, and major parts of South America and Africa).
  3. The Prime Meridian (0° Longitude): An imaginary vertical line running from the North Pole to the South Pole passing through Greenwich, England. It serves as the baseline for global standard time zones.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Equator definition (0° latitude) dividing hemispheres 1 Mark
Northern and Southern hemisphere distinctions with continental examples 1 Mark
Prime Meridian definition (0° longitude) through Greenwich 1 Mark
Q6 Ethics & Global Citizenship
What does the ancient Indian philosophical concept 'Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam' teach humanity about environmental stewardship?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam (वसुधैव कुटुम्बकम्): Sourced from the ancient *Maha Upanishad*, this Sanskrit verse translates to 'The World is One Family'.

Lessons for Environmental Stewardship:
  1. Universal Kinship Beyond Borders: It teaches that all human beings, animals, birds, forests, and oceans are inextricably linked members of a single planetary family sharing one common home.
  2. Living in Harmony with Nature: Instead of exploiting nature as commodities, humanity must act as humble caretakers (stewards), ensuring that development does not poison the air, water, or soil that other species rely on.
  3. Collective Global Responsibility: Global challenges like climate change, ocean acidification, and pandemics cannot be solved by one country alone; all nations must collaborate as one family to protect planet Earth.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Sanskrit origin and literal translation 'The World is One Family' 1 Mark
Ethical lesson on harmony with non-human living species 1 Mark
Global collaboration for planetary protection 1 Mark

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Youth Planetary Action Charter for a Zero-Waste, Net-Zero School

During World Environment Day, students learned that humanity currently consumes ecological resources 1.7 times faster than Earth's ecosystems can naturally regenerate, leading to severe biodiversity loss and plastic-filled oceans.

Analytical Task: Author a 5-point 'Youth Planetary Action Charter' that your school community will pledge to adopt to protect Earth as our shared home.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Youth Planetary Action Charter for Earth:
  1. Pledge for Single-Use Plastic Elimination: We pledge to completely eliminate single-use plastic water bottles, straws, and polybags on our school campus, opting exclusively for reusable, washable alternatives.
  2. Pledge for Energy & Water Conservation: We pledge to turn off all electrical appliances when leaving rooms and report running taps immediately, reducing our school's carbon and water footprint.
  3. Pledge for Biodiversity Protection: We pledge to plant and nurture at least five indigenous trees every year in our school or neighbourhood, creating safe mini-habitats for birds, butterflies, and squirrels.
  4. Pledge for Mindful Food Consumption: We pledge to respect farmers and the Earth by taking only what we can finish on our plates, ensuring zero food waste enters municipal landfills.
  5. Pledge for Spreading Environmental Awareness: We pledge to educate our families and neighbors about waste segregation, sustainable public transport, and the timeless principle of Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam.
Rubric: 2 Marks for practical school-level resource commitments (plastic ban, energy saving) + 3 Marks for biodiversity, food ethics, and community awareness leadership = 5 Marks
