Chapter 5: Seeds and Seeds

Seed Germination, Insectivorous Plants, Seed Dispersal Agents & George Mestral's Velcro | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • A seed contains an embryo (baby plant) and stored cotyledon food protected inside a seed coat.
  • Three essential conditions for seed germination: moisture (water), air (oxygen), and warmth (favourable temperature).
  • Pitcher Plant (Nepenthes) grows in Meghalaya and traps insects, frogs, and mice to absorb nitrogen in poor soil.
  • Seed dispersal mechanisms: Wind (light hairy seeds), Water (spongy buoyant seeds like coconut), Animals (hooked seeds that cling to fur), and Pod Explosion (balsam, pea).
  • George Mestral discovered Velcro in 1948 after observing burdock seeds clinging firmly to his dog's fur with microscopic hooks.
  • Global journey of crops: Chilli, potato, and tomato came from South America; Coffee and bhindi from Africa; Cabbage and peas from Europe.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Textbook Exercise
Describe an experiment to prove that seeds need both air and water to germinate. What happens if a seed receives only water but no air?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three-Bowl Chana Experiment:
  • Bowl 1 (Water only, No Air): Place 5 chana seeds completely submerged in water. Result: Seeds swell up but do NOT sprout because they lack oxygen.
  • Bowl 2 (Both Air and Water): Wrap 5 chana seeds in a damp cotton cloth and keep moist. Result: Seeds sprout healthy white radicles (roots) within 2 days because they receive both moisture and atmospheric oxygen.
  • Bowl 3 (Air only, No Water): Keep 5 dry chana seeds in an empty bowl. Result: No sprouting occurs because moisture is absent.
Conclusion: Seeds require both moisture to soften their coat and air for cellular respiration to germinate successfully.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Detailed description of Bowl 1, Bowl 2, and Bowl 3 conditions 3 Marks
Scientific conclusion on dual necessity of air and moisture 1 Mark
Q2 Textbook Exercise
What is an insectivorous plant? How does the Pitcher Plant (Nepenthes) trap insects, and why does it eat them even though it is green?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
An insectivorous plant is a green carnivorous plant that traps and digests small insects and animals.
Pitcher Plant (Nepenthes) of Meghalaya:
  • Its leaf blade is modified into a hollow pitcher-like flask covered with a leaf lid.
  • The mouth of the pitcher secretes sweet-smelling nectar that attracts flying insects.
  • The inner walls are slippery. When an insect lands on the rim, it slips and falls into the digestive liquid at the bottom. The lid snaps shut, trapping the prey.
  • Why it eats insects: Although Nepenthes has chlorophyll and performs photosynthesis to make carbohydrates, it grows in nitrogen-deficient, swampy acidic soil. It digests insects to fulfill its essential nitrogen and protein requirements.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Definition and geographic habitat in Meghalaya 1 Mark
Trap mechanism (scent, slippery rim, lid) 1.5 Marks
Reason for insectivory (nitrogen deficiency in soil) 1.5 Marks
Q3 Textbook Exercise
How did George Mestral get the idea to invent Velcro in 1948? Give three practical everyday uses of Velcro.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
In 1948, Swiss engineer George Mestral went on a nature walk with his dog. Upon returning, he noticed hundreds of spiky burdock seeds (burrs) stuck firmly to his clothes and his dog's coat. Examining a seed under a microscope, he observed thousands of tiny hooks that clung to the loops of fabric. Inspired by this nature design, he invented Velcro with one side made of tiny hooks and the other side of soft loops.
Everyday uses: Sneakers/shoes, school bags, watch straps, jacket fasteners, blood pressure monitor cuffs.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Microscopic observation of burdock seeds with hooks 1.5 Marks
Three everyday applications of Velcro 1.5 Marks
Q4 Textbook Exercise
Explain four distinct natural agents of seed dispersal and name two plants for each method.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Seeds travel to distant places through diverse natural agents:
  1. By Wind: Seeds are lightweight, small, and equipped with wings, tufts of hair, or parachutes. Examples: Dandelion, Drumstick, Maple, Calotropis (Madar).
  2. By Water: Seeds possess a spongy, fibrous, water-resistant outer coat that floats easily. Examples: Coconut, Lotus, Water Lily.
  3. By Animals & Humans: Seeds develop sharp hooks, spines, or sticky burrs that cling to animal fur or human clothing. Examples: Xanthium, Burdock, Urena.
  4. By Explosive Bursting: Dry fruit pods split open suddenly with a snap, scattering seeds away from the mother plant. Examples: Balsam, Soybean, Pea, Lady's Finger.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Wind and water dispersal features and examples 2 Marks
Animal hooks and explosive pod mechanisms and examples 2 Marks
Q5 Textbook Exercise
Why is seed dispersal essential for the survival and multiplication of plant species?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
If all seeds of a tree fell directly beneath its parent canopy:
  • Seedlings would intensely compete with the parent plant and each other for sunlight, water, soil minerals, and root space.
  • Most seedlings would wither and die in the dense shade.
  • Seed dispersal ensures plants spread to new geographic regions, establish new colonies, and adapt to diverse climatic conditions.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Avoiding overcrowding and competition for resources 1.5 Marks
Colonization of new geographic habitats 1.5 Marks
Q6 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'Seeds and Seeds', describe how students can conduct an experiential field study in their neighborhood or school eco-garden. What three primary ecological parameters should they document in their field log?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Field Observation Protocol:
1. Direct Inquiry: Students observe the local ecosystem, habitat structures, and environmental interactions linked to Seeds and Seeds during a guided outdoor trail.
2. Three Field Log Parameters:
• (a) Flora and fauna interactions (sensory adaptations, feeding habits, or nesting locations).
• (b) Environmental conditions (ambient temperature, soil moisture, sunlight exposure, and freshwater access).
• (c) Human footprint (waste disposal practices, water conservation structures, or tree cover density).
3. Conclusion: Maintaining a regular nature journal sharpens analytical thinking and nurtures an enduring respect for biodiversity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Observation methodology clearly outlined 1.5 M
Three tangible field parameters detailed 1.5 M
Q7 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles explored in 'Seeds and Seeds', classify living organisms or resources according to their ecological adaptations and state the scientific reasoning behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Natural Ecosystems / Renewable Cycles): Organisms and resources that actively contribute to natural nutrient replenishment, such as soil microorganisms, pollinators, and freshwater bodies.
• Category 2 (Anthropogenic Impact / High-Consumption Zones): Human-made systems that alter natural habitats, requiring sustainable management, recycling, and conservation laws.
• Scientific Rationale: Understanding ecological interdependence prevents habitat fragmentation and ensures equitable resource sharing across all living species.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q8 Community Living & Traditional Knowledge • Social Harmony
How does 'Seeds and Seeds' showcase the value of traditional indigenous knowledge, community water/soil management, or collaborative living? Provide two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Traditional Wisdom & Community Interdependence:
1. Indigenous Conservation Practices: Traditional Indian communities developed remarkable systems like stepwells (Bawris), rainwater harvesting ponds (Johads), and sacred groves that protected local ecosystems for centuries.
2. Social Interdependence: Sustainable resource management relies on shared community responsibility, where farmers, forest dwellers, and urban citizens protect shared commons together.
3. Concrete Examples: From water sharing traditions in arid regions to collective seed conservation banks, indigenous wisdom offers practical blueprints for modern climate resilience.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of traditional knowledge and stewardship 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting collective community heritage 1.5 M
Q9 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 5 student can lead at school and home inspired by the concepts of 'Seeds and Seeds'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Stewardship: Conduct an audit of leaking taps on the school campus, collect condensation water from air coolers to water garden beds, and practice mindful bucket bathing.
2. Waste Reduction & Composting: Segregate organic cafeteria peelings into a vermicompost pit and establish a 'zero single-use plastic' pledge in the classroom.
3. Habitat Creation: Install safe bird feeding platforms and shallow earthen water dishes on shaded tree branches, especially during hot summer afternoons.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Practical water conservation measure explained 1.0 M
Actionable waste segregation and composting system 1.0 M
Micro-habitat protection step for birds and urban wildlife 1.0 M
Q10 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze an environmental problem from 'Seeds and Seeds': If excessive groundwater depletion or forest deforestation threatens a local community, what comprehensive 3-step action plan should local authorities and citizens adopt?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Comprehensive Restoration Plan:
1. Strict Conservation Policy: Enforce groundwater recharge mandates for all buildings and strictly prohibit commercial tree cutting in ecological buffer zones.
2. Afforestation & Soil Moisture Retention: Plant indigenous drought-resistant tree species along slopes and waterways to prevent topsoil erosion and enhance the water table.
3. Mass Awareness & Citizen Monitoring: Empower youth eco-clubs to monitor local water levels and organize tree care circles to ensure 100% sapling survival.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate regulatory policy and preventive step 1.5 M
Long-term afforestation and community monitoring strategy 1.5 M
Q11 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate active empathy towards stray animals, local ecosystems, and vulnerable community members as emphasized in 'Seeds and Seeds'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Active Empathy in Everyday Life:
1. Compassion for Animals: Never tease, displace, or harm neighborhood animals; report injured birds or puppies to registered wildlife rescue groups.
2. Dignity of Labor & Social Respect: Treat sanitation staff, gardeners, and recycling workers with courtesy, ensuring wet and dry waste is hygienically separated.
3. Core Principle: Every organism plays an irreplaceable role in Earth's delicate balance. Living with humility and kindness creates a resilient, joyful world.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Compassionate treatment of animals and urban fauna 1.5 M
Respectful support towards essential community workers 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

How Chillies Conquered Indian Kitchens

Today, spicy chillies are an indispensable ingredient in every Indian kitchen. Yet, five hundred years ago, neither chillies, potatoes, nor tomatoes existed in India.

Analytical Task: From where did chillies come to India, who brought them, and name two other vegetables that originated in Africa.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Origin of Chillies: Chillies originated in South America. They were brought to India by Portuguese traders who sailed via trade routes from South America to the Malabar coast of India during the late 15th and early 16th centuries. Other Global Crops: - From Africa: Coffee beans and Bhindi (Lady's Finger / Okra). - From South America: Potatoes and Tomatoes. - From Europe: Cabbage and Green Peas.
Rubric: 2 Marks for South America & Portuguese traders + 3 Marks for other global crop origins = 5 Marks
