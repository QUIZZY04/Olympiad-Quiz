Chapter 8: A Treat for Mosquitoes

Malaria, Anopheles Mosquito, Anaemia & Iron, Ronald Ross Discovery & Vector Control | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Malaria is caused by a tiny single-celled protozoan parasite (Plasmodium) transmitted through the bite of a female Anopheles mosquito.
  • Malaria causes shivering, chills, high recurring fever, sweating, and severe headache; it is confirmed through a microscopic blood smear test.
  • Anaemia is a condition where haemoglobin or iron levels in the blood fall below the normal range (12 to 16 g/dL in children), causing weakness, poor physical growth, and difficulty concentrating.
  • Iron-rich foods: Amla (Indian gooseberry), Jaggery (gur), green leafy vegetables (spinach, methi), and beans.
  • Ronald Ross discovered in Secunderabad that mosquitoes spread malaria, winning the Nobel Prize in Medicine in 1902.
  • Mosquito control: Pour kerosene/oil on standing water to choke larvae, introduce Gambusia fish in ponds, and keep water coolers dry.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Textbook Exercise
What causes malaria, which mosquito transmits it, and what symptoms appear in a patient suffering from malaria?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Malaria is caused by a microscopic protozoan parasite called Plasmodium, transmitted from one person to another through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito (identifiable by its spotted wings and tilted posture while resting).
Symptoms: Shivering with chills, recurring high fever every alternative day, severe headache, muscle pains, nausea, and profuse sweating when fever drops.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Causative agent (Plasmodium parasite) 1 Mark
Vector identification (female Anopheles mosquito) 1 Mark
Key symptoms (chills, recurring fever, sweating) 1 Mark
Q2 Textbook Exercise
What is anaemia? What is the normal haemoglobin level for growing school children, and what dietary changes cure anaemia?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Anaemia is a blood condition that occurs when the amount of haemoglobin or red blood cells in the body drops below the healthy threshold.
  • Normal Haemoglobin Range: 12 to 16 grams per decilitre (g/dL) for children.
  • Effects: Weakness, dizziness, stunted physical growth, and reduced stamina and concentration during studies.
  • Dietary Cure: Foods rich in iron and Vitamin C—such as Amla (Indian gooseberry), Jaggery (gur), green leafy vegetables (spinach, fenugreek), beetroot, and pulses, along with iron folic acid tablets distributed in schools.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Definition of anaemia as haemoglobin deficiency 1 Mark
Normal range 12-16 g/dL & cognitive/physical impacts 1.5 Marks
Dietary sources (amla, jaggery, spinach, iron tablets) 1.5 Marks
Q3 Textbook Exercise
Describe the historic discovery made by Ronald Ross regarding malaria in Secunderabad.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
In the late 19th century, people believed malaria was caused by bad air from swampy marshes (mala-aria = bad air). British doctor Ronald Ross spent years in Secunderabad inspecting blood slides under microscopes. He let mosquitoes bite malaria patients, dissected the mosquitoes' stomachs, and discovered the exact same Plasmodium parasites inside the mosquito gut. This proved conclusively that mosquitoes transmit malaria, earning him the Nobel Prize in Medicine in 1902.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Disproving the 'bad air' myth through dissection 1.5 Marks
Verification of parasites in mosquito stomach & 1902 Nobel Prize 1.5 Marks
Q4 Textbook Exercise
Name three diseases transmitted by mosquitoes. How can we differentiate between a normal mosquito and an Anopheles mosquito?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three mosquito-borne diseases:
  1. Malaria (transmitted by female Anopheles mosquito).
  2. Dengue (transmitted by Aedes mosquito).
  3. Chikungunya (transmitted by Aedes mosquito).
Distinguishing features of Anopheles: Anopheles mosquitoes are brownish with spotted wings. When resting on a wall or skin, they tilt their bodies upward at a distinct 45-degree angle, unlike common house mosquitoes (Culex) that sit flat and parallel to the surface.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Naming Malaria, Dengue, and Chikungunya with vectors 1.5 Marks
Physical posture difference (45-degree tilt and spotted wings) 1.5 Marks
Q5 Textbook Exercise
Why do doctors advise hanging mosquito nets and applying wire mesh to windows rather than solely spraying chemical insect repellents?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Physical barriers like bed nets and window wire mesh are far safer and more effective:
  • They create a continuous physical barrier that stops mosquitoes from entering without exposing family members to toxic chemical fumes.
  • Chemical repellent coils and sprays emit harmful fumes containing pyrethroids, which irritate children's lungs and trigger asthma attacks.
  • Mosquitoes develop genetic resistance to repeated chemical sprays over time, whereas physical screens never lose effectiveness.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Physical barrier safety without toxic inhalants 1.5 Marks
Health hazards of chemical coils and insect resistance 1.5 Marks
Q6 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'A Treat for Mosquitoes', describe how students can conduct an experiential field study in their neighborhood or school eco-garden. What three primary ecological parameters should they document in their field log?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Field Observation Protocol:
1. Direct Inquiry: Students observe the local ecosystem, habitat structures, and environmental interactions linked to A Treat for Mosquitoes during a guided outdoor trail.
2. Three Field Log Parameters:
• (a) Flora and fauna interactions (sensory adaptations, feeding habits, or nesting locations).
• (b) Environmental conditions (ambient temperature, soil moisture, sunlight exposure, and freshwater access).
• (c) Human footprint (waste disposal practices, water conservation structures, or tree cover density).
3. Conclusion: Maintaining a regular nature journal sharpens analytical thinking and nurtures an enduring respect for biodiversity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Observation methodology clearly outlined 1.5 M
Three tangible field parameters detailed 1.5 M
Q7 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles explored in 'A Treat for Mosquitoes', classify living organisms or resources according to their ecological adaptations and state the scientific reasoning behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Natural Ecosystems / Renewable Cycles): Organisms and resources that actively contribute to natural nutrient replenishment, such as soil microorganisms, pollinators, and freshwater bodies.
• Category 2 (Anthropogenic Impact / High-Consumption Zones): Human-made systems that alter natural habitats, requiring sustainable management, recycling, and conservation laws.
• Scientific Rationale: Understanding ecological interdependence prevents habitat fragmentation and ensures equitable resource sharing across all living species.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q8 Community Living & Traditional Knowledge • Social Harmony
How does 'A Treat for Mosquitoes' showcase the value of traditional indigenous knowledge, community water/soil management, or collaborative living? Provide two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Traditional Wisdom & Community Interdependence:
1. Indigenous Conservation Practices: Traditional Indian communities developed remarkable systems like stepwells (Bawris), rainwater harvesting ponds (Johads), and sacred groves that protected local ecosystems for centuries.
2. Social Interdependence: Sustainable resource management relies on shared community responsibility, where farmers, forest dwellers, and urban citizens protect shared commons together.
3. Concrete Examples: From water sharing traditions in arid regions to collective seed conservation banks, indigenous wisdom offers practical blueprints for modern climate resilience.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of traditional knowledge and stewardship 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting collective community heritage 1.5 M
Q9 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 5 student can lead at school and home inspired by the concepts of 'A Treat for Mosquitoes'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Stewardship: Conduct an audit of leaking taps on the school campus, collect condensation water from air coolers to water garden beds, and practice mindful bucket bathing.
2. Waste Reduction & Composting: Segregate organic cafeteria peelings into a vermicompost pit and establish a 'zero single-use plastic' pledge in the classroom.
3. Habitat Creation: Install safe bird feeding platforms and shallow earthen water dishes on shaded tree branches, especially during hot summer afternoons.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Practical water conservation measure explained 1.0 M
Actionable waste segregation and composting system 1.0 M
Micro-habitat protection step for birds and urban wildlife 1.0 M
Q10 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze an environmental problem from 'A Treat for Mosquitoes': If excessive groundwater depletion or forest deforestation threatens a local community, what comprehensive 3-step action plan should local authorities and citizens adopt?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Comprehensive Restoration Plan:
1. Strict Conservation Policy: Enforce groundwater recharge mandates for all buildings and strictly prohibit commercial tree cutting in ecological buffer zones.
2. Afforestation & Soil Moisture Retention: Plant indigenous drought-resistant tree species along slopes and waterways to prevent topsoil erosion and enhance the water table.
3. Mass Awareness & Citizen Monitoring: Empower youth eco-clubs to monitor local water levels and organize tree care circles to ensure 100% sapling survival.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate regulatory policy and preventive step 1.5 M
Long-term afforestation and community monitoring strategy 1.5 M
Q11 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate active empathy towards stray animals, local ecosystems, and vulnerable community members as emphasized in 'A Treat for Mosquitoes'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Active Empathy in Everyday Life:
1. Compassion for Animals: Never tease, displace, or harm neighborhood animals; report injured birds or puppies to registered wildlife rescue groups.
2. Dignity of Labor & Social Respect: Treat sanitation staff, gardeners, and recycling workers with courtesy, ensuring wet and dry waste is hygienically separated.
3. Core Principle: Every organism plays an irreplaceable role in Earth's delicate balance. Living with humility and kindness creates a resilient, joyful world.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Compassionate treatment of animals and urban fauna 1.5 M
Respectful support towards essential community workers 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Preventing Dengue, Malaria, and Chikungunya

During the rainy season, stagnant water collected in old car tires, broken flowerpots, and cooler trays in a residential colony. Within two weeks, multiple residents fell sick with dengue and malaria.

Analytical Task: Explain four practical actions community residents must take to prevent mosquito breeding around their homes.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Four Essential Vector Control Actions: 1. Eliminate Stagnant Water: Empty, scrub, and dry out water coolers, flowerpot trays, buckets, and bird baths every week. 2. Cover Water Storage: Keep overhead and underground water storage tanks tightly sealed with lids. 3. Use Oil or Kerosene Film: If water cannot be drained from a pit or puddle, pour a few drops of kerosene or oil on top; this creates a thin film that stops mosquito larvae from breathing air. 4. Biological Control (Gambusia fish): Put small Gambusia fish into local decorative ponds and ditches; they voraciously consume mosquito larvae.
Rubric: 1 Mark each for 4 preventive actions + 1 Mark for biological control explanation = 5 Marks
