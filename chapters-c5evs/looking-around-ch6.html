Chapter 6: Every Drop Counts

Ghadsisar Lake, Stepwells (Baoli), Rainwater Harvesting (Tanka), Al-Biruni's Account & Water Conservation | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • King Ghadsi of Jaisalmer built the famous Ghadsisar lake 650 years ago, connecting nine lakes that stored rainwater without wasting a single drop.
  • Traditional rainwater harvesting: rain falling on sloping roofs flows through pipes into an underground covered tank called a Tanka.
  • Stepwells (Bawari / Baoli) feature deep stone steps descending to the water level, allowing travellers and communities to access fresh water.
  • Al-Biruni, a traveller from Uzbekistan, praised Indian water engineering, recording how massive stones and iron rods joined to form raised platforms (chabutras) around lakes.
  • In modern times, multi-storey buildings and paved asphalt have prevented rain from seeping into the ground, causing groundwater tables to drop drastically.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (24 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Textbook Exercise
Describe the architectural brilliance of King Ghadsi's Ghadsisar lake in Jaisalmer. How did the nine interconnected lakes prevent water wastage?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Ghadsisar lake in Jaisalmer was built 650 years ago by King Ghadsi with the help of his people:
  • Around the lake were ghats with stone steps, decorated verandas, large halls, and pavilions for festivals and music.
  • Interconnected 9-Lake System: The lake was engineered so that when Ghadsisar filled up to its brim with rainwater, the excess water flowed out into another lake at a lower level. When that filled, water overflowed into a third lake, continuing across all nine interconnected lakes.
  • Rainwater collected in this system served the desert city throughout the year without letting a single drop go to waste.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Description of ghats and community pavilions 1 Mark
Detailed explanation of 9 cascading interconnected lakes 2 Marks
Significance for year-round desert water security 1 Mark
Q2 Textbook Exercise
What is a Bawari (Stepwell)? How did stepwells differ from traditional bucket wells?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
A Bawari (Stepwell) is a deep multi-storey stone reservoir with beautiful flights of stone steps leading down directly to the water level.
Difference from ordinary wells: In an ordinary well, people pull water up from above using a rope and bucket. In a stepwell, the people themselves walk down the flights of stairs to reach the water. Stepwells served as community cooling pavilions, rest stops for travelling caravans, and social gathering centres.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Definition of a Bawari with descending stone steps 1.5 Marks
Distinction from bucket wells & community cooling role 1.5 Marks
Q3 Textbook Exercise
Explain the rooftop rainwater harvesting system (Tanka) used in the dry desert areas of Rajasthan.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
In arid regions of Rajasthan, houses are constructed with sloping roofs. Rainwater falling on the clean rooftop flows through a drain pipe down into an underground stone-and-lime chamber called a Tanka constructed in the courtyard. A wire mesh strainer at the pipe mouth filters out leaves and dirt. The tanka is kept covered with a lid to keep the stored water cool and pure for drinking all year.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Sloping roof and drainage pipe mechanism 1.5 Marks
Underground Tanka construction and filtration sieve 1.5 Marks
Q4 Textbook Exercise
What traditional customs, songs, and rituals were associated with water and rain in ancient Indian villages?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
In ancient and traditional Indian culture, water was revered as a sacred life-giver:
  • Whenever a lake or stepwell filled up with fresh monsoon rainwater, villagers gathered in colorful festive clothes to sing celebratory folk songs and dance.
  • New brides bowed in worship before the village pond, spring, or family water tap to seek blessings.
  • Water reservoirs were surrounded by stone carvings of deities, reminding communities that keeping water clean was a sacred religious duty.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Celebration of newly filled lakes and community folk songs 1.5 Marks
Customs of worship and cultural ethics of water reverence 1.5 Marks
Q5 Textbook Exercise
Mention four practical water-saving habits every household should adopt to prevent urban water scarcity.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Four vital water conservation practices:
  1. Turn off the tap while brushing teeth, washing face, or soaping hands (saves up to 6 litres per minute).
  2. Repair leaking taps, flush valves, and joint pipes immediately.
  3. Reuse RO wastewater and vegetable rinse water for mopping floors and watering garden plants.
  4. Install dual-flush toilet cisterns and water-saving aerator nozzles on household taps.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Turning off running taps & prompt leak repairs 1.5 Marks
Reusing greywater & modern aerator/dual-flush fittings 1.5 Marks

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Al-Biruni's Observations on Indian Hydraulic Systems

More than a thousand years ago, Central Asian scholar Al-Biruni arrived in India and was astonished by the pond-building skills of the Indian people. He wrote that his countrymen would be amazed to see such colossal masonry.

Analytical Task: What specific features of Indian ponds and reservoirs impressed Al-Biruni, and why are modern concrete cities facing severe floods and water shortages simultaneously?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Al-Biruni's Praise: He admired how Indian builders joined giant stone blocks with iron clamps to construct terraced chabutras (raised platforms) and separate ascending and descending staircases so people would never collide with each other. Modern Urban Crisis: Modern cities pave soil with asphalt and concrete. Rain cannot seep into the ground to recharge aquifers, causing storm water to cause flash floods while borewells run completely dry.
Rubric: 2 Marks for Al-Biruni's notes + 3 Marks for modern urban runoff analysis = 5 Marks
