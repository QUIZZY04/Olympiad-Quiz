Question 1
NCERT Exercise
Why are living organisms classified?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Biological Significance of Biological Classification:
The Earth is inhabited by an immense diversity of living organisms (approximately 1.7 to 1.8 million species described so far, termed global biodiversity). Classification is the scientific arrangement of organisms into distinct groups and categories based on their observable similarities, differences, and evolutionary affinities. Living organisms are classified for the following essential scientific reasons:

1. Ease of Study and Systematic Organization:
It is impossible to study every living organism individually. By classifying organisms into categories (such as kingdoms, phyla, and classes), the study of one or two representative members of a group provides a reliable general understanding of the characteristics of the entire group.

2. Universal Identification and Prevention of Confusion:
Living organisms possess diverse vernacular (local) names in different geographic regions and languages. Classification establishes a standardized global framework (Binomial Nomenclature), enabling scientists worldwide to refer unambiguously to the same organism by a unique, universally accepted scientific name.

3. Understanding Evolutionary and Phylogenetic Relationships:
Biological classification reflects the evolutionary trajectory (phylogeny) of organisms, demonstrating how complex multi-cellular forms originated and diversified from simpler ancestral unicellular forms over geological time.

4. Discovery and Placement of New Organisms:
Whenever a newly discovered organism is identified, classification provides an exact taxonomic hierarchy and framework where it can be properly categorized according to its morphological, physiological, anatomical, and genetic traits.

5. Applied Biological Sciences:
Accurate taxonomic classification is indispensable in agriculture, forestry, industry, biotechnology, pharmacognosy, and ecological conservation programs.

Question 2
NCERT Exercise
Why are the classification systems changing every now and then?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Dynamic Evolution of Biological Classification Systems:
Classification systems in biology are never static; they undergo continuous refinement and modification due to the progressive expansion of scientific knowledge, technological breakthroughs, and deeper insights into evolutionary biology:

1. Historical Shift from Superficial to Natural Criteria:
Early classification systems (e.g., Aristotle, Theophrastus, and Linnaeus) were largely Artificial Systems based merely on superficial morphological habits, habitats, and economic utility (e.g., classifying plants into herbs, shrubs, trees; or based solely on stamen numbers). As scientific inquiry advanced, Natural Systems (e.g., Bentham and Hooker) emerged, incorporating internal features such as anatomy, histology, embryology, and phytochemistry.

2. Advancement of Microscopy and Analytical Tools:
With the advent of high-resolution light microscopy and electron microscopy (TEM and SEM), cellular ultrastructure—such as distinguishing prokaryotes from eukaryotes, cellular organelles, and specialized wall compositions (e.g., peptidoglycan, chitin, cellulose)—became clearly observable. This necessitated the transition from the Two-Kingdom system to Ernst Haeckel’s Three-Kingdom, Copeland’s Four-Kingdom, and R.H. Whittaker’s Five-Kingdom system (1969).

3. Integration of Molecular Biology and Genetic Phylogeny:
Modern taxonomy heavily relies on molecular data, including DNA and RNA sequencing, 16S ribosomal RNA analysis (Carl Woese’s Three-Domain system), amino acid sequencing of conserved proteins (such as cytochrome c), and karyotype analyses. These molecular markers often reveal true genealogical affinities that diverge from deceptive phenotypic or convergent morphological traits.

4. Discovery of Novel Taxa:
Continuous exploration of extreme habitats (deep sea hydrothermal vents, acidic hot springs, Antarctic ice sheets) unearths novel taxa (e.g., Archaebacteria, extremophiles) that challenge existing taxonomic boundaries, requiring the creation of new categories and taxonomic adjustments.

Question 3
NCERT Exercise
What different criteria would you choose to classify people that you meet often?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Classification Criteria for Everyday Human Social Interactions:
In scientific taxonomy, criteria are chosen based on the objective of classification. Similarly, to classify people encountered regularly, both biological and sociological criteria can be utilized depending on the operational context:

1. Age and Developmental Phase:
• Children / Toddlers (0–12 years)
• Adolescents / Teenagers (13–19 years)
• Adults / Working Professionals (20–59 years)
• Senior Citizens (60+ years)

2. Nature of Social / Functional Relationship:
• Family members and immediate relatives
• Close personal friends and peer group
• Teachers, mentors, and academic instructors
• Classmates, study partners, and school peers
• Professional service providers (shopkeepers, bus drivers, doctors)
• Acquaintances / Strangers

3. Academic / Professional Activity Domain:
• Science stream, Commerce stream, Humanities/Arts stream
• Co-curricular activities: Sports/Athletics, Music/Arts, Debate/Literature, Coding/Robotics

4. Geographic / Linguistic Background:
• Neighborhood / Local community residents
• Regional origin and mother tongue (Hindi, English, Tamil, Bengali, Punjabi, etc.)

Scientific Significance:
This demonstrates that classification is inherently hierarchical and purposeful: no single criterion suffices for all situations, and categorized groups facilitate organized social communication, collaboration, and systematic understanding.

Question 4
NCERT Exercise
What do we learn from identification of individuals and populations?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Key Learnings from the Scientific Identification of Individuals and Populations:
Identification involves recognizing an unknown organism and assigning it to an already established taxonomic rank by comparing its diagnostic traits with known species. The biological insights gained include:

A. Learnings from Individual Identification:
1. Morphological & Anatomical Characteristics: Delineates structural features such as body symmetry, presence of notochord, leaf venation, floral formula, and reproductive organs.
2. Exact Taxonomic Position: Places the organism precisely into its correct domain, kingdom, phylum/division, class, order, family, genus, and species.
3. Physiological & Ecological Role: Identifies nutritional mode (autotroph, heterotroph, saprophyte), ecological niche, and environmental adaptations.
4. Economic & Medical Utility: Discovers potential medicinal value, agricultural utility, toxicity, or pathogenicity.

B. Learnings from Population Identification:
1. Intraspecific Genetic Variation: Reveals morphological polymorphism, genetic diversity, and phenotypic plasticity within the same species across varying geographical habitats.
2. Reproductive Dynamics & Gene Flow: Highlights interbreeding capabilities, isolating mechanisms, allele frequencies, and natural selection pressures.
3. Demographic Structure: Provides data on population size, age distribution, sex ratio, birth/death rates, and carrying capacity.
4. Ecological Interactions & Biogeography: Maps geographic distribution, habitat preference, symbiotic associations, predator-prey dynamics, and endangered/vulnerable conservation status.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Learnings at individual level: anatomical traits, exact taxonomic rank, and economic utility1.5 Marks
Learnings at population level: genetic diversity, interbreeding, allele frequency, and conservation status1.5 Marks
Question 5
NCERT Exercise
Given below is the scientific name of Mango. Identify the correctly written name:
Mangifera Indica
Mangifera indica
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Correct Identification & Botanical Nomenclature Analysis:

The correctly written scientific name is:
$$\mathbf{\textit{Mangifera indica}}$$ (Full author citation: $\textit{Mangifera indica}\text{ Linn.}$, where Linn. indicates that the species was first described by Carolus Linnaeus).

Universal Rules of Binomial Nomenclature Justification:
1. Two-Part Scientific Name: Each biological name consists of two Latinized words—the first word represents the Genus (generic name) and the second word represents the specific epithet.
2. Capitalization Rules: The generic name must always start with a Capital letter (e.g., Mangifera), whereas the specific epithet must strictly begin with a small letter (e.g., indica). Therefore, Mangifera Indica with a capital 'I' violates the International Code of Botanical Nomenclature (ICBN) and is invalid.
3. Typographical Formatting: When printed, biological names must be rendered in Italics to indicate their Latin origin. When handwritten, the generic name and specific epithet must be underlined separately (Mangifera indica).
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Correct selection of \textit{Mangifera indica} (small letter for specific epithet)1 Mark
Citation of ICBN rules regarding capitalization of genus and lowercase specific epithet1 Mark
Question 6
NCERT Exercise
Define a taxon. Give some examples of taxa at different hierarchical levels.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Definition of Taxon (Plural: Taxa):
A taxon is a scientific term used to designate any concrete unit or taxonomic group of organisms of any rank in a biological classification system. It represents real biological entities assigned to a recognized taxonomic category based on shared diagnostic characteristics.

Distinction between Category and Taxon: A 'category' is an abstract hierarchical rank (e.g., Kingdom, Class, Order, Genus), whereas a 'taxon' is the actual group of living organisms occupying that category (e.g., Plantae, Mammalia, Carnivora, Homo).

Examples of Taxa at Different Hierarchical Levels:

Division / Phylum Angiospermae Chordata / Arthropoda
Class Dicotyledonae / Monocotyledonae Mammalia / Insecta
Order Polymoniales / Sapindales Carnivora / Primata
Family Solanaceae / Anacardiaceae Felidae / Hominidae
Genus Solanum / Mangifera Panthera / Homo

Question 7
NCERT Exercise
Can you identify the correct sequence of taxonomical categories?
(a) Species ⟶ Order ⟶ Phylum ⟶ Kingdom
(b) Genus ⟶ Species ⟶ Order ⟶ Kingdom
(c) Species ⟶ Genus ⟶ Order ⟶ Phylum
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Evaluation of Taxonomic Sequences:

The standard ascending taxonomic hierarchy established by Carolus Linnaeus and modern ICBN/ICZN codes is:
$$\mathbf{\text{Species} \longrightarrow \text{Genus} \longrightarrow \text{Family} \longrightarrow \text{Order} \longrightarrow \text{Class} \longrightarrow \text{Phylum (or Division)} \longrightarrow \text{Kingdom}}$$
Analyzing the given options:
• Sequence (a): Species ⟶ Order ⟶ Phylum ⟶ Kingdom
This sequence is technically in the correct ascending order of hierarchy (Species is lower than Order, Order is lower than Phylum, and Phylum is lower than Kingdom), but intermediate categories (Genus, Family, Class) are omitted.

• Sequence (b): Genus ⟶ Species ⟶ Order ⟶ Kingdom
This sequence is incorrect because Genus is placed before Species; in an ascending hierarchy, Species must precede Genus.

• Sequence (c): Species ⟶ Genus ⟶ Order ⟶ Phylum
This sequence correctly reflects ascending taxonomic ranks (Species ⟶ Genus, then jumping through Family to Order, then Class to Phylum).

$$\mathbf{\text{Conclusion: Both (a) and (c) represent correct ascending hierarchical orders, with (c) being the most standard contiguous progression.}}$$
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Identification of (c) and (a) as valid ascending sequences and rejection of (b)1 Mark
Complete ascending hierarchy definition: Species → Genus → Family → Order → Class → Phylum → Kingdom1 Mark
Question 8
NCERT Exercise
Try to collect all the currently accepted meanings for the word 'species'. Discuss with your teacher the meaning of species in case of higher plants and animals on one hand, and bacteria on the other hand.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Accepted Scientific Concepts and Meanings of the Term 'Species':
• Biological Species Concept (Ernst Mayr, 1942): A species is a group of interbreeding or potentially interbreeding natural populations that are reproductively isolated from other such groups, producing viable and fertile offspring.
• Morphological Species Concept: A group of individuals that share distinct, consistent structural and morphological characteristics distinguishing them from others.
• Phylogenetic / Evolutionary Species Concept (Simpson & Cracraft): An irreducible lineage of organisms that maintains its identity from other lineages and has an independent evolutionary trajectory.
• Genetic / Molecular Species Concept: A cluster of organisms possessing a high degree of genetic identity and similarity across conserved genomic regions (e.g., DNA-DNA hybridization > 70% or 16S rRNA gene similarity > 97%).

2. Comparison: Higher Plants and Animals vs. Bacteria (Prokaryotes):
Parameter Higher Plants and Animals (Eukaryotes) Bacteria (Prokaryotes)
Reproduction Mode Predominantly sexual reproduction with gametogenesis, syngamy, and meiosis. Asexual reproduction via binary fission; lack true sexual reproduction.
Applicability of Biological Species Concept Strictly Applicable: Reproductive isolation reliably demarcates distinct species boundaries. Not Applicable: Because bacteria do not undergo sexual reproduction and natural interbreeding.
Gene Transfer Strict vertical gene transfer from parents to offspring. Extensive Horizontal Gene Transfer (HGT) via transformation, conjugation, and transduction across diverse strains.
Primary Delineation Criterion Morphology, reproductive compatibility, karyology, and anatomy. 16S rRNA gene sequencing, whole genome average nucleotide identity (ANI > 95–96%), and biochemical profiling.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Explanation of Biological Species Concept (Ernst Mayr: reproductive isolation and interbreeding)1 Mark
Description of Morphological and Phylogenetic/Molecular concepts1 Mark
Detailed contrast: higher organisms rely on sexual reproduction and reproductive isolation1 Mark
Detailed contrast: bacteria lack sexual reproduction, exhibit HGT, and are classified by 16S rRNA / genomics1 Mark
Question 9
NCERT Exercise
Define and understand the following terms:
(i) Phylum
(ii) Class
(iii) Family
(iv) Order
(v) Genus
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Scientific Definitions of Taxonomic Categories:

(i) Phylum (Division in Plants):
A primary taxonomic category above Class and below Kingdom. It comprises a group of related classes sharing major fundamental structural and architectural body plans.
• Animal Example: Phylum Chordata includes classes Mammalia, Aves, Reptilia, Amphibia, and Pisces due to common diagnostic features—presence of notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, and paired pharyngeal gill slits.
• Plant Example: Division Angiospermae.

(ii) Class:
A major taxonomic category comprising one or more related orders having a number of common diagnostic traits.
• Animal Example: Class Mammalia includes Order Primata (monkeys, apes, humans) and Order Carnivora (cats, dogs, tigers), all characterized by mammary glands, hair on skin, and three middle ear ossicles.
• Plant Example: Class Dicotyledonae includes orders Sapindales and Polymoniales.

(iii) Order:
An assemblage of related families that exhibit a few similar, distinctive floral or morphological characteristics.
• Plant Example: Order Polymoniales includes plant families Solanaceae and Convolvulaceae based mainly on floral characters.
• Animal Example: Order Carnivora includes family Felidae (cats) and family Canidae (dogs).

(iv) Family:
A taxonomic category encompassing a group of related genera with still fewer similarities compared to genus and species, characterized by both vegetative and reproductive/floral features.
• Plant Example: Family Solanaceae contains distinct genera: Solanum, Petunia, and Datura.
• Animal Example: Family Felidae includes genus Panthera (lion, tiger, leopard) and genus Felis (domestic cat).

(v) Genus:
A group of closely related species which have more characters in common in comparison to species of other genera. It is an aggregate of closely related species.
• Plant Example: Genus Solanum includes species tuberosum (potato), melongena (brinjal), and nigrum (black nightshade).
• Animal Example: Genus Panthera includes Panthera leo (lion), Panthera tigris (tiger), and Panthera pardus (leopard).

(i) Definition of Phylum/Division with chordate / angiosperm examples1 Mark
(ii) Definition of Class with Mammalia / Dicotyledonae examples1 Mark
(iii) Definition of Order with Polymoniales / Carnivora examples1 Mark
(iv) Definition of Family with Solanaceae / Felidae examples1 Mark

Question 10
NCERT Exercise
How is a key helpful in the identification and classification of an organism?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Role of Taxonomic Keys in Identification and Classification:
A taxonomic key is an indispensable analytical device used by biologists for identifying unknown plants and animals based on observable similarities and dissimilarities.

Key Structural Principles of a Taxonomic Key:
1. Couplet: Keys are based on contrasting characters generally arranged in pairs called a couplet.
2. Lead: Each statement in a taxonomic key is called a lead.
3. Analytical Dichotomy: The user evaluates the contrasting statements against the unknown specimen. Accepting one statement leads to the elimination/rejection of the alternate choice, which either directly identifies the organism or directs the user to the next subordinate couplet.
4. Separate Keys for Distinct Ranks: Separate taxonomic keys are developed for each taxonomic category such as family, genus, and species for comprehensive identification.

Types of Taxonomic Keys:
• Dichotomous Indented Key: Provides choices with equal indentation under a single lead, showing natural subdivisions.
• Bracketed Key: Pairs of contrasting statements are placed in adjacent numbered couplets with cross-references, providing swift, efficient diagnostic identification.

Practical Utility in Taxonomy:
• Enables rapid, accurate identification of newly collected specimens without requiring exhaustive reference literature.
• Synthesizes complex biological diagnostic information into a structured, step-by-step decision tree.
• Forms the foundation of botanical and zoological floras, monographs, and diagnostic field guides.
