Animal Tissues (Epithelial & Muscular) & Frog Morpho-Anatomy
NCERT Textbook Exercise 1 to 14: Animal Tissues, Epithelial Junctions, Cockroach Anatomy & Frog Sexual Dimorphism
Question 1
NCERT Exercise
Answer in one word or one sentence:
(i) Give the common name of Periplaneta americana.
(ii) How many spermathecae are found in earthworm / cockroach?
(iii) What is the position of ovaries in cockroach?
(iv) How many segments are present in the abdomen of cockroach?
(v) Where do you find Malpighian tubules?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Precise Anatomical Answers:

  • (i) Common name of Periplaneta americana:
    American Cockroach (common household pest).
  • (ii) Spermathecae in cockroach / earthworm:
    In female cockroach, there is one pair of spermathecae (situated in the 6th abdominal segment). (In earthworm, there are 4 pairs situated in 6th to 9th segments).
  • (iii) Position of ovaries in cockroach:
    A pair of large ovaries lies laterally in the 2nd to 6th abdominal segments embedded in fat bodies.
  • (iv) Number of segments in the abdomen of cockroach:
    The abdomen in both male and female adult cockroaches consists of 10 segments (embryo has 11 segments).
  • (v) Location of Malpighian tubules:
    At the junction of the midgut (mesenteron) and hindgut (ileum) in cockroach, floating freely in the hemolymph.

(i) American cockroach & (ii) One pair of spermathecae in female cockroach1 Mark
(iii) 2nd to 6th abdominal segments & (iv) 10 segments in abdomen1 Mark
(v) Junction of midgut and hindgut1 Mark
Question 2
NCERT Exercise
Answer the following: (i) What is the function of a nephron / nephridium? (ii) How many types of nephridia are found in earthworm based on location?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
(i) Function of a Nephron / Nephridium:
The nephron (in vertebrates) and nephridium (in invertebrates like annelids) is the microscopic structural and functional unit of the excretory system. Its primary physiological functions are:
  • Excretion: Ultrafiltration of blood/coelomic fluid, selective reabsorption of vital solutes (glucose, amino acids, ions), and active tubular secretion to eliminate metabolic nitrogenous wastes (urea, ammonia, or uric acid).
  • Osmoregulation: Regulates the volume, water content, and ionic electrolyte balance ($Na^+$, $K^+$, $Cl^-$) of body fluids.
(ii) Three Types of Nephridia in Earthworm Based on Location:
  • 1. Septal Nephridia: Present on both sides of intersegmental septa from segment 15 to the last segment opening into intestine (enteronephric).
  • 2. Integumentary Nephridia: Minute nephridia attached to the inner body wall lining from segment 3 to the last, discharging wastes directly to exterior (exonephric).
  • 3. Pharyngeal Nephridia: Present as three paired tufts in segments 4, 5, and 6 opening into the pharynx and buccal cavity (enteronephric).
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(i) Dual function: excretion of nitrogenous wastes and osmoregulatory fluid balance1.5 Marks
(ii) Three types based on location: Septal, Integumentary, and Pharyngeal nephridia1.5 Marks
Question 3
NCERT Exercise
Draw a neat labelled diagram of the alimentary canal of a cockroach.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Anatomical Organization of the Alimentary Canal of Cockroach (Periplaneta americana):
The digestive tract is divided into three distinct morphological regions:

1. Foregut (Stomodaeum) — Ectodermal, lined with cuticular intima:
  • Mouth & Pharynx: Opens into a short muscular pharynx, receiving ducts from paired salivary glands and salivary reservoirs.
  • Oesophagus: A narrow, thin-walled tube leading into a large, thin-walled sac called the crop (used for temporary food storage).
  • Gizzard (Proventriculus): A thick, circular muscular organ equipped internally with six chitinous cuticular teeth and bristles that grind food particles into a fine powder.
2. Midgut (Mesenteron / Ventriculus) — Endodermal, true digestive zone:
  • At the junction of gizzard and midgut, there is a ring of 6 to 8 blind tubular finger-like Hepatic (Gastric) Caeca that secrete digestive enzymes (amylases, maltases, lipases).
  • Midgut lumen is lined by the permeable peritrophic membrane protecting epithelial cells.
3. Hindgut (Proctodaeum) — Ectodermal, lined with cuticle:
  • At the junction of midgut and hindgut, there are 100 to 150 yellow, fine, hair-like Malpighian tubules (excretory in function).
  • Differentiated into: (i) short narrow ileum, (ii) long coiled colon, and (iii) broad rectum with 6 rectal papillae for water and salt reabsorption, opening at the anus.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Foregut components: pharynx, oesophagus, salivary glands, crop (storage), gizzard with 6 teeth1.5 Marks
Midgut with 6-8 hepatic caeca for digestive enzyme secretion1 Mark
Hindgut with Malpighian tubules (100-150), ileum, colon, and rectum with rectal papillae1.5 Marks
Question 4
NCERT Exercise
Compare the following: (a) Simple epithelium and compound epithelium (b) Cardiac muscle and striated muscle (c) Dense regular and dense irregular connective tissues (d) Adipose and blood tissue (e) Simple gland and compound gland.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Comparative Analysis of Tissue Pairs:

(a) Simple Epithelium vs. Compound Epithelium:
  • Simple: Composed of a single layer of cells resting on a basement membrane. Functions in absorption, secretion, and filtration (e.g., lining of stomach, renal tubules, lung alveoli).
  • Compound (Stratified): Composed of two or more layers of cells. Main function is providing mechanical and chemical protection against stress and wear-and-tear (e.g., skin epidermis, buccal cavity, pharynx).
(b) Cardiac Muscle vs. Striated (Skeletal) Muscle:
  • Cardiac Muscle: Involuntary, branched, uninucleate fibers located exclusively in the heart wall, possessing specialized zig-zag communicating junctions called intercalated discs with gap junctions. Never undergoes fatigue.
  • Striated Muscle: Voluntary, unbranched, multinucleate (syncytial) cylindrical fibers attached to skeletal bones. Subject to fatigue upon prolonged lactic acid accumulation.
(c) Dense Regular vs. Dense Irregular Connective Tissue:
  • Dense Regular: Collagen fibers are packed in parallel bundles between rows of fibroblasts, providing high unidirectional tensile strength (e.g., tendons connecting muscle to bone, ligaments connecting bone to bone).
  • Dense Irregular: Fibroblasts and fibers (predominantly collagen) are oriented in criss-cross, irregular random directions to resist mechanical forces from multiple angles (e.g., in the dermis of skin).
(d) Adipose Tissue vs. Blood:
  • Adipose: Loose connective tissue specialized in synthesizing and storing neutral fats (triglycerides) within adipocytes; acts as a subcutaneous heat insulator and shock-absorbing cushion.
  • Blood: Fluid connective tissue comprising plasma (55%) and formed cellular elements (RBCs, WBCs, platelets); translocates respiratory gases, nutrients, and hormones throughout the body.
(e) Simple Gland vs. Compound Gland:
  • Simple Gland: Has an unbranched duct leading from secretory units (e.g., sweat glands, gastric glands, crypts of Lieberkühn).
  • Compound Gland: Has a repeatedly branched duct system (e.g., salivary glands, exocrine pancreas, mammary glands).
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) Simple (single layer, absorption) vs Compound (multi-layered, protection)1 Mark
(b) Cardiac (branched, intercalated discs, involuntary) vs Striated (syncytial, voluntary)1 Mark
(c) Dense regular (parallel fibers, tendons) vs irregular (random criss-cross, dermis)1 Mark
(d) Adipose (fat storage, insulation) vs Blood (fluid plasma, gas transport)1 Mark
(e) Simple gland (unbranched duct) vs Compound gland (branched duct)1 Mark
Question 5
NCERT Exercise
What are the following and where do you find them in animal body: (a) Chondrocytes (b) Axons (c) Ciliated epithelium?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Definition, Identity & Anatomical Distribution:

(a) Chondrocytes:
  • What they are: Mature cartilage-forming cells that reside enclosed in fluid-filled cavities called lacunae within the pliable, solid, non-vascular chondroitin sulfate matrix of cartilage.
  • Where found: At the tip of the human nose, outer ear pinna, intervertebral discs, ring supports of trachea, and articular surfaces of long bones.
(b) Axons:
  • What they are: Long, slender, cylindrical cytoplasmic nerve fiber projections arising from the cyton (soma) of a neuron that conduct action potentials (nerve impulses) away from the cell body toward synaptic terminals.
  • Where found: In peripheral nerves, white matter tracts of the brain and spinal cord, and sensory/motor nerves throughout the body.
(c) Ciliated Epithelium:
  • What it is: A specialized epithelial tissue consisting of cuboidal or columnar cells bearing hundreds of hair-like protoplasmic projections called cilia on their apical free surface. Cilia beat rhythmically to propel particles, mucus, or fluids in a specific direction.
  • Where found: Inner lining of the respiratory tract (bronchioles and trachea) to sweep out inhaled dust particles and mucus, and inner lining of the Fallopian tubes (oviducts) to propel ova towards the uterus.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) Chondrocytes: definition (cartilage cells in lacunae) & location (nose tip, pinna, joints)1 Mark
(b) Axons: definition (neuron fiber conducting away from cyton) & location (nerves, CNS white matter)1 Mark
(c) Ciliated epithelium: definition (cilia beating) & location (bronchioles, fallopian tubes)1 Mark
Question 6
NCERT Exercise
Describe the various types of epithelial tissues with the help of labelled diagrams.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Comprehensive Classification of Epithelial Tissues:
Epithelial tissue forms protective coverings or inner linings for free surfaces, characterized by closely packed cells with minimal intercellular matrix, resting upon a non-cellular collagenous basement membrane.

A. Simple Epithelium (Single Layer of Cells):
  1. Squamous Epithelium (Pavement Epithelium):
    • Thin, flat, polygonal tile-like cells with irregular boundaries and central bulging nuclei.
    • Function: Forms a delicate diffusion boundary.
    • Location: Walls of blood vessels (endothelium), air sacs of lungs (alveoli), Bowman's capsule in nephrons.
  2. Cuboidal Epithelium:
    • Cube-like cells with spherical centrally located nuclei. In some regions (e.g., Proximal Convoluted Tubule / PCT of nephron), apical surfaces bear microvilli forming a brush border to expand absorptive surface area.
    • Function: Secretion and absorption.
    • Location: Ducts of glands and tubular parts of nephrons in kidneys.
  3. Columnar Epithelium:
    • Tall, pillar-like, cylindrical cells with elongated oval nuclei situated near the base. Free surface may possess microvilli or mucus-secreting goblet cells.
    • Function: Secretion of enzymes/mucus and active nutrient absorption.
    • Location: Lining of stomach, small intestine, and gall bladder.
  4. Ciliated Epithelium:
    • Columnar or cuboidal cells bearing motile cilia on their free surfaces.
    • Function: Moves particles or mucus in a unidirectional current.
    • Location: Bronchioles, trachea, fallopian tubes, ventricles of brain (ependyma).
B. Compound (Stratified) Epithelium:
Made of multiple cell layers with limited secretory/absorptive role. Provides mechanical, chemical, and thermal protection against abrasion. Location: Dry surface of skin (keratinized stratified squamous), moist surface of buccal cavity, pharynx, esophagus, and vagina.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Simple squamous: flat tiles, diffusion boundary, lung alveoli/capillaries1 Mark
Simple cuboidal: cube-shaped, secretion/absorption, PCT kidney tubules/ducts1 Mark
Simple columnar & ciliated: tall cells, basal nuclei, cilia function in bronchioles/oviducts1 Mark
Compound epithelium: multi-layered, protective barrier against abrasion, skin/pharynx1 Mark
Question 7
NCERT Exercise
Distinguish between: (a) Exocrine and endocrine glands (b) Tight junctions, adhering junctions and gap junctions.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Differences Between Exocrine and Endocrine Glands:

FeatureExocrine GlandsEndocrine Glands
1. Presence of DuctsDuct glands; discharge secretions via dedicated tubular ducts.Ductless glands; lack ducts completely.
2. Nature of SecretionMucus, saliva, digestive enzymes, bile, sweat, sebum, earwax, milk.Chemical messengers called hormones.
3. Route of TransportDischarged directly onto an epithelial surface or into a lumen.Secreted directly into venous capillary blood and lymph.
4. ExamplesSalivary glands, sweat glands, liver, exocrine pancreas.Pituitary, thyroid, adrenals, parathyroid, endocrine islets of Langerhans.

(b) Differences Between Specialized Cellular Junctions:

Junction TypeMolecular MechanismPrimary Physiological Role
1. Tight Junctions (Zonula occludens) Adjacent cell membranes fuse tightly by trans-membrane proteins (claudins and occludins). Prevents leakage of substances across an epithelial cell layer (e.g., intestinal mucosa, urinary bladder).
2. Adhering Junctions (Desmosomes) Cadherin cadherin-anchored protein plaques anchored to cytoplasmic keratin intermediate filaments. Performs cementing to keep neighboring cells firmly anchored together under mechanical shearing forces (e.g., epidermis, cardiac muscle).
3. Gap Junctions (Nexus) Porous hexameric protein channels called connexons bridge adjacent cytoplasm. Facilitates rapid intercellular communication by allowing direct passage of ions, small molecules, second messengers, and rapid electrical action potentials.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) Exocrine (ducts, enzymes/sweat) vs Endocrine (ductless, hormones in blood)2 Marks
(b) Precise roles: Tight (stop leakage), Adhering (cementing/strength), Gap (intercellular ionic flow)2 Marks
Question 8
NCERT Exercise
Draw a neat diagram of the digestive system of frog.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Anatomical Architecture of the Digestive System of Frog (Rana tigrina):
The digestive system consists of an alimentary canal and associated digestive glands:

1. Alimentary Canal (Relatively Short due to Carnivorous Diet):
  • Mouth & Buccopharyngeal Cavity: Bounded by wide jaws; upper jaw bears row of sharp maxillary teeth and two patches of vomerine teeth on palate (prevent prey escape). Possesses a muscular, bilobed, highly extensible tongue attached at the anterior rim and free posteriorly.
  • Oesophagus: Short broad muscular tube dilating into the stomach.
  • Stomach: Crescent-shaped muscular sac with gastric glands secreting $HCl$ and pepsinogen; produces acidic chyme.
  • Intestine:
    • Duodenum: Receives the hepatopancreatic duct carrying bile and pancreatic juice.
    • Ileum: Coiled loop lined by microvilli for nutrient digestion and absorption.
  • Rectum: Reabsorbs water, opening into a common terminal chamber called the cloaca, which opens outside via the cloacal aperture (common outlet for feces, urine, and gametes).
2. Associated Digestive Glands:
  • Liver: Multilobed, secretes bile stored in the green gall bladder (emulsifies fats).
  • Pancreas: Irregular gland lying between stomach and duodenum, secreting pancreatic juice containing trypsin, amylase, and lipase.

Mouth, vomerine/maxillary teeth, and anteriorly attached bilobed extensible tongue1 Mark
Stomach (acid chyme) and intestine (duodenum & coiled ileum)1 Mark
Digestive glands: multilobed liver with gall bladder, and pancreas1 Mark
Cloaca and cloacal aperture serving as common terminal chamber1 Mark
Question 9
NCERT Exercise
Mention the function of the following: (a) Ureters in frog (b) Malpighian tubules in cockroach (c) Body wall in earthworm.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Specific Physiological Functions:

(a) Ureters in Frog:
  • In Male Frog: The ureters act as urinogenital ducts. They emerge from kidneys, receive vasa efferentia carrying spermatozoa from testes via Bidder's canal, and transport both urine and sperm into the cloaca.
  • In Female Frog: The ureters function exclusively as urinary ducts to transport urine from kidneys into the cloaca (oviducts open separately into cloaca).
(b) Malpighian Tubules in Cockroach:
  • 100 to 150 fine, yellow, thread-like tubules floating in hemolymph that act as the primary excretory and osmoregulatory organs.
  • Lined by glandular and ciliated epithelium, they extract potassium urate and water from hemolymph, convert it into insoluble uric acid, and discharge it into the hindgut lumen, ensuring maximum water conservation (uricotelism).
(c) Body Wall in Earthworm:
  • Cutaneous Respiration: Highly vascularized epidermis secretes moist mucus; atmospheric oxygen dissolves into the moisture film and diffuses into blood capillaries.
  • Locomotion: Outer circular and inner longitudinal muscle layers contract antagonistically with chitinous S-shaped setae to produce peristaltic burrowing locomotion.
  • Protection: Secretes protective cuticle preventing internal organ damage and desiccation.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) Ureters in frog: urinogenital duct in males vs solely urinary in females1 Mark
(b) Malpighian tubules: extraction of urate wastes from hemolymph and uric acid excretion1 Mark
(c) Earthworm body wall: cutaneous gas exchange, muscle/setae locomotion, and protection1 Mark
Question 10
NCERT Exercise
Give the schematic diagram of an open circulatory system of cockroach.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Open Circulatory System of Cockroach (Periplaneta americana):
Cockroaches possess an open circulatory system where blood (hemolymph) does not flow through closed capillaries, but directly bathes visceral organs in open fluid spaces called sinuses within the hemocoel:

1. Structural Components:
  • Hemolymph: Colorless blood composed of clear fluid plasma and phagocytic hemocytes (lacks respiratory pigment like hemoglobin; does not transport oxygen).
  • Heart: Elongated, pulsating, dorsal muscular tube differentiated into 13 funnel-shaped chambers (3 thoracic and 10 abdominal). Each chamber possesses a pair of lateral openings guarded by valves called ostia.
  • Alary Muscles: 12 pairs of triangular fan-like muscles attached between heart chambers and terga that contract to expand the pericardial space.
  • Hemocoelic Sinuses: Partitioned by two horizontal fibro-muscular membranes (dorsal and ventral diaphragms) into three sinuses:
    1. Dorsal Pericardial Sinus: Encloses the heart.
    2. Middle Perivisceral Sinus: Largest space enclosing the alimentary canal and gonads.
    3. Ventral Perineural Sinus: Surrounds the ventral nerve cord.
Schematic Course of Circulation:
$$\text{Pericardial Sinus} \xrightarrow{\text{through Ostia}} \text{Heart (Chambers 13 to 1)} \xrightarrow{\text{Anterior Aorta}} \text{Head Sinus}$$ $$\downarrow$$ $$\text{Perivisceral \& Perineural Sinuses (visceral bathing)} \xrightarrow{\text{Diaphragm Pores}} \text{Pericardial Sinus}$$
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
13-chambered dorsal heart with ostia and 12 pairs of alary muscles1 Mark
Three hemocoelic sinuses: pericardial, perivisceral, and perineural separated by diaphragms1 Mark
Complete flow sequence from heart to head sinus, perivisceral sinuses and back via ostia1 Mark
Question 11
NCERT Exercise
Mention the role of the following in cockroach: (a) Antennae (b) Compound eyes (c) Maxillary palps (d) Anal cerci.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Sensory & Behavioral Roles in Cockroach:

(a) Antennae (Thread-like Tactile & Olfactory Organs):
  • Arise from membranous sockets in front of the eyes. Composed of scape, pedicel, and multi-segmented flagellum.
  • Rich in tactile, olfactory, and thermal receptors; monitor environmental surroundings, locate food, detect air currents, and perceive sex pheromones.
(b) Compound Eyes (Visual Photoreception):
  • A pair of kidney-shaped sessile compound eyes situated dorsolaterally on head capsule, each composed of roughly 2,000 hexagonal ommatidia.
  • Gives mosaic vision (apposition/superposition) with high sensitivity to motion in dim light (nocturnal vision) but low image resolution.
(c) Maxillary Palps (Gustatory & Manipulative Feeding):
  • Five-segmented sensory appendages borne on the first pair of maxillae.
  • Carry chemoreceptors (taste/smell) and mechanoreceptors used to inspect, taste, hold, and manipulate food particles into the pre-oral cavity during chewing.
(d) Anal Cerci (Acoustic & Seismic Vibration Detection):
  • A pair of jointed, 15-segmented sensory appendages projecting from the 10th abdominal tergum in both males and females.
  • Extremely sensitive to low-frequency substrate vibrations and subtle air movements, triggering instantaneous escape running reflexes when a predator approaches.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) Antennae: tactile, olfactory, and chemical environmental monitoring1 Mark
(b) Compound eyes: 2000 ommatidia per eye conferring mosaic nocturnal vision1 Mark
(c) Maxillary palps: taste, smell, and food manipulation in mouthparts1 Mark
(d) Anal cerci: detection of subtle ground and air vibrations triggering escape reflexes1 Mark
Question 12
NCERT Exercise
What are the cellular components of blood?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Formed Cellular Elements of Blood:
Human blood consists of fluid plasma (55%) and formed cellular elements (45%), which comprise three major cell types:

1. Red Blood Cells (RBCs / Erythrocytes):
  • Count & Shape: Most abundant (5.0 to 5.5 million/$\text{mm}^3$ of blood). Biconcave discs; lack nuclei, mitochondria, and Golgi apparatus at maturity in mammals to maximize hemoglobin volume.
  • Function: Contain iron-porphyrin protein hemoglobin (12–16 g/100 mL blood) which binds oxygen reversibly to form oxyhemoglobin for systemic tissue transport.
2. White Blood Cells (WBCs / Leukocytes):
  • Count & Characteristics: Nucleated cells (6,000–8,000/$\text{mm}^3$). Subdivided into:
    • Granulocytes (with cytoplasmic granules & lobed nuclei):
      • Neutrophils (60–65%): Phagocytic cells engulfing invading bacteria.
      • Eosinophils (2–3%): Resist parasitic infections and mediate allergic responses.
      • Basophils (0.5–1%): Secrete histamine, serotonin, and heparin in inflammatory responses.
    • Agranulocytes (without specific cytoplasmic granules):
      • Monocytes (6–8%): Large phagocytic cells maturing into tissue macrophages.
      • Lymphocytes (20–25%): B-cells (humoral antibody immunity) and T-cells (cell-mediated immunity).
3. Blood Platelets (Thrombocytes):
  • Cellular fragments (150,000 to 350,000/$\text{mm}^3$) budded from giant bone marrow cells called megakaryocytes.
  • Release thromboplastin and clotting factor cascades essential for hemostasis (blood coagulation).
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Erythrocytes (RBCs): biconcave, enucleate, hemoglobin transport of oxygen1 Mark
Leukocytes (WBCs): granulocytes (neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils) and agranulocytes (monocytes, lymphocytes)1 Mark
Thrombocytes (Platelets): origin from megakaryocytes and function in blood clotting1 Mark
Question 13
NCERT Exercise
Describe the female reproductive system of cockroach with a labelled diagram.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Anatomical Organization of Female Reproductive System in Cockroach:

1. Ovaries:
  • A pair of large, yellowish ovaries lying laterally in the 2nd to 6th abdominal segments embedded in fat bodies.
  • Each ovary is formed of a group of 8 ovarian tubules or ovarioles containing a linear chain of developing ova in various maturation stages.
2. Oviducts & Common Oviduct (Vagina):
  • The individual ovarioles of each ovary unite posteriorly into a short, wide lateral oviduct.
  • The two lateral oviducts join in the midline to form a single, broad median oviduct called the vagina, opening into the female genital chamber (bursa copulatrix).
3. Spermathecae:
  • A pair of unequal, pyriform sacs located in the 6th abdominal segment, opening by a common pore into the dorsal wall of the genital chamber. They receive and store viable spermatozoa delivered during copulation.
4. Collaterial Glands:
  • A pair of large, branched, tubular glands situated behind the ovaries. They secrete the hard, proteinaceous, dark-brown sclerotized casing of the egg capsule called the ootheca.
5. Genital Pouch (Chamber) & Gonapophyses:
  • Formed by the 7th, 8th, and 9th sterna (7th sternum is boat-shaped). Contains three pairs of chitinous valvate blades called gonapophyses (ovipositor) to guide eggs into oothecae.
  • Each ootheca encapsulates 16 fertilized eggs arranged in two parallel rows.

Pair of ovaries in 2nd-6th segments, each having 8 ovarioles with linear chains of ova1 Mark
Lateral oviducts uniting into median vagina opening into genital chamber1 Mark
Pair of spermathecae for storing sperm & collaterial glands forming ootheca1 Mark
Genital pouch (7th, 8th, 9th sterna) and 16 eggs per ootheca1 Mark
Question 14
NCERT Exercise
Distinguish between a male frog and a female frog.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Morphological & Anatomical Sexual Dimorphism in Frog (Rana tigrina):

Characteristic Feature Male Frog Female Frog
1. Vocal Sacs Present. Possesses a pair of bluish wrinkled vocal sacs beneath the throat that amplify croaking to attract females during the monsoon breeding season. Absent. Vocal sacs are completely absent; produces only faint sounds.
2. Copulatory (Nuptial) Pad Present. Develops a thickened, swollen, sticky nuptial pad on the ventral base of the first digit of the forelimb to firmly grip the female during amplexus. Absent. Forelimb digits are smooth with no copulatory pad.
3. Body Size & Abdomen Relatively smaller, slender body with a narrower abdomen. Noticeably larger in body size; abdomen becomes visibly distended and bulky during the breeding season due to thousands of ova.
4. Ureter Function Ureter functions as a urinogenital duct (conducts both urine and spermatozoa into cloaca). Ureter acts strictly as a urinary duct (transports only urine; coiled oviducts open separately into cloaca).
5. Bidder's Canal Present inside the kidney to receive sperm from vasa efferentia. Completely absent.
