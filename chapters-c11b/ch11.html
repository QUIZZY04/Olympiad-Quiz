Question 1
NCERT Exercise
By looking at a plant externally can you tell whether a plant is C3 or C4? Why and how?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Answer & Scientific Explanation:
No, by merely observing the external morphology of a plant, one cannot definitively tell whether a plant possesses the $C_3$ or $C_4$ photosynthetic pathway.

Scientific Reasons:
  • The distinction between $C_3$ and $C_4$ plants is grounded entirely in internal leaf anatomy (Kranz anatomy) and sub-cellular biochemistry (the initial carboxylation enzyme: RuBisCO in $C_3$ vs. PEP carboxylase in $C_4$), not in gross macroscopic leaf morphology.
  • However, an informed ecological deduction can often be made based on habitat and taxonomic family:
    • $C_4$ plants are predominantly tropical and subtropical grasses adapted to arid, high-temperature, high-light environments (e.g., Maize, Sugarcane, Sorghum, Amaranth).
    • $C_4$ plants frequently possess xerophytic features (thick cuticle, vertically oriented linear leaves to minimize transpiration) and maintain high vigor under intense sunlight and drought without wilting.
    • Conversely, typical temperate crops like Wheat, Rice, Barley, and Spinach are $C_3$ plants.
Conclusion: Definitive confirmation requires examining a transverse section of the leaf under a microscope to check for Kranz anatomy.

Question 2
NCERT Exercise
By looking at which internal structure of a plant can you tell whether a plant is C3 or C4? Explain.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Internal Anatomical Structure: Kranz Anatomy
A plant can be definitively classified as $C_3$ or $C_4$ by examining the microscopic transverse section of its leaf for the presence or absence of Kranz anatomy (from the German word 'Kranz' meaning wreath or crown):

1. Anatomical Features of $C_4$ Plants (Kranz Anatomy Present):
  • Bundle Sheath Ring: Vascular bundles are encircled by one or several concentric layers of large, tightly packed cells called bundle sheath cells, forming a prominent wreath-like ring.
  • Dimorphic Chloroplasts:
    • Bundle sheath chloroplasts: Exceptionally large, lack grana (agranal), and store abundant starch grains. Rich in RuBisCO.
    • Mesophyll chloroplasts: Smaller, possess well-developed grana (granal), and rich in PEP carboxylase (lack RuBisCO).
  • No Intercellular Spaces: Bundle sheath cells have thick walls impervious to gaseous exchange and lack intercellular spaces, trapping $CO_2$.
2. Anatomical Features of $C_3$ Plants (Kranz Anatomy Absent):
  • Leaves lack specialized bundle sheath rings. Mesophyll is differentiated into palisade and spongy parenchyma, and all chloroplasts are uniform (monomorphic) and granal.

Question 3
NCERT Exercise
Even though very few cells in a C4 plant carry out the biosynthetic-Calvin pathway, yet they are highly productive. Can you discuss why?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Why $C_4$ Plants Exhibit Superior Photosynthetic Productivity:
In $C_4$ plants (e.g., Maize, Sugarcane), the Calvin cycle occurs exclusively in the specialized bundle sheath cells. Despite this spatial localization, $C_4$ plants have nearly double the photosynthetic yield of $C_3$ plants due to the following evolutionary adaptations:

1. Complete Elimination of Photorespiration ($C_2$ Cycle):
  • In $C_3$ plants, RuBisCO binds $O_2$ under high light and temperature, initiating photorespiration which wastes over 25–40% of fixed carbon and ATP without producing sugars.
  • In $C_4$ plants, photorespiration is completely absent.
2. Efficient $CO_2$-Concentrating Mechanism:
  • Primary $CO_2$ fixation occurs in mesophyll cells via PEP carboxylase, an enzyme with exceptionally high affinity for $HCO_3^-$ and zero affinity for oxygen ($O_2$).
  • $C_4$ organic acids (malate / aspartate) are shuttled into bundle sheath cells and decarboxylated, releasing a massive surge of $CO_2$ directly around RuBisCO.
  • This elevates the internal $CO_2$ concentration at the RuBisCO active site to saturating levels, completely suppressing its oxygenase activity and ensuring 100% carboxylation efficiency.
3. Tolerance to High Temperature and Water Stress:
  • $C_4$ plants have higher temperature optima (30°C–45°C) and twice the water-use efficiency of $C_3$ plants, losing half as much water per gram of fixed $CO_2$.

Question 4
NCERT Exercise
RuBisCO is an enzyme that acts both as a carboxylase and oxygenase. Why do you think RuBisCO carries out more carboxylation in C4 plants?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Why RuBisCO Carboxylase Activity Dominates in $C_4$ Plants:

1. Dual Catalytic Affinity of RuBisCO:
RuBisCO (Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase-oxygenase) possesses a single active site that binds both $CO_2$ and $O_2$ competitively. Whether it acts as a carboxylase (synthesizing $PGA$) or an oxygenase (initiating photorespiration forming phosphoglycolate) is determined by the relative ratio of $CO_2$ to $O_2$ concentration at its catalytic site.

2. Spatial Separation and the $C_4$ Biochemical Pump:
  • In $C_4$ plants, RuBisCO is strictly localized inside the bundle sheath cells (completely absent in mesophyll cells).
  • Mesophyll cells fix atmospheric $CO_2$ into 4-carbon malate/aspartate via PEP carboxylase.
  • These $C_4$ acids are translocated through plasmodesmata into bundle sheath cells, where they are actively decarboxylated to release $CO_2$ and pyruvate.
  • This active decarboxylation maintains an artificially enormous partial pressure of $CO_2$ immediately around RuBisCO.
  • Furthermore, bundle sheath chloroplasts lack granal thylakoids (agranal), meaning photolysis of water ($O_2$ evolution) does not occur in bundle sheath cells; hence, local $O_2$ levels remain negligible.
Conclusion: With overwhelming $CO_2$ concentration and virtually no $O_2$ competition, RuBisCO functions exclusively as a carboxylase.

Question 5
NCERT Exercise
Suppose there were plants that had a high concentration of Chlorophyll b, but lacked chlorophyll a, would it carry out photosynthesis? Then why do plants have accessory pigments?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Could a Plant Without Chlorophyll a Carry Out Photosynthesis?
No, the plant would not be able to carry out photosynthesis.
Biological Justification: Chlorophyll $a$ is the essential primary reaction center pigment (forming $P680$ in PS II and $P700$ in PS I). Only chlorophyll $a$ has the unique biophysical redox property of ejecting high-energy electrons upon photo-excitation to drive the photochemical electron transport chain and photolysis of water. All other pigments merely funnel excitation resonance energy to chlorophyll $a$.

2. Why Do Plants Have Accessory Pigments (Chlorophyll b, Carotenoids, Xanthophylls)?
Accessory pigments are indispensable for two vital adaptive reasons:
  • Broadening the Absorption Spectrum of Light: Chlorophyll $a$ absorbs light primarily in the blue and red wavelengths. Accessory pigments absorb light at intermediary wavelengths (blue-green, yellow) where chlorophyll $a$ has low absorption, and transfer this harvested photon energy to the reaction center via resonance energy transfer (inductive resonance).
  • Photoprotection Against Photo-Oxidation: Under intense solar irradiance, excess light can generate toxic reactive oxygen species (singlet oxygen) that destroy chlorophyll $a$. Carotenoids (carotenes and xanthophylls) act as molecular "shields" that quench excess excitation energy and dissipate it safely as heat, preventing photo-oxidative solarization.

Question 6
NCERT Exercise
Why is the colour of a leaf kept in the dark frequently yellow, or pale green? Which pigment do you think is more stable?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Why Leaves Kept in the Dark Turn Yellow or Pale Green (Etiolation):
  • The biosynthesis of green chlorophyll is light-dependent: light is strictly required to convert the precursor protochlorophyllide into active chlorophyll $a$ and $b$.
  • When a green plant is placed in prolonged darkness, new chlorophyll cannot be synthesized. Furthermore, existing chlorophyll molecules undergo continuous enzymatic degradation and breakdown by the enzyme chlorophyllase.
  • As chlorophyll degrades, the underlying yellow/orange carotenoid pigments (carotenes and xanthophylls) are unmasked, turning the leaf pale yellow — a physiological condition called etiolation.
2. Which Pigment is More Stable?
Carotenoids are significantly more chemically stable than chlorophylls.
  • Chlorophyll molecules possess a delicate porphyrin ring with a central magnesium atom that rapidly degrades in the dark or under extreme stress.
  • Carotenoids are stable tetraterpenoid hydrocarbons that persist much longer in the absence of light, accounting for the characteristic yellow foliage in dark-kept plants and autumn leaves.

Question 7
NCERT Exercise
Look at leaves of the same plant on the shady side and on the sunny side of a tree, or compare the leaves of potted plant kept in sunlight with one kept in shade. Which of them has a darker green colour? Why?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Answer & Scientific Explanation:
The leaves grown on the shady side (or shade-adapted plant) have a distinctly darker green colour than leaves exposed to direct bright sunlight.

Physiological & Photobiological Reasons:
  • 1. Compensatory Pigment Synthesis in Low Light: In shade conditions, light intensity is a limiting factor. To optimize photon capture in dim, diffused light, shade-adapted leaves synthesize a higher concentration of chlorophyll (especially chlorophyll $b$) and package more thylakoids per granum. This elevated chlorophyll density per unit leaf area imparts a rich, dark-green appearance.
  • 2. Horizontal Chloroplast Orientation: In shade leaves, chloroplasts orient themselves parallel to the leaf surface (perpendicular to incident light rays) along the upper walls of palisade cells to intercept maximum available photons.
  • 3. Sun Leaves Adapt to Avoid Photo-Oxidation: Leaves on the sunny side receive saturating light intensities. They possess fewer chlorophyll molecules per chloroplast to avoid photo-inhibition and light-induced solarization. Their chloroplasts align along vertical lateral cell walls (parastrophy) to minimize excessive light absorption, making sun leaves appear lighter, yellowish-green.

Analysis of Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors Light Curve:

  • (a) At which point/s is light a limiting factor?
    Light is a limiting factor at Point A (and along the linear ascending slope between A and B). In this region, any increase in light intensity results in a direct, proportional increase in the rate of photosynthesis.
  • (b) What could be the limiting factor/s in region A?

    • Point C represents Light Saturation: The plateau point where photosynthesis reaches its maximum velocity ($V_{max}$). Here, light is no longer limiting; other factors ($CO_2$ concentration or enzymatic temperature) have become limiting. Light saturation occurs at roughly 10% of full sunlight.
    • Region D represents Photo-inhibition / Solarization: If light intensity is increased beyond point D, the rate of photosynthesis does not increase; rather, excessive radiation causes the breakdown (photo-oxidation) of chlorophyll pigments and destructive photo-inhibition.

Question 10
NCERT Exercise
What are the external and internal factors that affect photosynthesis?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Factors Regulating the Rate of Photosynthesis (Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors):

A. External (Environmental) Factors:
  • 1. Light: Governed by light intensity, wavelength quality (maximum in red and blue regions), and photoperiod duration. Light saturation occurs at ~10% full sunlight.
  • 2. Carbon Dioxide Concentration: Major limiting factor in atmosphere (~0.04% or 400 ppm). Increasing $CO_2$ up to 0.05% (500 ppm) increases photosynthesis ($C_4$ saturates at 360 ppm, $C_3$ saturates at 450 ppm; exploited in greenhouse crops like tomatoes and bell peppers).
  • 3. Temperature: Dark enzymatic reactions are temperature-sensitive. $C_4$ plants have a high optimum (30°C–45°C), whereas $C_3$ plants have a lower optimum (20°C–25°C).
  • 4. Water: Water stress causes stomatal closure, choking $CO_2$ supply, and causes leaf wilting, reducing surface area.
B. Internal (Plant) Factors:
  • 1. Number, Size, Age and Orientation of Leaves: Young fully expanded leaves perpendicular to sunlight display maximal photosynthetic rates.
  • 2. Mesophyll Cells & Chloroplasts: Higher number of chloroplasts per cell accelerates photosynthetic capacity.
  • 3. Internal $CO_2$ Concentration: Maintained by stomatal aperture conductance.
  • 4. Amount of Chlorophyll: Adequate chlorophyll concentration is necessary for light harvesting.
