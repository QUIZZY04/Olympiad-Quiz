Question 1
NCERT Exercise
Define Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR).
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Definition of Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR):
The Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) is the total volume of protein-free filtrate formed in the Bowman's capsules of both kidneys combined per unit of time (typically expressed per minute).

Standard Physiological Values:
  • In a healthy adult human, the normal GFR is approximately 125 mL/minute.
  • This equates to a staggering 180 Litres per day ($125 \text{ mL/min} \times 60 \times 24 = 180,000\text{ mL/day}$).
Significance of Massive Tubular Reabsorption:
Although 180 Litres of filtrate are produced daily, the average volume of urine released is only about 1.5 Litres per day. This demonstrates that approximately 99% of the glomerular filtrate is reabsorbed by the renal tubules back into blood peritubular capillaries.

Question 2
NCERT Exercise
Explain the autoregulatory mechanism of GFR. (Myogenic mechanism and Juxtaglomerular Apparatus - JGA via Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System / RAAS).
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Autoregulatory Feedback Mechanisms of GFR in Kidneys:
Human kidneys possess an efficient intrinsic autoregulatory mechanism that maintains a relatively constant GFR despite wide physiological fluctuations in systemic arterial blood pressure (between 80 and 180 mm Hg):

1. Juxtaglomerular Apparatus (JGA) & RAAS Feedback:
  • Structure of JGA: A specialized microscopic neuro-endocrine structure formed by the cellular modification of the distal convoluted tubule (macula densa) and the afferent arteriole (juxtaglomerular / JG cells) at their point of contact.
  • Activation Cascade:
    1. A fall in glomerular blood flow / blood pressure / GFR stimulates the JG cells to synthesize and release the proteolytic enzyme Renin into circulation.
    2. Renin cleaves circulating plasma angiotensinogen into angiotensin I.
    3. Angiotensin Converting Enzyme (ACE) in lung capillaries converts it into Angiotensin II, a potent systemic vasoconstrictor that elevates glomerular hydrostatic pressure and GFR.
    4. Angiotensin II also stimulates the adrenal cortex to secrete Aldosterone, which drives active $Na^+$ and water reabsorption in the DCT and collecting duct, expanding blood volume and normalizing GFR.
2. Myogenic Autoregulatory Mechanism:
When renal arterial pressure rises, the smooth muscle of the afferent arteriole automatically stretches and reflexively constricts (myogenic contraction), increasing vascular resistance to prevent damage to delicate glomerular capillaries.

Question 3
NCERT Exercise
Indicate whether the following statements are true or false:
(a) Micturition is carried out by a reflex.
(b) ADH helps in water elimination, making urine hypotonic.
(c) Protein-free fluid is filtered from blood plasma into the Bowman's capsule.
(d) Henle's loop plays an important role in concentrating the urine.
(e) Glucose is actively reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule.
[2.5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Validation of Statements with Scientific Explanations:

  • (a) Micturition is carried out by a reflex. — TRUE
    Reason: Stretching of the urinary bladder wall stimulates sensory stretch receptors, transmitting signals to the CNS to initiate the autonomic micturition reflex.
  • (b) ADH helps in water elimination, making urine hypotonic. — FALSE
    Correction: ADH (Antidiuretic Hormone) prevents diuresis; it facilitates water reabsorption from the DCT and collecting duct, making the excreted urine concentrated and hypertonic.
  • (c) Protein-free fluid is filtered from blood plasma into the Bowman's capsule. — TRUE
    Reason: Ultrafiltration across the three-layered glomerular membrane allows water, glucose, urea, and ions to pass, but completely blocks large plasma proteins and formed cellular elements.
  • (d) Henle's loop plays an important role in concentrating the urine. — TRUE
    Reason: The Loop of Henle functions as a counter-current multiplier, establishing an osmotic gradient from 300 to 1200 mOsm/L in the medullary interstitium to extract water.
  • (e) Glucose is actively reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule. — TRUE
    Reason: 100% of filtered glucose is reabsorbed in the PCT via secondary active $Na^+$-glucose co-transporters (SGLT-2).

Question 4
NCERT Exercise
Give a brief account of the counter current mechanism.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
The Counter-Current Mechanism of Urine Concentration in Mammals:
Humans excrete urine that is nearly four times more concentrated (1200 mOsm/L) than initial glomerular filtrate (300 mOsm/L). This exceptional water-conserving capacity is generated by the counter-current mechanism operating between the Loop of Henle (multiplier) and the Vasa Recta (exchanger) in juxtamedullary nephrons:

1. Meaning of Counter-Current Flow:
  • In the two limbs of the Loop of Henle, tubular filtrate flows in opposite directions (downward in descending limb, upward in ascending limb).
  • Similarly, blood flows in opposite directions in the two parallel limbs of the capillary vasa recta.
2. Differential Permeability of the Loop of Henle (The Multiplier):
  • Descending Limb: Highly permeable to water ($H_2O$) via abundant aquaporin-1 channels, but virtually impermeable to electrolytes ($NaCl$). As filtrate descends, water is drawn into the hypertonic medullary interstitium by osmosis, concentrating the filtrate up to 1200 mOsm/L at the hairpin tip.
  • Ascending Limb: Completely impermeable to water, but permeable to electrolytes. Its thick segment actively pumps out $NaCl$ into the medullary interstitium, maintaining high medullary hyperosmolarity while diluting tubular filtrate.
3. Role of Vasa Recta (The Exchanger):
  • $NaCl$ enters the descending limb of vasa recta and is returned to interstitium by ascending limb. Water absorbed from the descending loop of Henle is carried away by ascending vasa recta, preventing dilution of the medullary gradient.
4. Final Concentration in Collecting Duct:
As tubular fluid passes down the collecting duct through the hypertonic medullary gradient (300 → 600 → 900 → 1200 mOsm/L), water is continuously reabsorbed into blood under ADH regulation, producing hypertonic urine.

Question 5
NCERT Exercise
Describe the role of liver, lungs and skin in excretion.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Excretory Roles of Accessory Organs Beyond Kidneys:

1. Lungs:
  • Continuously eliminate massive amounts of gaseous metabolic wastes: approximately 200 mL of carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) per minute (~18 Litres/day).
  • Also eliminate significant amounts of water vapor (~400 mL/day) in expired air.
2. Liver (The Primary Detoxifying & Urea Factory):
  • Synthesizes Urea via the Ornithine (Urea) Cycle from highly toxic ammonia ($NH_3$) produced during protein deamination.
  • Excretes bile pigments (Bilirubin and Biliverdin) derived from the catabolic degradation of hemoglobin from worn-out erythrocytes.
  • Excretes cholesterol, degraded steroid hormones, vitamins, and inactivated drugs through bile into the digestive tract, ultimately expelled in feces.
3. Skin (Integumentary Excretion):
  • Sweat Glands: Secrete watery sweat primarily for thermoregulation, which carries metabolic wastes including $NaCl$, small amounts of urea, lactic acid, and amino acids.
  • Sebaceous Glands: Secrete an oily lipid fluid called sebum, which eliminates sterols, fatty acids, hydrocarbons, and waxes, providing a protective antibacterial mantle.

Question 6
NCERT Exercise
Explain micturition.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Physiological Process of Micturition (Urination):

1. Definition:
Micturition is the physiological act of voluntarily releasing and emptying stored urine from the urinary bladder through the urethra to the outside of the body.

2. The Micturition Neural Reflex Arc:
  1. Bladder Filling & Stretch Activation: As urine accumulates in the bladder, its muscular walls stretch. When volume reaches roughly 300 to 400 mL, mechanoreceptive stretch receptors in the bladder wall are stimulated.
  2. Transmission to CNS: Stretch receptors generate sensory afferent action potentials transmitted to the sacral spinal cord micturition center and cerebral cortex.
  3. Parasympathetic Motor Outflow: In response, autonomic motor nerve fibers fire efferent impulses causing:
    • Active, sustained contraction of the smooth detrusor muscle of the bladder wall.
    • Simultaneous relaxation of the involuntary internal urethral sphincter.
  4. Voluntary Control: The external urethral sphincter consists of skeletal muscle innervated by the somatic pudendal nerve, controlled consciously by the cerebral cortex. When voluntary relaxation of the external sphincter is initiated, urine is forcefully expelled.

Question 8
NCERT Exercise
What is meant by the term osmoregulation?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Scientific Definition of Osmoregulation:
Osmoregulation is the physiological homeostatic process by which an organism actively maintains and regulates the optimal water balance and osmotic concentration (osmolarity) of its internal body fluids (blood, lymph, and intercellular tissue fluid) within narrow physiological limits, irrespective of fluctuations in external environmental salinity or humidity.

Core Objectives of Osmoregulation:
  • Prevents excessive intracellular swelling (cytolysis) in hypotonic environments.
  • Prevents cellular dehydration, shrinkage, and crenation in hypertonic environments.
  • Maintains strict electrolyte concentrations ($Na^+, K^+, Cl^-, Ca^{2+}, HCO_3^-$) to preserve the membrane resting potential of excitable neurons and muscle cells.
Primary Organs: Human kidneys, contractile vacuoles in freshwater amoeba, antennal green glands in prawns, and salt glands in marine birds.

Question 9
NCERT Exercise
Terrestrial animals are generally either ureotelic or uricotelic, not ammonotelic, why?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Why Terrestrial Animals Avoid Ammonotelism and Evolve Ureotelism / Uricotelism:

1. High Toxicity and Water Requirement of Ammonia:
  • Ammonia ($NH_3$) is the direct, primary metabolic product of amino acid deamination. However, it is extremely toxic to protoplasm because it readily crosses cell membranes, raises intracellular pH, and disrupts mitochondrial ATP synthesis.
  • To eliminate ammonia safely without poisoning tissues, it must be excreted in a highly dilute solution. The excretion of 1 gram of ammonia requires approximately 300 to 500 mL of water.
  • Only aquatic animals (aquatic amphibians, bony fishes, aquatic invertebrates) that have an unlimited supply of surrounding water can afford this massive water expenditure (ammonotelism).
2. Evolutionary Imperative of Water Conservation on Land:
  • Terrestrial animals face the chronic challenge of continuous water loss (transpiration, evaporation, feces) and limited freshwater availability.
  • Losing 500 mL of water per gram of nitrogen would lead to fatal dehydration in land animals.
  • Consequently, terrestrial animals utilize metabolic energy (ATP) to convert ammonia into far less toxic nitrogenous wastes:
    • Ureotelism (Mammals, adult amphibians): Ammonia is converted in the liver via the Ornithine cycle into Urea, which is 100,000 times less toxic than ammonia and requires only ~50 mL of water per gram.
    • Uricotelism (Reptiles, birds, land snails, insects): Ammonia is converted into Uric Acid, which is practically non-toxic, highly insoluble in water, and excreted as a thick semisolid paste with minimal water loss (~5 to 10 mL of water per gram), an adaptation crucial for flight weight reduction and cleidoic egg development.

Question 10
NCERT Exercise
What is the significance of juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA) in kidney function?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Crucial Physiological Significance of the Juxtaglomerular Apparatus (JGA):

1. Real-Time Sensing of Renal Perfusion and Osmolarity:
The JGA acts as an intelligent microscopic auto-feedback control center that constantly monitors:
  • Glomerular blood pressure and renal blood flow (via baroreceptive JG cells in the afferent arteriole).
  • Sodium chloride concentration ($[Na^+]$ and $[Cl^-]$) in the late tubular fluid (via chemoreceptive macula densa cells of the DCT).
2. The Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) Cascade:
When blood pressure, renal blood flow, or GFR drops: $$\text{JG Cells} \xrightarrow{\text{secrete}} \mathbf{Renin} \xrightarrow{+ \text{Angiotensinogen}} \text{Angiotensin I} \xrightarrow{+ \text{ACE}} \mathbf{Angiotensin II}$$
  • Potent Vasoconstriction: Angiotensin II constricts systemic arterioles and efferent glomerular arterioles, raising glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure and immediately restoring normal GFR.
  • Aldosterone Secretion: Stimulates adrenal cortex to release aldosterone, which increases active $Na^+$ and water reabsorption in the DCT and collecting duct.
  • Thirst Stimulation & ADH: Stimulates hypothalamus to induce drinking behavior and release ADH.
Conclusion: JGA prevents catastrophic renal failure during hypovolemia, hemorrhage, or severe dehydration.
