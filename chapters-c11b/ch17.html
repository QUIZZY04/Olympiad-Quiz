Figure 17.1: Microscopic Sarcomere Architecture & Sliding Filament Cross-Bridge Cycle
NCERT Textbook Exercise 1 to 10: Sarcomere Anatomy, Sliding Filament Theory, Muscle Contraction, Joints & Skeletal System
Question 1
NCERT Exercise
Draw the diagram of a sarcomere of skeletal muscle showing different regions.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Microscopic Diagrammatic Representation of a Sarcomere:
A sarcomere is the basic structural and functional contractile unit of a myofibril in skeletal muscle, extending from one Z-line to the adjacent Z-line (~2.5 μm in relaxed state).

Diagram: Structure of a Sarcomere (Relaxed Muscle Fiber)
Detailed Anatomical Regions of the Sarcomere:
  • 1. Z-Line (Krause's Membrane): A thin, dark, zigzag elastic protein disc (consisting of α-actinin) that bisects each I-band and firmly anchors the thin actin filaments. The repeating unit between two successive Z-lines constitutes a sarcomere.
  • 2. I-Band (Isotropic Band): The light band containing only thin actin filaments (does not contain thick filaments). It is isotropic under polarized light.
  • 3. A-Band (Anisotropic Band): The dark central band containing the entire length of the thick myosin filaments, partially overlapped at its lateral edges by thin actin filaments. Its length remains strictly constant during contraction.
  • 4. H-Zone (Hensen's Zone): The paler central region of the A-band that is not overlapped by thin actin filaments in the relaxed state. Contains thick filaments only.
  • 5. M-Line (Mittelscheibe): A delicate proteinaceous membrane running centrally down the H-zone that holds adjacent thick myosin filaments together.

Explanation of A-band (myosin), H-zone (un-overlapped zone), and central M-line1 Mark
Question 2
NCERT Exercise
Define the sliding filament theory of muscle contraction.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Definition of Sliding Filament Theory (H.E. Huxley and A.F. Huxley, 1954):
The sliding filament theory states that the contraction of a muscle fibre occurs by the sliding of thin (actin) filaments over thick (myosin) filaments towards the center of the sarcomere (M-line), without any physical shortening or change in length of either the actin or myosin filaments themselves.

Key Morphological Consequences During Contraction:
  • The two successive Z-lines are pulled closer together, leading to the overall shortening (contraction) of the sarcomere.
  • The I-bands (light bands) shorten in length as actin filaments overlap more deeply with myosin.
  • The H-zone narrows progressively and disappears completely during maximal contraction.
  • The A-bands (dark bands) retain their original, constant length throughout contraction and relaxation, because the length of thick myosin filaments does not change.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Precise definition: sliding of thin actin filaments over thick myosin filaments toward sarcomere center1 Mark
Structural changes: Z-lines pulled inward, I-bands shorten, H-zone disappears, A-band remains constant1 Mark
Question 3
NCERT Exercise
Describe the important steps in muscle contraction.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Sequential Physiological Steps in Skeletal Muscle Contraction:

  1. Neural Signal & Action Potential:
    A motor impulse from the Central Nervous System (CNS) travels along a somatic motor neuron to reach the neuromuscular junction (motor end-plate). The axon terminal releases the neurotransmitter acetylcholine (ACh) into the synaptic cleft, generating an action potential in the sarcolemma (muscle cell membrane).
  2. T-Tubule Transmission & Calcium Release:
    The action potential propagates rapidly over the sarcolemma and travels deep inside through transverse T-tubules. This depolarisation triggers the release of large quantities of stored calcium ions ($Ca^{2+}$) from the terminal cisternae of the sarcoplasmic reticulum into the sarcoplasm.
  3. Unmasking Active Sites on Actin:
    In relaxed muscle, the protein tropomyosin physically covers and masks the myosin-binding sites on actin. The released $Ca^{2+}$ binds specifically to the Troponin C subunit, inducing a conformational change that pulls tropomyosin aside, completely unmasking the active catalytic binding sites on F-actin.
  4. Cross-Bridge Formation & Power Stroke:
    The globular head of myosin acts as an active ATPase enzyme. It hydrolyzes ATP: $$ATP \xrightarrow{\text{Myosin ATPase}} ADP + P_i + \text{Energy}$$ The energized myosin head binds to the exposed active site on actin to form a cross-bridge.
    Myosin releases $ADP$ and $P_i$ and tilts its head $45^\circ$ toward the M-line (the power stroke), pulling the attached actin filaments inward toward the center of the sarcomere.
  5. Cross-Bridge Detachment & Reactivation:
    A fresh molecule of ATP binds to the myosin head, breaking the cross-bridge. Hydrolysis of this new ATP recocks the myosin head into its energized state, ready for another cycle of binding and pulling as long as $Ca^{2+}$ remains available.
  6. Relaxation:
    When motor nerve firing ceases, active calcium pumps ($Ca^{2+}$-ATPase) pump $Ca^{2+}$ ions back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum. With sarcoplasmic $[Ca^{2+}]$ dropping below $10^{-7}\text{ M}$, troponin returns to its resting state, tropomyosin re-masks the active sites on actin, and the thin filaments slide back to their relaxed position.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
1. Motor neuron impulse, ACh release, and sarcolemmal action potential1 Mark
2. T-tubule depolarization and Ca2+ release from sarcoplasmic reticulum1 Mark
3. Ca2+ binding to Troponin C and unmasking of active sites on actin1 Mark
4. ATP hydrolysis, cross-bridge formation, power stroke, and inward sliding of actin1 Mark
5. Cross-bridge detachment by fresh ATP and active Ca2+ reuptake leading to relaxation1 Mark
Question 4
NCERT Exercise
Write true or false. If false, change the statement so that it is true:
(a) Actin is present in the thin filament.
(b) H-zone of striated muscle fibre represents both thick and thin filaments.
(c) The human skeleton has 206 bones.
(d) There are 11 pairs of ribs in man.
(e) The sternum is present on the ventral side of the body.
[2.5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Evaluation and Scientific Corrections of Statements:

  • (a) Actin is present in the thin filament. — TRUE
    Scientific Basis: Thin myofilaments consist of two helically wound filamentous F-actin polymers, accompanied by tropomyosin and troponin regulatory proteins.
  • (b) H-zone of striated muscle fibre represents both thick and thin filaments. — FALSE
    Corrected Statement: The H-zone of a striated muscle fibre represents only thick (myosin) filaments (it is the central region of the A-band not overlapped by thin actin filaments in the relaxed state).
  • (c) The human skeleton has 206 bones. — TRUE
    Scientific Basis: The adult human skeleton is composed of exactly 206 bones (80 in the axial skeleton and 126 in the appendicular skeleton).
  • (d) There are 11 pairs of ribs in man. — FALSE
    Corrected Statement: There are 12 pairs (24 total) of ribs in humans (pairs 1–7 are true vertebrosternal ribs, pairs 8–10 are false vertebrochondral ribs, and pairs 11–12 are floating vertebral ribs).
  • (e) The sternum is present on the ventral side of the body. — TRUE
    Scientific Basis: The sternum (breastbone) is a flat bone situated along the mid-ventral axis of the thoracic cage.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) True0.5 Mark
(b) False & corrected: H-zone contains only thick myosin filaments without actin overlap0.5 Mark
(c) True0.5 Mark
(d) False & corrected: 12 pairs of ribs in humans0.5 Mark
(e) True0.5 Mark
Question 5
NCERT Exercise
Write the difference between:
(a) Actin and Myosin
(b) Red and White muscles
(c) Pectoral and Pelvic girdle
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Differences Between Actin and Myosin Filaments:
Feature Actin (Thin Filament) Myosin (Thick Filament)
Diameter & Structure Thin (~7 nm); polymer of two helically wound F-actins. Thick (~15 nm); polymer of ~300 meromyosin molecules.
Associated Proteins Contains regulatory proteins tropomyosin and troponin. Composed of heavy meromyosin (HMM) and light meromyosin (LMM).
Location in Sarcomere Found in the light isotropic I-band; anchored to the Z-line. Forms the dark anisotropic A-band; held centrally by the M-line.
Enzymatic Activity Lacks intrinsic ATPase activity. Globular head possesses active ATPase site and actin-binding site.
(b) Differences Between Red and White Muscle Fibres:
Feature Red Muscle Fibres (Type I / Aerobic) White Muscle Fibres (Type II / Anaerobic)
Myoglobin Content High concentration (gives deep red appearance). Very low / scarce (gives pale, whitish appearance).
Mitochondria & Respiration Abundant mitochondria; relies on aerobic respiration. Fewer mitochondria; relies on anaerobic glycolysis.
Contraction & Fatigue Slow, sustained contraction; highly resistant to fatigue (e.g., extensor back muscles). Fast, powerful twitch; fatigues rapidly due to lactic acid build-up (e.g., eyeball muscles).
Sarcoplasmic Reticulum Moderate development. Extensively developed for explosive, fast $Ca^{2+}$ release.
(c) Differences Between Pectoral and Pelvic Girdle:
Feature Pectoral Girdle (Shoulder) Pelvic Girdle (Hip)
Location & Limb Supported Located in shoulder region; articulates the upper forelimbs. Located in hip/pelvic region; articulates the lower hindlimbs.
Bone Composition Composed of 2 halves; each has 2 bones: clavicle (collar bone) and scapula. Composed of 2 coxal bones; each formed by the fusion of 3 bones: ilium, ischium, and pubis.
Articulating Cavity Features a shallow glenoid cavity for the head of the humerus (high mobility). Features a deep cup-shaped acetabulum for the head of the femur (weight-bearing & stability).
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) Actin (thin, I-band, troponin/tropomyosin) vs Myosin (thick, A-band, HMM/LMM, ATPase)1.5 Marks
(b) Red muscle (high myoglobin, aerobic, fatigue-resistant) vs White muscle (anaerobic, rapid fatigue)2 Marks
(c) Pectoral girdle (clavicle, scapula, glenoid cavity) vs Pelvic girdle (ilium, ischium, pubis, acetabulum)1.5 Marks

Question 7
NCERT Exercise
What are the different types of movements exhibited by the cells of the human body?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
The Three Major Types of Movements Exhibited by Human Cells:

1. Amoeboid Movement:
  • Mechanism: Effected by streaming of cytoplasm (protoplasm) and the formation of blunt pseudopodial projections, driven by actin microfilaments of the cytoskeleton.
  • Examples in Humans: Macrophage leukocytes and neutrophil leukocytes crawling through capillary endothelial walls via diapedesis to engulf cellular debris and invading pathogens in infected tissues.
2. Ciliary Movement:
  • Mechanism: Coordinated, rhythmic, wave-like beating of thousands of hair-like cilia ($9 + 2$ axoneme structure) lining luminal surfaces.
  • Examples in Humans:
    • Inner ciliated epithelium of the respiratory tract (trachea and bronchi) sweeping inhaled dust particles and trapped mucus upward toward the throat.
    • Inner ciliated lining of the Fallopian tubes (oviducts) propelling the ovum from the ovary toward the uterus.
3. Muscular Movement:
  • Mechanism: Produced by the coordinated contraction and relaxation of specialized muscular tissues containing actin-myosin contractile machinery.
  • Examples in Humans: Movement of the limbs (walking, running), jaws (mastication), tongue (speech and swallowing), and peristalsis of the digestive canal.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
1. Amoeboid movement: pseudopodia, actin microfilaments, macrophages/neutrophils (diapedesis)1 Mark
2. Ciliary movement: coordinated cilia in respiratory tract & Fallopian tubes1 Mark
3. Muscular movement: contractile actin-myosin in limbs, jaw, tongue, and locomotion1 Mark
Question 8
NCERT Exercise
How do you distinguish between a skeletal muscle and a cardiac muscle?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Comparative Analysis: Skeletal Muscle vs. Cardiac Muscle:

Feature Skeletal Muscle Cardiac Muscle
Location Attached to bones of skeleton (biceps, triceps, quadriceps). Confined exclusively to the myocardium (wall of the heart).
Voluntary / Involuntary Voluntary (under conscious control of the somatic nervous system). Involuntary (governed automatically by cardiac pacemakers & ANS).
Fibre Morphology Long, cylindrical, unbranched fibres. Short, cylindrical, branched and interconnected fibres.
Nuclear Condition Multinucleated (syncytial); nuclei located peripherally. Uninucleated (or binucleated); nucleus centrally located.
Intercalated Discs Absent. Present (specialized gap junctions ensuring wave synchronization as a functional syncytium).
Fatigue Resistance Fatigues relatively quickly with prolonged vigorous exertion. Never fatigues; contracts rhythmically throughout lifetime without rest.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Location and voluntary control vs involuntary cardiac regulation1 Mark
Morphology (unbranched syncytial vs branched uninucleated)1 Mark
Presence of intercalated discs and fatigue resistance in cardiac muscle1 Mark
Question 9
NCERT Exercise
Name the type of joint between the following:
(a) Atlas / axis
(b) Carpal / metacarpal of thumb
(c) Between phalanges
(d) Femur / acetabulum
(e) Between cranial bones
(f) Between pubic bones in the pelvic girdle.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Precise Anatomical Identification of Joint Types:

Articulating Bones Specific Joint Classification Movement Permitted
(a) Atlas and Axis (C1 & C2) Pivot Joint (Synovial) Uniaxial rotation around dens odontoid process ("no" movement of head).
(b) Carpal and Metacarpal of Thumb Saddle Joint (Synovial) Biaxial movement permitting opposable thumb rotation and grasp.
(c) Between Phalanges Hinge Joint (Synovial) Uniaxial flexion and extension in a single angular plane.
(d) Femur and Acetabulum Ball and Socket Joint (Hip Joint) Multiaxial movement in all planes with high stability for locomotion.
(e) Between Cranial Bones Fibrous Joint (Sutures) Completely immovable synarthrosis protecting the delicate brain.
(f) Between Pubic Bones in Pelvic Girdle Cartilaginous Joint (Pubic Symphysis) Slightly movable amphiarthrosis; relaxes via relaxin hormone during childbirth.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) Pivot joint & (b) Saddle joint1 Mark
(c) Hinge joint & (d) Ball and socket joint1 Mark
(e) Fibrous joint (sutures) & (f) Cartilaginous joint (pubic symphysis)1 Mark
Question 10
NCERT Exercise
Fill in the blank spaces:
(a) All mammals (except a few) have _______ cervical vertebra.
(b) The number of phalanges in each limb of human is _______.
(c) Thin filament of myofibril contains 2 'F' actins and two other proteins namely _______ and _______.
(d) In a muscle fibre $Ca^{++}$ is stored in _______.
(e) _______ and _______ pairs of ribs are called floating ribs.
(f) The human cranium is made up of _______ bones.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Accurate Textual Completions:

  • (a) All mammals (except a few like sloths and manatees) have seven (7) cervical vertebrae.
  • (b) The number of phalanges in each limb of human is 14 (fourteen) (phalangeal formula: $2, 3, 3, 3, 3$).
  • (c) Thin filament of myofibril contains 2 'F' actins and two other proteins namely tropomyosin and troponin.
  • (d) In a muscle fibre $Ca^{++}$ is stored in the sarcoplasmic reticulum (cisternae).
  • (e) 11th and 12th pairs of ribs are called floating ribs (their ventral ends are completely free and unattached).
  • (f) The human cranium is made up of eight (8) bones (1 frontal, 2 parietal, 2 temporal, 1 occipital, 1 sphenoid, 1 ethmoid).
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) seven (7) cervical vertebrae & (b) 14 phalanges1 Mark
(c) tropomyosin and troponin & (d) sarcoplasmic reticulum1 Mark
