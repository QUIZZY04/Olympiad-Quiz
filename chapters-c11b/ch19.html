Question 1
NCERT Exercise
Define the following:
(a) Exocrine gland
(b) Endocrine gland
(c) Hormone
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Precise Scientific Definitions:

  • (a) Exocrine Gland: Glands that pour their secretions (enzymes, mucus, sweat, sebum, saliva) through specialized ducts or tubes directly to target surfaces or internal cavities. Examples: Salivary glands, sweat glands, sebaceous glands, gastric glands, and exocrine pancreas (acini).
  • (b) Endocrine Gland: Ductless glands that lack excretory ducts and pour their secretions (hormones) directly into the surrounding extracellular fluid and venous bloodstream, which transports them to distant target tissues. Examples: Pituitary gland, thyroid gland, adrenal gland, and parathyroid gland.
  • (c) Hormone (Modern NCERT Definition): Hormones are non-nutrient chemicals that act as intercellular messengers, are produced in trace amounts by endocrine glands or specialized neurosecretory tissues, and are transported via the circulatory system to regulate physiological and biochemical processes in distant specific target cells.

Question 2
NCERT Exercise
Diagrammatically indicate the location of the various endocrine glands in our body.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Anatomical Locations of Major Endocrine Glands in the Human Body:

Diagram: Spatial Distribution of Human Endocrine Glands
Detailed Anatomical Distribution:
  • 1. Hypothalamus: Basal part of the diencephalon, forebrain.
  • 2. Pituitary Gland: Located in a bony cavity of the sphenoid bone termed the sella turcica, attached to the hypothalamus via an infundibular stalk.
  • 3. Pineal Gland: Situated on the dorsal side of the forebrain.
  • 4. Thyroid Gland: Located in the neck on either side of the trachea, interconnected across the midline by a thin flap of tissue called the isthmus.
  • 5. Parathyroid Glands: Four small glands embedded on the posterior surface of the two lobes of the thyroid gland (one pair on each lobe).
  • 6. Thymus Gland: Located in the anterior thoracic mediastinum between the lungs, dorsal to the sternum on the ventral side of the aorta.
  • 7. Adrenal Glands: One pair of suprarenal glands situated atop the anterior pole of each kidney.
  • 8. Pancreas: Located in the loop of the duodenum in the abdominal cavity (heterocrine gland; endocrine part consists of Islets of Langerhans).
  • 9. Gonads: Testes located extra-abdominally inside the scrotal sac in males; Ovaries located in the lower abdominal pelvic cavity in females.

Question 3
NCERT Exercise
List the hormones secreted by the following:
(a) Hypothalamus
(b) Pituitary
(c) Thyroid
(d) Parathyroid
(e) Adrenal
(f) Pancreas
(g) Testis
(h) Ovary
(i) Thymus
(j) Atrium
(k) Kidney
(l) G-I Tract
[6 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Complete Comprehensive Hormonal Secretory Inventory:

  • (a) Hypothalamus: Releasing hormones: GnRH, TRH, CRH, GHRH, PRH; Inhibiting hormones: Somatostatin (GHIH) and Dopamine (PIH). Synthesizes Oxytocin and Vasopressin (ADH).
  • (b) Pituitary Gland:
    • Anterior Pituitary (Pars distalis): Growth Hormone (GH), Thyroid-Stimulating Hormone (TSH), Adrenocorticotropic Hormone (ACTH), Luteinizing Hormone (LH), Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH), and Prolactin (PRL).
    • Intermediate Lobe (Pars intermedia): Melanocyte-Stimulating Hormone (MSH).
    • Posterior Pituitary (Pars nervosa): Releases Oxytocin and Vasopressin (synthesized by hypothalamus).
  • (c) Thyroid Gland: Tetraiodothyronine / Thyroxine ($T_4$), Triiodothyronine ($T_3$), and Thyrocalcitonin (TCT — hypocalcemic hormone).
  • (d) Parathyroid Gland: Parathyroid Hormone (PTH / Collip's hormone — hypercalcemic hormone).
  • (e) Adrenal Glands:
    • Adrenal Cortex: Mineralocorticoids (Aldosterone), Glucocorticoids (Cortisol), and Androgenic corticoids (DHEA).
    • Adrenal Medulla: Catecholamines — Adrenaline (Epinephrine) and Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine).
  • (f) Pancreas (Islets of Langerhans): α-cells secrete Glucagon (hyperglycemic); β-cells secrete Insulin (hypoglycemic); δ-cells secrete Somatostatin.
  • (g) Testis: Androgens, primarily Testosterone (secreted by Leydig / interstitial cells) and Inhibin.
  • (h) Ovary: Estrogen (secreted by developing ovarian follicles), Progesterone (secreted by corpus luteum), Relaxin, and Inhibin.
  • (i) Thymus Gland: Thymosins (promotes differentiation and maturation of T-lymphocytes).
  • (j) Atrium of Heart: Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF) (causes vasodilation and lowers blood pressure).
  • (k) Kidney (JG Cells): Erythropoietin (EPO) (stimulates erythropoiesis in bone marrow) and Renin.
  • (l) Gastro-Intestinal (G-I) Tract: Gastrin (stimulates HCl & pepsinogen), Secretin (stimulates water and bicarbonate secretion from pancreas), Cholecystokinin (CCK) (stimulates bile from gallbladder and pancreatic enzymes), and Gastric Inhibitory Peptide (GIP) (inhibits gastric secretion).

Question 5
NCERT Exercise
Explain the mechanism of hormone action.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Dual Molecular Mechanisms of Hormone Action:
Hormones produce biochemical effects on target tissues by binding specifically to high-affinity target proteins called hormone receptors. A hormone binds only to its specific receptor, forming a hormone-receptor complex (H-R complex). Hormones act via two major biochemical pathways depending on their chemical nature:

1. Mechanism of Peptide / Water-Soluble Hormones (Membrane Receptor & Second Messengers):
• Hormones such as FSH, LH, TSH, ACTH, Insulin, and Glucagon are water-soluble proteins that cannot traverse the hydrophobic lipid bilayer of the plasma membrane.
• They bind to extracellular membrane-bound receptors on the cell surface.
• Formation of the H-R complex activates G-proteins and membrane-bound adenylate cyclase (or phospholipase C).
• This catalyzes the conversion of intracellular ATP into secondary messengers, primarily cyclic AMP (cAMP), $IP_3$, or $Ca^{2+}$.
• These second messengers activate specific protein kinases, initiating enzyme cascades and cellular biochemical responses (e.g., glycogenolysis, steroidogenesis, ovarian follicular growth).

2. Mechanism of Steroid and Iodothyronine Hormones (Intracellular Receptors & Gene Expression):
• Hormones such as Estrogen, Progesterone, Testosterone, Cortisol, Aldosterone, and Thyroid hormones ($T_3, T_4$) are lipid-soluble and diffuse freely across the plasma membrane.
• They bind to intracellular receptors (located either in the cytoplasm or mostly inside the nucleus).
• The hormone-receptor complex translocates into the nucleus and binds to specific Hormone Response Elements (HRE) on chromosomal DNA.
• This regulates transcription of target genes into messenger RNA (mRNA), directing the de novo synthesis of specific functional and structural proteins/enzymes, resulting in long-term physiological and morphological responses (e.g., uterine growth, tissue differentiation, metabolic rate regulation).

Question 6
NCERT Exercise
Give example(s) of:
(a) Hyperglycemic hormone and hypoglycemic hormone
(b) Hypercalcemic hormone
(c) Gonadotrophic hormones
(d) Progestational hormone
(e) Blood pressure lowering hormone
(f) Androgens and estrogens
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Precise Physiological Examples of Hormones:

  • (a) Hyperglycemic & Hypoglycemic Hormones:
    • Hyperglycemic Hormone (elevates blood glucose): Glucagon (also Cortisol, Adrenaline, Growth Hormone).
    • Hypoglycemic Hormone (lowers blood glucose): Insulin.
  • (b) Hypercalcemic Hormone:
    Parathyroid Hormone (PTH / Collip's hormone) (increases blood $Ca^{2+}$ levels; contrasted with hypocalcemic Thyrocalcitonin).
  • (c) Gonadotrophic Hormones:
    Luteinizing Hormone (LH) and Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH) (secreted by anterior pituitary gonadotrophs).
  • (d) Progestational Hormone:
    Progesterone (secreted by corpus luteum; maintains the endometrium for pregnancy).
  • (e) Blood Pressure Lowering Hormone:
    Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF) (released by heart atrial walls in response to elevated blood pressure; causes vasodilation and natriuresis).
  • (f) Androgens and Estrogens:
    • Androgen: Testosterone (and Dihydrotestosterone).
    • Estrogen: Estradiol (and Estrone, Estriol).

Question 7
NCERT Exercise
Which hormonal deficiency is responsible for the following:
(a) Diabetes mellitus
(b) Goitre
(c) Cretinism
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Hormonal Deficiencies and Associated Clinical Pathology:

  • (a) Diabetes Mellitus:
    • Deficient Hormone: Insulin (secreted by β-cells of Islets of Langerhans in the pancreas).
    • Pathology: Hyposecretion or cellular resistance to insulin prevents cellular glucose uptake, leading to prolonged hyperglycemia, glucosuria (glucose excretion in urine), and formation of harmful ketone bodies (ketoacidosis).
  • (b) Simple Goitre:
    • Deficient Hormone: Thyroxine ($T_4$) and Triiodothyronine ($T_3$) (due to dietary iodine deficiency).
    • Pathology: In the absence of dietary iodine, thyroid hormone production plummets. Lack of negative feedback triggers excessive pituitary TSH secretion, which overstimulates and enlarges the thyroid gland, presenting as a visible neck swelling.
  • (c) Cretinism:
    • Deficient Hormone: Thyroid hormones ($T_4, T_3$) during embryonic development, infancy, or early childhood (congenital hypothyroidism).
    • Pathology: Characterized by stunted physical growth, severe intellectual disability / mental retardation, deaf-mutism, abnormal skin, and delayed skeletal development.

Question 8
NCERT Exercise
Briefly mention the mechanism of action of FSH.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Mechanism of Action of Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH):

  1. Chemical Nature & Inability to Enter Cell: FSH is a glycoprotein (peptide) hormone. Because it is water-soluble and lipid-insoluble, it cannot cross the hydrophobic phospholipid bilayer of target ovarian granulosa cells.
  2. Membrane Receptor Binding: FSH binds specifically to high-affinity membrane-bound FSH receptors located on the outer surface of the ovarian cell membrane, forming a hormone-receptor complex.
  3. Generation of Second Messenger (cAMP): The formation of the hormone-receptor complex activates a stimulatory G-protein ($G_s$), which activates the enzyme adenylate cyclase on the inner face of the cell membrane. Adenylate cyclase converts ATP into cyclic AMP (cAMP) (the intracellular second messenger).
  4. Biochemical Cascades: Cyclic AMP activates protein kinase A (PKA), which phosphorylates specific intracellular enzymes and transcriptional factors.
  5. Physiological Response: These biochemical cascades stimulate ovarian follicular growth, maturation of Graafian follicles, and secretion of estrogen.

Question 10
NCERT Exercise
What are the differences between diabetes mellitus and diabetes insipidus?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Clinical Comparison: Diabetes Mellitus vs. Diabetes Insipidus:

Parameter Diabetes Mellitus Diabetes Insipidus
Hormonal Etiology Deficiency or cellular resistance to Insulin from pancreatic β-cells. Deficiency of Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH / Vasopressin) from hypothalamus/posterior pituitary.
Blood Glucose Level Elevated (Hyperglycemia > 140–200 mg/dL). Completely normal blood glucose level.
Urine Characteristics Contains glucose (glucosuria), high specific gravity, contains ketone bodies in severe cases. Contains no glucose; extremely dilute, tasteless (insipid) urine with very low specific gravity.
Polyuria Mechanism Osmotic diuresis caused by unreabsorbed luminal glucose pulling water. Failure of water reabsorption in collecting ducts due to absence of ADH-induced aquaporins (copious water loss up to 15–20 L/day).
Treatment Insulin injections, dietary carbohydrate regulation, metformin. Administration of synthetic vasopressin / desmopressin nasal spray.
