Question 1
NCERT Exercise
Differentiate between: (a) Respiration and Combustion (b) Glycolysis and Fermentation (c) Aerobic respiration and Anaerobic respiration.
[5 Marks]

TemperatureOccurs at constant physiological body temperature (~37°C).Occurs at high ignition temperatures.
Energy ReleaseStepwise release trapped in chemical bonds of ATP (~40% efficiency).Sudden uncontrolled release as light and heat.

(b) Differences Between Glycolysis and Fermentation:
ParameterGlycolysis (EMP Pathway)Fermentation (Anaerobic)

Question 2
NCERT Exercise
What are respiratory substrates? Name the most common respiratory substrate.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Definition of Respiratory Substrates:
Respiratory substrates are energy-rich organic compounds present within living protoplasm that undergo intracellular enzymatic oxidation (catabolic cleavage of carbon-carbon covalent bonds) during cellular respiration to release metabolic energy stored in the form of ATP.

Major Types of Substrates:
  • Carbohydrates: (Glucose, fructose, sucrose, starch, glycogen).
  • Fats / Lipids: (Triglycerides, fatty acids — yielding maximum energy per gram upon $eta$-oxidation).
  • Proteins: (Hydrolyzed into amino acids and oxidized during starvation or prolonged fasting — protoplasmic respiration).
  • Organic Acids: (Malic acid, citric acid in succulents).
2. Most Common Respiratory Substrate:
The most universally preferred and primary respiratory substrate in all living organisms is Glucose ($C_6H_{12}O_6$). Complex carbohydrates (starch, sucrose) are initially enzymatically hydrolyzed into hexoses (glucose/fructose) before entering the glycolytic pathway (floating respiration).

Question 3
NCERT Exercise
Give the schematic representation of glycolysis.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Schematic 10-Step Sequence of Glycolysis (EMP Pathway — Embden, Meyerhof, Parnas):
Occurs in the cytosol (cytoplasm) of all living cells; anaerobic process.

1. Glucose (6C) + ATP → Glucose-6-phosphate (6C) + ADP  [Enzyme: Hexokinase]
2. Glucose-6-phosphate ↔ Fructose-6-phosphate (6C)  [Phosphoglucoisomerase]
3. Fructose-6-phosphate + ATP → Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate (6C) + ADP  [Phosphofructokinase - PFK]
4. Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate ↔ Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (PGAL — 3C) + DHAP (3C)  [Aldolase]
5. DHAP ↔ Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (PGAL — 3C)  [Triose phosphate isomerase]
(From this point, all reactions occur twice per glucose molecule):
6. 2 PGAL + 2 $NAD^+ + 2 H_3PO_4$ ↔ 2 molecules of 1,3-Bisphosphoglycerate (1,3-BPGA) + 2 $NADH + 2H^+$  [GAPDH]
7. 2 (1,3-BPGA) + 2 ADP ↔ 2 molecules of 3-Phosphoglycerate (3-PGA) + 2 ATP  [Phosphoglycerate kinase]
8. 2 (3-PGA) ↔ 2 molecules of 2-Phosphoglycerate (2-PGA)  [Phosphoglycerate mutase]
9. 2 (2-PGA) ↔ 2 molecules of Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP — 3C) + $2 H_2O$  [Enolase]
10. 2 PEP + 2 ADP → 2 molecules of Pyruvic Acid (3C) + 2 ATP  [Pyruvate kinase]

Net Balance Sheet of Glycolysis:
$$\text{Glucose} + 2\text{ADP} + 2\text{P}_i + 2\text{NAD}^+ \longrightarrow 2\text{ Pyruvate} + 2\text{ATP (net)} + 2\text{NADH} + 2\text{H}^+ + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

Question 4
NCERT Exercise
What are the main steps in aerobic respiration? Where does it take place?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Four Sequential Stages of Aerobic Respiration and Their Cellular Sites:

1. Glycolysis (EMP Pathway):
  • Site: Cytoplasm (Cytosol) of the cell.
  • Process: Partial oxidation of 1 glucose (6C) into 2 pyruvic acid (3C), yielding net 2 ATP and 2 $NADH+H^+$. Independent of $O_2$.
2. Oxidative Decarboxylation of Pyruvate (Link Reaction / Gateway Step):
  • Site: Mitochondrial Matrix.
  • Process: Pyruvate dehydrogenase multienzyme complex converts 2 pyruvates into 2 molecules of Acetyl-CoA (2C), releasing 2 $CO_2$ and 2 $NADH+H^+$.
3. Tricarboxylic Acid Cycle (TCA Cycle / Krebs Cycle):
  • Site: Mitochondrial Matrix (except succinate dehydrogenase on inner membrane).
  • Process: Complete cyclical oxidation of Acetyl-CoA. Two turns of the cycle yield 4 $CO_2$, 6 $NADH+H^+$, 2 $FADH_2$, and 2 GTP (ATP) via substrate-level phosphorylation.
4. Oxidative Phosphorylation & Electron Transport System (ETS):
  • Site: Inner Mitochondrial Membrane (Cristae).
  • Process: High-energy electrons from NADH and $FADH_2$ traverse electron carrier complexes (I, II, III, IV), pumping $H^+$ into the intermembrane space. The proton gradient drives ATP synthesis via $F_0-F_1$ ATP synthase complex (Complex V), while molecular oxygen acts as terminal electron and proton acceptor, forming metabolic water ($H_2O$).

Question 5
NCERT Exercise
Explain ETS (Electron Transport System) and oxidative phosphorylation.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Comprehensive Architecture of Mitochondrial ETS & Oxidative Phosphorylation:

1. Electron Transport System (ETS):
Located in the inner mitochondrial membrane, ETS consists of five multiprotein complexes and mobile electron carriers:
  • Complex I (NADH Dehydrogenase): Oxidizes mitochondrial matrix $NADH+H^+$, transferring electrons through FMN and Fe-S clusters to mobile lipid carrier Ubiquinone (CoQ), pumping 4 $H^+$ across into the intermembrane space.
  • Complex II (Succinate Dehydrogenase): Transfers electrons from succinate-derived $FADH_2$ via Fe-S to Ubiquinone (does not pump protons). Reduced ubiquinone ($UQH_2$) transfers electrons to Complex III.
  • Complex III (Cytochrome $bc_1$ Complex): Passes electrons from $UQH_2$ to mobile peripheral protein carrier Cytochrome c, pumping 4 $H^+$ across into intermembrane space.
  • Complex IV (Cytochrome c Oxidase): Contains cytochromes $a$ and $a_3$, and two copper centers ($Cu_A, Cu_B$). Transfers 4 electrons to terminal acceptor: molecular oxygen ($O_2$), which combines with matrix $H^+$ to form metabolic water ($2H^+ + \frac{1}{2}O_2 + 2e^- \rightarrow H_2O$), pumping 2 $H^+$ across.
2. Oxidative Phosphorylation & Peter Mitchell's Chemiosmotic Hypothesis:
  • Proton pumping creates a steep electrochemical proton gradient (proton motive force, $\Delta p$) across the inner membrane: intermembrane space becomes highly acidic with positive charge relative to the matrix.
  • Complex V ($F_0-F_1$ ATP Synthase): Protons flow back into matrix down their gradient through the transmembrane channel $F_0$. The passage of protons induces conformational rotation of the catalytic $F_1$ headpiece, condensing ADP and $P_i$ into ATP.
  • Stoichiometry: Oxidation of 1 molecule of $NADH+H^+$ yields approximately 3 ATP; oxidation of 1 molecule of $FADH_2$ yields approximately 2 ATP.

Question 7
NCERT Exercise
What are the assumptions made during the calculation of net gain of ATP?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Theoretical Assumptions Made in Calculating the Theoretical Net 36–38 ATP Yield:
The classic textbook calculation of a net gain of 36 or 38 ATP per oxidized glucose molecule is an idealized theoretical estimate based on the following specific assumptions:

  • 1. Sequential, Stepwise Orderly Pathway: It is assumed that glycolysis, the link reaction, the TCA cycle, and the ETS occur in a strict, continuous, unbroken linear pipeline, with one pathway immediately feeding into the next.
  • 2. 100% Transfer of Cytosolic NADH into Mitochondria: Assumes that the 2 $NADH$ synthesized during glycolysis in the cytoplasm are quantitatively transferred across mitochondrial membranes via glycerol-phosphate shuttle or malate-aspartate shuttle into ETS for oxidative phosphorylation without loss.
  • 3. No Intermediates Diverted for Biosynthesis: Assumes that none of the biochemical intermediates in the pathway are siphoned off into anabolic side pathways (e.g., assumes glucose-6-P is not drawn into pentose shunt, acetyl-CoA is not used for fatty acid synthesis, $alpha$-ketoglutarate is not transaminated into glutamate, and oxaloacetate is not used for pyrimidines).
  • 4. Glucose as Sole Substrate: Assumes that glucose is the exclusive, solitary respiratory substrate utilized, and no fats, amino acids, or organic acids enter intermediate stages of the cycle.
Reality in Living Cells: In living protoplasm, all pathways operate simultaneously; intermediates are constantly diverted for cellular anabolism, meaning the actual physiological ATP yield is typically around 30 to 32 ATP.

Question 8
NCERT Exercise
Discuss "The respiratory pathway is an amphibolic pathway."
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Why the Respiratory Pathway is Classified as Amphibolic (Dual Function):

1. Traditional View — Catabolic Pathway:
Traditionally, cellular respiration was regarded purely as a catabolic pathway (destructive breakdown process) because large organic macromolecules (carbohydrates, lipids, proteins) are broken down into simple molecules ($CO_2$ and $H_2O$) to release energy in the form of ATP.

2. Modern Understanding — Anabolic Intermediary Roles:
Biochemical evidence demonstrates that respiration does not merely break down substrates; its intermediates serve as the vital starting precursors for synthesizing diverse essential cellular biomolecules (anabolism):
  • Acetyl-CoA: When the cell requires fatty acids, sterols (cholesterol), carotenoids, or gibberellins, Acetyl-CoA is withdrawn from the respiratory gateway and channeled into fatty acid / lipid synthesis.
  • $alpha$-Ketoglutarate: An intermediate of the Krebs cycle that undergoes reductive amination to synthesize glutamic acid and subsequent amino acids.
  • Oxaloacetate (OAA): Transaminated into aspartic acid, which serves as a building block for pyrimidine nitrogenous bases.
  • Succinyl-CoA: Withdrawn from the TCA cycle to synthesize the porphyrin rings of chlorophyll, cytochromes, and hemoglobin.
  • Dihydroxyacetone Phosphate (DHAP): Reduced to glycerol for lipid synthesis.
Conclusion:
Because the respiratory pathway is simultaneously involved in both catabolism (breakdown) and anabolism (synthesis), it is scientifically termed an amphibolic pathway rather than simply catabolic.

Question 9
NCERT Exercise
Define RQ (Respiratory Quotient). What is its value for fats, carbohydrates and proteins?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Definition of Respiratory Quotient (RQ / Respiratory Ratio):
The Respiratory Quotient (RQ) is the volumetric ratio of carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) evolved to the volume of oxygen ($O_2$) consumed in cellular respiration over a given period: $$\mathbf{RQ = \frac{\text{Volume of } CO_2 \text{ evolved}}{\text{Volume of } O_2 \text{ consumed}}}$$ The value of RQ depends entirely on the chemical nature of the respiratory substrate being oxidized.

Exact RQ Values for Different Substrates:

1. Carbohydrates — $\text{RQ} = 1.0$:
$$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Energy}$$ $$\text{RQ} = \frac{6\text{ CO}_2}{6\text{ O}_2} = \mathbf{1.0}$$ Because carbohydrates contain equal proportions of oxygen and hydrogen atoms, the volume of $CO_2$ evolved equals the volume of $O_2$ consumed.

2. Fats / Lipids — $\text{RQ} = 0.7$ (Less than 1.0):
Example: Oxidation of Triolein / Tripalmitin ($C_{51}H_{98}O_6$): $$2\text{C}_{51}\text{H}_{98}\text{O}_6 + 145\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 102\text{CO}_2 + 98\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Energy}$$ $$\text{RQ} = \frac{102\text{ CO}_2}{145\text{ O}_2} = \mathbf{0.7}$$ Fats are oxygen-poor and hydrogen-rich; therefore, they consume far more external oxygen to oxidize their hydrocarbon chains.

3. Proteins — $\text{RQ} \approx 0.9$:
Proteins (amino acids) also contain less oxygen relative to carbon and hydrogen, yielding an average RQ of approximately 0.85 to 0.9.

Additional Values: Organic acids (Malate/Citrate) have $\text{RQ} > 1.0$ (e.g., Malic acid RQ = 1.33); Anaerobic respiration has $\text{RQ} = \infty$ ($CO_2/0$).
