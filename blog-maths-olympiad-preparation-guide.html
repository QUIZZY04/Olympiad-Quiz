Ultimate Guide to Acing the Maths Olympiad (IMO)

📅 August 08, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read ✍️ OlympiadQuiz Team

The International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) is one of the most competitive academic exams a school student can attempt — and it rewards a very different skill set than your regular school maths exam. Speed, pattern recognition, and the ability to handle unfamiliar problem formats matter just as much as knowing the syllabus.

Whether you're appearing for the first time or aiming to convert a Level 1 qualification into an International Rank at Level 2, this guide walks through exactly how toppers structure their preparation.

1. Understand the IMO Exam Pattern

Before opening a single practice book, know exactly what you're up against. Most Maths Olympiads, including the IMO, are structured around four broad sections:

  • Logical Reasoning: Pattern completion, series, classification, and spatial reasoning questions that test how you think, not just what you've memorized.
  • Mathematical Reasoning: Core curriculum topics — number systems, fractions, geometry, mensuration, and algebra — based on your grade's syllabus.
  • Everyday Mathematics: Word problems that apply maths to real-life scenarios like time, money, and measurement.
  • Achievers Section: Higher-Order Thinking Skills (HOTS) questions worth extra marks each — usually the deciding factor for a top International Rank.

Knowing this breakdown up front lets you allocate practice time proportionally instead of spending 80% of your effort on a section worth only 20% of the marks.

2. Build Rock-Solid Fundamentals First

Olympiad questions rarely ask you to recall a formula directly — they twist a familiar concept into an unfamiliar shape. That only works in your favor if your fundamentals are genuinely strong, not just exam-ready for school tests.

Go back to basics for topics like divisibility rules, HCF/LCM, properties of shapes, and place value. Instead of memorizing a formula for the area of a triangle, understand why it works — that intuition is what lets you solve a question that presents the same idea in a completely different wrapper.

Pro Tip: Keep a "concept notebook" separate from your regular school notes. Every time an Olympiad problem stumps you, write down the underlying concept it tested — not just the answer. Revising this notebook weekly compounds faster than rereading entire chapters.

3. Master Speed Calculation Techniques

The IMO gives you very little time per question. Students who calculate every step longhand run out of time before reaching the Achievers Section — often the section with the most marks per question.

Practice mental math shortcuts for squaring numbers close to multiples of 10, quick percentage-to-fraction conversions, and estimation techniques to eliminate obviously wrong multiple-choice options before calculating precisely. Five minutes of daily mental math drills, done consistently for a month, noticeably cuts down per-question time on exam day.

4. Practice Chapter-wise Before Attempting Full Mocks

Jumping straight into full-length mock tests before you've secured individual chapters is one of the most common mistakes students make. Use Chapterwise Practice to isolate weak topics — geometry, for instance — and drill them until they stop costing you marks, before mixing them into a timed, full-syllabus paper.

This chapter-by-chapter approach also makes it much easier to track exactly where your rank is being lost, instead of getting a single vague score at the end of a 60-minute mock.

5. Simulate Real Exam Conditions with Mock Tests

Once your fundamentals are solid, shift entirely to full-length, timed mock tests that mirror the real IMO's format and negative marking (if any). Attempt our Free IMO Mock Tests under strict exam conditions — no pausing, no calculator, same time limit as the real exam.

After every mock, spend at least 15 minutes reviewing your mistakes. Separate them into two buckets: concept gaps (you didn't know how to solve it) and silly errors (you knew how, but rushed or misread the question). The second bucket is pure lost marks — and it's the easiest to fix with disciplined review.

6. Crack the Achievers Section Deliberately

Don't leave the Achievers Section for "if there's time left." Since it's typically worth more marks per question, a deliberate strategy here has an outsized effect on your final rank. Practice HOTS-style questions specifically — ones that combine two or three concepts into a single problem — rather than only drilling single-concept questions from the regular sections.

Many toppers actually attempt a couple of Achievers questions early via the "2-minute scan," securing the ones they can solve quickly, before committing to the longer core sections.

Conclusion

Acing the Maths Olympiad isn't about knowing more formulas than everyone else — it's about pairing strong fundamentals with speed, disciplined chapter-wise practice, and a deliberate strategy for the high-value Achievers Section. Build that combination consistently over a few months, and a top International Rank becomes a realistic target rather than a stroke of luck.
