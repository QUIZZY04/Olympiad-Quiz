Chapter 2: Hide and Seek

Top View, Front View, Side View, Grid Directions & Spatial Mapping | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Understanding that 3D objects look different when viewed from different angles: Top View (Plan), Front View (Elevation), and Side View.
  • Connecting 2D perspective drawings with actual 3D objects like tables, cars, houses, and shoe boxes.
  • Navigating simple grid maps using cardinal directions: North, South, East, West, Left, and Right.
  • Solving bird's-eye view problems and matching aerial views with ground elevations.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (24 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Perspective Identification
Draw or describe what the following objects look like from the TOP and from the SIDE: (a) A tea cup, (b) A railway track, (c) A pressure cooker, (d) A staircase.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
(a) A Tea Cup:
• Top View: A circular rim with a smaller concentric circle inside (the base), and the handle sticking out on one side like a small loop.
• Side View: A curved cylindrical or tapered profile with the full vertical handle visible.

(b) A Railway Track:
• Top View: Two parallel straight lines connected by equidistant perpendicular wooden or concrete rectangular sleepers.
• Side View / Front Perspective: The two tracks appear wide near your feet and converge toward a point in the distance.

(c) A Pressure Cooker:
• Top View: A round circle with a long black handle extending outwards and a central weight (whistle).
• Side View: The body height, curved metal base, safety valve, and side handle profile.

(d) A Staircase:
• Top View: A series of adjacent rectangles of identical width.
• Side View: A zig-zag step pattern (up and across) resembling a stepped right-angled triangle.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate description of top views for all 4 items 2.0 M
Accurate description of side views for all 4 items 2.0 M
Q2 Grid Map & Navigation
Meera is standing at position (2, 1) on a square grid. She walks 3 steps North and then 4 steps East. (a) What are her new grid coordinates? (b) What is the shortest direct path in total steps along the grid lines back to her starting point?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step 1: Finding New Coordinates:
• Starting position: (X = 2, Y = 1).
• Walking 3 steps North increases the Y-coordinate by 3: New Y = 1 + 3 = 4.
• Walking 4 steps East increases the X-coordinate by 4: New X = 2 + 4 = 6.
• Final Coordinates: (6, 4).

Step 2: Shortest path along grid lines back to starting point:
• To return to (2, 1) from (6, 4):
- She must walk 4 steps West (6 - 2 = 4 steps).
- She must walk 3 steps South (4 - 1 = 3 steps).
• Total steps along grid lines = 4 + 3 = 7 steps.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct final coordinate (6, 4) with working 1.5 M
Correct calculation of return steps (7 steps) 1.5 M
Q3 Spatial Perspectives • 3D Block Towers
A boy builds a tower using 8 identical unit cubes. If viewed from the front, it looks 4 cubes high and 2 cubes wide. How does the view change when observed from directly above (top view)?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Top View Analysis:
The top view shows only the arrangement of cubes on the floor grid (the footprint), regardless of their height. If the tower is 2 cubes wide and built against a straight base, the top view shows 2 square faces side by side (a 2 × 1 rectangle). Looking from directly above, the height of 4 cubes cannot be seen; only the roof surface is visible.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Top view shows footprint independent of vertical height 1.0 M
Correct geometric representation (2 side-by-side squares) 1.0 M
Q4 Coordinate Grids • Route Mapping
On a map grid, a school is at (2, 3) and a playground is at (6, 7). How many units East and how many units North must a child walk to reach the playground from the school?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Grid Movement Calculation:
• Initial Position (School): X = 2, Y = 3
• Destination (Playground): X = 6, Y = 7

1. Movement East (along X-axis):
6 - 2 = 4 units East.

2. Movement North (along Y-axis):
7 - 3 = 4 units North.

Total distance walked along grid lines = 4 + 4 = 8 units.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Horizontal calculation (4 units East) 1.0 M
Vertical calculation (4 units North) 1.0 M

Q9 HOTS & Mathematical Reasoning Puzzle
Higher-Order Thinking Puzzle from 'Hide and Seek': I am thinking of a 2-digit number. When I multiply it by 3 and then subtract 15, the result is 156. What is my mystery number? Show full algebraic proof.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Mathematical Reasoning & Working:
1. Let the mystery number be $N$.
2. Equation: $(3 \times N) - 15 = 156$.
3. Add 15 to both sides: $3 \times N = 156 + 15 = 171$.
4. Divide by 3: $N = \frac{171}{3} = \mathbf{57}$.
5. Verification: (57 × 3) - 15 = 171 - 15 = 156. Verified!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Forming equation: (3 × N) - 15 = 156 1.5 M
Solving correctly: N = 57 with check 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Drone Photography of the School Sports Ground

A drone flies directly above the sports field and takes an aerial snapshot. The football goalpost, which looks like a tall upside-down U-shaped white frame from the ground, looks like a single thin white horizontal line from the sky.

Analytical Task: Why does the goalpost look like a single thin straight line from above? What happens to the two vertical posts?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Explanation: From directly above (bird's-eye view), the vertical posts align straight downward beneath the crossbar along the camera's line of sight. Therefore, the upright posts appear as tiny dots, while the horizontal crossbar spans across the top, making the entire goalpost look like a single straight line from the air.
Rubric: 1 Mark for explaining line of sight; 1 Mark for vertical post alignment.
