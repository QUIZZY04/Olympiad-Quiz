Chapter 1: Building with Bricks

Brick Patterns, Arch Designs (Mehrab, Jharokha), Brick Geometry & Brick Kiln Arithmetic | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Architectural brick patterns from Jagriti School, Murshidabad: floor patterns, circular patterns, and mirror patterns.
  • Geometric dimensions and faces of a standard brick: 6 rectangular faces, 12 edges, 8 corners.
  • Traditional Indian architectural arches: Mehrab, circular arches, and decorative Jharokhas.
  • Large number calculations: Indian brick kilns producing 1,00,000 (one lakh) bricks; calculating the cost of thousands of bricks.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (15 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Textbook Exercise • Geometric Properties of a Brick
Answer the following questions about a brick: (a) How many faces does a brick have? (b) Is any face of a standard brick a square? (c) Draw the smallest face of a brick. (d) Which of the following shapes can be a face of a brick: Triangle, Square, Rectangle, Circle?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
(a) A standard brick has 6 rectangular faces.
(b) No, in standard construction bricks, all 6 faces are rectangles of varying dimensions (length × width, length × height, width × height). None of the faces is a square.
(c) Smallest face: The side face measuring width × height (approximately 10 cm × 7 cm) is the smallest face.
(d) Face shape: Only a Rectangle can be a face of a standard brick (triangles and circles cannot form brick faces).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct face count (6) and shape verification 1.5 M
Smallest face description and rectangle identification 1.5 M
Q2 Brick Kiln Cost Calculation
Bhajan went to buy bricks. The rate was ₹1,800 for 1,000 old bricks, ₹2,000 for 1,000 new bricks from Brickabad, and ₹1,200 for 1,000 bricks from Itapur. If Bhajan buys 3,000 new bricks from Brickabad and 500 old bricks, how much money will he pay in total?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step 1: Cost of 3,000 new bricks from Brickabad:
• Cost of 1,000 new bricks = ₹2,000.
• Cost of 3,000 new bricks = 3 × ₹2,000 = ₹6,000.

Step 2: Cost of 500 old bricks:
• Cost of 1,000 old bricks = ₹1,800.
• Cost of 500 bricks (half of 1,000) = ₹1,800 ÷ 2 = ₹900.

Step 3: Total Payment:
• Total = ₹6,000 + ₹900 = ₹6,900.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Calculation for 3,000 new bricks (₹6,000) 1.0 M
Calculation for 500 old bricks (₹900) 1.0 M
Total payment ₹6,900 with clear working 1.0 M
Q3 Architectural Patterns • Arches & Jali
What is an 'Arch' (कमान) in brick architecture? Where can you see traditional brick jali patterns in historical Indian monuments?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Brick Arch (Kaman):
An arch is a curved symmetrical structural opening built of wedge-shaped bricks arranged over an entrance or bridge. The bricks support each other by pressing against one another, transferring heavy downward weight safely to the side pillars without needing a horizontal wooden beam.

2. Traditional Brick Jali (Lattice):
Brick jalis are decorative screen walls created by leaving systematic hollow spaces between bricks during masonry. They allow cool breeze and soft geometric light patterns to enter while keeping harsh sun and rain out. Famous examples include the Jaagrithi School in Murshidabad (West Bengal) and historical Mughal monuments.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Structural principle of brick arch and weight distribution 1.5 M
Jali pattern description and historical monument examples 1.5 M
Q4 Commercial Arithmetic • Cost of Bricks
Bhajan went to buy bricks. The price of new bricks from Brickabad was ₹2,000 for 1,000 bricks. He bought 3,000 bricks. If he also bought 500 old bricks at ₹1,200 for 1,000 bricks, how much total money did he pay?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Calculation:
1. Cost of New Bricks from Brickabad:
• Cost of 1,000 bricks = ₹2,000.
• Cost of 3,000 bricks = 3 × ₹2,000 = ₹6,000.

2. Cost of Old Bricks:
• Cost of 1,000 bricks = ₹1,200.
• Cost of 500 bricks (half of 1,000) = ₹1,200 ÷ 2 = ₹600.

3. Total Money Paid:
₹6,000 + ₹600 = ₹6,600.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Cost of new bricks (₹6,000) 1.0 M
Cost of old bricks (₹600) 1.0 M
Total sum: ₹6,600 1.0 M
Q5 Building with Bricks • Brick Geometry & Measurement
A standard brick has length 20 cm, breadth 10 cm, and height 7 cm.
(a) What is the shape of a brick?
(b) What is the area of its largest face?
(c) How many bricks of 20 cm length placed end-to-end make a line 1 metre (100 cm) long?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Brick Mathematics:
• (a) Shape: A standard brick is a cuboid.
• (b) Area of largest face: Length × Breadth = 20 cm × 10 cm = 200 square centimetres (cm²).
• (c) Number of bricks in 1 m: 1 m = 100 cm. Number of bricks = 100 ÷ 20 = 5 bricks.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Cuboid shape 1 M
Area = 200 cm² 1 M
5 bricks 1 M
Q6 A Trip to Bhopal • Diesel Fuel Budget
During a school trip to Bhopal, 4 buses stop at a petrol pump to fill diesel. Each bus takes 50 litres of diesel. If 1 litre of diesel costs ₹70:
(a) How many litres of diesel are filled in total?
(b) What is the total bill amount the school must pay?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
• (a) Total diesel: 4 buses × 50 L = 200 litres.
• (b) Total bill amount: 200 L × ₹70/L = ₹14,000.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
200 litres 1.5 M
₹14,000 bill 1.5 M

Q8 Tick-Tick-Tick • Expiry Dates on Food
A packet of butter shows 'Manufactured: 15/01/26' and 'Best before 180 days from manufacture'. Can it be consumed safely on 15th April 2026? Explain.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Date Verification:
• From 15th Jan to 15th April is exactly 3 months (approx. 90 days).
• Since 90 days is well within the 180-day shelf life, the butter is completely safe to consume on 15th April 2026.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Time elapsed is ~90 days 1 M
Safe to consume within 180 days 1 M
Q9 The Junk Seller • Loan and Interest
Kiran took a loan of ₹300 from a bank to set up her scrap shop. She pays back ₹51 every month for six months.
(a) How much money does she pay back in total?
(b) How much extra money (interest) did she pay to the bank?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Loan Repayment:
• (a) Total paid: 6 months × ₹51/month = ₹306.
• (b) Extra money (interest): ₹306 - ₹300 = ₹6.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Total repayment = ₹306 1.5 M
Interest = ₹6 1.5 M
Q10 Jugs and Mugs • Measuring Medicine Dose
A doctor prescribes a cough syrup bottle of 150 mL for a child. The child takes a 5 mL dose three times a day.
(a) How much cough syrup does the child take in one day?
(b) For how many days will the 150 mL bottle last?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Dosage Calculations:
• (a) Daily intake: 3 doses × 5 mL = 15 mL per day.
• (b) Days bottle lasts: 150 mL ÷ 15 mL/day = 10 days.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
15 mL daily 1.5 M
Bottle lasts 10 days 1.5 M
Q11 Carts and Wheels • Circle Geometry
A cyclist has a bicycle wheel with a radius of 35 cm.
(a) What is the diameter of the wheel?
(b) What is the relationship between the radius and diameter of any circle?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Circle Properties:
• (a) Diameter: Diameter = 2 × Radius = 2 × 35 cm = 70 cm.
• (b) Relationship: The diameter is always twice the length of the radius (D = 2r), passing straight through the circle's center.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Diameter = 70 cm 1 M
Formula D = 2r stated 1 M
Q12 Halves and Quarters • Fraction Sharing
Raju has ₹100. He spends 1/2 of his money on a book, and 1/4 of his total money on a pen.
(a) How much does the book cost?
(b) How much does the pen cost?
(c) How much money does Raju have left?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Fractional Expenditure:
• (a) Book (1/2): ₹100 ÷ 2 = ₹50.
• (b) Pen (1/4): ₹100 ÷ 4 = ₹25.
• (c) Money Left: ₹100 - (₹50 + ₹25) = ₹100 - ₹75 = ₹25.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Book = ₹50 1 M
Pen = ₹25 1 M
Remaining = ₹25 1 M
Q13 Play with Patterns • Secret Message Cipher
In a secret code, each letter is replaced by its position in the alphabet (A=1, B=2, C=3, ... Z=26).
Decode the secret message: '13 - 1 - 20 - 8 - 19'. What word does it spell?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Decoding Cipher:
• 13 = M
• 1 = A
• 20 = T
• 8 = H
• 19 = S
The secret message spells MATHS.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct decoding to spell MATHS 2 M
Q14 Tables and Shares • Division with Remainder
A florist has 58 roses. She wants to make bouquets containing 6 roses each.
(a) How many complete bouquets can she make?
(b) How many roses will be left over?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Division:
• 58 ÷ 6 = 9 with a remainder of 4.
• (a) Complete bouquets: 9 bouquets (9 × 6 = 54 roses).
• (b) Leftover roses: 58 - 54 = 4 roses left over.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
9 bouquets 1 M
4 leftover roses 1 M
Q15 Review Questions • True or False
State True or False:
(a) A square has 4 equal sides and its perimeter is 4 × side.
(b) 1 kilometre equals 100 metres.
(c) Half past 4 in the afternoon is written as 16:30 in 24-hour time.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answers:
• (a) True: Perimeter = 4 × side.
• (b) False: 1 km = 1000 metres.
• (c) True: 4:30 PM = 12:00 + 4:30 = 16:30 hours.

True, False, True with explanations 3 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Estimating Bricks for a Compound Wall

A mason calculates that 1 metre length of a 1-metre high boundary wall requires 100 bricks. The school desires to construct a 45-metre long and 1-metre high wall.

Analytical Task: How many bricks should the school order? If each truck carries 2,500 bricks, how many truckloads are needed?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Step 1: Total bricks needed:
45 m × 100 bricks/m = 4,500 bricks.
Step 2: Number of truckloads:
4,500 ÷ 2,500 = 1 full truck (2,500 bricks) with 2,000 bricks remaining.
Therefore, 2 truckloads must be ordered so the project has enough bricks.
Rubric: 1 Mark for 4,500 bricks; 1 Mark for practical answer of 2 truckloads.
