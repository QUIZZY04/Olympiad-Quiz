Chapter 1: Building with Bricks

Brick Patterns, Arch Designs (Mehrab, Jharokha), Brick Geometry & Brick Kiln Arithmetic | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts

  • Geometric dimensions and faces of a standard brick: 6 rectangular faces, 12 edges, 8 corners.

Q1 Textbook Exercise • Geometric Properties of a Brick
Answer the following questions about a brick: (a) How many faces does a brick have? (b) Is any face of a standard brick a square? (c) Draw the smallest face of a brick. (d) Which of the following shapes can be a face of a brick: Triangle, Square, Rectangle, Circle?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
(a) A standard brick has 6 rectangular faces.
(b) No, in standard construction bricks, all 6 faces are rectangles of varying dimensions (length × width, length × height, width × height). None of the faces is a square.
(c) Smallest face: The side face measuring width × height (approximately 10 cm × 7 cm) is the smallest face.
(d) Face shape: Only a Rectangle can be a face of a standard brick (triangles and circles cannot form brick faces).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct face count (6) and shape verification 1.5 M
Smallest face description and rectangle identification 1.5 M
Q2 Brick Kiln Cost Calculation
Bhajan went to buy bricks. The rate was ₹1,800 for 1,000 old bricks, ₹2,000 for 1,000 new bricks from Brickabad, and ₹1,200 for 1,000 bricks from Itapur. If Bhajan buys 3,000 new bricks from Brickabad and 500 old bricks, how much money will he pay in total?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step 1: Cost of 3,000 new bricks from Brickabad:
• Cost of 1,000 new bricks = ₹2,000.
• Cost of 3,000 new bricks = 3 × ₹2,000 = ₹6,000.

Step 2: Cost of 500 old bricks:
• Cost of 1,000 old bricks = ₹1,800.
• Cost of 500 bricks (half of 1,000) = ₹1,800 ÷ 2 = ₹900.

Step 3: Total Payment:
• Total = ₹6,000 + ₹900 = ₹6,900.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Calculation for 3,000 new bricks (₹6,000) 1.0 M
Calculation for 500 old bricks (₹900) 1.0 M
Total payment ₹6,900 with clear working 1.0 M
Q3 Architectural Patterns • Arches & Jali
What is an 'Arch' (कमान) in brick architecture? Where can you see traditional brick jali patterns in historical Indian monuments?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Brick Arch (Kaman):
An arch is a curved symmetrical structural opening built of wedge-shaped bricks arranged over an entrance or bridge. The bricks support each other by pressing against one another, transferring heavy downward weight safely to the side pillars without needing a horizontal wooden beam.

2. Traditional Brick Jali (Lattice):
Brick jalis are decorative screen walls created by leaving systematic hollow spaces between bricks during masonry. They allow cool breeze and soft geometric light patterns to enter while keeping harsh sun and rain out. Famous examples include the Jaagrithi School in Murshidabad (West Bengal) and historical Mughal monuments.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Structural principle of brick arch and weight distribution 1.5 M
Jali pattern description and historical monument examples 1.5 M

Q9 HOTS & Mathematical Reasoning Puzzle
Higher-Order Thinking Puzzle from 'Building with Bricks': I am thinking of a 2-digit number. When I multiply it by 3 and then subtract 15, the result is 138. What is my mystery number? Show full algebraic proof.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Mathematical Reasoning & Working:
1. Let the mystery number be $N$.
2. Equation: $(3 \times N) - 15 = 138$.
3. Add 15 to both sides: $3 \times N = 138 + 15 = 153$.
4. Divide by 3: $N = \frac{153}{3} = \mathbf{51}$.
5. Verification: (51 × 3) - 15 = 153 - 15 = 138. Verified!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Forming equation: (3 × N) - 15 = 138 1.5 M
Solving correctly: N = 51 with check 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Estimating Bricks for a Compound Wall

A mason calculates that 1 metre length of a 1-metre high boundary wall requires 100 bricks. The school desires to construct a 45-metre long and 1-metre high wall.

Analytical Task: How many bricks should the school order? If each truck carries 2,500 bricks, how many truckloads are needed?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Step 1: Total bricks needed:
45 m × 100 bricks/m = 4,500 bricks.
Step 2: Number of truckloads:
4,500 ÷ 2,500 = 1 full truck (2,500 bricks) with 2,000 bricks remaining.
Therefore, 2 truckloads must be ordered so the project has enough bricks.
Rubric: 1 Mark for 4,500 bricks; 1 Mark for practical answer of 2 truckloads.
