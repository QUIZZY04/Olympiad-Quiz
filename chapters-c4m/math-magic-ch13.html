Chapter 13: Fields and Fences

Perimeter of Irregular Polygons, Wire Fencing Calculations & Grid Square Area Counting | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Perimeter is the total continuous length of the outer boundary of a closed planar shape.
  • Calculating perimeter by adding lengths of all straight boundary edges.
  • Fencing fields with wire: single round vs. multiple rounds (e.g., 3-wire barbed fence).
  • Counting whole and half squares on a square centimetre grid to estimate surface area.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (26 Questions)

Q1 Boundary Length & Wire Requirement
Rahmat is a farmer. He has a field with sides measuring 9 m, 15 m, 21 m, and 9 m. (a) What is the length of the boundary of Rahmat's field? (b) Rahmat bought a roll of 70 metres of barbed wire to fence his field. How much wire will be left over after putting one complete round of fence?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
(a) Length of boundary (Perimeter):
Sum of all sides = 9 m + 15 m + 21 m + 9 m = 54 metres.

(b) Wire remaining:
• Total wire in roll = 70 metres.
• Wire used for 1 round = 54 metres.
• Wire left over = 70 m - 54 m = 16 metres.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct boundary sum 54 metres 1.5 M
Correct remaining wire 16 metres 1.5 M
Q2 Square Grid Area & Perimeter
A square has a side of 6 cm. (a) What is its perimeter? (b) If four 1 cm squares are cut out from its four corners, what is the perimeter of the new shape? Explain why the perimeter changes or stays the same.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
(a) Perimeter of original square:
Perimeter = 4 × side = 4 × 6 cm = 24 cm.

(b) Perimeter after cutting 1 cm squares from each corner:
• When a 1 cm square is removed from a corner, the two outer 1 cm edges that are removed are replaced by two inner 1 cm edges that form the new stepped corner.
• Therefore, the total boundary length remains exactly 24 cm!
• Conclusion: The perimeter stays the same (24 cm), even though the area has decreased by 4 sq cm.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Original perimeter 24 cm 1.0 M
Explains corner cut effect and confirms perimeter remains 24 cm 2.0 M
Q3 Area & Perimeter • Grid Squares
A shape is drawn on a 1 cm square grid. It covers 12 complete squares and 6 half squares. (a) What is the area of this shape in square centimetres? (b) If each square has side 1 cm, what is the area of 1 unit square?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Grid Area Calculation:
• Area of 1 unit square with side 1 cm = 1 cm × 1 cm = 1 sq cm.

1. 12 complete squares = 12 × 1 sq cm = 12 sq cm.
2. 6 half squares = 6 × (1/2 sq cm) = 3 sq cm.

Total Area of the Shape:
12 + 3 = 15 sq cm.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Area of 1 unit square = 1 sq cm 1.0 M
Combining complete and half squares accurately (12 + 3) 1.0 M
Total area: 15 sq cm 1.0 M
Q4 Field Fencing • Barbed Wire Math
Ganpat has a field with sides 15 m, 20 m, 18 m, and 27 m. He bought a 100-metre roll of barbed wire to fence the boundary once. (a) What is the perimeter of his field? (b) How much wire will be left over after fencing?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Perimeter of the Field:
Perimeter = Sum of all 4 sides
= 15 m + 20 m + 18 m + 27 m = 80 metres.

2. Wire Left Over:
Wire roll length = 100 m
Wire used = 80 m
Leftover wire = 100 m - 80 m = 20 metres.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct sum of sides = 80 m 1.5 M
Subtraction 100 - 80 = 20 m left 1.5 M

Q9 HOTS & Mathematical Reasoning Puzzle
Higher-Order Thinking Puzzle from 'Fields and Fences': I am thinking of a 2-digit number. When I multiply it by 3 and then subtract 15, the result is 354. What is my mystery number? Show full algebraic proof.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Mathematical Reasoning & Working:
1. Let the mystery number be $N$.
2. Equation: $(3 \times N) - 15 = 354$.
3. Add 15 to both sides: $3 \times N = 354 + 15 = 369$.
4. Divide by 3: $N = \frac{369}{3} = \mathbf{123}$.
5. Verification: (123 × 3) - 15 = 369 - 15 = 354. Verified!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Forming equation: (3 × N) - 15 = 354 1.5 M
Solving correctly: N = 123 with check 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Three-Strand Wire Fence Budget

A farmer wants to put a 3-strand barbed wire fence around a rectangular field that is 40 metres long and 25 metres wide. Fencing wire costs ₹15 per metre.

Analytical Task: Calculate the total length of wire needed and the total cost.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Step 1: Perimeter:
2 × (40 + 25) = 2 × 65 = 130 metres.
Step 2: 3 strands:
130 m × 3 = 390 metres of wire.
Step 3: Total cost:
390 m × ₹15/m = ₹5,850.
Rubric: 1 Mark for perimeter (130 m); 1 Mark for 3 strands (390 m); 1 Mark for ₹5,850.
