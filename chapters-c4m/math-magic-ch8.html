Chapter 8: Carts and Wheels

Circles, Centre, Radius, Diameter, Compass Drawing & Spinning Tops (Charkhi) | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Identifying round circular objects: bullock cart wheels, bicycle wheels, bangles, coins, chapati.
  • Key circle vocabulary: Centre, Radius (r), Diameter (d), and Circumference.
  • Relationship between radius and diameter: Diameter = 2 × Radius; Radius = Diameter ÷ 2.
  • Technique for drawing a circle using a nail and string, or a geometric compass.
  • Making spinning tops (charkhi) and balancing the stick at the exact centre.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (26 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Radius and Diameter Relationships
Fill in the blanks: (a) If the radius of a circle is 4 cm, its diameter is __ cm. (b) If the diameter of a wheel is 18 cm, its radius is __ cm. (c) Which circle is larger: Circle A with radius 6 cm or Circle B with diameter 10 cm?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
(a) Formula: Diameter = 2 × Radius
• Diameter = 2 × 4 cm = 8 cm.

(b) Formula: Radius = Diameter ÷ 2
• Radius = 18 cm ÷ 2 = 9 cm.

(c) Comparison between Circle A and Circle B:
• Circle A radius = 6 cm (so Diameter = 2 × 6 = 12 cm).
• Circle B diameter = 10 cm (so Radius = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 cm).
• Since 6 cm > 5 cm, Circle A is larger.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct answers for (a) and (b) 1.5 M
Accurate comparison showing Circle A is larger 1.5 M
Q2 Spinning Top Physics & Circle Centre
Zakir, Appu, Naina, and Guddo made cardboard spinning tops. Zakir put his stick near the edge, Appu put it halfway between center and edge, Naina put it slightly off-center, while Guddo put her stick at the exact geometric centre. (a) Whose top will not spin at all? (b) Whose top will spin the best? Explain why.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
(a) Top that will not spin at all:
Zakir's top will not spin at all (or will wobble and instantly fall over) because placing the stick at the outer edge creates an extreme weight imbalance.

(b) Top that will spin the best:
Guddo's top will spin the best.
• Reason: When the pivot stick is placed at the exact center of the circle, the radius is equal in all directions. This distributes mass uniformly and balances the gravitational forces evenly around the axis of rotation, allowing the top to spin smoothly and stably for a long time.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identifies Zakir's top as failing 1.0 M
Identifies Guddo's top as best with center balance explanation 2.0 M

Q4 Compass Construction • Drawing Circles
Describe how to draw a circle of radius 4 cm using a ruler and a geometric compass. What is the role of the fixed sharp point?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Steps to Draw a Circle:
1. Setting the Radius: Open the arms of the compass. Place the sharp metal pointer at 0 cm mark on the ruler and expand the pencil tip to exactly the 4 cm mark.
2. Marking the Centre: Press the sharp metal pointer firmly onto paper without shifting; this point is the Centre (O) of the circle.
3. Drawing: Holding the compass strictly by the top handle, rotate the pencil 360° smoothly around the centre to trace the circular boundary.
The fixed sharp point ensures that every point on the circumference remains at an exact constant distance of 4 cm from the centre.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Setting 4 cm measurement on ruler 1.0 M
Center anchor and 360° pencil rotation 1.0 M
Explaining constant distance from center 1.0 M

(a) Convert 9 meters into centimeters.

(c) Which is longer: 10 m or 945 cm? By how much?

Q9 HOTS & Mathematical Reasoning Puzzle
Higher-Order Thinking Puzzle from 'Carts and Wheels': I am thinking of a 2-digit number. When I multiply it by 3 and then subtract 15, the result is 264. What is my mystery number? Show full algebraic proof.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Mathematical Reasoning & Working:
1. Let the mystery number be $N$.
2. Equation: $(3 \times N) - 15 = 264$.
3. Add 15 to both sides: $3 \times N = 264 + 15 = 279$.
4. Divide by 3: $N = \frac{279}{3} = \mathbf{93}$.
5. Verification: (93 × 3) - 15 = 279 - 15 = 264. Verified!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Forming equation: (3 × N) - 15 = 264 1.5 M
Solving correctly: N = 93 with check 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Playground Circle Marker with String

The sports teacher needs to draw a circular kabaddi ring on the ground with a diameter of 8 metres. She has a wooden peg and a coil of rope.

Analytical Task: How long should she cut the rope to tie between the central peg and the chalk stick?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Answer: 4 metres.
Explanation: The length of the rope between the central fixed peg (centre) and the rotating chalk stick represents the radius of the circle.
Radius = Diameter ÷ 2 = 8 m ÷ 2 = 4 metres.
Rubric: 1 Mark for identifying rope as radius; 1 Mark for calculating 4 metres.
