Chapter 8: Weigh it, Pour it

Grams, Kilograms, Millilitres, Litres, Balance Scales & Liquid Capacities | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Standard units of mass: Gram (g) and Kilogram (kg); 1 kg = 1,000 g.
  • Standard units of capacity: Millilitre (ml) and Litre (l); 1 l = 1,000 ml.
  • Balancing pans: Understanding equilibrium on a two-pan balance beam.
  • Comparing light and heavy objects; estimating volume in daily household containers.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (15 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Mass & Capacity Metric Arithmetic
Solve: (a) A grocer has a 5 kg bag of sugar. He sells 1 kg 750 g. How much sugar remains? (b) A jug holds 2 litres of mango juice. If each glass holds 250 ml, how many glasses can be filled?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
(a) Remaining Sugar:
• Convert 5 kg to grams: 5 kg = 5,000 g.
• Convert 1 kg 750 g to grams: 1,000 g + 750 g = 1,750 g.
• Subtraction: 5,000 g - 1,750 g = 3,250 g.
• Converting back to kg and g: 3 kg 250 g of sugar remains.

(b) Number of glasses of mango juice:
• 1 litre = 1,000 ml, so 2 litres = 2 × 1,000 = 2,000 ml.
• Capacity of 1 glass = 250 ml.
• Number of glasses = 2,000 ÷ 250 = 8 glasses.
Check: 8 × 250 ml = 2,000 ml = 2 l.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct subtraction for sugar (3 kg 250 g) 2.0 M
Correct division for juice glasses (8 glasses) 2.0 M
Q2 Measurement of Capacity • Mixing Solutions
A doctor prescribes 5 mL of cough syrup three times a day to a patient for 6 days. (a) How much syrup does the patient consume in one day? (b) Will a 100 mL bottle be sufficient for the full course?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
Dosage per intake = 5 mL

1. Daily Consumption:
3 doses × 5 mL = 15 mL per day.

2. Total Consumption in 6 Days:
15 mL × 6 = 90 mL.

3. Sufficiency of Bottle:
The bottle contains 100 mL, and the patient requires 90 mL.
Since 100 mL > 90 mL, yes, the 100 mL bottle will be sufficient (with 10 mL left over).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Daily dosage calculation (15 mL) 1.0 M
Total 6-day requirement (90 mL) 1.0 M
Comparison and positive conclusion (100 > 90) 1.0 M
Q3 Weight Balancing • Pan Scales
You have only three standard weights: 100 g, 250 g, and 500 g. How will you measure exactly 350 g of flour on a two-pan balance in one step?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Pan Balance Arrangement:
To measure 350 g:
• Place the 100 g and 250 g weights together in the left pan.
• Total weight in left pan = 100 g + 250 g = 350 g.
• Pour flour into the right pan until both pans balance horizontally.
The weighed flour will be exactly 350 g.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identifying 100 g + 250 g combination 1.0 M
Explaining balance equilibrium 1.0 M
Q4 Capacity Arithmetic • Milk Delivery
A milkman has a 20-litre milk can. In the morning, he sells 4 L 750 mL to House A and 6 L 500 mL to House B. How much milk is still left in his can?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Calculation:
1. Total Milk Sold:
  4 L 750 mL + 6 L 500 mL = 10 L 1,250 mL = 11 L 250 mL.

2. Milk Left in the 20 L Can:
  20 L 000 mL - 11 L 250 mL
= 19 L 1,000 mL - 11 L 250 mL = 8 L 750 mL.

The milkman has 8 litres 750 millilitres of milk left.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Adding milk sold (11 L 250 mL) 1.5 M
Borrowing and subtracting from 20 L (8 L 750 mL) 1.5 M
Q5 Textbook Exercise • Real-World Context
In Chapter 8 ('Weigh it, Pour it'), a classroom project investigates real-world mathematics: If a school bus travels 48 kilometres on 4 litres of diesel, what is the mileage (kilometres per litre) of the bus? How much diesel is needed for a 120-kilometre journey?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Mileage calculation:
• Distance = 48 km, Diesel used = 4 L.
• Mileage = 48 ÷ 4 = 12 km per litre.
2. Diesel for 120 km:
• Required diesel = Total distance ÷ Mileage = 120 ÷ 12 = 10 litres.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Mileage = 12 km/L 1.5 M
Diesel needed = 10 L 1.5 M
Q6 Practice Time • Measurement & Conversions
Convert the following measurements into standard units:
(a) 4 metres 65 centimetres into centimetres.
(b) 3500 millilitres into litres and millilitres.
(c) 2 kilograms 750 grams into grams.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Conversions:
• (a) 1 m = 100 cm → (4 × 100) + 65 = 465 cm.
• (b) 1000 mL = 1 L → 3500 mL = 3 L 500 mL.
• (c) 1 kg = 1000 g → (2 × 1000) + 750 = 2750 g.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
465 cm 1 M
3 L 500 mL 1 M
2750 g 1 M
Q7 Try These • Geometry and Shapes
Look at a solid brick:
(a) How many faces does a brick have?
(b) What is the shape of each face?
(c) How many straight edges and how many corners (vertices) does a brick have?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Properties of a Brick (Cuboid):
• (a) Faces: A brick has 6 flat faces.
• (b) Shape of faces: Each face is a rectangle.
• (c) Edges and Corners: It has 12 straight edges and 8 corners (vertices).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
6 faces 1 M
Rectangular faces 1 M
12 edges and 8 corners 1 M
Q8 Money & Accounts • Rate List Calculation
At a recycling shop, the rate list shows: Waste paper = ₹12 per kg, Plastic = ₹18 per kg, Iron = ₹25 per kg.
Ramesh sells 10 kg of waste paper, 5 kg of plastic, and 4 kg of iron.
(a) Find the cost of each item sold.
(b) What is the total money Ramesh earns?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Calculations:
• Waste paper: 10 × ₹12 = ₹120.
• Plastic: 5 × ₹18 = ₹90.
• Iron: 4 × ₹25 = ₹100.
• Total Earnings: ₹120 + ₹90 + ₹100 = ₹310.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Individual costs (₹120, ₹90, ₹100) 1.5 M
Total = ₹310 1.5 M
Q9 Fractions Exploration • Parts of a Whole
A rectangular chocolate bar has 16 equal pieces. Reena gives 1/4 of the chocolate to her brother and 1/2 of it to her friend.
(a) How many pieces does her brother receive?
(b) How many pieces does her friend receive?
(c) How many pieces are left for Reena?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Fractional Breakdown:
• Total pieces = 16.
• Brother's share (1/4): 16 ÷ 4 = 4 pieces.
• Friend's share (1/2): 16 ÷ 2 = 8 pieces.
• Remaining for Reena: 16 - (4 + 8) = 16 - 12 = 4 pieces (which is 1/4 of the bar).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Brother = 4 pieces 1 M
Friend = 8 pieces 1 M
Reena = 4 pieces 1 M
Q10 Time & Clocks • Elapsed Time
A school cricket match began at 9:30 AM and concluded at 1:15 PM.
(a) Convert 1:15 PM into 24-hour railway clock time.
(b) How long did the cricket match last?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Time Calculations:
• (a) 24-hour time: 1:15 PM = 12:00 + 1:15 = 13:15 hours.
• (b) Duration:
• From 9:30 AM to 12:30 PM = 3 hours.
• From 12:30 PM to 1:15 PM = 45 minutes.
• Total duration = 3 hours and 45 minutes.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
13:15 hours in 24-hr clock 1 M
Duration = 3 hours 45 minutes with steps 2 M
Q11 Perimeter & Area • Fencing a Field
A square vegetable garden has a side length of 15 metres.
(a) What is the total length of wire fence needed to go around the garden once?
(b) If fencing costs ₹20 per metre, find the total cost of fencing.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Perimeter & Cost:
• (a) Perimeter of square: 4 × Side = 4 × 15 m = 60 metres.
• (b) Total Cost: Perimeter × Rate = 60 m × ₹20/m = ₹1,200.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Perimeter = 60 m 1.5 M
Cost = ₹1,200 1.5 M
Q12 Mental Math • Division and Multiplication
Solve mentally using number breakdown:
(a) 72 ÷ 6 = ?
(b) 15 × 8 = ?
(c) 25 × 40 = ?
(d) 96 ÷ 8 = ?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solutions:
• (a) 72 ÷ 6 = (60 ÷ 6) + (12 ÷ 6) = 10 + 2 = 12.
• (b) 15 × 8 = (10 × 8) + (5 × 8) = 80 + 40 = 120.
• (c) 25 × 40 = (25 × 4) × 10 = 100 × 10 = 1000.
• (d) 96 ÷ 8 = (80 ÷ 8) + (16 ÷ 8) = 10 + 2 = 12.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
All 4 mental math facts correct 2 M
Q13 Patterns & Symmetries • Rotational Turns
Look at the letter 'H' and the letter 'N':
(a) What does each look like after a half-turn (180° rotation)?
(b) What does each look like after a quarter-turn (90° rotation)?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Rotational Symmetry:
• (a) Half-turn (180°): Both 'H' and 'N' look exactly the same after a half-turn.
• (b) Quarter-turn (90°): 'H' rotates to look like 'I' with sleeping bars, and 'N' rotates to look like 'Z'.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Half-turn explanation (same) 1 M
Quarter-turn explanation 1 M
Q14 Smart Charts • Data Interpretation
In a Class 4 survey of 40 children, 20 like Cricket, 10 like Football, and 10 like Badminton.
(a) What fraction of children like Cricket?
(b) Draw a simple circular pie chart (Chapati Chart) representing this data.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Data & Chapati Chart:
• (a) Fraction: 20 out of 40 = 20/40 = 1/2 (half of the class).
• (b) Chapati Chart: A circle divided in half represents Cricket (1/2). The other half is split equally into two quarters (1/4 for Football and 1/4 for Badminton).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Fraction = 1/2 for cricket 1.5 M
Pie chart sectors explained (1/2, 1/4, 1/4) 1.5 M
Q15 Chapter Review • True or False
State True or False:
(a) A circle has no corners and no straight edges.
(b) 1 litre is equal to 100 millilitres.
(c) The perimeter of any rectangle is 2 × (Length + Breadth).
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answers:
• (a) True: A circle is a smooth curve with 0 corners.
• (b) False: 1 Litre is equal to 1000 millilitres.
• (c) True: Perimeter = 2 × (L + B).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
True, False, True with explanations 3 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Measuring Cough Syrup Doses

Doctor advises a patient to take 5 ml of cough syrup three times a day for 7 days. The chemist gives a bottle containing 100 ml of syrup.

Analytical Task: Is the 100 ml bottle sufficient for the complete course? How much syrup is used, and how much is left over?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Working:
• Daily dosage = 5 ml × 3 times = 15 ml per day.
• Dosage for 7 days = 15 ml × 7 = 105 ml.
Conclusion:
No, the 100 ml bottle is NOT sufficient because the patient needs 105 ml (short by 5 ml).
Rubric: 1 Mark for calculating 105 ml total required; 1 Mark for concluding 100 ml is not sufficient.
