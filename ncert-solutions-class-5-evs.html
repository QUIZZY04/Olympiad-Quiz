Chapter 1: Super Senses (कैसे पहचाना चींटी ने दोस्त को?)

NCERT Looking Around (Class 5 EVS) • Animal Senses, Communication, Sensory Adaptations, Tiger Conservation & Sanctuaries • CBSE 2026-27

📌 Essential Chapter Core Concepts
  • Animals have amazing senses: sights, sounds, smells, and taste far exceeding human capabilities.
  • Ants leave behind a chemical scent (pheromones) to guide other ants in a continuous single file.
  • Silk moth can detect its female partner from many kilometres away by her distinct scent.
  • Dogs mark their own territory with urine and faeces; they sniff out intruders by their scent.
  • Birds have eyes on opposite sides of their heads, giving them wide panoramic vision; birds like eagles and kites can see 4 times further than humans.
  • Night animals like owls and bats see mostly in black and white.
  • Tigers possess acute hearing (rotating ears), night vision 6 times sharper than humans, and sensitive whiskers that detect minute vibrations in air.
  • National Parks like Jim Corbett (Uttarakhand) and Ghana (Bharatpur, Rajasthan) protect endangered species from hunting and poaching.
NCERT Textbook Exercise & Activity Solutions (100% Coverage)
Q1
How did the ant recognize its friend and identify that other ants did not belong to its own group?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Ants identify their group members primarily through their extraordinary sense of smell. As ants walk, they secrete specific chemical substances called pheromones on the ground. Each colony has a distinct chemical signature. When an unfamiliar ant approaches, the guard ant uses its antennae (feelers) to detect the scent and immediately recognizes whether the newcomer belongs to its own nest or is an outsider.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Mention of chemical scent / pheromones 1 Mark
Role of antennae as sensory smell organs 1 Mark
Explanation of distinct colony scent recognition 1 Mark
Q2
Why do dogs sniff everywhere along the road? What does a dog deduce by sniffing another dog's urine or faeces?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Dogs mark out their own territory on streets by urinating or defecating at specific posts, poles, and corners. A dog can instantly deduce whether another dog has entered its territory by sniffing the scent left in urine and faeces. The scent reveals whether the intruder is a male or female, familiar or a stranger, and how recently it passed through that area.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Concept of territorial marking by urination/defecation 1 Mark
Identification of intruder via scent detection 1 Mark
Information extracted (gender, freshness, familiarity) 1 Mark
Q3
Most birds have their eyes fixed on opposite sides of their heads. How does this eye placement help them, and why do they frequently move their necks?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Because most birds have eyes positioned on opposite sides of their heads, their eyes can focus on two completely different objects simultaneously, providing a wide panoramic field of view to spot predators and food. However, because their eyeballs cannot rotate within their sockets (they are fixed), birds must turn their whole head and neck to look directly in front or focus closely on a single object.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Wide panoramic vision covering two directions 1.5 Marks
Fixed eyeballs requiring neck movement to change focal angle 1.5 Marks
Q4
Name five animals whose ears can be seen clearly and five animals whose ears are hidden under feathers or skin.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
  • Animals with visible external ears (pinnae): Elephant, Cow, Dog, Cat, Horse, Deer, Rabbit.
  • Animals with hidden ears (internal ear holes): Snake (feels vibrations through the ground), Lizard, Pigeon, Sparrow, Crocodile, Crow.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Accurate list of 5 animals with visible pinnae 1.5 Marks
Accurate list of 5 animals with hidden ear holes 1.5 Marks
Q5
How does a tiger use its different senses (whiskers, ears, night vision) to hunt efficiently in the wild?
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
The tiger is one of the most alert predators on Earth due to its integrated super senses:
  1. Sensitive Whiskers (Vibrissae): The whiskers are extremely sensitive to air movements and vibrations. They help the tiger navigate safely in complete pitch darkness and locate hidden prey moving through grass.
  2. Acute Directional Hearing: A tiger's ears can rotate independently in different directions to catch the faintest sound from far away. It can distinguish between the rustling of leaves and an animal walking on grass.
  3. Super Night Vision: A tiger's night vision is six times sharper than human night vision, allowing it to ambush prey seamlessly after dusk.
  4. Roar Communication: A tiger's roar can be heard up to 3 kilometres away.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Sensory role of whiskers in vibration detection 1.5 Marks
Rotational ears and sound distinction capability 1.5 Marks
6x sharper night vision than humans 1 Mark
Summary of predatory efficiency and territory calls 1 Mark
🎯 NEP 2020 Competency Case Study: Case Study & Environmental Policy

Wildlife Conservation & Anti-Poaching Framework

Due to poaching for tiger skins, bones, and elephant tusks, wild animal populations experienced steep declines. In response, the Government of India declared several forested regions as protected National Parks and Sanctuaries where human interference, hunting, and grazing are strictly prohibited.

Question: Name two renowned National Parks in India and explain why the government strictly prohibits hunting and woodcutting within their core boundaries.

Model Answer:
Two famous National Parks in India are: 1. Jim Corbett National Park in Uttarakhand (India's oldest national park, famous for Bengal Tigers). 2. Kaziranga National Park in Assam (famous for the Great Indian One-Horned Rhinoceros). Reasons for strict legal prohibition: - Protection from Extinction: Poachers kill tigers for their skins, claws, and bones, and elephants for their ivory tusks. Strict laws prevent species extinction. - Preserving Ecological Balance: If apex predators like tigers disappear, herbivore populations explode, destroying vegetation and collapsing the food web. - Habitat Restoration: Tree cutting degrades the natural ecosystem that wild animals depend on for breeding, shelter, and water.
Marking Scheme: 2 Marks for naming National Parks + 3 Marks for ecological and anti-poaching rationale = 5 Marks

Chapter 2: A Snake Charmer's Story (कहानी सपेरों की)

NCERT Looking Around (Class 5 EVS) • Kalbeliyas, Indian Snakes, Venom, Fangs, Antivenom & Wildlife Laws • CBSE 2026-27

📌 Essential Chapter Core Concepts
  • The Kalbeliya community shares a cultural and traditional bond with snakes, passing down knowledge across generations.
  • Musical instruments used by snake charmers: Been, Tumba, Khanjiri, and Dhol.
  • Out of hundreds of snake species in India, only four varieties are dangerously poisonous: Cobra (नाग), Common Krait (करैत), Russell's Viper (दुबोइया), and Saw-scaled Viper (अफ़ई).
  • Poisonous snakes have two hollow fangs through which venom enters the bloodstream when they bite.
  • Snake venom medicine (antivenom) is manufactured directly from the venom of the snakes themselves and is stocked in government hospitals.
  • Snakes are friends of farmers: they eat rats in fields and prevent crop destruction.
  • The Wildlife Protection Act made it illegal to catch and keep wild animals, prompting snake charmers to adapt into musical performers and educators.
NCERT Textbook Exercise & Activity Solutions (100% Coverage)
Q1
Why are snakes described as 'friends of farmers'? What would happen to crops if all snakes were removed from agricultural fields?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Snakes are beneficial friends of farmers because they eat rats and mice in agricultural fields. Rats reproduce rapidly and destroy standing grains, roots, and stored food. If all snakes were killed or removed, the rodent population would explode uncontrollable, resulting in massive destruction of food crops and widespread agricultural losses.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Identification of snakes as rodent predators 1.5 Marks
Consequence of snake removal on food crops and farmer livelihood 1.5 Marks
Q2
How many types of poisonous snakes are found in India? Name each of them and state how snake venom enters a victim's body.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Out of the numerous snake species found across India, only four varieties are venomous:
  1. Cobra (नाग)
  2. Common Krait (करैत)
  3. Russell's Viper (दुबोइया)
  4. Saw-scaled Viper (अफ़ई)
When a poisonous snake bites, venom travels through its two hollow teeth (called fangs), which act like hypodermic injection needles, pumping poison directly into the victim's tissues and bloodstream.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Correct number (4) and names of all four venomous snakes 2 Marks
Explanation of fangs as hollow injection needles 2 Marks
Q3
How is snake antivenom (the medicine for snake bites) prepared, and where can people find it during an emergency?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Snake antivenom is made directly from snake venom itself. Small, controlled doses of snake venom are collected by extracting it from live snakes. It is then injected into animals like horses to produce antibodies, which are purified into antivenom serum. Snake bite antivenom is available at all government primary healthcare centres and district hospitals across the country.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Explanation that antivenom is manufactured from snake venom 1.5 Marks
Availability in government hospitals & health centres 1.5 Marks
Q4
What law did the government enact regarding wild animals, and what dilemma did it create for the Kalbeliya community?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
The Indian government enacted the Wildlife Protection Act, which makes it illegal to catch, keep, or sell wild animals, including snakes.
Dilemma for Kalbeliyas: Snake charming was the traditional profession and sole source of livelihood for Kalbeliya families. The ban suddenly took away their source of income. To adapt, many Kalbeliyas have preserved their cultural heritage by transitioning into folk dancers, musical performers, and wildlife education guides.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Statement of the Wildlife Protection law prohibiting capture 1.5 Marks
Impact on Kalbeliya livelihood and cultural adaptation 1.5 Marks
🎯 NEP 2020 Competency Case Study: First Aid & Medical Protocol

Emergency Response to Snake Bites

While working in a paddy field during the monsoon, a farmer was bitten on the ankle by a snake. Some villagers suggested cutting the wound with a blade and sucking out the blood, while a school teacher insisted on taking him directly to the district hospital.

Question: Explain why traditional myths like cutting the wound or sucking venom are dangerous, and list the correct scientific first-aid steps for snake bite.

Model Answer:
Why cutting or sucking the wound is hazardous: Cutting tissue causes severe bleeding, tissue damage, and life-threatening infections. Sucking venom is ineffective because venom spreads swiftly into the lymphatic system and blood, while putting the rescuer at risk of poisoning through mouth cuts. Correct Scientific First Aid Protocol: 1. Keep the patient calm and still: Any panic, running, or movement speeds up heart rate, accelerating venom distribution. 2. Immobilize the bitten limb: Keep the bitten leg or arm below heart level and apply a clean, broad splint (like a wooden stick or cloth bandage) to prevent movement. 3. Remove tight items: Remove rings, bangles, or tight shoes before swelling occurs. 4. Rush to a hospital immediately: The only effective cure is intravenous administration of Polyvalent Antivenom Serum at a medical facility.
Marking Scheme: 2 Marks for debunking harmful myths + 3 Marks for 4 correct first-aid steps = 5 Marks

Chapter 3: From Tasting to Digesting (चखने से पचने तक)

NCERT Looking Around (Class 5 EVS) • Taste Buds, Salivary Digestion, Stomach Acid, Dr. Beaumont's Discovery & Balanced Nutrition • CBSE 2026-27

📌 Essential Chapter Core Concepts
  • Different parts of our tongue detect different primary tastes: sweet, salty, sour, and bitter.
  • Digestion begins in the mouth: chewing food thoroughly mixes it with saliva, where enzymes break down complex starch into simple sweet sugars.
  • Dr. William Beaumont conducted historic experiments on soldier Alexis St. Martin (who had an unhealed stomach gunshot wound), proving that the stomach churns food and secretes digestive juices.
  • Stomach juices are strongly acidic in nature.
  • Food takes roughly 1.5 to 4.5 hours to digest in the stomach depending on whether it is boiled milk, raw eggs, or cooked meat.
  • Proper food (संतुलित आहार) is the fundamental right of every child: malnutrition (undernourishment) causes weakness, while fast junk food leads to obesity and lethargy.
  • Kalahandi district in Odisha exports large quantities of rice, yet many poor labourers and children there suffer from extreme starvation and poverty.
NCERT Textbook Exercise & Activity Solutions (100% Coverage)
Q1
Why are we advised to chew our food 30 to 32 times before swallowing? What change in taste occurs when plain chapati or boiled rice is chewed for a long time?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Chewing food 30 to 32 times breaks solid food into fine particles and thoroughly mixes it with saliva secreted by salivary glands. Saliva contains the enzyme ptyalin (amylase), which chemically converts complex, tasteless starch molecules into simple sugars (maltose/glucose). When boiled rice or plain chapati is chewed for a prolonged period, it begins to taste noticeably sweet.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Physical breakdown and thorough saliva mixing 1 Mark
Enzymatic conversion of starch to sugar 1 Mark
Taste transformation to sweet 1 Mark
Q2
Who was Alexis St. Martin and how did Dr. William Beaumont use his medical condition to unlock the secrets of human digestion?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
In 1822, a soldier named Alexis St. Martin was accidentally shot in the stomach. Dr. William Beaumont treated the wound, and although St. Martin recovered, the wound healed leaving an open fistula (a hole) covered by a loose flap of skin that allowed Dr. Beaumont to peer directly inside a living human stomach.
Key Discoveries by Dr. Beaumont:
  • The stomach churns food vigorously to break it down.
  • The stomach secretes acidic digestive juices.
  • Digestion occurs significantly faster inside the warm, living stomach than when the same digestive juices are placed in a glass test tube outside the body.
  • Mental distress or sadness slows down the digestive process.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Explanation of St. Martin's stomach window condition 1.5 Marks
Discovery of stomach churning and digestive juices 1.5 Marks
Comparison of in-vivo vs in-vitro digestion rates 1 Mark
Q3
Why is a glucose drip given to a patient who is suffering from severe vomiting and loose motions?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
When a person suffers from continuous vomiting and diarrhoea (loose motions), the body loses huge amounts of water, essential salts, and glucose, leading to severe dehydration and weakness. The stomach cannot retain or digest oral food. A glucose drip is administered directly into the patient's bloodstream through an intravenous (IV) line, providing instant energy and rehydration without requiring the digestive system to work.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Explanation of dehydration and nutrient loss from vomiting 1.5 Marks
Direct IV absorption providing instant energy without digestion 1.5 Marks
Q4
Compare the health conditions of Rashmi (3 years old, undernourished) and Kailash (7 years old, obese on junk food). What defines 'Proper Food'?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
  • Rashmi (Undernutrition): Looks much younger than her age, has very thin arms and legs and a bloated pot-belly. She falls sick often because she does not get even one full meal of rice or roti a day.
  • Kailash (Malnutrition from Junk Food): He is overweight, chubby, and experiences pain in his legs. He finds it difficult to run or walk fast, prefers watching TV, and refuses home-cooked lentils, vegetables, and rice in favour of chips, burgers, and soft drinks.
Proper Food (Balanced Diet): Food that contains adequate quantities of all essential nutrients—carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, roughage, and clean water—tailored to support healthy growth and disease resistance.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Accurate contrast between undernourished vs junk food conditions 2 Marks
Comprehensive definition of proper balanced diet 2 Marks
🎯 NEP 2020 Competency Case Study: Nutritional Economics & Social Justice

The Kalahandi Hunger Paradox

In Kalahandi district in Odisha, plenty of rice is cultivated and sent to other states, with rich farmers making substantial profits. In the same district, however, many landless families and children die of hunger and starvation due to lack of purchasing power.

Question: Why does starvation persist in a region that produces surplus rice, and what government policies are designed to guarantee food security to every child?

Model Answer:
Reasons for the Paradox: 1. Lack of Purchasing Power: Poor tribal and agricultural labourers do not own land and earn very low daily wages, leaving them unable to buy market rice even when grain warehouses are full. 2. Grain Hoarding & Inefficient Distribution: Rice is often hoarded by middlemen or exported for commercial profit rather than distributed equitably. Government Countermeasures: - Public Distribution System (PDS / Ration Shops): Provides subsidized food grains to below-poverty-line (BPL) families. - PM POSHAN (Mid-Day Meal Scheme): Guarantees at least one freshly cooked, nutritious, hot meal every school day to all primary school students. - Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY): Supplies highly subsidized rice and wheat to the poorest of the poor households.
Marking Scheme: 2 Marks for root cause analysis + 3 Marks for government welfare schemes = 5 Marks

Chapter 4: Mangoes Round the Year (खाएं आम बारहों महीने)

NCERT Looking Around (Class 5 EVS) • Food Spoilage, Food Preservation, Bread Mould Experiment & Making Mamidi Tandra • CBSE 2026-27

📌 Essential Chapter Core Concepts
  • Difference between food spoilage (decay caused by microorganisms) and food wasting (preparing excess food and throwing it away).
  • Factors causing food spoilage: moisture, warmth, air, bacteria, and fungal spores.
  • Bread mould (fungus) grows visibly over 6 days when bread is kept moist in a closed box.
  • Traditional preservation techniques: boiling, drying in sunlight, pickling with salt and mustard oil, keeping in sugar syrup (Murabba).
  • Mamidi Tandra (Aam Papad) is prepared in Andhra Pradesh using ripe mango pulp, jaggery, and sugar, dried layer by layer under the sun over four weeks.
  • Information on packed food labels: expiry date, manufacturing date, ingredients, net weight, and maximum retail price (MRP).
NCERT Textbook Exercise & Activity Solutions (100% Coverage)
Q1
How can you tell by looking, smelling, or touching whether a food item has spoiled? Describe the bread mould experiment.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
We can detect food spoilage through several clear indicators:
  • Smell: Spoiled food emits a foul, sour, or pungent odour.
  • Appearance & Colour: Greenish, white, or black fuzzy spots of fungus appear on the surface.
  • Touch & Texture: The food becomes slimy, sticky, excessively soft, or discoloured.
Bread Mould Experiment: Sprinkle a few drops of water on a fresh slice of bread, place it in a closed box, and observe daily for 6 days with a magnifying glass: Day 1: No change. Day 2: Slight moisture on surface. Day 3: Faint white cottony threads appear. Day 4: Grayish patches form. Day 5: Greenish-black fuzzy layer covers the bread. Day 6: Dense black fungal spore heads (Rhizopus mould) with a strong foul smell.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Listing smell, colour, and texture indicators of spoilage 1.5 Marks
Detailed 6-day observation progression of bread mould 2.5 Marks
Q2
Explain step-by-step how Mamidi Tandra (Aam Papad) is prepared from ripe mangoes in Atreyapuram, Andhra Pradesh.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
The traditional preparation of Mamidi Tandra requires four weeks of dedication and hot sunny weather:
  1. Extracting Pulp: Fully ripe mangoes are selected, washed, peeled, and crushed to extract sweet pulp. The pulp is strained through a fine cloth to remove fibrous strands.
  2. Adding Sugar & Jaggery: Crushed jaggery and sugar in equal amounts are thoroughly stirred into the pulp until dissolved.
  3. Spreading the Layer: A mat woven from palm leaves is tied onto poles in a sunny yard. A thin layer of the sweet mango pulp is spread evenly on the mat and left to dry in the blazing sun all day.
  4. Building Thickness: At sunset, the mat is covered with a clean sari to keep dust off. Next day, another layer of pulp is poured over the previous layer. This process is repeated daily for 4 weeks until the golden jelly slab grows about 4 cm thick.
  5. Cutting into Pieces: The solidified dried sheet is peeled off the mat and sliced into gleaming square pieces.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Pulp extraction and filtration 1 Mark
Addition of jaggery and sugar 1 Mark
Layer-by-layer sun drying over 4 weeks 1.5 Marks
Final cutting into jelly cakes 0.5 Mark
Q3
Why is it important to check the date of expiry, manufacturing date, and ingredient list before buying packaged food items from the market?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Checking food package labels is critical for consumer health and safety:
  • Expiry Date / Best Before: Indicates the final date after which the food is no longer safe for consumption and could cause severe food poisoning.
  • Manufacturing Date: Informs the buyer how fresh the product is.
  • Ingredients: Helps buyers check for allergens (nuts, gluten, dairy), excess preservatives, artificial colours, or unhealthy sugars.
  • MRP (Maximum Retail Price): Ensures the shopkeeper does not overcharge the customer.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Importance of Expiry date for preventing food poisoning 1.5 Marks
Significance of manufacturing date and ingredients 1.5 Marks
Q4
What is the difference between food spoiling and food wasting? Give real-life examples of both.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
  • Food Spoiling: Occurs when cooked or raw food decays due to microorganisms (bacteria, yeast, mould) or enzymes, becoming unwholesome, foul-smelling, and harmful to consume. Example: Milk turning sour on a hot day or bread developing fungal mould.
  • Food Wasting: Occurs when perfectly fresh, wholesome edible food is discarded or left uneaten due to over-purchasing, excess cooking at banquets, or neglect. Example: Leaving half a plate of fresh rice at a wedding feast and throwing it in the garbage bin.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Definition and example of microbial food spoilage 1.5 Marks
Definition and example of unnecessary food wastage 1.5 Marks
Q5
Name four common food items and describe simple traditional home methods to prevent them from spoiling for a few days.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Traditional home methods of short-term preservation:
  1. Milk: Boil it thoroughly 1-2 times daily to kill spoilage bacteria.
  2. Cooked Rice: Place the bowl in a shallow basin of cold water or store in a refrigerator.
  3. Green Coriander (Dhaniya): Wrap loosely in a damp, breathable cotton cloth.
  4. Onion and Garlic: Keep in an open, dry, and well-ventilated basket away from moisture.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Scheme & Rubric:
Boiling milk & shallow water basin for rice 2 Marks
Damp cloth for coriander & dry basket for onions 2 Marks
🎯 NEP 2020 Competency Case Study: Modern Food Science vs Traditional Wisdom

Preservation Principles: Salt, Sugar, and Oil

Grandmother prepares raw mango pickle in large glass jars filled with mustard oil and salt, which stay fresh for two years without refrigeration. In contrast, fresh mango slices turn black and rot within three days.

Question: Explain the scientific principle of how heavy salt and oil prevent bacteria and fungi from spoiling pickles.

Model Answer:
Scientific Mechanism of Pickling: 1. High Salt Concentration (Osmosis): Salt draws out moisture from bacterial and fungal cells through osmosis. Without water, microorganisms cannot survive or reproduce. 2. Oil Seal (Anaerobic Barrier): The layer of oil floating on top cuts off contact with atmospheric air and oxygen. Most spoilage bacteria and moulds require oxygen to grow; the oil layer chokes them off completely. 3. Acidity of Raw Mangoes: Natural acids in raw mangoes create a low pH environment that naturally inhibits microbial growth.
Marking Scheme: 2 Marks for Osmosis principle + 2 Marks for Oil barrier + 1 Mark for Acidity = 5 Marks

Chapter 5: Seeds and Seeds (बीज, बीज, बीज)

NCERT Looking Around (Class 5 EVS) • Seed Germination, Insectivorous Plants, Seed Dispersal Agents & George Mestral's Velcro • CBSE 2026-27

📌 Essential Chapter Core Concepts
  • A seed contains an embryo (baby plant) and stored cotyledon food protected inside a seed coat.
  • Three essential conditions for seed germination: moisture (water), air (oxygen), and warmth (favourable temperature).
  • Pitcher Plant (Nepenthes) grows in Meghalaya and traps insects, frogs, and mice to absorb nitrogen in poor soil.
  • Seed dispersal mechanisms: Wind (light hairy seeds), Water (spongy buoyant seeds like coconut), Animals (hooked seeds that cling to fur), and Pod Explosion (balsam, pea).
  • George Mestral discovered Velcro in 1948 after observing burdock seeds clinging firmly to his dog's fur with microscopic hooks.
  • Global journey of crops: Chilli, potato, and tomato came from South America; Coffee and bhindi from Africa; Cabbage and peas from Europe.

Q1

Q2
Who was Gregor Mendel? What groundbreaking discovery did he make regarding pea plants?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Gregor Mendel was an Austrian monk born in 1822 in a poor farming family. Over seven years, he conducted meticulous breeding experiments on over 28,000 pea plants in his monastery garden:
  • He discovered that plant traits are passed down in discrete pairs and do not blend into an intermediate mix.
  • For instance, crossing tall and dwarf pea plants produced either tall or dwarf offspring, not medium-height plants; seed coats were either smooth or wrinkled, never halfway.
  • His work laid the fundamental foundation for the modern science of genetics.

Q3
Is Polio hereditary? A relative told Saroja that her newborn might catch polio because her aunt had polio. Is this scientifically true? What causes polio, and how can it be prevented?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
No, this is completely false scientifically.
  • Polio is NOT hereditary: Polio is an infectious disease caused by a microscopic virus (Poliovirus), not by parental genes. It never passes down through family lineage or birth traits.
  • Transmission: The poliovirus spreads through contaminated drinking water, unhygienic food, or dirty hands, attacking the spinal cord and nervous system, leading to paralysis of limbs (most commonly legs).
  • Prevention: Polio can be completely prevented through the Pulse Polio oral vaccine drops (दो बूंद जिंदगी की) given to every child under five years of age.
