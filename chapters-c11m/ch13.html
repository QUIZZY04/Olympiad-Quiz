NCERT Exercise 13.1: Mean Deviation about Mean and Median for Discrete & Continuous Data
Question 1
Exercise 13.1
Find the mean deviation about the mean for the data: $4, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 17$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Number of observations $n = 8$.
• Calculate Mean $\bar{x}$:
$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n} = \frac{4 + 7 + 8 + 9 + 10 + 12 + 13 + 17}{8} = \frac{80}{8} = 10$$
• Calculate absolute deviations $|x_i - \bar{x}|$ from mean ($10$):
- $|4 - 10| = 6$
- $|7 - 10| = 3$
- $|8 - 10| = 2$
- $|9 - 10| = 1$
- $|10 - 10| = 0$
- $|12 - 10| = 2$
- $|13 - 10| = 3$
- $|17 - 10| = 7$
• Sum of absolute deviations: $\sum |x_i - \bar{x}| = 6 + 3 + 2 + 1 + 0 + 2 + 3 + 7 = 24$.
• Mean Deviation about Mean:
$$MD(\bar{x}) = \frac{\sum |x_i - \bar{x}|}{n} = \frac{24}{8} = \mathbf{3}$$

Question 3
Exercise 13.1
Find the mean deviation about the median for the data: $13, 17, 16, 14, 11, 13, 10, 16, 11, 18, 12, 17$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Number of observations $n = 12$ (even).
• Arranging the data in ascending order:
$$10, 11, 11, 12, 13, 13, 14, 16, 16, 17, 17, 18$$
• Calculate Median $M$:
$$M = \frac{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\text{th}} \text{ term} + \left(\frac{n}{2} + 1\right)^{\text{th}} \text{ term}}{2} = \frac{6^{\text{th}} \text{ term} + 7^{\text{th}} \text{ term}}{2} = \frac{13 + 14}{2} = 13.5$$
• Absolute deviations $|x_i - 13.5|$:
$|10 - 13.5| = 3.5, \quad |11 - 13.5| = 2.5, \quad |11 - 13.5| = 2.5, \quad |12 - 13.5| = 1.5$
$|13 - 13.5| = 0.5, \quad |13 - 13.5| = 0.5, \quad |14 - 13.5| = 0.5, \quad |16 - 13.5| = 2.5$
$|16 - 13.5| = 2.5, \quad |17 - 13.5| = 3.5, \quad |17 - 13.5| = 3.5, \quad |18 - 13.5| = 4.5$
• $\sum |x_i - M| = 3.5 + 2.5 + 2.5 + 1.5 + 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5 + 2.5 + 2.5 + 3.5 + 3.5 + 4.5 = 28$.
• Mean Deviation about Median:
$$MD(M) = \frac{\sum |x_i - M|}{n} = \frac{28}{12} = \frac{7}{3} = \mathbf{2.33}$$

Question 5
Exercise 13.1
Find the mean deviation about the mean for the data:
$x_i$: $5, 10, 15, 20, 25$
$f_i$: $7, 4, 6, 3, 5$
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Total frequency $N = \sum f_i = 7 + 4 + 6 + 3 + 5 = 25$.
• Calculating $\sum f_i x_i$:
$$\sum f_i x_i = (5 \times 7) + (10 \times 4) + (15 \times 6) + (20 \times 3) + (25 \times 5)$$
$$= 35 + 40 + 90 + 60 + 125 = 350$$
• Mean $\bar{x}$:
$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{N} = \frac{350}{25} = 14$$
• Calculating $\sum f_i |x_i - \bar{x}|$:
- For $x_1 = 5$: $|5 - 14| = 9 \implies f_1|x_1 - \bar{x}| = 7 \times 9 = 63$
- For $x_2 = 10$: $|10 - 14| = 4 \implies f_2|x_2 - \bar{x}| = 4 \times 4 = 16$
- For $x_3 = 15$: $|15 - 14| = 1 \implies f_3|x_3 - \bar{x}| = 6 \times 1 = 6$
- For $x_4 = 20$: $|20 - 14| = 6 \implies f_4|x_4 - \bar{x}| = 3 \times 6 = 18$
- For $x_5 = 25$: $|25 - 14| = 11 \implies f_5|x_5 - \bar{x}| = 5 \times 11 = 55$
$$\sum f_i |x_i - \bar{x}| = 63 + 16 + 6 + 18 + 55 = 158$$
• Mean Deviation about Mean:
$$MD(\bar{x}) = \frac{\sum f_i |x_i - \bar{x}|}{N} = \frac{158}{25} = \mathbf{6.32}$$

NCERT Exercise 13.2: Variance and Standard Deviation (Direct & Shortcut Methods)
Question 1
Exercise 13.2
Find the mean and variance for the data: $6, 7, 10, 12, 13, 4, 8, 12$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Number of observations $n = 8$.
• Mean $\bar{x}$:
$$\bar{x} = \frac{6 + 7 + 10 + 12 + 13 + 4 + 8 + 12}{8} = \frac{72}{8} = \mathbf{9}$$
• Deviations and squared deviations $(x_i - 9)^2$:
- $(6 - 9)^2 = (-3)^2 = 9$
- $(7 - 9)^2 = (-2)^2 = 4$
- $(10 - 9)^2 = 1^2 = 1$
- $(12 - 9)^2 = 3^2 = 9$
- $(13 - 9)^2 = 4^2 = 16$
- $(4 - 9)^2 = (-5)^2 = 25$
- $(8 - 9)^2 = (-1)^2 = 1$
- $(12 - 9)^2 = 3^2 = 9$
$$\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 = 9 + 4 + 1 + 9 + 16 + 25 + 1 + 9 = 74$$
• Variance $\sigma^2$:
$$\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n} = \frac{74}{8} = \mathbf{9.25}$$
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Calculating mean $\bar{x} = 9$1 Mark
Calculating variance $\sigma^2 = 74/8 = 9.25$1 Mark
Question 2
Exercise 13.2
Find the mean and variance for the first $n$ natural numbers.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Data: $1, 2, 3, \dots, n$.
• Mean $\bar{x}$:
$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i}{n} = \frac{\frac{n(n + 1)}{2}}{n} = \mathbf{\frac{n + 1}{2}}$$ • Variance $\sigma^2$:
Using formula $\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum x_i^2 - (\bar{x})^2$:
$$\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 = \frac{n(n + 1)(2n + 1)}{6}$$
$$\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n}\left[\frac{n(n + 1)(2n + 1)}{6}\right] - \left(\frac{n + 1}{2}\right)^2$$
$$= \frac{(n + 1)(2n + 1)}{6} - \frac{(n + 1)^2}{4} = \frac{n + 1}{2} \left[\frac{2n + 1}{3} - \frac{n + 1}{2}\right]$$
$$= \frac{n + 1}{2} \left[\frac{4n + 2 - 3n - 3}{6}\right] = \frac{n + 1}{2} \left[\frac{n - 1}{6}\right] = \mathbf{\frac{n^2 - 1}{12}}$$
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Mean of first $n$ natural numbers $= (n+1)/2$1 Mark
Variance algebraic derivation using $\sum x_i^2$1 Mark
Final simplified variance $= (n^2 - 1)/12$1 Mark
Question 3
Exercise 13.2
Find the mean and variance for the first $10$ multiples of $3$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Data: $3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30$. ($n = 10$).
• This can be written as $x_i = 3(i)$ for $i = 1, 2, \dots, 10$.
• Mean $\bar{x}$:
$$\bar{x} = 3 \times (\text{mean of first 10 natural numbers}) = 3 \times \frac{10 + 1}{2} = 3 \times 5.5 = \mathbf{16.5}$$
• Variance $\sigma^2$:
Since each observation is multiplied by $3$, variance is multiplied by $3^2 = 9$:
$$\text{Variance of first 10 natural numbers} = \frac{10^2 - 1}{12} = \frac{99}{12} = \frac{33}{4} = 8.25$$
$$\sigma^2 = 9 \times 8.25 = \mathbf{74.25}$$

The mean and variance of eight observations are $9$ and $9.25$, respectively. If six of the observations are $6, 7, 10, 12, 12$ and $13$, find the remaining two observations.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Let the remaining two observations be $x$ and $y$.
• Total observations $n = 8$, mean $\bar{x} = 9$, variance $\sigma^2 = 9.25$.
• Step 1: Using Mean:
$$\frac{6 + 7 + 10 + 12 + 12 + 13 + x + y}{8} = 9$$
$$60 + x + y = 72 \implies x + y = 12 \implies y = 12 - x \quad \dots (1)$$
• Step 2: Using Variance:
$$\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum x_i^2 - (\bar{x})^2$$
$$9.25 = \frac{1}{8}\sum x_i^2 - 9^2 = \frac{1}{8}\sum x_i^2 - 81$$
$$\frac{1}{8}\sum x_i^2 = 90.25 \implies \sum x_i^2 = 8 \times 90.25 = 722$$
• Sum of squares of known observations:
$$6^2 + 7^2 + 10^2 + 12^2 + 12^2 + 13^2 = 36 + 49 + 100 + 144 + 144 + 169 = 642$$
$$642 + x^2 + y^2 = 722 \implies x^2 + y^2 = 722 - 642 = 80 \quad \dots (2)$$
• Substitute $(1)$ into $(2)$:
$$x^2 + (12 - x)^2 = 80 \implies x^2 + 144 - 24x + x^2 = 80$$
$$2x^2 - 24x + 64 = 0 \implies x^2 - 12x + 32 = 0$$
$$(x - 4)(x - 8) = 0 \implies x = 4 \text{ or } x = 8$$
• If $x = 4$, $y = 8$; if $x = 8$, $y = 4$.
• Hence, the remaining two observations are $\mathbf{4}$ and $\mathbf{8}$.

The mean and standard deviation of six observations are $8$ and $4$, respectively. If each observation is multiplied by $3$, find the new mean and new standard deviation of the resulting observations.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Let the original observations be $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_6$.
• Given original mean $\bar{x} = 8$ and standard deviation $\sigma = 4$.
• Let the new observations be $y_i = 3 x_i$ for $i = 1, 2, \dots, 6$.
• 1. New Mean $\bar{y}$:
$$\bar{y} = \frac{\sum y_i}{6} = \frac{\sum 3x_i}{6} = 3\left(\frac{\sum x_i}{6}\right) = 3\bar{x} = 3(8) = \mathbf{24}$$

$$\sigma_y^2 = \frac{1}{6}\sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2 = \frac{1}{6}\sum (3x_i - 3\bar{x})^2 = 3^2 \cdot \frac{1}{6}\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 = 9\sigma^2$$
$$\sigma_y = \sqrt{9\sigma^2} = 3\sigma = 3(4) = \mathbf{12}$$
• Hence, the new mean is $\mathbf{24}$ and the new standard deviation is $\mathbf{12}$.

Given that $\bar{x}$ is the mean and $\sigma^2$ is the variance of $n$ observations $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$. Prove that the mean and variance of the observations $ax_1, ax_2, \dots, ax_n$ are $a\bar{x}$ and $a^2\sigma^2$, respectively, ($a \neq 0$).
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Given observations $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ with mean $\bar{x} = \frac{1}{n}\sum x_i$ and variance $\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2$.
• Let new observations be $y_i = a x_i$.
• Proof for Mean:
$$\bar{y} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n y_i = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (a x_i) = a \left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\right) = a\bar{x}$$ (Proved).
• Proof for Variance:
$$\sigma_y^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \bar{y})^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (a x_i - a\bar{x})^2$$
$$= \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n a^2(x_i - \bar{x})^2 = a^2 \left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})^2\right] = a^2 \sigma^2$$ (Proved).
• Hence proved.
