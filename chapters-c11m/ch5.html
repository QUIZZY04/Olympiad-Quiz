📘 Key Concepts, Axioms & Interval Rules

Rules of Inequalities:
• Equal numbers may be added to (or subtracted from) both sides without changing the sign: $a \le b \implies a \pm c \le b \pm c$.
• Both sides can be multiplied/divided by the same positive number without changing sign: $c > 0 \implies ac \le bc$.
• If both sides are multiplied/divided by a negative number, the sign of inequality is reversed: $c < 0, a \le b \implies ac \ge bc$.
Interval Notations on Real Line:
• Open interval: $(a, b) = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : a < x < b\}$ (Open circles at $a, b$).
• Closed interval: $[a, b] = \{x \in \mathbb{R} : a \le x \le b\}$ (Solid dots at $a, b$).
• Semi-open / Semi-closed: $[a, b) = \{x : a \le x < b\}$ and $(a, b] = \{x : a < x \le b\}$.
• Infinite intervals: $(a, \infty) = \{x : x > a\}$, $(-\infty, b] = \{x : x \le b\}$.

Question 1
Exercise 5.1
Solve $24x < 100$, when
(i) $x$ is a natural number
(ii) $x$ is an integer.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Given linear inequality:
$$24x < 100$$
• Dividing both sides by the positive number $24$:
$$x < \frac{100}{24} = \frac{25}{6} = 4\frac{1}{6} \approx 4.167$$
• (i) When $x$ is a natural number ($x \in \mathbb{N} = \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$):
The natural numbers satisfying $x < \frac{25}{6}$ are $1, 2, 3, 4$.
$$\text{Solution set} = \mathbf{\{1, 2, 3, 4\}}$$
• (ii) When $x$ is an integer ($x \in \mathbb{Z} = \{\dots, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, \dots\}$):
The integers satisfying $x < \frac{25}{6}$ are all integers less than or equal to $4$.
$$\text{Solution set} = \mathbf{\{\dots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}}$$

Question 23
Exercise 5.1
Find all pairs of consecutive odd positive integers both of which are smaller than 10 such that their sum is more than 11.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Let the smaller odd positive integer be $x$.
• Then the next consecutive odd positive integer is $x + 2$.
• Condition 1: Both integers are smaller than $10$:
$$x + 2 < 10 \implies x < 8$$
Since $x$ is an odd positive integer, the possible values for $x$ are $\{1, 3, 5, 7\}$.

• Condition 2: Their sum is more than $11$:
$$x + (x + 2) > 11$$
$$2x + 2 > 11 \implies 2x > 9 \implies x > 4.5$$
• Combining both conditions: $4.5 < x < 8$.
• The odd positive integers satisfying this double inequality are $x = 5$ and $x = 7$:
- If $x = 5$, the pair is $(5, 5 + 2) = \mathbf{(5, 7)}$ (Check: $5 + 7 = 12 > 11$, both $< 10$).
- If $x = 7$, the pair is $(7, 7 + 2) = \mathbf{(7, 9)}$ (Check: $7 + 9 = 16 > 11$, both $< 10$).

• Conclusion: The required pairs of consecutive odd positive integers are (5, 7) and (7, 9).

Question 25
Exercise 5.1
The longest side of a triangle is 3 times the shortest side and the third side is 2 cm shorter than the longest side. If the perimeter of the triangle is at least 61 cm, find the minimum length of the shortest side.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Let the length of the shortest side of the triangle be $x\text{ cm}$.
• According to the problem:
- Longest side $= 3x\text{ cm}$
- Third side $= (3x - 2)\text{ cm}$
• The perimeter of the triangle is the sum of the three sides:
$$\text{Perimeter} = x + 3x + (3x - 2) = (7x - 2)\text{ cm}$$
• Given that the perimeter is at least $61\text{ cm}$:

A man wants to cut three lengths from a single piece of board of length 91cm. The second length is to be 3cm longer than the shortest and the third length is to be twice as long as the shortest. What are the possible lengths of the shortest board if the third piece is to be at least 5cm longer than the second?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• Let the length of the shortest piece be $x\text{ cm}$.
• Then:
- Second piece length $= (x + 3)\text{ cm}$
- Third piece length $= 2x\text{ cm}$

• Condition 1: Total length of three pieces cannot exceed 91 cm:
$$x + (x + 3) + 2x \le 91$$
$$4x + 3 \le 91 \implies 4x \le 88 \implies x \le 22$$

• Condition 2: The third piece is at least 5 cm longer than the second:
$$2x \ge (x + 3) + 5$$
$$2x \ge x + 8 \implies 2x - x \ge 8 \implies x \ge 8$$

$$8 \le x \le 22$$
• Conclusion: The possible length of the shortest board is any length between 8 cm and 22 cm inclusive, i.e., $x \in [8, 22]$.
