Q 9.1
Exercise 9.1
A small candle, $2.5 \text{ cm}$ in size is placed at $27 \text{ cm}$ in front of a concave mirror of radius of curvature $36 \text{ cm}$. At what distance from the mirror should a screen be placed in order to obtain a sharp image? Describe the nature and size of the image. If the candle is moved closer to the mirror, how would the screen have to be moved?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Size of candle (object height) $h = +2.5 \text{ cm}$
Object distance $u = -27 \text{ cm}$
Radius of curvature $R = -36 \text{ cm} \implies f = \frac{R}{2} = -18 \text{ cm}$

Step 1: Image Distance $v$:
From mirror formula: \[ \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} \implies \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u} = -\frac{1}{18} - \left(-\frac{1}{27}\right) = -\frac{1}{18} + \frac{1}{27} = \frac{-3 + 2}{54} = -\frac{1}{54} \] \[ v = -54 \text{ cm} \] The screen should be placed at a distance of $54 \text{ cm}$ in front of the mirror.

Step 2: Size and Nature of Image:
\[ m = -\frac{v}{u} = -\frac{-54}{-27} = -2 \] \[ h' = m h = -2 \times 2.5 \text{ cm} = -5.0 \text{ cm} \] The image is real, inverted, magnified ($5.0 \text{ cm}$ high).

Movement of Screen:
As the candle is moved closer to the focus ($u \to -18 \text{ cm}$), the image recedes away from the mirror ($v \to -\infty$), so the screen must be moved further away from the mirror. When the candle moves inside the focal point ($u < 18 \text{ cm}$), the image becomes virtual and cannot be captured on a screen.

Magnification and image height $h' = -5.0\text{ cm}$ with nature1 Mark
Screen movement explanation0.5 Mark
Q 9.2
Exercise 9.2
A $4.5 \text{ cm}$ needle is placed $12 \text{ cm}$ away from a convex mirror of focal length $15 \text{ cm}$. Give the location of the image and the magnification. What happens as the needle is moved farther from the mirror?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Object height $h = +4.5 \text{ cm}$
Object distance $u = -12 \text{ cm}$
Focal length of convex mirror $f = +15 \text{ cm}$

Step 1: Location of Image $v$:
\[ \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} \implies \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{15} - \left(-\frac{1}{12}\right) = \frac{1}{15} + \frac{1}{12} = \frac{4 + 5}{60} = \frac{9}{60} = \frac{3}{20} \] \[ v = +\frac{20}{3} \text{ cm} \approx +6.67 \text{ cm} \] The image is located at $6.67 \text{ cm}$ behind the mirror (between pole and focus).

Step 2: Magnification and Size:
\[ m = -\frac{v}{u} = -\frac{20/3}{-12} = +\frac{20}{36} = +\frac{5}{9} \approx +0.556 \] \[ h' = m h = \frac{5}{9} \times 4.5 = +2.5 \text{ cm} \] The image is virtual, erect, diminished ($2.5 \text{ cm}$ high).

Effect of Moving Needle Farther:
As $u \to -\infty$, $v \to +f = +15 \text{ cm}$ and $m \to 0$. The image moves towards the focus behind the mirror and progressively diminishes in size to a point.

Q 9.3
Exercise 9.3
A tank is filled with water to a height of $12.5 \text{ cm}$. The apparent depth of a needle lying at the bottom of the tank is measured by a microscope to be $9.4 \text{ cm}$. What is the refractive index of water? If water is replaced by a liquid of refractive index $1.63$ up to the same height, by what distance would the microscope have to be moved to focus on the needle again?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Real depth $h = 12.5 \text{ cm}$
Apparent depth in water $h' = 9.4 \text{ cm}$

Part 1: Refractive Index of Water:
\[ \mu = \frac{\text{Real Depth}}{\text{Apparent Depth}} = \frac{h}{h'} = \frac{12.5}{9.4} \approx 1.33 \]

Part 2: New Apparent Depth in Liquid ($\mu' = 1.63$):
\[ h'' = \frac{h}{\mu'} = \frac{12.5}{1.63} \approx 7.67 \text{ cm} \]

Distance Microscope Must be Moved:
The needle now appears closer to the surface: \[ d = h' - h'' = 9.4 \text{ cm} - 7.67 \text{ cm} = 1.73 \text{ cm} \] The microscope must be moved $1.73 \text{ cm}$ downwards (towards the liquid surface).

Calculation of $\mu_{water} = 1.33$1 Mark
Calculation of new apparent depth $h'' = 7.67\text{ cm}$1 Mark
Microscope displacement $d = 1.73\text{ cm}$ downwards1 Mark
Q 9.4
Exercise 9.4
Refraction of a ray in air at $60^\circ$ incidence gives angle of refraction $35^\circ$ in glass, and $47^\circ$ in water. Predict the angle of refraction in glass for a ray incident in water at $45^\circ$ with the normal to the water-glass interface.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Refractive Indices relative to air:
• Glass-air interface: \[ ^a\mu_g = \frac{\sin 60^\circ}{\sin 35^\circ} = \frac{0.8660}{0.5736} \approx 1.51 \] • Water-air interface: \[ ^a\mu_w = \frac{\sin 60^\circ}{\sin 47^\circ} = \frac{0.8660}{0.7314} \approx 1.184 \quad (\text{or standard } 1.33) \]

Step 2: Refractive Index of Glass with respect to Water:
\[ ^w\mu_g = \frac{^a\mu_g}{^a\mu_w} = \frac{\sin 47^\circ}{\sin 35^\circ} = \frac{0.7314}{0.5736} \approx 1.275 \]

Step 3: Angle of Refraction in Glass for $i = 45^\circ$:
\[ \frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = ^w\mu_g \implies \sin r = \frac{\sin 45^\circ}{^w\mu_g} = \frac{0.7071}{1.275} \approx 0.5546 \] \[ r = \arcsin(0.5546) \approx 38.68^\circ \approx 38.7^\circ \]

CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Relation $^w\mu_g = \frac{^a\mu_g}{^a\mu_w}$1 Mark
Calculation yielding $r \approx 38.7^\circ$1 Mark
Q 9.5
Exercise 9.5
A small bulb is placed at the bottom of a tank containing water to a depth of $80 \text{ cm}$. What is the area of the surface of water through which light from the bulb can emerge out? Refractive index of water is $1.33$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Depth $d = 80 \text{ cm} = 0.80 \text{ m}$
Refractive index $\mu = 1.33 = \frac{4}{3}$

Step 1: Critical Angle $i_c$:
Light emerges only within a cone of half-angle equal to the critical angle $i_c$: \[ \sin i_c = \frac{1}{\mu} = \frac{3}{4} = 0.75 \] \[ \tan i_c = \frac{\sin i_c}{\cos i_c} = \frac{1/\mu}{\sqrt{1 - 1/\mu^2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu^2 - 1}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(4/3)^2 - 1}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{7/9}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{7}} \]

Step 2: Radius of Circular Patch $r$:
\[ r = d \tan i_c = \frac{d}{\sqrt{\mu^2 - 1}} = \frac{0.80 \times 3}{\sqrt{7}} = \frac{2.4}{2.6458} \approx 0.907 \text{ m} \]

Step 3: Area of Circular Surface $A$:
\[ A = \pi r^2 = \frac{\pi d^2}{\mu^2 - 1} = \frac{\pi \times (0.80)^2}{(4/3)^2 - 1} = \frac{0.64\pi}{7/9} = \frac{5.76\pi}{7} \approx 2.585 \text{ m}^2 \approx 2.6 \text{ m}^2 \]

CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Critical angle relation $\sin i_c = 1/\mu$1 Mark
Radius formula $r = d / \sqrt{\mu^2 - 1}$1 Mark
Area calculation $A \approx 2.6\text{ m}^2$1 Mark
Q 9.6
Exercise 9.6
A prism is made of glass of unknown refractive index. A parallel beam of light is incident on a face of the prism. The angle of minimum deviation is measured to be $40^\circ$. What is the refractive index of the material of the prism? The refracting angle of the prism is $60^\circ$. If the prism is placed in water ($\mu_w = 1.33$), predict the new angle of minimum deviation.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Prism angle $A = 60^\circ$
Angle of minimum deviation in air $D_m = 40^\circ$

Part 1: Refractive Index in Air $\mu$:
\[ \mu = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{A + D_m}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)} = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{60^\circ + 40^\circ}{2}\right)}{\sin 30^\circ} = \frac{\sin 50^\circ}{0.5} = \frac{0.7660}{0.5} = 1.532 \]

Part 2: In Water ($\mu_w = 1.333$):
Relative refractive index: \[ \mu_{rel} = \frac{\mu}{\mu_w} = \frac{1.532}{1.333} \approx 1.149 \] \[ \mu_{rel} = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{A + D_m'}{2}\right)}{\sin(A/2)} \implies \sin\left(\frac{60^\circ + D_m'}{2}\right) = 1.149 \times \sin 30^\circ = 1.149 \times 0.5 = 0.5746 \] \[ \frac{60^\circ + D_m'}{2} = \arcsin(0.5746) \approx 35.07^\circ \] \[ 60^\circ + D_m' = 70.14^\circ \implies D_m' \approx 10.14^\circ \approx 10.1^\circ \]

CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Formula $\mu = \sin((A+D_m)/2)/\sin(A/2)$ and evaluation $\mu = 1.532$1.5 Marks
Calculation in water yielding $D_m' \approx 10.1^\circ$1.5 Marks
Q 9.7
Exercise 9.7
Double-convex lenses are to be manufactured from a glass of refractive index $1.55$, with both faces of the same radius of curvature. What is the radius of curvature required if the focal length is to be $20 \text{ cm}$?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Refractive index $\mu = 1.55$
Focal length $f = +20 \text{ cm}$
Radii of curvature: $R_1 = +R$, $R_2 = -R$ (equi-convex lens)

Lens Maker’s Formula:
\[ \frac{1}{f} = (\mu - 1) \left( \frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2} \right) = (\mu - 1) \left( \frac{1}{R} - \left(-\frac{1}{R}\right) \right) = (\mu - 1) \frac{2}{R} \]

Calculation:
\[ \frac{1}{20} = (1.55 - 1) \frac{2}{R} = 0.55 \times \frac{2}{R} = \frac{1.10}{R} \] \[ R = 1.10 \times 20 = 22.0 \text{ cm} \]

Result: The required radius of curvature is $22 \text{ cm}$.

Q 9.8
Exercise 9.8
A beam of light converges at a point $P$. Now a lens is placed in the path of the convergent beam $12 \text{ cm}$ from $P$. At what point does the beam converge if the lens is:
(a) a convex lens of focal length $20 \text{ cm}$,
(b) a concave lens of focal length $16 \text{ cm}$?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Concept: Point $P$ acts as a virtual object for the lens, so $u = +12 \text{ cm}$.

(a) Convex Lens ($f = +20 \text{ cm}$):
\[ \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} \implies \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{20} + \frac{1}{12} = \frac{3 + 5}{60} = \frac{8}{60} = \frac{2}{15} \] \[ v = +\frac{15}{2} = +7.5 \text{ cm} \] The beam converges at a real point $7.5 \text{ cm}$ to the right of the convex lens (closer to lens than $P$).

(b) Concave Lens ($f = -16 \text{ cm}$):
\[ \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} + \frac{1}{u} = -\frac{1}{16} + \frac{1}{12} = \frac{-3 + 4}{48} = +\frac{1}{48} \] \[ v = +48 \text{ cm} \] The beam converges at a real point $48 \text{ cm}$ to the right of the concave lens.

CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Identification of virtual object $u = +12\text{ cm}$1 Mark
Calculation for convex lens $v = +7.5\text{ cm}$1 Mark
Calculation for concave lens $v = +48\text{ cm}$1 Mark
Q 9.9
Exercise 9.9
An object of size $3.0 \text{ cm}$ is placed $14 \text{ cm}$ in front of a concave lens of focal length $21 \text{ cm}$. Describe the image produced by the lens. What happens if the object is moved further away from the lens?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Height $h = +3.0 \text{ cm}$, $u = -14 \text{ cm}$, $f = -21 \text{ cm}$

Step 1: Image Distance $v$:
\[ \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} \implies \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} + \frac{1}{u} = -\frac{1}{21} + \left(-\frac{1}{14}\right) = -\frac{2 + 3}{42} = -\frac{5}{42} \] \[ v = -\frac{42}{5} = -8.4 \text{ cm} \] The image is virtual and located $8.4 \text{ cm}$ in front of the lens on the same side as the object.

Step 2: Magnification and Image Height:
\[ m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{-8.4}{-14} = +0.60 \] \[ h' = m h = 0.60 \times 3.0 = +1.8 \text{ cm} \] The image is virtual, erect, diminished ($1.8 \text{ cm}$ high).

Effect of Moving Object Further Away:
As $u \to -\infty$, $v \to f = -21 \text{ cm}$ and $m \to 0$. The image shifts towards the focus and diminishes progressively in size, but never moves beyond the focal point.

Q 9.10
Exercise 9.10
What is the focal length of a convex lens of focal length $30 \text{ cm}$ in contact with a concave lens of focal length $20 \text{ cm}$? Is the system a converging or a diverging lens? Ignore thickness of the lenses.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
$f_1 = +30 \text{ cm} = +0.30 \text{ m}$ (convex)
$f_2 = -20 \text{ cm} = -0.20 \text{ m}$ (concave)

Combination of Thin Lenses in Contact:
\[ \frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{f_1} + \frac{1}{f_2} = \frac{1}{30} - \frac{1}{20} = \frac{2 - 3}{60} = -\frac{1}{60} \] \[ F = -60 \text{ cm} \] Total optical power: \[ P = \frac{100}{F (\text{cm})} = -\frac{100}{60} \approx -1.67 \text{ D} \]

Nature: Since the effective focal length is negative ($F = -60 \text{ cm}$), the combination behaves as a diverging lens system.

Q 9.11
Exercise 9.11
A compound microscope consists of an objective lens of focal length $2.0 \text{ cm}$ and an eyepiece of focal length $6.25 \text{ cm}$ separated by a distance of $15 \text{ cm}$. How far from the objective should an object be placed in order to obtain the final image at:
(a) the least distance of distinct vision ($25 \text{ cm}$), and
(b) at infinity?
What is the magnifying power of the microscope in each case?
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
$f_o = 2.0 \text{ cm}$, $f_e = 6.25 \text{ cm}$, Lens separation $L = 15 \text{ cm}$, $D = 25 \text{ cm}$

(a) Final image at near point ($v_e = -25 \text{ cm}$):
1. For eyepiece: \[ \frac{1}{v_e} - \frac{1}{u_e} = \frac{1}{f_e} \implies \frac{1}{u_e} = -\frac{1}{25} - \frac{1}{6.25} = -\frac{1}{25} - \frac{4}{25} = -\frac{5}{25} = -\frac{1}{5} \implies u_e = -5.0 \text{ cm} \] 2. Since $L = v_o + |u_e| = 15 \text{ cm} \implies v_o = 15 - 5 = +10.0 \text{ cm}$.
3. For objective: \[ \frac{1}{v_o} - \frac{1}{u_o} = \frac{1}{f_o} \implies \frac{1}{u_o} = \frac{1}{10} - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1 - 5}{10} = -\frac{4}{10} = -\frac{2}{5} \implies u_o = -2.5 \text{ cm} \] 4. Magnifying power: \[ m = m_o \times m_e = \left(-\frac{v_o}{|u_o|}\right) \left(1 + \frac{D}{f_e}\right) = \left(-\frac{10}{2.5}\right) \left(1 + \frac{25}{6.25}\right) = (-4) \times (1 + 4) = -20 \]

(b) Final image at infinity ($v_e = \infty$):
1. Here $|u_e| = f_e = 6.25 \text{ cm}$.
2. $v_o = L - f_e = 15 - 6.25 = +8.75 \text{ cm}$.
3. For objective: \[ \frac{1}{u_o} = \frac{1}{v_o} - \frac{1}{f_o} = \frac{1}{8.75} - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2 - 8.75}{17.5} = -\frac{6.75}{17.5} \implies u_o \approx -2.59 \text{ cm} \] 4. Magnifying power: \[ m = \left(-\frac{v_o}{|u_o|}\right) \left(\frac{D}{f_e}\right) = \left(-\frac{8.75}{2.59}\right) \left(\frac{25}{6.25}\right) = (-3.38) \times 4 \approx -13.5 \]

Calculations for near point: $u_o = -2.5\text{ cm}, m = -20$2.5 Marks
Calculations for normal adjustment (infinity): $u_o = -2.59\text{ cm}, m = -13.5$2.5 Marks
Q 9.12
Exercise 9.12
A person with a normal near point ($25 \text{ cm}$) using a compound microscope with objective of focal length $8.0 \text{ mm}$ and an eyepiece of focal length $2.5 \text{ cm}$ can bring an object placed at $9.0 \text{ mm}$ from the objective in sharp focus. What is the separation between the two lenses? Calculate the magnifying power of the microscope.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
$f_o = 8.0 \text{ mm} = 0.8 \text{ cm}$
$f_e = 2.5 \text{ cm}$
$u_o = -9.0 \text{ mm} = -0.9 \text{ cm}$
Final image at near point $v_e = -25 \text{ cm}$

Step 1: Image distance for Objective $v_o$:
\[ \frac{1}{v_o} - \frac{1}{u_o} = \frac{1}{f_o} \implies \frac{1}{v_o} = \frac{1}{0.8} + \frac{1}{-0.9} = \frac{10}{8} - \frac{10}{9} = \frac{90 - 80}{72} = \frac{10}{72} \] \[ v_o = +7.2 \text{ cm} \]

Step 2: Object distance for Eyepiece $u_e$:
\[ \frac{1}{u_e} = \frac{1}{v_e} - \frac{1}{f_e} = -\frac{1}{25} - \frac{1}{2.5} = -\frac{1}{25} - \frac{10}{25} = -\frac{11}{25} \implies u_e = -\frac{25}{11} \approx -2.27 \text{ cm} \]

Step 3: Lens Separation and Magnifying Power:
Separation: \[ L = v_o + |u_e| = 7.2 + 2.27 = 9.47 \text{ cm} \] Magnifying power: \[ m = m_o \times m_e = \left(-\frac{v_o}{|u_o|}\right) \left(1 + \frac{D}{f_e}\right) = \left(-\frac{7.2}{0.9}\right) \left(1 + \frac{25}{2.5}\right) = (-8) \times (1 + 10) = -88 \]

Q 9.14
Exercise 9.14
(a) A giant refracting telescope at an observatory has an objective lens of focal length $15 \text{ m}$. If an eyepiece of focal length $1.0 \text{ cm}$ is used, what is the angular magnification of the telescope?
(b) If this telescope is used to view the moon, what is the diameter of the image of the moon formed by the objective lens? The diameter of the moon is $3.48 \times 10^6 \text{ m}$, and the radius of lunar orbit is $3.8 \times 10^8 \text{ m}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
$f_o = 15 \text{ m} = 1500 \text{ cm}$, $f_e = 1.0 \text{ cm}$

(a) Angular Magnification:
\[ m = -\frac{f_o}{f_e} = -\frac{1500 \text{ cm}}{1.0 \text{ cm}} = -1500 \]

(b) Image Diameter of the Moon:
Diameter of moon $d = 3.48 \times 10^6 \text{ m}$, Distance to moon $r = 3.8 \times 10^8 \text{ m}$.
Angle subtended by the moon at the objective: \[ \theta = \frac{d}{r} = \frac{3.48 \times 10^6 \text{ m}}{3.8 \times 10^8 \text{ m}} \approx 9.158 \times 10^{-3} \text{ rad} \] The objective lens forms an image at its focal plane ($v = f_o = 15 \text{ m}$): \[ d' = f_o \theta = 15 \text{ m} \times (9.158 \times 10^{-3} \text{ rad}) \approx 0.1374 \text{ m} = 13.74 \text{ cm} \]

Q 9.15
Exercise 9.15
Use the mirror equation to deduce that:
(a) An object placed between $f$ and $2f$ of a concave mirror produces a real image beyond $2f$.
(b) A convex mirror always produces a virtual image independent of the location of the object.
(c) The virtual image produced by a convex mirror is always diminished in size and is located between the focus and the pole.
(d) An object placed between the pole and focus of a concave mirror produces a virtual and enlarged image.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Mirror Equation: $\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u}$

(a) Object between $f$ and $2f$ of concave mirror ($f < 0$):
Here $2f < u < f < 0 \implies \frac{1}{2f} > \frac{1}{u} > \frac{1}{f}$.
Subtracting $\frac{1}{f}$: \[ \frac{1}{2f} - \frac{1}{f} > \frac{1}{u} - \frac{1}{f} > 0 \implies -\frac{1}{2|f|} > -\frac{1}{v} > 0 \implies \frac{1}{v} < \frac{1}{2f} \] Since $f < 0$, this means $v < 2f$ (i.e. $v$ is negative and $|v| > 2|f|$). Thus, a real image is formed beyond $2f$.

(b) Convex mirror ($f > 0$) always virtual:
For a real object, $u < 0$, so $-\frac{1}{u} > 0$.
\[ \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} + \frac{1}{|u|} > 0 \] Since $\frac{1}{v} > 0$, $v$ is strictly positive for all values of $u$. A positive $v$ means the image is always formed behind the mirror, hence always virtual.

(c) Convex mirror image diminished and between $P$ and $F$:
Since $\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} + \frac{1}{|u|} > \frac{1}{f} \implies v < f$. Since $v > 0$, we have $0 < v < f$ (located between pole and focus).
Magnification $m = -\frac{v}{u} = \frac{v}{|u|}$. Since $v < |u|$, $m < 1$, the image is strictly diminished.

(d) Object between $P$ and $F$ of concave mirror ($f < u < 0$):
$|u| < |f| \implies \frac{1}{|u|} > \frac{1}{|f|} \implies -\frac{1}{u} > -\frac{1}{f} \implies \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u} > 0$.
Hence $v > 0$, forming a virtual image. Also $|v| > |u|$, so $m = \frac{v}{|u|} > 1$, making the image enlarged.

Answer the following conceptual questions:
(a) Plane and convex mirrors produce virtual images. Can they produce real images under some circumstances?

✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

(b) Seeing virtual images:
No contradiction. When rays diverge from a virtual image, the convex lens of the human eye converges these diverging rays onto the retina, forming a real image on the retinal screen.

Yes, the apparent depth decreases further when viewed obliquely because rays at larger angles of incidence suffer greater deviation, making the bottom appear raised higher.

Yes. Diamond has a very high refractive index ($\mu \approx 2.42$), giving it an extremely small critical angle ($i_c \approx 24.4^\circ$). Exercise 9.23
You are given prisms made of crown glass and flint glass with a wide variety of angles. Suggest a combination of prisms which will:
(a) deviate a pencil of white light without much dispersion (Achromatic combination),
(b) disperse (and displace) a pencil of white light without much deviation (Direct vision spectroscope).
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Basic Principles of Prism Combinations:
Let $A$ and $A'$ be the angles of crown and flint glass prisms, with refractive indices $\mu, \mu'$ and dispersive powers $\omega, \omega'$.

(a) Deviation without Dispersion (Achromatic Prism):
• Two prisms are placed with their refracting angles in opposite orientations.
• The condition for zero angular dispersion is: \[ (\mu_v - \mu_r) A = (\mu_v' - \mu_r') A' \implies \frac{A'}{A} = -\frac{\mu_v - \mu_r}{\mu_v' - \mu_r'} \] • The net deviation is non-zero: $\delta_{net} = (\mu - 1)A - (\mu' - 1)A' \ne 0$.
• This combination deviates the beam without splitting it into colors (used in achromatic optical instruments).

(b) Dispersion without Deviation (Direct-Vision Spectroscope):
• Prisms are placed in opposition such that the mean deviations cancel each other: \[ \delta = (\mu - 1) A = (\mu' - 1) A' \implies \frac{A'}{A} = \frac{\mu - 1}{\mu' - 1} \] • The net dispersion is non-zero: $\theta_{net} = (\mu_v - \mu_r) A - (\mu_v' - \mu_r') A' \ne 0$.
• White light enters and emerges in the same general direction but is spread out into its constituent spectral colors.

What is the primary difference between a refracting telescope and a Cassegrain reflecting telescope? State two advantages of reflecting telescopes over refracting telescopes.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Primary Difference:
• A refracting telescope uses a large convex objective lens to collect and focus incoming light via refraction.
• A Cassegrain reflecting telescope uses a large concave parabolic primary mirror to collect light by reflection, which is then reflected by a secondary convex mirror through a central hole in the primary mirror to an eyepiece.

  4. Higher Light Gathering Power: Reflecting mirrors can be manufactured with enormous diameters ($> 10 \text{ m}$), enabling observation of extremely faint, distant cosmological objects.

Derivation:
Consider a thin double-convex lens of refractive index $\mu_2 = \mu$ placed in a medium of refractive index $\mu_1 = 1$ (air).
Let $R_1$ and $R_2$ be the radii of curvature of the first and second refracting surfaces.

Step 1: Refraction at the First Surface ($R_1$):
A point object $O$ is placed at distance $u$. Refraction produces an image $I_1$ at distance $v_1$: \[ \frac{\mu}{v_1} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{\mu - 1}{R_1} \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)} \]

Step 2: Refraction at the Second Surface ($R_2$):
$I_1$ serves as a virtual object for the second surface. Since light travels from glass ($\mu$) to air ($1$): \[ \frac{1}{v} - \frac{\mu}{v_1} = \frac{1 - \mu}{R_2} = -\frac{\mu - 1}{R_2} \quad \text{--- (Equation 2)} \]

Step 3: Adding Equations (1) and (2):
\[ \left( \frac{\mu}{v_1} - \frac{1}{u} \right) + \left( \frac{1}{v} - \frac{\mu}{v_1} \right) = \frac{\mu - 1}{R_1} - \frac{\mu - 1}{R_2} \] \[ \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = (\mu - 1) \left( \frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2} \right) \quad \text{--- (Equation 3)} \]

Step 4: Defining Focal Length $f$:
When the object is at infinity ($u = -\infty$), the image is formed at the principal focus ($v = f$): \[ \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{\infty} = (\mu - 1) \left( \frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2} \right) \] \[ \frac{1}{f} = (\mu - 1) \left( \frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2} \right) \] This is the celebrated Lens Maker's Formula.
