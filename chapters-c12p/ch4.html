Exercise 4.1
NCERT Exercise
A circular coil of wire consisting of $100$ turns, each of radius $8.0\text{ cm}$ carries a current of $0.40\text{ A}$. What is the magnitude of the magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$ at the centre of the coil?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
Number of turns, $N = 100$
Radius of coil, $R = 8.0\text{ cm} = 0.08\text{ m} = 8 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m}$
Current, $I = 0.40\text{ A}$
Permeability of free space, $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\text{ T}\cdot\text{m/A}$

Formula for Magnetic Field at the Centre of a Circular Coil: $$B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2 R}$$
Calculation: $$B = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 100 \times 0.40}{2 \times (8 \times 10^{-2})}$$ $$B = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 40}{0.16} = \frac{160\pi \times 10^{-7}}{0.16} = 1000\pi \times 10^{-7} = \pi \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$$ $$B \approx 3.14 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$$ The magnitude of the magnetic field at the centre is $3.14 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Formula: B = μ₀NI / (2R)1 Mark
Computation: B = π × 10⁻⁴ T ≈ 3.14 × 10⁻⁴ T1 Mark
Exercise 4.2
NCERT Exercise
A long straight wire carries a current of $35\text{ A}$. What is the magnitude of the field $\mathbf{B}$ at a point $20\text{ cm}$ from the wire?
[1 Mark]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
Current in the wire, $I = 35\text{ A}$
Distance from wire, $r = 20\text{ cm} = 0.20\text{ m}$

Formula for Magnetic Field due to an Infinitely Long Straight Wire: $$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$$
Calculation: $$B = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 35}{2\pi \times 0.20} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-7} \times 35}{0.20} = \frac{70 \times 10^{-7}}{0.20} = \mathbf{3.5 \times 10^{-5}\text{ T}}$$ The magnitude of the magnetic field is $3.5 \times 10^{-5}\text{ T}$ ($0.35\text{ G}$).
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Formula: B = μ₀I / (2πr) and computation B = 3.5 × 10⁻⁵ T1 Mark
Exercise 4.3
NCERT Exercise
A long straight wire in the horizontal plane carries a current of $50\text{ A}$ in north to south direction. Give the magnitude and direction of $\mathbf{B}$ at a point $2.5\text{ m}$ east of the wire.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
Current, $I = 50\text{ A}$ (towards South)
Distance, $r = 2.5\text{ m}$ (towards East)

1. Magnitude of $\mathbf{B}$:
$$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 50}{2\pi \times 2.5} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-7} \times 50}{2.5} = \mathbf{4.0 \times 10^{-6}\text{ T}}$$
2. Direction using Right-Hand Thumb Rule:
Point the right thumb in the direction of current (South). The fingers curl around the wire.
At a point to the east of the wire, the fingers emerge out of the horizontal plane, directed vertically upwards.
Therefore, the magnetic field is $4.0 \times 10^{-6}\text{ T}$, directed vertically upward.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Magnitude calculation: B = 4.0 × 10⁻⁶ T1 Mark
Direction determination using Right-Hand Thumb Rule: vertically upward1 Mark
Exercise 4.4
NCERT Exercise
A horizontal overhead power line carries a current of $90\text{ A}$ in east to west direction. What is the magnitude and direction of the magnetic field due to the current $1.5\text{ m}$ below the line?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
Current, $I = 90\text{ A}$ (East to West)
Distance below the line, $r = 1.5\text{ m}$ (vertically downward)

1. Magnitude of $\mathbf{B}$:
$$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r} = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 90}{2\pi \times 1.5} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-7} \times 90}{1.5} = \mathbf{1.2 \times 10^{-5}\text{ T}}$$
2. Direction:
By the Right-Hand Rule: Point right thumb towards West. Below the wire, the fingers point towards the South.
Thus, the magnetic field at $1.5\text{ m}$ below the wire is $1.2 \times 10^{-5}\text{ T}$, directed towards South.

Exercise 4.5
NCERT Exercise
What is the magnitude of magnetic force per unit length on a wire carrying a current of $8\text{ A}$ and making an angle of $30^\circ$ with the direction of a uniform magnetic field of $0.15\text{ T}$?
[1 Mark]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
Current, $I = 8\text{ A}$
Angle, $\theta = 30^circ$
Magnetic field, $B = 0.15\text{ T}$

Formula for Magnetic Force per Unit Length ($F/L$): $$F = I L B \sin\theta \implies \frac{F}{L} = I B \sin\theta$$
Calculation: $$\frac{F}{L} = 8\text{ A} \times 0.15\text{ T} \times \sin 30^circ = 1.20 \times 0.5 = \mathbf{0.6\text{ N/m}}$$ The magnetic force per unit length on the wire is $0.6\text{ N/m}$.

Exercise 4.6
NCERT Exercise
A $3.0\text{ cm}$ wire carrying a current of $10\text{ A}$ is placed inside a solenoid perpendicular to its axis. The magnetic field inside the solenoid is given to be $0.27\text{ T}$. What is the magnetic force on the wire?
[1 Mark]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
Length of wire, $L = 3.0\text{ cm} = 0.03\text{ m} = 3 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m}$
Current in wire, $I = 10\text{ A}$
Magnetic field inside solenoid (along axis), $B = 0.27\text{ T}$
Since the wire is perpendicular to the solenoid axis: $\theta = 90^circ$

Formula for Magnetic Force: $$F = I L B \sin\theta$$
Calculation: $$F = 10\text{ A} \times (0.03\text{ m}) \times 0.27\text{ T} \times \sin 90^circ = 0.3 \times 0.27 \times 1 = \mathbf{8.1 \times 10^{-2}\text{ N}}$$ The magnetic force on the wire is $8.1 \times 10^{-2}\text{ N}$ (directed perpendicular to both wire and solenoid axis by Fleming's left-hand rule).
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)

Exercise 4.7
NCERT Exercise
Two long and parallel straight wires $A$ and $B$ carrying currents of $8.0\text{ A}$ and $5.0\text{ A}$ in the same direction are separated by a distance of $4.0\text{ cm}$. Estimate the force on a $10\text{ cm}$ section of wire $A$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
$I_A = 8.0\text{ A}$, $I_B = 5.0\text{ A}$ (parallel currents)
Separation, $d = 4.0\text{ cm} = 0.04\text{ m}$
Length of section on wire $A$, $L = 10\text{ cm} = 0.10\text{ m}$

Formula for Force between Two Parallel Current Wires: $$F = \frac{\mu_0 I_A I_B}{2\pi d} \times L$$
Calculation: $$F = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 8.0 \times 5.0}{2\pi \times 0.04} \times 0.10$$ $$F = \frac{2 \times 10^{-7} \times 40}{0.04} \times 0.10 = \frac{80 \times 10^{-7}}{0.04} \times 0.10 = 2000 \times 10^{-7} \times 0.10 = \mathbf{2.0 \times 10^{-5}\text{ N}}$$ Since both currents flow in the same direction (parallel), the force is attractive (directed towards wire $B$).

Exercise 4.8
NCERT Exercise
A closely wound solenoid $80\text{ cm}$ long has $5$ layers of windings of $400$ turns each. The diameter of the solenoid is $1.8\text{ cm}$. If the current carried is $8.0\text{ A}$, estimate the magnitude of $\mathbf{B}$ inside the solenoid near its centre.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
Length of solenoid, $L = 80\text{ cm} = 0.80\text{ m}$
Total number of turns, $N = 5 \times 400 = 2000\text{ turns}$
Current, $I = 8.0\text{ A}$
Turns per unit length, $n = \frac{N}{L} = \frac{2000}{0.80\text{ m}} = 2500\text{ turns/m}$

Formula for Magnetic Field inside a Long Solenoid: $$B = \mu_0 n I = \mu_0 \left(\frac{N}{L}\right) I$$
Calculation: $$B = (4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 2500 \times 8.0$$ $$B = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 20000 = 80000\pi \times 10^{-7} = 8\pi \times 10^{-3}\text{ T}$$ $$B \approx 8 \times 3.1416 \times 10^{-3} = \mathbf{2.51 \times 10^{-2}\text{ T} \approx 2.5 \times 10^{-2}\text{ T}}$$ The magnetic field inside the solenoid near its centre is $2.5 \times 10^{-2}\text{ T}$ ($250\text{ G}$).

A square coil of side $10\text{ cm}$ consists of $20$ turns and carries a current of $12\text{ A}$. The coil is suspended vertically and the normal to the plane of the coil makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the direction of a uniform horizontal magnetic field of magnitude $0.80\text{ T}$. What is the magnitude of torque experienced by the coil?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Side of square coil, $s = 10\text{ cm} = 0.10\text{ m}$

Exercise 4.10
NCERT Exercise
Two moving coil meters, $M_1$ and $M_2$ have the following particulars:
$R_1 = 10\ \Omega, N_1 = 30, A_1 = 3.6 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2, B_1 = 0.25\text{ T}$
$R_2 = 14\ \Omega, N_2 = 42, A_2 = 1.8 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2, B_2 = 0.50\text{ T}$
(The spring constants are identical, $k_1 = k_2$). Determine the ratio of (a) current sensitivity and (b) voltage sensitivity of $M_2$ and $M_1$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Definitions:
Deflection $\theta = \frac{N B A}{k} I$.
Current sensitivity: $I_s = \frac{\theta}{I} = \frac{N B A}{k}$
Voltage sensitivity: $V_s = \frac{\theta}{V} = \frac{\theta}{I R} = \frac{I_s}{R} = \frac{N B A}{k R}$

(a) Ratio of Current Sensitivity ($I_{s2} / I_{s1}$):
Since $k_1 = k_2 = k$: $$\frac{I_{s2}}{I_{s1}} = \frac{N_2 B_2 A_2}{N_1 B_1 A_1} = \frac{42 \times 0.50 \times (1.8 \times 10^{-3})}{30 \times 0.25 \times (3.6 \times 10^{-3})}$$ $$\frac{I_{s2}}{I_{s1}} = \frac{42}{30} \times \frac{0.50}{0.25} \times \frac{1.8}{3.6} = 1.4 \times 2 \times 0.5 = \mathbf{1.4}$$
(b) Ratio of Voltage Sensitivity ($V_{s2} / V_{s1}$):
$$\frac{V_{s2}}{V_{s1}} = \left(\frac{I_{s2}}{I_{s1}}\right) \times \left(\frac{R_1}{R_2}\right) = 1.4 \times \frac{10}{14} = 1.4 \times \frac{5}{7} = \mathbf{1.0}$$ The ratio of current sensitivity is $1.4$ and voltage sensitivity is $1.0$.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) Current sensitivity ratio: Is2 / Is1 = 1.41 Mark
(b) Voltage sensitivity ratio: Vs2 / Vs1 = 1.01 Mark
Exercise 4.11
NCERT Exercise
In a chamber, a uniform magnetic field of $6.5\text{ G}$ ($1\text{ G} = 10^{-4}\text{ T}$) is maintained. An electron is shot into the field with a speed of $4.8 \times 10^6\text{ m/s}$ normal to the field. Explain why the path of the electron is a circle. Determine the radius of the circular orbit. ($e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}, m_e = 9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$).
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Why the Path is a Circle:
When a charged particle moves with velocity $\mathbf{v}$ perpendicular to a magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$ ($\theta = 90^circ$): The magnetic Lorentz force is $\mathbf{F} = q(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$.
This force is always strictly perpendicular to the instantaneous velocity vector $\mathbf{v}$ ($F \perp v$).
Therefore, the magnetic force does zero work ($W = \int \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0$), leaving the kinetic energy and speed $v$ strictly constant.
A constant perpendicular force of constant magnitude $F = q v B$ acts purely as a centripetal force, constraining the electron to move in a uniform circular path.

2. Radius of the Circular Orbit ($r$):
Equating centripetal force to magnetic force: $$\frac{m v^2}{r} = e v B \implies r = \frac{m v}{e B}$$
Calculation: Given: $B = 6.5\text{ G} = 6.5 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$, $v = 4.8 \times 10^6\text{ m/s}$ $$r = \frac{(9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}) \times (4.8 \times 10^6\text{ m/s})}{(1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}) \times (6.5 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T})}$$ $$r = \frac{43.68 \times 10^{-25}}{10.4 \times 10^{-23}} = 4.2 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m} = \mathbf{4.2\text{ cm}}$$ The radius of the circular orbit is $4.2\text{ cm}$.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Explanation of perpendicular force providing centripetal acceleration without altering speed1.5 Marks
Radius calculation: r = mv / (eB) = 4.2 cm1.5 Marks
Exercise 4.12
NCERT Exercise
In Exercise 4.11 obtain the frequency of revolution of the electron in its circular orbit. Does the answer depend on the speed of the electron? Explain.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Formula for Frequency of Revolution (Cyclotron Frequency):
Period of revolution is the time taken to complete one circumference: $$T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi (m v / e B)}{v} = \frac{2\pi m}{e B}$$ Frequency of revolution is the reciprocal of time period: $$\nu = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{e B}{2\pi m}$$
2. Calculation:
Using $B = 6.5 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$, $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$, $m = 9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$: $$\nu = \frac{(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times (6.5 \times 10^{-4})}{2 \times 3.1416 \times (9.1 \times 10^{-31})}$$ $$\nu = \frac{1.04 \times 10^{-22}}{5.7177 \times 10^{-30}} \approx \mathbf{1.82 \times 10^7\text{ Hz} = 18.2\text{ MHz}}$$ The frequency of revolution is $18.2\text{ MHz}$.

3. Independence from Speed:
No, the frequency $\nu$ is strictly independent of the speed ($v$) and radius ($r$) of the electron. If the speed increases, the radius increases in the exact same proportion ($r \propto v$), keeping the time taken per revolution ($T = 2\pi r / v$) perfectly constant. This fundamental property is the working principle of the cyclotron.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Frequency formula and calculation: ν = eB / (2πm) ≈ 18.2 MHz1 Mark
Explanation of independence of frequency from electron speed (cyclotron principle)1 Mark
Exercise 4.13
NCERT Exercise
(a) A circular coil of $30$ turns and radius $8.0\text{ cm}$ carrying a current of $6.0\text{ A}$ is suspended vertically in a uniform horizontal magnetic field of magnitude $1.0\text{ T}$. The field lines make an angle of $60^\circ$ with the normal of the coil. Calculate the magnitude of the counter torque that must be applied to prevent the coil from turning.
(b) Would your answer change, if the circular coil in (a) were replaced by a planar coil of some irregular shape that encloses the same area?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Magnitude of Counter Torque:
To prevent the coil from turning, the applied counter torque must equal the deflecting magnetic torque $\tau$ in magnitude: $$\tau = N I A B \sin\theta$$ Given Data: $N = 30$, $r = 8.0\text{ cm} = 0.08\text{ m} \implies A = \pi r^2 = \pi (0.08)^2 = 0.0201\text{ m}^2$
$I = 6.0\text{ A}$, $B = 1.0\text{ T}$, $\theta = 60^circ$
$$\tau = 30 \times 6.0 \times (\pi \times 0.0064) \times 1.0 \times \sin 60^circ$$ $$\tau = 180 \times 0.0201 \times 0.866 \approx \mathbf{3.13\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$$ The required counter torque is $3.13\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.

(b) If replaced by an irregular planar coil of the same area:
No, the answer will not change.
The magnetic dipole moment of any planar loop is given by $\mathbf{M} = N I \mathbf{A}$, which depends only on the enclosed planar area $A$ and is completely independent of the shape of the boundary. Since the area $A$ and angle $\theta$ remain the same, the torque is unchanged.

(a) Counter torque calculation: τ = 3.13 N·m1 Mark
(b) Explanation that magnetic torque depends solely on enclosed area A, not shape1 Mark
Exercise 4.14
NCERT Exercise
Two concentric circular coils $X$ and $Y$ of radii $16\text{ cm}$ and $10\text{ cm}$, respectively, lie in the same vertical plane containing the north to south direction. Coil $X$ has $20$ turns and carries a current of $16\text{ A}$; coil $Y$ has $25$ turns and carries a current of $18\text{ A}$. The sense of the current in $X$ is anticlockwise, and in $Y$, clockwise, for an observer looking at the coils facing west. Give the magnitude and direction of the net magnetic field due to the coils at their centre.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Field due to Coil $X$:
$N_X = 20$, $R_X = 16\text{ cm} = 0.16\text{ m}$, $I_X = 16\text{ A}$ (anticlockwise facing west)
$$B_X = \frac{\mu_0 N_X I_X}{2 R_X} = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 20 \times 16}{2 \times 0.16} = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 320}{0.32} = 4\pi \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$$ By right-hand rule, anticlockwise current produces a field directed towards the West: $$B_X = 4\pi \times 10^{-4}\text{ T} \approx 1.26 \times 10^{-3}\text{ T} \text{ (West)}$$
Field due to Coil $Y$:
$N_Y = 25$, $R_Y = 10\text{ cm} = 0.10\text{ m}$, $I_Y = 18\text{ A}$ (clockwise facing west)
$$B_Y = \frac{\mu_0 N_Y I_Y}{2 R_Y} = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 25 \times 18}{2 \times 0.10} = \frac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 450}{0.20} = 9\pi \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$$ By right-hand rule, clockwise current produces a field directed towards the East: $$B_Y = 9\pi \times 10^{-4}\text{ T} \approx 2.83 \times 10^{-3}\text{ T} \text{ (East)}$$
Net Magnetic Field at the Centre:
Since the two fields are collinear and oppositely directed, and $B_Y > B_X$: $$B_{\text{net}} = B_Y - B_X = (9\pi - 4\pi) \times 10^{-4} = 5\pi \times 10^{-4}\text{ T} \approx \mathbf{1.57 \times 10^{-3}\text{ T} \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-3}\text{ T}}$$ The net magnetic field has magnitude $1.6 \times 10^{-3}\text{ T}$ ($16\text{ G}$), directed towards East.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Calculation of BX = 4π × 10⁻⁴ T directed West1 Mark
Calculation of BY = 9π × 10⁻⁴ T directed East1 Mark
Net field B_net = 5π × 10⁻⁴ T ≈ 1.6 × 10⁻³ T towards East1 Mark
Exercise 4.15
NCERT Exercise
A magnetic field of $100\text{ G}$ ($10^{-2}\text{ T}$) is required which is uniform in a region of linear dimension about $10\text{ cm}$ and area of cross-section about $10^{-3}\text{ m}^2$. The maximum current-carrying capacity of a given coil of wire is $15\text{ A}$ and the number of turns per unit length that can be wound round a core is at most $1000\text{ turns/m}$. Suggest some appropriate design particulars of a solenoid for the required purpose. Assume the core is not ferromagnetic.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Design Requirements:
Magnetic field, $B = 100\text{ G} = 10^{-2}\text{ T}$
Cross-sectional area $A = 10^{-3}\text{ m}^2 \implies$ radius $r = \sqrt{A/\pi} = \sqrt{10^{-3}/3.14} \approx 1.8\text{ cm} \implies$ diameter $D \approx 3.6\text{ cm}$
Maximum current $I \le 15\text{ A}$, maximum winding density $n \le 1000\text{ turns/m}$

Solenoid Field Equation: $$B = \mu_0 n I \implies n I = \frac{B}{\mu_0} = \frac{10^{-2}\text{ T}}{4\pi \times 10^{-7}\text{ T}\cdot\text{m/A}} = \frac{10^5}{4\pi} \approx 7958\text{ A}\cdot\text{turns/m}$$
Design Choice:
To produce uniform field over a region of $10\text{ cm}$, the solenoid length must be much larger than its radius (typically $L \ge 50\text{ cm}$):
  • Select operating current safely below maximum capacity: say, $I = 10\text{ A}$.
  • Then required turns per unit length: $n = \frac{7958}{10} \approx 800\text{ turns/m}$ (well within the $1000\text{ turns/m}$ constraint).
  • Length of solenoid: $L = 50\text{ cm} = 0.5\text{ m}$.
  • Total turns required: $N = n \times L = 800 \times 0.5 = 400\text{ turns}$.
Suggested Parameters: Solenoid of length $L = 50\text{ cm}$, diameter $\sim 4\text{ cm}$, carrying $I = 10\text{ A}$ with $N = 400\text{ turns}$ in two layers.

Exercise 4.16
NCERT Exercise
For a circular coil of radius $R$ and $N$ turns carrying current $I$, the magnetic field on its axis at distance $x$ is $B = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2 N}{2(x^2 + R^2)^{3/2}}$.
(a) Show that this reduces to the familiar result for field at the centre of the coil.
(b) Consider two parallel co-axial circular coils of equal radius $R$, and number of turns $N$, carrying equal currents in the same direction, and separated by a distance $R$ (Helmholtz coils). Show that the field on the axis around the mid-point between the coils is uniform over a distance that is small as compared to $R$, and is given by $B \approx 0.72 \frac{\mu_0 N I}{R}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Reduction to Centre ($x = 0$):
At the centre of the coil, axial distance $x = 0$: $$B(0) = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2 N}{2(0^2 + R^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{\mu_0 I R^2 N}{2 R^3} = \mathbf{\frac{\mu_0 N I}{2 R}}$$ This is the standard expression for the magnetic field at the centre of a flat circular coil.

(b) Helmholtz Coils Field Uniformity:
Let the midpoint between the coils be $O$. Midpoint is at distance $x_0 = R/2$ from each coil.
Consider a small axial displacement $d$ from the midpoint: distance from coil 1 is $(R/2 + d)$ and from coil 2 is $(R/2 - d)$.
Total field: $$B(d) = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2} \left[ \left( \left(\frac{R}{2} + d\right)^2 + R^2 \right)^{-3/2} + \left( \left(\frac{R}{2} - d\right)^2 + R^2 \right)^{-3/2} \right]$$ At $d = 0$: $$B(0) = 2 \times \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{2 \left( \frac{R^2}{4} + R^2 \right)^{3/2}} = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{\left(\frac{5}{4} R^2\right)^{3/2}} = \frac{\mu_0 N I R^2}{\left(\frac{5}{4}\right)^{3/2} R^3} = \left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{3/2} \frac{\mu_0 N I}{R}$$ $$\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{3/2} = (0.8)^{1.5} \approx 0.7155 \approx \mathbf{0.72}$$ $$B(0) \approx \mathbf{0.72 \frac{\mu_0 N I}{R}}$$
Moreover, taking derivatives shows that both the first derivative $\frac{dB}{dd} = 0$ and the second derivative $\frac{d^2B}{dd^2} = 0$ vanish identically at $d = 0$, guaranteeing remarkable flatness and uniformity of field near the centre.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) Substitution x = 0 yielding B = μ₀NI / (2R)1 Mark
(b) Superposition at midpoint x = R/2: B = (4/5)³/² · (μ₀NI/R) ≈ 0.72 μ₀NI/R2 Marks
Exercise 4.17
NCERT Exercise
A toroid has a core of inner radius $25\text{ cm}$ and outer radius $26\text{ cm}$, around which $3500$ turns of a wire are wound. If the current in the wire is $11\text{ A}$, what is the magnetic field: (a) outside the toroid, (b) inside the core of the toroid, (c) in the empty space surrounded by the toroid?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Outside the Toroid:
For any closed Amperian loop outside the outer radius of the toroid, the net current enclosed is zero (each turn crosses the loop once in one direction and once in the opposite direction).
By Ampere's Circuital Law: $\mathbf{B = 0\text{ T}}$.

(b) Inside the Core of the Toroid:
Mean radius: $r = \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2} = \frac{25 + 26}{2} = 25.5\text{ cm} = 0.255\text{ m}$
Total turns, $N = 3500$, Current, $I = 11\text{ A}$
$$B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{2\pi r} = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 3500 \times 11}{2\pi \times 0.255} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-7} \times 38500}{0.255} = \frac{7.7 \times 10^{-3}}{0.255} \approx \mathbf{3.02 \times 10^{-2}\text{ T} \approx 3.0 \times 10^{-2}\text{ T}}$$

(c) In the Empty Space Surrounded by the Toroid (interior cavity):
For any Amperian loop inside the central hole ($r < r_1$), no turns are enclosed ($I_{\text{enc}} = 0$).
By Ampere's Law: $\mathbf{B = 0\text{ T}}$.

(a) B = 0 outside toroid1 Mark
(b) Mean radius calculation & core field B ≈ 3.0 × 10⁻² T1 Mark
(c) B = 0 in internal empty space1 Mark
Exercise 4.18
NCERT Exercise
Answer the following questions:
(a) A magnetic field that varies in magnitude from point to point but has a constant direction (east to west) is set up in a chamber. A charged particle enters the chamber and travels undeflected along a straight path with constant speed. What can you say about the initial velocity of the particle?
(b) A charged particle enters an environment of a strong and non-uniform magnetic field varying from point to point both in magnitude and direction, and comes out of it following a complicated trajectory. Would its final speed equal the initial speed if it suffered no collisions with the environment?
(c) An electron travelling west to east enters a chamber having a uniform electrostatic field in north to south direction. Specify the direction in which a uniform magnetic field should be set up to prevent the electron from deflecting from its straight line path.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Initial Velocity:
Magnetic force is $\mathbf{F} = q(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$. The particle travels undeflected if and only if $\mathbf{F} = 0$.
This requires $\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B} = 0 \implies \sin\theta = 0$.
Therefore, the initial velocity vector $\mathbf{v}$ must be strictly parallel or antiparallel to the magnetic field (i.e., traveling either due East or due West).

(b) Constancy of Speed in Pure Magnetic Field:
Yes, the final speed will be strictly equal to the initial speed.
The magnetic force $\mathbf{F} = q(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$ is at every instant perpendicular to the instantaneous velocity $\mathbf{v}$.
Power delivered: $P = \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0$. The magnetic force can do no work ($W = 0$) on a charged particle. By the work-energy theorem, the kinetic energy and hence the speed remains completely constant throughout any trajectory.

(c) Preventing Electron Deflection (Velocity Selector):
Electric field is North to South. Since the electron carries a negative charge ($-e$), the electric force acts in the opposite direction (South to North).
To prevent deflection, the magnetic force must act in the exact opposite direction: North to South.
Using Fleming's left-hand rule for negative charge moving East: The required uniform magnetic field must be directed vertically downwards.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) v must be parallel or antiparallel to B (East or West)1 Mark
(b) Final speed equals initial speed because magnetic force does no work (F ⊥ v)1 Mark
(c) Magnetic field must be directed vertically downwards1 Mark
Exercise 4.19
NCERT Exercise
An electron emitted by a heated cathode and accelerated through a potential difference of $2.0\text{ kV}$, enters a region with uniform magnetic field of $0.15\text{ T}$. Determine the trajectory of the electron if the field:
(a) is transverse to its initial velocity,
(b) makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the initial velocity.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Velocity Acquired by Electron:
Kinetic energy = Electric work done: $$\frac{1}{2} m v^2 = e V_0 \implies v = \sqrt{\frac{2 e V_0}{m}}$$ $$v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times (1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times 2000}{9.1 \times 10^{-31}}} = \sqrt{\frac{6.4 \times 10^{-16}}{9.1 \times 10^{-31}}} = \sqrt{7.033 \times 10^{14}} = \mathbf{2.65 \times 10^7\text{ m/s}}$$
(a) Field Transverse to Velocity ($\theta = 90^circ$):
The trajectory is a circle.
Radius of circular orbit: $$r = \frac{m v}{e B} = \frac{(9.1 \times 10^{-31}) \times (2.65 \times 10^7)}{(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times 0.15} = \frac{2.411 \times 10^{-23}}{2.4 \times 10^{-20}} = 1.005 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m} = \mathbf{1.0\text{ mm}}$$
(b) Field at $\theta = 30^circ$:
Velocity resolves into two components:
  • Parallel component: $v_\parallel = v \cos 30^circ = 2.65 \times 10^7 \times 0.866 = 2.295 \times 10^7\text{ m/s}$ (produces uniform translation along $\mathbf{B}$).
  • Perpendicular component: $v_\perp = v \sin 30^circ = 2.65 \times 10^7 \times 0.5 = 1.325 \times 10^7\text{ m/s}$ (produces circular motion).
The resultant trajectory is a helix.
Radius of helix: $$r = \frac{m v_\perp}{e B} = \frac{m v \sin 30^circ}{e B} = \frac{1.0\text{ mm}}{2} = \mathbf{0.5\text{ mm}}$$
Pitch of helix (axial distance moved in one period $T = 2\pi m / eB$): $$p = v_\parallel T = \frac{2\pi m v_\parallel}{e B} = \frac{2\pi \times (9.1 \times 10^{-31}) \times (2.295 \times 10^7)}{(1.6 \times 10^{-19}) \times 0.15} = \mathbf{5.47 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m} = 5.5\text{ mm}}$$

(a) Circular trajectory with radius r = 1.0 mm1.5 Marks
(b) Helical trajectory with radius r = 0.5 mm and pitch p = 5.5 mm1.5 Marks
Exercise 4.20
NCERT Exercise
A magnetic field set up using Helmholtz coils is uniform in a small region and has a magnitude of $0.75\text{ T}$. In the same region, a uniform electrostatic field is maintained by a parallel plate capacitor. What is the potential difference between the plates of separation $1.0\text{ cm}$ to allow $15\text{ keV}$ electrons to pass undeflected?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Condition for Undeflected Motion (Velocity Selector):
Electric force must balance magnetic force: $$e E = e v B \implies E = v B$$ Potential difference between plates separated by distance $d$: $$V = E d = v B d$$
Velocity of $15\text{ keV}$ Electron:
$$K = 15\text{ keV} = 15 \times 10^3 \times (1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}) = 2.4 \times 10^{-15}\text{ J}$$ $$v = \sqrt{\frac{2K}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 2.4 \times 10^{-15}\text{ J}}{9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}}} = \sqrt{5.275 \times 10^{15}} = \mathbf{7.26 \times 10^7\text{ m/s}}$$
Required Potential Difference:
$$V = (7.26 \times 10^7\text{ m/s}) \times 0.75\text{ T} \times (0.01\text{ m}) = \mathbf{5.45 \times 10^5\text{ V} = 545\text{ kV}}$$ The required potential difference between the plates is $545\text{ kV}$.

Exercise 4.21
NCERT Exercise
A straight horizontal conducting rod of length $0.45\text{ m}$ and mass $60\text{ g}$ is suspended by two vertical wires at its ends. A current of $5.0\text{ A}$ is set up in the rod through the wires.
(a) What magnetic field normal to the conductor must be set up in order that the tension in the wires is zero?
(b) What will be the total tension in the wires if the direction of current is reversed keeping the magnetic field same as before? (Take $g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$).
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
Length of rod, $L = 0.45\text{ m}$
Mass, $m = 60\text{ g} = 0.06\text{ kg}$
Weight of rod, $W = m g = 0.06 \times 9.8 = 0.588\text{ N}$ (acting vertically downward)
Current, $I = 5.0\text{ A}$

(a) Magnetic Field for Zero Tension ($T = 0$):
The upward magnetic force must exactly balance the downward gravitational weight: $$F_m = I L B = m g \implies B = \frac{m g}{I L}$$ $$B = \frac{0.588\text{ N}}{5.0\text{ A} \times 0.45\text{ m}} = \frac{0.588}{2.25} = \mathbf{0.261\text{ T} \approx 0.26\text{ T}}$$
(b) Total Tension when Current is Reversed:
When the current direction is reversed, the magnetic force points downwards in the same direction as gravity.
Total tension supported by the wires: $$T = W + F_m = m g + I L B = 0.588\text{ N} + 0.588\text{ N} = \mathbf{1.176\text{ N} \approx 1.18\text{ N}}$$

NCERT Exercise
The wires which connect the battery of an automobile to its starting motor carry a current of $300\text{ A}$ (for a short time). What is the force per unit length between the wires if they are $70\text{ cm}$ long and $1.5\text{ cm}$ apart? Is the force attractive or repulsive?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
Current in wires, $I_1 = I_2 = 300\text{ A}$ (opposite antiparallel directions: one carrying current to motor, the other returning it)
Distance of separation, $d = 1.5\text{ cm} = 1.5 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m} = 0.015\text{ m}$

Formula for Force per Unit Length: $$\frac{F}{L} = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi d}$$
Calculation: $$\frac{F}{L} = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 300 \times 300}{2\pi \times 0.015} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-7} \times 90000}{0.015} = \frac{1.8 \times 10^{-2}}{0.015} = \mathbf{1.2\text{ N/m}}$$
Nature of Force:
Since the two wires carry currents in opposite directions (antiparallel return circuit), the force between them is repulsive.

Exercise 4.23
NCERT Exercise
A uniform magnetic field of $1.5\text{ T}$ exists in a cylindrical region of radius $10.0\text{ cm}$, its direction parallel to the axis along east to west. A wire carrying current of $7.0\text{ A}$ in the north to south direction passes through this region. What is the magnitude and direction of the force on the wire if:
(a) the wire intersects the axis,
(b) the wire is turned from N-S to northeast-northwest direction,
(c) the wire in the N-S direction is lowered from the axis by a distance of $6.0\text{ cm}$?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given: $B = 1.5\text{ T}$ (East-West), current $I = 7.0\text{ A}$ (North-South), cylinder radius $R = 10.0\text{ cm}$.

(a) Wire intersects the axis:
The length of wire inside the magnetic region is the diameter of the cylinder: $$L = 2 R = 2 \times 0.10\text{ m} = 0.20\text{ m}$$ Angle between current and field is $\theta = 90^circ$: $$F = I L B = 7.0\text{ A} \times 0.20\text{ m} \times 1.5\text{ T} = \mathbf{2.1\text{ N} \text{ (vertically downwards)}}$$
(b) Wire turned to northeast-northwest direction (making angle $\theta = 45^circ$ with axis):
The length of the wire inside the cylinder increases to $L' = \frac{2R}{\sin 45^circ} = \frac{0.20}{\sin 45^circ}$.
Force: $$F = I L' B \sin 45^circ = I \left(\frac{2R}{\sin 45^circ}\right) B \sin 45^circ = I (2R) B = \mathbf{2.1\text{ N} \text{ (vertically downwards)}}$$ The force remains unchanged!

(c) Wire lowered by $d = 6.0\text{ cm}$:
The chord length inside the circular cross-section is: $$L'' = 2\sqrt{R^2 - d^2} = 2\sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = 2\sqrt{100 - 36} = 2\sqrt{64} = 16\text{ cm} = 0.16\text{ m}$$ $$F = I L'' B = 7.0\text{ A} \times 0.16\text{ m} \times 1.5\text{ T} = \mathbf{1.68\text{ N} \approx 1.7\text{ N} \text{ (vertically downwards)}}$$

Exercise 4.24
NCERT Exercise
A galvanometer coil has a resistance of $12\ \Omega$ and the meter shows full scale deflection for a current of $3\text{ mA}$. How will you convert the meter into:
(a) a voltmeter of range $0$ to $18\text{ V}$?
(b) an ammeter of range $0$ to $6\text{ A}$?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
Galvanometer resistance, $G = 12\ \Omega$
Full-scale deflection current, $I_g = 3\text{ mA} = 3 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A}$

(a) Conversion into Voltmeter ($0$ to $18\text{ V}$):
A voltmeter is constructed by connecting a high resistance $R$ in series with the galvanometer coil: $$V = I_g (G + R) \implies G + R = \frac{V}{I_g} \implies R = \frac{V}{I_g} - G$$ $$R = \frac{18\text{ V}}{3 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A}} - 12\ \Omega = 6000 - 12 = \mathbf{5988\ \Omega}$$ Method: Connect a resistance of $5988\ \Omega$ in series with the galvanometer.

(b) Conversion into Ammeter ($0$ to $6\text{ A}$):
An ammeter is constructed by connecting a very small shunt resistance $S$ in parallel with the galvanometer coil: $$I_g G = (I - I_g) S \implies S = \frac{I_g G}{I - I_g}$$ Given $I = 6\text{ A}$: $$S = \frac{(3 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A}) \times 12\ \Omega}{6 - 0.003} = \frac{0.036}{5.997} \approx \mathbf{0.006\ \Omega = 6 \times 10^{-3}\ \Omega = 6\text{ m}\Omega}$$ Method: Connect a shunt resistance of $6 \times 10^{-3}\ \Omega$ ($6\text{ m}\Omega$) in parallel with the galvanometer.
