Q 7.1
Exercise 7.1
A $100 \ \Omega$ resistor is connected to a $220 \text{ V}, 50 \text{ Hz}$ ac supply.
(a) What is the rms value of current in the circuit?
(b) What is the net power consumed over a full cycle?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Resistance $R = 100 \ \Omega$
RMS voltage $V_{rms} = 220 \text{ V}$
Frequency $\nu = 50 \text{ Hz}$

(a) RMS Current $I_{rms}$:
For a purely resistive circuit, the impedance is simply $Z = R$: \[ I_{rms} = \frac{V_{rms}}{R} = \frac{220 \text{ V}}{100 \ \Omega} = 2.20 \text{ A} \]

(b) Net Power Consumed over a Full Cycle:
In a purely resistive circuit, current and voltage are in phase ($\phi = 0^\circ$, power factor $\cos\phi = 1$): \[ P = V_{rms} I_{rms} \cos \phi = 220 \times 2.20 \times 1 = 484 \text{ W} \] Alternatively: \[ P = I_{rms}^2 R = (2.2)^2 \times 100 = 4.84 \times 100 = 484 \text{ W} \]

Q 7.2
Exercise 7.2
(a) The peak voltage of an ac supply is $300 \text{ V}$. What is the rms voltage?
(b) The rms value of current in an ac circuit is $10 \text{ A}$. What is the peak current?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

(a) RMS Voltage ($V_{rms}$):
Given peak voltage $V_0 = 300 \text{ V}$.
The relation between rms voltage and peak voltage is: \[ V_{rms} = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}} = 0.707 V_0 \] \[ V_{rms} = \frac{300}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{300 \times 1.414}{2} = 150 \times 1.4142 \approx 212.1 \text{ V} \]

(b) Peak Current ($I_0$):
Given rms current $I_{rms} = 10 \text{ A}$.
\[ I_0 = \sqrt{2} \, I_{rms} = 1.4142 \times 10 = 14.14 \text{ A} \]

Q 7.3
Exercise 7.3
A $44 \text{ mH}$ inductor is connected to $220 \text{ V}, 50 \text{ Hz}$ ac supply. Determine the rms value of the current in the circuit.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Inductance $L = 44 \text{ mH} = 44 \times 10^{-3} \text{ H}$
Supply voltage $V_{rms} = 220 \text{ V}$
Frequency $\nu = 50 \text{ Hz} \implies \omega = 2\pi \nu = 2\pi \times 50 = 100\pi \text{ rad s}^{-1}$

Step 1: Inductive Reactance ($X_L$):
\[ X_L = \omega L = 2\pi \nu L = 100\pi \times (44 \times 10^{-3}) = 4.4\pi \ \Omega \] Using $\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}$ or $3.1416$: \[ X_L = 4.4 \times 3.1416 \approx 13.823 \ \Omega \]

Step 2: RMS Current ($I_{rms}$):
For a purely inductive circuit, impedance $Z = X_L$: \[ I_{rms} = \frac{V_{rms}}{X_L} = \frac{220}{4.4\pi} = \frac{220 \times 7}{4.4 \times 22} = \frac{10 \times 7}{4.4} = \frac{70}{4.4} \approx 15.91 \text{ A} \]

Result: The rms value of current in the inductor is $15.9 \text{ A}$.

Q 7.4
Exercise 7.4
A $60 \ \mu\text{F}$ capacitor is connected to a $110 \text{ V}, 60 \text{ Hz}$ ac supply. Determine the rms value of the current in the circuit.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Capacitance $C = 60 \ \mu\text{F} = 60 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F}$
Voltage $V_{rms} = 110 \text{ V}$
Frequency $\nu = 60 \text{ Hz} \implies \omega = 2\pi \nu = 2\pi \times 60 = 120\pi \text{ rad s}^{-1}$

Step 1: Capacitive Reactance ($X_C$):
\[ X_C = \frac{1}{\omega C} = \frac{1}{2\pi \nu C} = \frac{1}{120\pi \times 60 \times 10^{-6}} = \frac{10^6}{7200\pi} = \frac{10^4}{72\pi} \approx 44.21 \ \Omega \]

Step 2: RMS Current ($I_{rms}$):
\[ I_{rms} = \frac{V_{rms}}{X_C} = V_{rms} \times (2\pi \nu C) \] \[ I_{rms} = 110 \times 120\pi \times (60 \times 10^{-6}) = 110 \times 7.2\pi \times 10^{-3} = 0.792\pi \approx 2.488 \text{ A} \approx 2.49 \text{ A} \]

Result: The rms current is $2.49 \text{ A}$.

Q 7.5
Exercise 7.5
In Exercises 7.3 and 7.4, what is the net power absorbed by each circuit over a complete cycle? Explain your answer.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Answer: The net power absorbed over a complete cycle in both circuits is zero ($0 \text{ W}$).

Scientific Explanation:
Average power in an ac circuit is given by: \[ P_{avg} = V_{rms} I_{rms} \cos \phi \] where $\phi$ is the phase difference between applied voltage and resulting current, and $\cos \phi$ is the power factor.

1. Purely Inductive Circuit (Exercise 7.3):
• In an ideal inductor, current lags behind voltage by $\frac{\pi}{2}$ radians ($90^\circ$), so $\phi = 90^\circ$.
• Power factor $\cos(90^\circ) = 0$.
• Therefore, $P_{avg} = V_{rms} I_{rms} \times 0 = 0$.
Physical meaning: The inductor absorbs electrical energy from the source during the first quarter cycle to establish a magnetic field ($U_B = \frac{1}{2} L I^2$), and releases this stored magnetic energy completely back to the source during the next quarter cycle as the field collapses.

2. Purely Capacitive Circuit (Exercise 7.4):
• In an ideal capacitor, current leads voltage by $\frac{\pi}{2}$ radians ($90^\circ$), so $\phi = -90^\circ$.
• Power factor $\cos(-90^\circ) = 0$.
• Therefore, $P_{avg} = V_{rms} I_{rms} \times 0 = 0$.
Physical meaning: The capacitor charges during one quarter cycle storing energy in its electric field ($U_E = \frac{1}{2} C V^2$) and discharges during the subsequent quarter cycle, returning all stored energy back to the ac supply.

Q 7.6
Exercise 7.6
Obtain the resonant frequency $\omega_r$ of a series LCR circuit with $L = 2.0 \text{ H}, C = 32 \ \mu\text{F}$ and $R = 10 \ \Omega$. What is the $Q$-value of this circuit?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Inductance $L = 2.0 \text{ H}$
Capacitance $C = 32 \ \mu\text{F} = 32 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F}$
Resistance $R = 10 \ \Omega$

Step 1: Resonant Angular Frequency ($\omega_r$):
At resonance, $X_L = X_C \implies \omega_r L = \frac{1}{\omega_r C}$: \[ \omega_r = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2.0 \times 32 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{64 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{8 \times 10^{-3}} = 125 \text{ rad s}^{-1} \] (Resonant linear frequency $\nu_r = \frac{\omega_r}{2\pi} = \frac{125}{2\pi} \approx 19.9 \text{ Hz}$).

Step 2: Quality Factor ($Q$-value):
The sharpness of resonance is given by: \[ Q = \frac{\omega_r L}{R} = \frac{1}{R} \sqrt{\frac{L}{C}} \] \[ Q = \frac{125 \times 2.0}{10} = \frac{250}{10} = 25 \]

Result: $\omega_r = 125 \text{ rad s}^{-1}$ and $Q = 25$ (dimensionless).

Q 7.7
Exercise 7.7
A charged $30 \ \mu\text{F}$ capacitor is connected to a $27 \text{ mH}$ inductor. What is the angular frequency of free oscillations of the circuit?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Capacitance $C = 30 \ \mu\text{F} = 30 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F}$
Inductance $L = 27 \text{ mH} = 27 \times 10^{-3} \text{ H}$

Formula for LC Oscillations:
The differential equation governing ideal LC oscillations is: \[ \frac{d^2q}{dt^2} + \frac{1}{LC} q = 0 \implies \omega = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} \]

Calculation:
\[ LC = (27 \times 10^{-3}) \times (30 \times 10^{-6}) = 810 \times 10^{-9} = 81 \times 10^{-8} \text{ s}^2 \] \[ \omega = \frac{1}{\sqrt{81 \times 10^{-8}}} = \frac{1}{9 \times 10^{-4}} = \frac{10^4}{9} \approx 1.11 \times 10^3 \text{ rad s}^{-1} \]

Result: The natural angular frequency of free oscillations is $1.11 \times 10^3 \text{ rad s}^{-1}$.

Q 7.8
Exercise 7.8
Suppose the initial charge on the capacitor in Exercise 7.7 is $6 \text{ mC}$. What is the total energy stored in the circuit initially? What is the total energy at later time?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Capacitance $C = 30 \ \mu\text{F} = 30 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F}$
Initial charge $Q_0 = 6 \text{ mC} = 6 \times 10^{-3} \text{ C}$
Initial current $I = 0$

(a) Total Energy Stored Initially ($E_0$):
Initially, all energy is purely electrostatic, stored entirely in the electric field of the capacitor: \[ E_0 = \frac{Q_0^2}{2C} = \frac{(6 \times 10^{-3})^2}{2 \times (30 \times 10^{-6})} = \frac{36 \times 10^{-6}}{60 \times 10^{-6}} = \frac{36}{60} = 0.60 \text{ J} \]

(b) Total Energy at Later Time:
For an ideal LC circuit (with zero internal resistance, $R = 0$, and no electromagnetic radiation loss), the total electromagnetic energy is conserved at all times: \[ E_{total} = U_E(t) + U_B(t) = \frac{q(t)^2}{2C} + \frac{1}{2} L i(t)^2 = E_0 = 0.60 \text{ J} \] Energy oscillates back and forth between electric field of capacitor and magnetic field of inductor, but the sum remains constant at $0.60 \text{ J}$.

Q 7.9
Exercise 7.9
A series LCR circuit with $R = 20 \ \Omega, L = 1.5 \text{ H}$ and $C = 35 \ \mu\text{F}$ is connected to a variable-frequency $200 \text{ V}$ ac supply. When the frequency of the supply equals the natural frequency of the circuit, what is the average power transferred to the circuit in one complete cycle?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
$R = 20 \ \Omega$, $L = 1.5 \text{ H}$, $C = 35 \ \mu\text{F}$, $V_{rms} = 200 \text{ V}$
Condition: $\omega = \omega_r = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$ (resonance condition)

Condition at Resonance:
At the natural resonance frequency: \[ X_L = X_C \implies Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2} = R = 20 \ \Omega \] The power factor is: \[ \cos \phi = \frac{R}{Z} = \frac{R}{R} = 1 \]

RMS Current at Resonance:
\[ I_{rms} = \frac{V_{rms}}{Z} = \frac{V_{rms}}{R} = \frac{200 \text{ V}}{20 \ \Omega} = 10 \text{ A} \]

Average Power Transferred:
\[ P_{avg} = V_{rms} I_{rms} \cos \phi = 200 \times 10 \times 1 = 2000 \text{ W} = 2 \text{ kW} \] Alternatively: \[ P_{avg} = I_{rms}^2 R = (10)^2 \times 20 = 100 \times 20 = 2000 \text{ W} \]

Q 7.10
Exercise 7.10
A radio can tune over the frequency range of a portion of MW broadcast band: ($800 \text{ kHz}$ to $1200 \text{ kHz}$). If its LC circuit has an effective inductance of $200 \ \mu\text{H}$, what must be the range of its variable capacitor?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
Inductance $L = 200 \ \mu\text{H} = 200 \times 10^{-6} \text{ H} = 2 \times 10^{-4} \text{ H}$
Minimum frequency $\nu_1 = 800 \text{ kHz} = 800 \times 10^3 \text{ Hz} = 8 \times 10^5 \text{ Hz}$
Maximum frequency $\nu_2 = 1200 \text{ kHz} = 1200 \times 10^3 \text{ Hz} = 1.2 \times 10^6 \text{ Hz}$

Formula:
\[ \nu = \frac{1}{2\pi \sqrt{LC}} \implies \nu^2 = \frac{1}{4\pi^2 L C} \implies C = \frac{1}{4\pi^2 \nu^2 L} \]

1. Maximum Capacitance $C_{max}$ (corresponding to $\nu_{min} = 800 \text{ kHz}$):
\[ C_{max} = \frac{1}{4\pi^2 (8 \times 10^5)^2 (2 \times 10^{-4})} = \frac{1}{4\pi^2 (64 \times 10^{10}) (2 \times 10^{-4})} = \frac{1}{512\pi^2 \times 10^6} \] Using $\pi^2 \approx 9.87$: \[ C_{max} = \frac{1}{512 \times 9.87 \times 10^6} = \frac{1}{5053.4 \times 10^6} \approx 1.978 \times 10^{-10} \text{ F} \approx 197.8 \text{ pF} \]

2. Minimum Capacitance $C_{min}$ (corresponding to $\nu_{max} = 1200 \text{ kHz}$):
\[ C_{min} = C_{max} \times \left( \frac{\nu_1}{\nu_2} \right)^2 = 197.8 \times \left( \frac{800}{1200} \right)^2 = 197.8 \times \left( \frac{2}{3} \right)^2 = 197.8 \times \frac{4}{9} \approx 87.9 \text{ pF} \]

Capacitance Range: The variable capacitor must tune continuously from approximately $87.9 \text{ pF}$ to $197.8 \text{ pF}$ (or roughly $88 \text{ pF}$ to $198 \text{ pF}$).

Q 7.11
Exercise 7.11
A series LCR circuit has $L = 5.0 \text{ H}, C = 80 \ \mu\text{F}, R = 40 \ \Omega$ connected to a variable frequency $230 \text{ V}$ source.
(a) Determine the source frequency which drives the circuit in resonance.
(b) Obtain the impedance of the circuit and the amplitude of current at the resonating frequency.
(c) Determine the rms potential drops across the three elements of the circuit. Show that the potential drop across the LC combination is zero at the resonating frequency.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
$L = 5.0 \text{ H}$, $C = 80 \ \mu\text{F} = 80 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F}$, $R = 40 \ \Omega$, $V_{rms} = 230 \text{ V}$

(a) Resonant Frequency $\omega_r$:
\[ \omega_r = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5.0 \times 80 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{400 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{20 \times 10^{-3}} = 50 \text{ rad s}^{-1} \] Linear frequency $\nu_r = \frac{\omega_r}{2\pi} = \frac{50}{2\pi} \approx 7.96 \text{ Hz}$.

(b) Impedance and Current Amplitude at Resonance:
At resonance, $X_L = X_C$, hence: \[ Z = R = 40 \ \Omega \] RMS Current: \[ I_{rms} = \frac{V_{rms}}{Z} = \frac{230}{40} = 5.75 \text{ A} \] Current amplitude (peak current $I_0$): \[ I_0 = \sqrt{2} \, I_{rms} = 1.414 \times 5.75 \approx 8.13 \text{ A} \]

(c) RMS Potential Drops Across Elements:
1. Across resistor: \[ V_R = I_{rms} R = 5.75 \times 40 = 230 \text{ V} \] 2. Across inductor: \[ X_L = \omega_r L = 50 \times 5.0 = 250 \ \Omega \] \[ V_L = I_{rms} X_L = 5.75 \times 250 = 1437.5 \text{ V} \] 3. Across capacitor: \[ X_C = \frac{1}{\omega_r C} = \frac{1}{50 \times 80 \times 10^{-6}} = \frac{10^6}{4000} = 250 \ \Omega \] \[ V_C = I_{rms} X_C = 5.75 \times 250 = 1437.5 \text{ V} \] Potential Drop Across LC Combination:
The voltages across the inductor and capacitor are exactly $180^\circ$ out of phase with each other: \[ V_{LC} = V_L - V_C = 1437.5 \text{ V} - 1437.5 \text{ V} = 0 \text{ V} \] Hence, the net potential drop across the LC series combination is strictly zero at resonance.

Q 7.12
Exercise 7.12
An LC circuit contains a $20 \text{ mH}$ inductor and a $50 \ \mu\text{F}$ capacitor with an initial charge of $10 \text{ mC}$. The resistance of the circuit is negligible. Let the instant the circuit is closed be $t = 0$.
(a) What is the total energy stored initially? Is it conserved during LC oscillations?
(b) What is the natural frequency of the circuit?
(c) At what times is the energy stored: (i) completely electrical, (ii) completely magnetic?
(d) At what times is the total energy shared equally between inductor and capacitor?
(e) If a resistor is inserted, what form does the energy eventually take?
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
$L = 20 \text{ mH} = 20 \times 10^{-3} \text{ H}$
$C = 50 \ \mu\text{F} = 50 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F}$
$Q_0 = 10 \text{ mC} = 10 \times 10^{-3} \text{ C} = 10^{-2} \text{ C}$

(a) Total Initial Energy:
\[ E_0 = \frac{Q_0^2}{2C} = \frac{(10^{-2})^2}{2 \times (50 \times 10^{-6})} = \frac{10^{-4}}{10^{-4}} = 1.0 \text{ J} \] Yes, since $R = 0$, the total energy is strictly conserved.

(b) Natural Frequency:
\[ \omega_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{20 \times 10^{-3} \times 50 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{10^{-6}}} = 10^3 \text{ rad s}^{-1} \] \[ \nu = \frac{\omega_0}{2\pi} = \frac{1000}{2\pi} \approx 159.2 \text{ Hz} \] Time period: $T = \frac{1}{\nu} = \frac{2\pi}{1000} \approx 6.28 \times 10^{-3} \text{ s} = 6.28 \text{ ms}$.

(c) Times of Completely Electrical and Magnetic Energy:
The charge varies as $q(t) = Q_0 \cos \omega_0 t$.
• Completely Electrical ($q = \pm Q_0$): At $\omega_0 t = 0, \pi, 2\pi, \dots \implies t = 0, \frac{T}{2}, T, \frac{3T}{2}, \dots$
i.e., at $t = 0, 3.14 \text{ ms}, 6.28 \text{ ms}, \dots$
• Completely Magnetic ($q = 0, i = \pm I_0$): At $\omega_0 t = \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2}, \dots \implies t = \frac{T}{4}, \frac{3T}{4}, \frac{5T}{4}, \dots$
i.e., at $t = 1.57 \text{ ms}, 4.71 \text{ ms}, \dots$

(d) Times of Equal Energy Sharing ($U_E = U_B = \frac{1}{2} E_0$):
\[ \frac{q^2}{2C} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{Q_0^2}{2C} \right) \implies q = \frac{Q_0}{\sqrt{2}} \] \[ \cos \omega_0 t = \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \implies \omega_0 t = (2n + 1) \frac{\pi}{4} \implies t = (2n + 1) \frac{T}{8} \] For $n = 0, 1, 2, 3$: $t = \frac{T}{8}, \frac{3T}{8}, \frac{5T}{8}, \frac{7T}{8} = 0.785 \text{ ms}, 2.355 \text{ ms}, 3.925 \text{ ms}, 5.495 \text{ ms}$.

(e) Insertion of Resistor:
If a resistor $R$ is inserted, the circuit undergoes damped oscillations. The electromagnetic energy is gradually converted into thermal energy (Joule heat) and dissipated into the surroundings.

Q 7.13
Exercise 7.13
A coil of inductance $0.50 \text{ H}$ and resistance $100 \ \Omega$ is connected to a $240 \text{ V}, 50 \text{ Hz}$ ac supply.
(a) What is the maximum current in the coil?
(b) What is the time lag between the voltage maximum and the current maximum?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
$L = 0.50 \text{ H}$, $R = 100 \ \Omega$, $V_{rms} = 240 \text{ V}$, $\nu = 50 \text{ Hz}$

Angular frequency $\omega = 2\pi \nu = 2\pi \times 50 = 100\pi \approx 314.16 \text{ rad s}^{-1}$
Inductive reactance: \[ X_L = \omega L = 100\pi \times 0.50 = 50\pi \approx 157.08 \ \Omega \] Impedance of RL series circuit: \[ Z = \sqrt{R^2 + X_L^2} = \sqrt{(100)^2 + (157.08)^2} = \sqrt{10000 + 24674} = \sqrt{34674} \approx 186.2 \ \Omega \]

(a) Maximum Current $I_0$:
Peak voltage: $V_0 = \sqrt{2} V_{rms} = 1.414 \times 240 \approx 339.4 \text{ V}$
\[ I_0 = \frac{V_0}{Z} = \frac{339.4 \text{ V}}{186.2 \ \Omega} \approx 1.82 \text{ A} \]

Q 7.14
Exercise 7.14
Obtain the answers (a) and (b) in Exercise 7.13 if the circuit is connected to a high frequency supply ($240 \text{ V}, 10 \text{ kHz}$). Hence, explain the statement that at very high frequency, an inductor in a circuit nearly amounts to an open circuit. How does an inductor behave in a dc circuit after the steady state?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
$L = 0.50 \text{ H}$, $R = 100 \ \Omega$, $V_{rms} = 240 \text{ V}$, $\nu = 10 \text{ kHz} = 10^4 \text{ Hz}$
$\omega = 2\pi \nu = 2\pi \times 10^4 = 2\pi \times 10^4 \approx 6.283 \times 10^4 \text{ rad s}^{-1}$

Step 1: Inductive Reactance and Impedance:
\[ X_L = \omega L = (2\pi \times 10^4) \times 0.50 = \pi \times 10^4 \approx 3.1416 \times 10^4 \ \Omega = 31416 \ \Omega \] Since $X_L \gg R$ ($31416 \gg 100$): \[ Z = \sqrt{R^2 + X_L^2} \approx X_L \approx 3.14 \times 10^4 \ \Omega \]

(a) Maximum Current $I_0$:
\[ I_0 = \frac{V_0}{Z} = \frac{\sqrt{2} \times 240}{3.1416 \times 10^4} = \frac{339.4}{31416} \approx 1.08 \times 10^{-2} \text{ A} = 10.8 \text{ mA} \]

(b) Phase Angle and Time Lag:
\[ \tan \phi = \frac{X_L}{R} = \frac{31416}{100} = 314.16 \implies \phi \approx 89.82^\circ \approx \frac{\pi}{2} \text{ rad} \] \[ \Delta t = \frac{\phi}{\omega} \approx \frac{\pi/2}{2\pi \times 10^4} = \frac{1}{4 \times 10^4} = 2.5 \times 10^{-5} \text{ s} = 25 \ \mu\text{s} \]

Explanation of Physical Behavior:
• At very high frequency ($\nu \to \infty$): Reactance $X_L = 2\pi\nu L \to \infty$. The opposition to current becomes extremely large, reducing the current practically to zero ($I \to 0$). Hence, an inductor behaves as an open circuit for high-frequency AC.
• In a DC circuit ($\nu = 0$): In steady state, $X_L = 0$. The inductor offers zero inductive reactance, acting simply as an ideal short circuit (zero resistance wire).

Q 7.17
Exercise 7.17
Keeping the source frequency equal to the resonating frequency for the series LCR circuit in Exercise 7.11, if the three elements are connected in parallel, show that the total current is minimum.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Parallel LCR Circuit Admittance Analysis:
When $R, L,$ and $C$ are connected in parallel across an AC source $v = V_0 \sin \omega t$, the same potential difference appears across each branch.
The branch currents are: \[ I_R = \frac{V}{R} \quad (\text{in phase with } V) \] \[ I_L = \frac{V}{X_L} = \frac{V}{\omega L} \quad (\text{lags } V \text{ by } 90^\circ) \] \[ I_C = \frac{V}{X_C} = V \omega C \quad (\text{leads } V \text{ by } 90^\circ) \]

Total Current Amplitude:
Since $I_L$ and $I_C$ are $180^\circ$ out of phase with each other, the net reactive current is $(I_C - I_L)$.
By phasor addition: \[ I_{total} = \sqrt{I_R^2 + (I_C - I_L)^2} = V \sqrt{\frac{1}{R^2} + \left( \omega C - \frac{1}{\omega L} \right)^2} \]

Condition at Resonating Frequency ($\omega = \omega_r = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}$):
At the resonant frequency: \[ \omega_r C = \frac{1}{\omega_r L} \implies I_C - I_L = 0 \] Therefore: \[ I_{total, min} = I_R = \frac{V}{R} \] The inductive and capacitive branch currents cancel each other completely. The admittance $Y$ is at its absolute minimum ($Y_{min} = \frac{1}{R}$), which means the parallel circuit impedance $Z_p$ is maximum ($Z_p = R$). Consequently, the total current drawn from the source is at its absolute minimum (anti-resonance / rejector circuit).

Obtain the resonant frequency and $Q$-factor of a series LCR circuit with $L = 3.0 \text{ H}, C = 27 \ \mu\text{F}$, and $R = 7.4 \ \Omega$. It is desired to improve the sharpness of the resonance of the circuit by reducing its 'full width at half maximum' by a factor of 2. Suggest a suitable way.

✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Given:
$L = 3.0 \text{ H}$
$C = 27 \ \mu\text{F} = 27 \times 10^{-6} \text{ F}$
$R = 7.4 \ \Omega$

Step 1: Resonant Frequency $\omega_r$:
\[ \omega_r = \frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3.0 \times 27 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{81 \times 10^{-6}}} = \frac{1}{9 \times 10^{-3}} = \frac{1000}{9} \approx 111.1 \text{ rad s}^{-1} \]

Step 2: Quality Factor ($Q$):
\[ Q = \frac{\omega_r L}{R} = \frac{111.1 \times 3.0}{7.4} = \frac{333.3}{7.4} \approx 45.05 \approx 45 \]

Step 3: Improving Sharpness (Reducing FWHM by Factor of 2):
The full width at half maximum (FWHM) or bandwidth of resonance is: \[ \Delta \omega = \frac{R}{L} \] To reduce the bandwidth $\Delta \omega$ by a factor of 2 (i.e. to double the sharpness and double the $Q$-factor) without altering the resonant frequency $\omega_r$: \[ \Delta \omega' = \frac{\Delta \omega}{2} \implies R' = \frac{R}{2} \] \[ R' = \frac{7.4 \ \Omega}{2} = 3.7 \ \Omega \] Recommendation: Reduce the resistance of the circuit to half its original value, i.e., replace the $7.4 \ \Omega$ resistor with a $3.7 \ \Omega$ resistor.
