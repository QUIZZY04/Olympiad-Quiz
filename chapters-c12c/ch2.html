In-Text 2.1
NCERT In-Text
How would you determine the standard electrode potential of the system $\text{Mg}^{2+}|\text{Mg}$?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Experimental Procedure:
  1. Cell Setup: Set up an electrochemical cell by coupling the magnesium half-cell with a Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) as the reference electrode. $$\text{Mg}(s) \mid \text{Mg}^{2+}(1\text{ M}) \;\parallel\; \text{H}^+(1\text{ M}) \mid \text{H}_2(g, 1\text{ bar}) \mid \text{Pt}(s)$$
  2. Measurement: Connect the two electrodes through a voltmeter and insert a salt bridge between the two solutions. Measure the cell electromotive force ($E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$).
  3. Calculations: Since magnesium acts as the anode (electrons flow from $\text{Mg}$ to $\text{SHE}$) and SHE acts as the cathode ($E^\circ_{\text{SHE}} = 0.00\text{ V}$): $$E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}} = E^\circ_{\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2} - E^\circ_{\text{Mg}^{2+}/\text{Mg}}$$ $$E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = 0.00\text{ V} - E^\circ_{\text{Mg}^{2+}/\text{Mg}} \implies E^\circ_{\text{Mg}^{2+}/\text{Mg}} = -E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$$ The measured cell emf is $+2.37\text{ V}$, therefore $E^\circ_{\text{Mg}^{2+}/\text{Mg}} = \mathbf{-2.37\text{ V}}$.

In-Text 2.2
NCERT In-Text
Can you store copper sulphate solution in a zinc pot?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Answer: No, copper sulphate solution cannot be stored in a zinc pot.

Scientific Reason:
From the electrochemical series, the standard reduction potentials are: $$E^\circ_{\text{Zn}^{2+}/\text{Zn}} = -0.76\text{ V}, \quad E^\circ_{\text{Cu}^{2+}/\text{Cu}} = +0.34\text{ V}$$ Since zinc has a more negative reduction potential than copper, zinc is more reactive and acts as a stronger reducing agent. Zinc will displace copper from $\text{CuSO}_4$ solution: $$\text{Zn}(s) + \text{CuSO}_4(aq) \rightarrow \text{ZnSO}_4(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$$ $$\text{Zn}(s) + \text{Cu}^{2+}(aq) \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$$ $$E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{Cu}^{2+}/\text{Cu}} - E^\circ_{\text{Zn}^{2+}/\text{Zn}} = +0.34\text{ V} - (-0.76\text{ V}) = +1.10\text{ V} > 0$$ Since $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} > 0$ (spontaneous reaction), zinc dissolves into solution forming pits/holes in the pot.

Consult the table of standard electrode potentials and suggest three substances that can oxidise ferrous ions under suitable conditions.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Oxidation of Ferrous Ions:
The oxidation of ferrous ions to ferric ions is represented as: $$\text{Fe}^{2+} \rightarrow \text{Fe}^{3+} + e^-, \quad E^\circ_{\text{Fe}^{3+}/\text{Fe}^{2+}} = +0.77\text{ V}$$ Any oxidizing agent whose standard reduction potential is greater than $+0.77\text{ V}$ can oxidize $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ to $\text{Fe}^{3+}$.

In-Text 2.4
NCERT In-Text
Calculate the potential of hydrogen electrode in contact with a solution whose $\text{pH}$ is $10$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Electrode reaction:
$$\text{H}^+(aq) + e^- \rightarrow \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2(g, 1\text{ bar})$$
2. Nernst Equation for hydrogen electrode at $298\text{ K}$:
$$E_{\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2} = E^\circ_{\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2} - \frac{0.0591}{n} \log \frac{1}{[\text{H}^+]}$$
Since $E^\circ_{\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2} = 0.00\text{ V}$, $n = 1$, and $\text{pH} = -\log[\text{H}^+] = 10 \implies [\text{H}^+] = 10^{-10}\text{ M}$:
$$E_{\text{H}^+/\text{H}_2} = 0 - \frac{0.0591}{1} \log \left(\frac{1}{10^{-10}}\right) = -0.0591 \log(10^{10}) = -0.0591 \times 10 = \mathbf{-0.591\text{ V}}$$

NCERT In-Text
Calculate the emf of the cell in which the following reaction takes place:
$$\text{Ni}(s) + 2\text{Ag}^+(0.002\text{ M}) \rightarrow \text{Ni}^{2+}(0.160\text{ M}) + 2\text{Ag}(s)$$
given that $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = 1.05\text{ V}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Identify $n$ (electrons transferred):
$$\text{Ni}(s) \rightarrow \text{Ni}^{2+} + 2e^-, \quad 2\text{Ag}^+ + 2e^- \rightarrow 2\text{Ag}(s) \implies n = 2$$
2. Nernst Equation:
$$E_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cell}} - \frac{0.0591}{n} \log \frac{[\text{Ni}^{2+}]}{[\text{Ag}^+]^2}$$
$$E_{\text{cell}} = 1.05\text{ V} - \frac{0.0591}{2} \log \frac{0.160}{(0.002)^2}$$
3. Evaluation of log term:
$$\frac{0.160}{(0.002)^2} = \frac{0.160}{4 \times 10^{-6}} = \frac{1.6 \times 10^{-1}}{4 \times 10^{-6}} = 4 \times 10^4$$
$$\log(4 \times 10^4) = \log 4 + \log 10^4 = 0.6021 + 4 = 4.6021$$
4. Calculation of $E_{\text{cell}}$:
$$E_{\text{cell}} = 1.05 - \frac{0.0591}{2} \times 4.6021 = 1.05 - (0.02955 \times 4.6021) = 1.05 - 0.1360 = \mathbf{0.914\text{ V}}$$

The cell in which the following reaction occurs:
$$2\text{Fe}^{3+}(aq) + 2\text{I}^-(aq) \rightarrow 2\text{Fe}^{2+}(aq) + \text{I}_2(s)$$
has $E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = 0.236\text{ V}$ at $298\text{ K}$. Calculate the standard Gibbs energy and the equilibrium constant of the cell reaction.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Number of electrons transferred ($n$):
$2\text{Fe}^{3+} + 2e^- \rightarrow 2\text{Fe}^{2+}$, $\quad 2\text{I}^- \rightarrow \text{I}_2 + 2e^- \implies n = 2$

2. Standard Gibbs Free Energy ($\Delta_r G^\circ$):
$$\Delta_r G^\circ = -n F E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = -2 \times 96487\text{ C mol}^{-1} \times 0.236\text{ V}$$
$$\Delta_r G^\circ = -45541.86\text{ J mol}^{-1} = \mathbf{-45.54\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}$$

3. Equilibrium Constant ($K_c$):
$$\log K_c = \frac{n E^\circ_{\text{cell}}}{0.0591} = \frac{2 \times 0.236}{0.0591} = \frac{0.472}{0.0591} = 7.9865$$
$$K_c = \text{Antilog}(7.9865) = \mathbf{9.69 \times 10^7}$$

In-Text 2.7
NCERT In-Text
Why does the conductivity of a solution decrease with dilution?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Explanation:
  1. Definition of Conductivity ($\kappa$): Conductivity (or specific conductance) is the conductance of ions present in a unit volume ($1\text{ cm}^3$ or $1\text{ mL}$) of the electrolytic solution.
  2. Effect of Dilution: Upon dilution, the total volume of the solution increases, but the total number of ions does not increase proportionately. Consequently, the number of current-carrying ions per unit volume decreases.
  3. Conclusion: Since electrical conductance is directly proportional to the ion population density in that unit volume, the conductivity ($\kappa$) inevitably decreases as the solution is diluted.

In-Text 2.8
NCERT In-Text
Suggest a way to determine the $\Lambda_m^\circ$ value of water.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Method using Kohlrausch’s Law of Independent Migration of Ions:
Water is an extremely weak electrolyte with an immeasurably low degree of dissociation: $$\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+ + \text{OH}^-$$ According to Kohlrausch's Law, the limiting molar conductivity of water is: $$\Lambda_m^\circ(\text{H}_2\text{O}) = \lambda^\circ(\text{H}^+) + \lambda^\circ(\text{OH}^-)$$
We can determine this by selecting three strong electrolytes whose limiting molar conductivities can be experimentally measured by extrapolation:
  1. Hydrochloric acid: $\Lambda_m^\circ(\text{HCl}) = \lambda^\circ(\text{H}^+) + \lambda^\circ(\text{Cl}^-)$
  2. Sodium hydroxide: $\Lambda_m^\circ(\text{NaOH}) = \lambda^\circ(\text{Na}^+) + \lambda^\circ(\text{OH}^-)$
  3. Sodium chloride: $\Lambda_m^\circ(\text{NaCl}) = \lambda^\circ(\text{Na}^+) + \lambda^\circ(\text{Cl}^-)$
Combining these three equations: $$\mathbf{\Lambda_m^\circ(\text{H}_2\text{O}) = \Lambda_m^\circ(\text{HCl}) + \Lambda_m^\circ(\text{NaOH}) - \Lambda_m^\circ(\text{NaCl})}$$ This algebraic summation completely cancels out the $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{Cl}^-$ contributions, yielding $\Lambda_m^\circ(\text{H}_2\text{O})$.

The molar conductivity of $0.025\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ methanoic acid is $46.1\text{ S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}$. Calculate its degree of dissociation and dissociation constant. Given $\lambda^\circ(\text{H}^+) = 349.6\text{ S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}$ and $\lambda^\circ(\text{HCOO}^-) = 54.6\text{ S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Limiting molar conductivity of methanoic acid ($\text{HCOOH}$):
$$\Lambda_m^\circ(\text{HCOOH}) = \lambda^\circ(\text{H}^+) + \lambda^\circ(\text{HCOO}^-) = 349.6 + 54.6 = \mathbf{404.2\text{ S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}}$$

2. Degree of dissociation ($\alpha$):
$$\alpha = \frac{\Lambda_m}{\Lambda_m^\circ} = \frac{46.1\text{ S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}}{404.2\text{ S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}} = \mathbf{0.114}$$

3. Dissociation constant ($K_a$):
$$K_a = \frac{C \alpha^2}{1 - \alpha} = \frac{0.025 \times (0.114)^2}{1 - 0.114} = \frac{0.025 \times 0.012996}{0.886} = \frac{0.0003249}{0.886} = \mathbf{3.67 \times 10^{-4}\text{ mol L}^{-1}}$$

If a current of $0.5\text{ ampere}$ flows through a metallic wire for $2\text{ hours}$, then how many electrons would flow through the wire?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Total charge ($Q$):
$I = 0.5\text{ A}$, $t = 2\text{ hours} = 2 \times 3600\text{ s} = 7200\text{ s}$
$$Q = I \times t = 0.5\text{ A} \times 7200\text{ s} = 3600\text{ C}$$

2. Charge of 1 electron:
$$e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$$

3. Number of electrons ($N$):
$$N = \frac{Q}{e} = \frac{3600\text{ C}}{1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}} = \mathbf{2.247 \times 10^{22}\text{ electrons } (\approx 2.25 \times 10^{22})}$$

Write the chemistry of recharging the lead storage battery, highlighting all the materials that are involved during recharging.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Recharging Chemistry of Lead Storage Battery:
During recharging, an external electrical source forces the non-spontaneous reverse reaction, converting electrical energy into chemical energy.

  1. At Cathode (Reduction of $\text{PbSO}_4$ to spongy $\text{Pb}$): $$\text{PbSO}_4(s) + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Pb}(s) + \text{SO}_4^{2-}(aq)$$

  3. Overall Recharging Reaction: $$2\text{PbSO}_4(s) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \xrightarrow{\text{Recharging}} \text{Pb}(s) + \text{PbO}_2(s) + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(aq)$$
Materials involved & regenerated: $\text{PbSO}_4$ is consumed, while spongy $\text{Pb}$ (at negative plate), lead dioxide $\text{PbO}_2$ (at positive plate), and sulphuric acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, increasing electrolyte density to $\approx 1.30\text{ g mL}^{-1}$) are regenerated.

In-Text 2.15
NCERT In-Text
Explain how rusting of iron is envisaged as setting up of an electrochemical cell.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Electrochemical Mechanism of Rusting:
Rusting occurs when iron is exposed to moisture and oxygen. Water containing dissolved $\text{CO}_2$ forms carbonic acid ($\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$), providing $\text{H}^+$ ions as electrolyte.

  1. Anodic site (Oxidation of Iron): Iron atoms lose electrons and enter solution as ferrous ions: $$\text{Fe}(s) \rightarrow \text{Fe}^{2+}(aq) + 2e^-, \quad E^\circ = -0.44\text{ V} \; (\times 2)$$
  2. Cathodic site (Reduction of Oxygen): Electrons released flow through the metallic iron to another spot on the surface where oxygen is reduced in the presence of $\text{H}^+$ ions: $$\text{O}_2(g) + 4\text{H}^+(aq) + 4e^- \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l), \quad E^\circ = +1.23\text{ V}$$
  3. Overall Cell Reaction: $$2\text{Fe}(s) + \text{O}_2(g) + 4\text{H}^+(aq) \rightarrow 2\text{Fe}^{2+}(aq) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l), \quad E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = 1.23 - (-0.44) = +1.67\text{ V}$$
  4. Formation of Rust: Ferrous ions are further oxidized by atmospheric oxygen to ferric oxide, which hydrates to form rust: $$4\text{Fe}^{2+}(aq) + \text{O}_2(g) + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \rightarrow 2\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3(s) + 8\text{H}^+(aq)$$ $$\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + x\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O} \; (\text{Rust})}$$

Question 2.7
NCERT Exercise
Define conductivity and molar conductivity for the solution of an electrolyte. Discuss their variation with concentration.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Definitions:
• Conductivity ($\kappa$): Specific conductance is the conductance of a solution of $1\text{ cm}$ length and $1\text{ cm}^2$ cross-sectional area (conductance of $1\text{ cm}^3$ solution). Unit: $\text{S cm}^{-1}$.
• Molar Conductivity ($\Lambda_m$): It is the conducting power of all the ions produced by dissolving one mole of an electrolyte in a given volume of solution. $$\Lambda_m = \frac{\kappa \times 1000}{C}$$ Unit: $\text{S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}$.

2. Variation with Concentration:
• Conductivity ($\kappa$): Decreases with decrease in concentration (upon dilution) for both strong and weak electrolytes because the number of current-carrying ions per unit volume decreases.
• Molar Conductivity ($\Lambda_m$): Increases with decrease in concentration (upon dilution). For strong electrolytes, it increases gradually as interionic attractions decrease (governed by Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation: $\Lambda_m = \Lambda_m^\circ - A\sqrt{C}$). For weak electrolytes, it increases steeply at infinite dilution due to a rapid increase in the degree of dissociation ($\alpha$).

    • At Anode: $2\text{Cl}^- \rightarrow \text{Cl}_2(g) + 2e^-$ [$\text{Cl}_2$ gas evolved due to oxygen overpotential]
