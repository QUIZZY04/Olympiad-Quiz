In-Text 5.3
NCERT In-Text
Indicate the types of isomerism exhibited by the following complexes and draw the structures for these isomers:
(i) $\text{K}[\text{Cr(H}_2\text{O)}_2(\text{C}_2\text{O}_4)_2]$
(ii) $[\text{Co(en)}_3]\text{Cl}_3$
(iii) $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5(\text{NO}_2)](\text{NO}_3)_2$
(iv) $[\text{Pt(NH}_3)(\text{H}_2\text{O})\text{Cl}_2]$
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Types of Isomerism:
  1. $\text{K}[\text{Cr(H}_2\text{O)}_2(\text{C}_2\text{O}_4)_2]$:
    • Geometrical isomerism: Exists as cis and trans isomers.
    • Optical isomerism: The cis isomer is chiral and non-superimposable on its mirror image (exists as $d$ and $l$ optical isomers), while the trans isomer has a plane of symmetry and is optically inactive.
  2. $[\text{Co(en)}_3]\text{Cl}_3$:
    • Exhibits Optical isomerism. It exists as a pair of enantiomers: dextro ($d$) and laevo ($l$) forms. (No geometrical isomerism because all three ligands are identical bidentate ligands).
  3. $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5(\text{NO}_2)](\text{NO}_3)_2$:
    • Linkage isomerism: Due to ambidentate nitro ligand (bonded through N: nitro $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5(\text{NO}_2)]^{2+}$ (yellow) vs bonded through O: nitrito $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5(\text{ONO})]^{2+}$ (red)).
    • Ionisation isomerism: $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5(\text{NO}_2)](\text{NO}_3)_2$ vs $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5(\text{NO}_3)](\text{NO}_2)(\text{NO}_3)$.
  4. $[\text{Pt(NH}_3)(\text{H}_2\text{O})\text{Cl}_2]$:
    • Square planar complex of type $[\text{MABX}_2]$. Exhibits Geometrical isomerism: cis (both $\text{Cl}^-$ ligands adjacent) and trans (both $\text{Cl}^-$ ligands opposite at $180^\circ$).

In-Text 5.4
NCERT In-Text
Give evidence that $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5\text{Cl}]\text{SO}_4$ and $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5\text{SO}_4]\text{Cl}$ are ionisation isomers.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Experimental Chemical Evidence:
Ionisation isomers produce different ions in aqueous solution that give distinct characteristic precipitation reactions:

  1. Testing with aqueous Barium Chloride ($ ext{BaCl}_2$):
    • $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5\text{Cl}]\text{SO}_4$ ionises to give sulphate ion $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ outside the coordination sphere. It forms a white precipitate of $ ext{BaSO}_4$: $$[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5\text{Cl}]\text{SO}_4(aq) + \text{BaCl}_2(aq) \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{BaSO}_4 \downarrow (\text{white ppt})} + [\text{Co(NH}_3)_5\text{Cl}]\text{Cl}_2$$ • $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5\text{SO}_4]\text{Cl}$ gives no precipitate with $\text{BaCl}_2$ because sulphate is coordinated inside the sphere.
  2. Testing with aqueous Silver Nitrate ($ ext{AgNO}_3$):
    • $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5\text{SO}_4]\text{Cl}$ ionises to give free chloride ion $\text{Cl}^-$. It reacts with $\text{AgNO}_3$ to give a curdy white precipitate of $ ext{AgCl}$: $$[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5\text{SO}_4]\text{Cl}(aq) + \text{AgNO}_3(aq) \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{AgCl} \downarrow (\text{white ppt})} + [\text{Co(NH}_3)_5\text{SO}_4]\text{NO}_3$$ • $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_5\text{Cl}]\text{SO}_4$ gives no precipitate with $\text{AgNO}_3$ as chloride is bonded inside.

In-Text 5.5
NCERT In-Text
Explain on the basis of valence bond theory that $[\text{Ni(CN)}_4]^{2-}$ ion with square planar structure is diamagnetic and the $[\text{NiCl}_4]^{2-}$ ion with tetrahedral geometry is paramagnetic.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
VBT Analysis for Nickel ($Z = 28$, $ ext{Ni}^{2+}: [ ext{Ar}] 3d^8$):

1. Case of $[\text{Ni(CN)}_4]^{2-}$:
  • Cyanide ($\text{CN}^-$) is a strong field ligand.
  • It forces the two unpaired electrons in $3d^8$ subshell to pair up against Hund's rule, leaving one vacant $3d$ orbital.
  • The central $\text{Ni}^{2+}$ undergoes $dsp^2$ hybridization involving one $3d$, one $4s$, and two $4p$ orbitals.
  • Geometry: Square planar.
  • Magnetic property: Since all electrons are paired ($n = 0$), it is diamagnetic.

2. Case of $[\text{NiCl}_4]^{2-}$:
  • Chloride ($\text{Cl}^-$) is a weak field ligand.
  • It cannot cause pairing of electrons in the $3d^8$ subshell. The electronic arrangement remains $3d^8$ with 2 unpaired electrons.
  • The central $\text{Ni}^{2+}$ utilizes one $4s$ and three $4p$ orbitals for $sp^3$ hybridization.
  • Geometry: Tetrahedral.
  • Magnetic property: Because of 2 unpaired electrons ($n = 2$, $\mu = \sqrt{8} \approx 2.83\text{ BM}$), it is paramagnetic.

[NiCl₄]²⁻: Cl⁻ weak ligand, no pairing → sp³ hybridization, tetrahedral, 2 unpaired e⁻ (paramagnetic)1½ Marks
In-Text 5.6
NCERT In-Text
$[\text{NiCl}_4]^{2-}$ is paramagnetic while $[\text{Ni(CO)}_4]$ is diamagnetic though both are tetrahedral. Why?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Comparison:

1. $[\text{NiCl}_4]^{2-}$:
  • Oxidation state of $\text{Ni} = +2$. Electronic configuration: $[\text{Ar}] 3d^8 4s^0$.
  • $\text{Cl}^-$ is a weak field ligand and does not cause electron pairing in $3d^8$.
  • There are 2 unpaired electrons in $3d$ orbitals. It undergoes $sp^3$ hybridization (tetrahedral) and is paramagnetic.

2. $[\text{Ni(CO)}_4]$:
  • Oxidation state of $\text{Ni} = 0$. Electronic configuration: $[\text{Ar}] 3d^8 4s^2$.
  • $\text{CO}$ is an extremely strong field ligand. It forces both $4s$ electrons to shift into the $3d$ orbitals, fully pairing all electrons into a $3d^{10}$ configuration.
  • Vacant $4s$ and three $4p$ orbitals undergo $sp^3$ hybridization (tetrahedral geometry).
  • Since all electrons are completely paired ($n = 0$), $[\text{Ni(CO)}_4]$ is diamagnetic.

In-Text 5.7
NCERT In-Text
$[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{3+}$ is strongly paramagnetic whereas $[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{3-}$ is weakly paramagnetic. Explain.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Oxidation State of Iron: In both complexes, iron is in the $+3$ oxidation state ($ ext{Fe}^{3+}: [ ext{Ar}] 3d^5$).

1. $[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{3+}$:
  • $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is a weak field ligand ($\Delta_o < P$).
  • It cannot cause pairing of electrons. All 5 electrons in $3d^5$ remain unpaired ($n = 5$).
  • It forms a high-spin outer orbital complex ($sp^3d^2$).
  • Magnetic moment: $\mu = \sqrt{5(5+2)} = \sqrt{35} \approx \mathbf{5.92\text{ BM}}$ (strongly paramagnetic).

2. $[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{3-}$:
  • $\text{CN}^-$ is a strong field ligand ($\Delta_o > P$).
  • It causes pairing of $3d$ electrons, leaving only 1 unpaired electron ($n = 1$).
  • It forms a low-spin inner orbital complex ($d^2sp^3$, $t_{2g}^5$).
  • Magnetic moment: $\mu = \sqrt{1(1+2)} = \sqrt{3} \approx \mathbf{1.73\text{ BM}}$ (weakly paramagnetic).
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
[Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺: H₂O weak ligand → no pairing, 5 unpaired e⁻ (μ = 5.92 BM, strongly paramagnetic)1 Mark
[Fe(CN)₆]³⁻: CN⁻ strong ligand → pairing occurs, 1 unpaired e⁻ (μ = 1.73 BM, weakly paramagnetic)1 Mark
In-Text 5.8
NCERT In-Text
Explain $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$ is an inner orbital complex whereas $[\text{Ni(NH}_3)_6]^{2+}$ is an outer orbital complex.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Explanation based on Hybridization:

1. $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$:
  • Cobalt is in $+3$ oxidation state: $\text{Co}^{3+}: [\text{Ar}] 3d^6$.
  • With $\text{Co}^{3+}$, $\text{NH}_3$ behaves as a strong field ligand, forcing pairing of the six $3d$ electrons into three $3d$ orbitals.
  • This vacates two inner $3d$ orbitals, allowing $d^2sp^3$ hybridization using inner $(n-1)d$ orbitals.
  • Hence, it is an inner orbital (low spin) complex.

2. $[\text{Ni(NH}_3)_6]^{2+}$:
  • Nickel is in $+2$ oxidation state: $\text{Ni}^{2+}: [\text{Ar}] 3d^8$.
  • Even if pairing occurs, only one $3d$ orbital can become empty, but octahedral hybridization requires two vacant $d$-orbitals.
  • Therefore, inner $3d$ orbitals cannot be used. The complex utilizes outer $4d$ orbitals, undergoing $sp^3d^2$ hybridization.
  • Hence, it is an outer orbital (high spin) complex.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Co³⁺ (3d⁶): NH₃ causes pairing yielding two vacant 3d orbitals → d²sp³ (inner orbital)1 Mark
Ni²⁺ (3d⁸): Cannot provide two inner 3d orbitals → forced sp³d² using 4d (outer orbital)1 Mark
In-Text 5.9
NCERT In-Text
Predict the number of unpaired electrons in the square planar $[\text{Pt(CN)}_4]^{2-}$ ion.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Analysis:
Platinum belongs to the $5d$ transition series ($Z = 78$).
Oxidation state of $\text{Pt} = +2$. Electronic configuration: $\text{Pt}^{2+}: [\text{Xe}] 5d^8$.

Crystal Field & Spin Pairing:
For heavier transition metal ions ($4d$ and $5d$ series), crystal field splitting energy is exceptionally high ($Delta$ is ~50% larger than $3d$). Furthermore, $\text{CN}^-$ is a strong field ligand.
Consequently, electron pairing energy is much smaller than crystal field splitting ($P \ll \Delta$). All 8 electrons in the $5d^8$ configuration are completely paired up into the lower four $d$-orbitals, vacating the $d_{x^2-y^2}$ orbital for $dsp^2$ hybridization.

Conclusion: The number of unpaired electrons is $n = 0$ (diamagnetic).

Configuration of Pt²⁺ (5d⁸) and square planar dsp² geometry1 Mark
Complete spin-pairing due to large 5d splitting → 0 unpaired electrons1 Mark
In-Text 5.10
NCERT In-Text
The hexaquo manganese(II) ion contains five unpaired electrons, while the hexacyanoion contains only one unpaired electron. Explain using Crystal Field Theory.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
CFT Explanation:
In both complexes, manganese is in $+2$ oxidation state: $\text{Mn}^{2+}: [\text{Ar}] 3d^5$.
In an octahedral crystal field, the $d$-orbitals split into lower energy $t_{2g}$ (triply degenerate) and higher energy $e_g$ (doubly degenerate) levels by energy $\Delta_o$.

  1. $[\text{Mn(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{2+}$:
    $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is a weak field ligand, so $\mathbf{\Delta_o < P}$ (pairing energy is greater than crystal field splitting).
    Electrons occupy orbitals singly following Hund's rule: configuration is $\mathbf{t_{2g}^3 e_g^2}$.
    Number of unpaired electrons = 5 (high spin).
  2. $[\text{Mn(CN)}_6]^{4-}$:
    $\text{CN}^-$ is a strong field ligand, so $\mathbf{\Delta_o > P}$ (crystal field splitting is greater than pairing energy).
    Electrons pair up preferentially in lower $t_{2g}$ orbitals: configuration is $\mathbf{t_{2g}^5 e_g^0}$.
    Number of unpaired electrons = 1 (low spin).

In-Text 5.11
NCERT In-Text
Calculate the overall complex dissociation equilibrium constant for the $[\text{Cu(NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$ ion, given that $\beta_4$ for this complex is $2.1 \times 10^{13}$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Relation between Formation and Dissociation Constants:
The overall complex formation (stability) constant $\beta_4$ and the overall instability (dissociation) constant $K_d$ are reciprocal to each other: $$K_d = \frac{1}{\beta_4}$$
Calculation: Given: $\beta_4 = 2.1 \times 10^{13}$ $$K_d = \frac{1}{2.1 \times 10^{13}} = \frac{1}{2.1} \times 10^{-13} = 0.476 \times 10^{-13} = \mathbf{4.76 \times 10^{-14}}$$ Answer: The overall dissociation equilibrium constant is $4.76 \times 10^{-14}$.

Exercise 5.1
NCERT Exercise
Explain the bonding in coordination compounds in terms of Werner's postulates.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Werner's Postulates:
  1. Two Types of Valencies: In coordination compounds, metals exhibit two types of valency: Primary Valency and Secondary Valency.
  2. Primary Valency:
    • Ionisable.
    • Corresponds to the oxidation state of the central metal atom/ion.
    • Satisfied only by negative ions (anions).
  3. Secondary Valency:
    • Non-ionisable.
    • Corresponds to the coordination number of the central metal ion and is fixed for a metal in a given state.
    • Satisfied by neutral molecules (e.g., $ ext{NH}_3, ext{H}_2 ext{O}$) or negative ions (e.g., $ ext{Cl}^-, ext{CN}^-$).
  4. Directional Nature & Geometry: Secondary valencies are directed towards fixed spatial positions around the central metal atom, giving rise to definite geometric shapes (octahedral, tetrahedral, square planar).

Exercise 5.2
NCERT Exercise
$\text{FeSO}_4$ solution mixed with $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4$ in $1:1$ molar ratio gives the test of $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ion but $\text{CuSO}_4$ solution mixed with aqueous ammonia in $1:4$ molar ratio does not give the test of $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ion. Explain why?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Reason:

1. $\text{FeSO}_4$ and $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4$ (Mohr's Salt):
When mixed in $1:1$ ratio, they form a double salt, Mohr's salt $[\text{FeSO}_4 \cdot (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}]$.
Double salts dissociate completely into their individual constituent simple ions in aqueous solution: $$\text{FeSO}_4 \cdot (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}(s) \xrightarrow{\text{water}} \mathbf{\text{Fe}^{2+}} + 2\text{NH}_4^+ + 2\text{SO}_4^{2-} + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$$ Because free $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ions exist in solution, it gives the characteristic test for $\text{Fe}^{2+}$.

2. $\text{CuSO}_4$ and $\text{NH}_3$ (Complex Compound):
When $\text{CuSO}_4$ is mixed with aqueous ammonia in $1:4$ ratio, it forms a coordination complex, tetraamminecopper(II) sulphate: $$\text{CuSO}_4 + 4\text{NH}_3 \rightarrow [\text{Cu(NH}_3)_4]\text{SO}_4$$ In aqueous solution, the coordination entity $[\text{Cu(NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$ does not dissociate into simple $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ions: $$[\text{Cu(NH}_3)_4]\text{SO}_4(aq) \rightleftharpoons [\text{Cu(NH}_3)_4]^{2+}(aq) + \text{SO}_4^{2-}(aq)$$ Due to the absence of free $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ions, it fails to give the test for $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ion.

Exercise 5.3
NCERT Exercise
Explain with two examples each of the following: coordination entity, ligand, coordination number, coordination polyhedron, homoleptic and heteroleptic.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Definitions with Examples:
  1. Coordination Entity: A central metal atom/ion bonded to a fixed number of ions or molecules enclosed in square brackets.
    Examples: $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_6]^{3+}, [\text{Ni(CO)}_4]$.
  2. Ligand: Neutral molecules or ions bound to the central metal atom/ion through coordinate bonds.
    Examples: $\text{Cl}^-, \text{NH}_3, \text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{CN}^-$.
  3. Coordination Number (CN): Total number of ligand donor atoms directly bonded to the central metal ion.
    Examples: In $[\text{PtCl}_6]^{2-}$, $\text{CN} = 6$; in $[\text{Ni(NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$, $\text{CN} = 4$.
  4. Coordination Polyhedron: The spatial arrangement of the ligand donor atoms directly attached to the central metal ion.
    Examples: Octahedral ($[\text{Cr(NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$), Tetrahedral ($[\text{Ni(CO)}_4]$), Square planar ($[\text{PtCl}_4]^{2-}$).
  5. Homoleptic Complex: Complexes in which the metal is bound to only one kind of donor ligand.
    Examples: $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_6]^{3+}, [\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{4-}$.
  6. Heteroleptic Complex: Complexes in which the metal is bound to more than one kind of donor ligand.
    Examples: $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_4\text{Cl}_2]^+, [\text{Pt(NH}_3)_2\text{Cl}_2]$.

Exercise 5.4
NCERT Exercise
What is meant by unidentate, didentate and ambidentate ligands? Give two examples for each.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Types of Ligands:
  1. Unidentate (Monodentate) Ligand: A ligand that binds to the central metal ion through only a single donor atom.
    Examples: Chloride ion ($\text{Cl}^-$), Ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$), Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
  2. Didentate (Bidentate) Ligand: A ligand that can bind through two donor atoms simultaneously.
    Examples:
    • Ethane-1,2-diamine ($ ext{H}_2 ext{NCH}_2 ext{CH}_2 ext{NH}_2$, abbreviated as 'en').
    • Oxalate ion ($ ext{C}_2 ext{O}_4^{2-}$ or $ ext{ox}^{2-}$).
  3. Ambidentate Ligand: A unidentate ligand that has two different donor atoms and can coordinate through either of the two atoms to the central metal atom.
    Examples:
    • $\text{NO}_2^-$ ion: can bind through Nitrogen ($\text{M}-\text{NO}_2$, nitro) or Oxygen ($\text{M}-\text{ONO}$, nitrito).
    • $\text{SCN}^-$ ion: can bind through Sulfur ($\text{M}-\text{SCN}$, thiocyanato) or Nitrogen ($\text{M}-\text{NCS}$, isothiocyanato).

Exercise 5.5
NCERT Exercise
Specify the oxidation numbers of the metals in the following coordination entities:
(i) $[\text{Co(H}_2\text{O)(CN)(en)}_2]^{2+}$
(ii) $[\text{CoBr}_2(\text{en})_2]^+$
(iii) $[\text{PtCl}_4]^{2-}$
(iv) $\text{K}_3[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]$
(v) $[\text{Cr(NH}_3)_3\text{Cl}_3]$
[2½ Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Calculations:
  1. $[\text{Co(H}_2\text{O)(CN)(en)}_2]^{2+}$:
    $x + 0 + (-1) + 2(0) = +2 \implies x - 1 = +2 \implies \mathbf{x = +3}$ (Cobalt is in $+3$ state).
  2. $[\text{CoBr}_2(\text{en})_2]^+$:
    $x + 2(-1) + 2(0) = +1 \implies x - 2 = +1 \implies \mathbf{x = +3}$ (Cobalt is in $+3$ state).
  3. $[\text{PtCl}_4]^{2-}$:
    $x + 4(-1) = -2 \implies x - 4 = -2 \implies \mathbf{x = +2}$ (Platinum is in $+2$ state).
  4. $\text{K}_3[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]$:
    Coordination entity is $[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{3-}$.

In both complexes, iron is in the $+2$ oxidation state ($ ext{Fe}^{2+}: [ ext{Ar}] 3d^6$).
However, the colour of a coordination compound depends on the energy difference $\Delta_o$ between the $t_{2g}$ and $e_g$ orbitals, because: $$\Delta_o = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$
  • $\text{CN}^-$ is a strong field ligand, causing a very large crystal field splitting energy ($\Delta_o$ is large). Consequently, it absorbs higher energy light (shorter wavelength, blue-violet region) and transmits yellow light.
  • $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is a weak field ligand, causing a much smaller crystal field splitting energy ($\Delta_o$ is small). It absorbs lower energy light (longer wavelength, red region) and transmits pale green light.
Since different ligands create different magnitudes of crystal field splitting $\Delta_o$, different wavelengths of light are absorbed, resulting in different observed complementary colours.

Exercise 5.22
NCERT Exercise
Discuss the nature of bonding in metal carbonyls.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Synergic Bonding in Metal Carbonyls:
The metal-carbon bond in metal carbonyls possesses both $\sigma$ and $\pi$ character, forming a self-reinforcing synergic bond:

  1. $\text{M} \leftarrow \text{C} \quad \sigma\text{-Bond (Dative covalent)}:
    Formed by the donation of a lone pair of electrons from the carbon atom of the carbonyl ligand into a suitable vacant hybrid orbital of the transition metal atom.
  2. $\text{M} \rightarrow \text{C} \quad \pi\text{-Backbonding}:
    Formed by the back-donation of electrons from a filled non-bonding $d$-orbital of the metal into the vacant antibonding $\pi^*$ ($2p$) molecular orbitals of the carbon monoxide molecule.

Synergic Effect:
The $\sigma$-bond donation increases the electron density on the metal atom, which facilitates stronger $\pi$-backdonation from metal to ligand. In return, $\pi$-backdonation relieves excess negative charge on the metal, strengthening the $\sigma$-bond. This mutual reinforcement (synergic effect) creates a remarkably strong $\text{M}-\text{C}$ bond while weakening the $\text{C}\equiv\text{O}$ bond.

Exercise 5.25
NCERT Exercise
What is meant by stability of a coordination compound in solution? State the factors which govern stability of complexes.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Meaning of Stability:
The stability of a coordination compound in solution refers to the degree of association between the metal ion and the ligands involved in the state of equilibrium: $$\text{M} + n\text{L} \rightleftharpoons \text{ML}_n \quad \beta_n = \frac{[\text{ML}_n]}{[\text{M}][\text{L}]^n}$$ A larger value of the stability constant $\beta_n$ indicates greater thermodynamic stability of the complex.

2. Factors Governing Stability:
  1. Charge on Central Metal Ion: Higher positive charge increases electrostatic attraction for ligands, increasing stability ($ ext{Fe}^{3+} > ext{Fe}^{2+}$).
  2. Size of Metal Ion (Charge-to-Radius Ratio): Smaller metal ions have higher charge density, leading to more stable complexes (Irving-Williams order: $ ext{Ba}^{2+} < ext{Sr}^{2+} < ext{Ca}^{2+} < ext{Mg}^{2+} < ext{Mn}^{2+} < ext{Fe}^{2+} < ext{Co}^{2+} < ext{Ni}^{2+} < ext{Cu}^{2+} > ext{Zn}^{2+}$).
  3. Basic Nature of Ligand: More basic ligands donate their lone pairs more readily, forming more stable complexes ($ ext{CN}^- > ext{NH}_3 > ext{H}_2 ext{O}$).
  4. Chelate Effect: Complexes containing multidentate (chelating) ligands that form 5- or 6-membered ring structures are significantly more stable than analogous monodentate complexes due to positive entropy change ($Delta S > 0$).
