In-Text 3.1
NCERT In-Text
For the reaction $R \rightarrow P$, the concentration of a reactant changes from $0.03\text{ M}$ to $0.02\text{ M}$ in $25\text{ minutes}$. Calculate the average rate of reaction using units of time both in minutes and seconds.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Formula:
The average rate of disappearance of reactant $R$ is: $$r_{\text{avg}} = -\frac{\Delta [R]}{\Delta t} = -\frac{[R]_2 - [R]_1}{t_2 - t_1}$$ Given: $[R]_1 = 0.03\text{ M}$, $[R]_2 = 0.02\text{ M}$, $\Delta t = 25\text{ min}$

1. Rate in minutes: $$r_{\text{avg}} = -\frac{0.02 - 0.03}{25\text{ min}} = \frac{0.01}{25} = \mathbf{4.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ mol L}^{-1}\text{ min}^{-1}}$$
2. Rate in seconds: $$\Delta t = 25 \times 60 = 1500\text{ s}$$ $$r_{\text{avg}} = -\frac{0.02 - 0.03}{1500\text{ s}} = \frac{0.01}{1500} = \mathbf{6.67 \times 10^{-6}\text{ mol L}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}}$$

Formula with negative sign and rate in mol L⁻¹ min⁻¹ ($4.0 \times 10^{-4}$)1 Mark
Time conversion to seconds and rate in mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹ ($6.67 \times 10^{-6}$)1 Mark
In-Text 3.2
NCERT In-Text
In a reaction, $2A \rightarrow \text{Products}$, the concentration of $A$ decreases from $0.5\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ to $0.4\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ in $10\text{ minutes}$. Calculate the rate during this interval.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Formula with Stoichiometric Coefficient:
For the reaction $2A \rightarrow \text{Products}$, the rate of reaction is: $$\text{Rate} = -\frac{1}{2} \frac{\Delta [A]}{\Delta t}$$ Given: $[A]_1 = 0.5\text{ mol L}^{-1}$, $[A]_2 = 0.4\text{ mol L}^{-1}$, $\Delta t = 10\text{ min}$

Calculation: $$\text{Rate} = -\frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{0.4 - 0.5}{10} \right) = -\frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{-0.1}{10} \right) = \frac{0.1}{20} = \mathbf{5.0 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol L}^{-1}\text{ min}^{-1}}$$ In seconds: $$\text{Rate} = \frac{5.0 \times 10^{-3}}{60} = \mathbf{8.33 \times 10^{-5}\text{ mol L}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}}$$

In-Text 3.3
NCERT In-Text
For a reaction, $A + B \rightarrow \text{Product}$; the rate law is given by, $r = k [A]^{1/2} [B]^2$. What is the overall order of reaction?
[1 Mark]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Definition:
The overall order of a reaction is the sum of the powers of the concentration terms of reactants in the rate law equation.

Given rate law: $r = k [A]^{1/2} [B]^2$
$$\text{Overall Order } (n) = \frac{1}{2} + 2 = \frac{1 + 4}{2} = \frac{5}{2} = \mathbf{2.5}$$ Answer: The overall order of reaction is 2.5 (or $5/2$).

In-Text 3.4
NCERT In-Text
The conversion of molecules $X$ to $Y$ follows second order kinetics. If concentration of $X$ is increased to three times how will it affect the rate of formation of $Y$?
[1 Mark]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Rate Law Formulation:
Since the conversion $X \rightarrow Y$ follows second order kinetics: $$r_1 = k [X]^2$$ When $[X]$ is increased to 3 times, new concentration $[X'] = 3[X]$: $$r_2 = k [X']^2 = k (3[X])^2 = 9 k [X]^2 = 9 r_1$$ Conclusion: The rate of formation of $Y$ will become 9 times the initial rate.

In-Text 3.5
NCERT In-Text
A first order reaction has a rate constant $1.15 \times 10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}$. How long will $5\text{ g}$ of this reactant take to reduce to $3\text{ g}$?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Integrated Rate Equation for First Order Reaction: $$t = \frac{2.303}{k} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]}$$ Given: $[R]_0 = 5\text{ g}$, $[R] = 3\text{ g}$, $k = 1.15 \times 10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}$

Substitution: $$t = \frac{2.303}{1.15 \times 10^{-3}} \log \left( \frac{5}{3} \right)$$ $$\log \left( \frac{5}{3} \right) = \log 5 - \log 3 = 0.6990 - 0.4771 = 0.2219$$ $$t = \frac{2.303 \times 0.2219}{1.15 \times 10^{-3}} = \frac{0.5110}{1.15 \times 10^{-3}} = \mathbf{444.35\text{ s}} \approx \mathbf{444\text{ s}}$$ Answer: It will take approximately 444 seconds (or $7.41\text{ minutes}$).

In-Text 3.6
NCERT In-Text
Time required to decompose $\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}_2$ to half of its initial amount is $60\text{ minutes}$. If the decomposition is a first order reaction, calculate the rate constant of the reaction.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Relation between Half-Life and Rate Constant:
For a first order reaction, half-life $t_{1/2}$ is given by: $$t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k} \implies k = \frac{0.693}{t_{1/2}}$$ Given: $t_{1/2} = 60\text{ min} = 60 \times 60 = 3600\text{ s}$

Rate Constant Calculations:
1. In $\text{min}^{-1}$: $$k = \frac{0.693}{60\text{ min}} = \mathbf{0.01155\text{ min}^{-1}} = \mathbf{1.155 \times 10^{-2}\text{ min}^{-1}}$$ 2. In $\text{s}^{-1}$: $$k = \frac{0.693}{3600\text{ s}} = \mathbf{1.925 \times 10^{-4}\text{ s}^{-1}}$$

In-Text 3.7
NCERT In-Text
What will be the effect of temperature on rate constant?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. General Observation:
The rate constant of a chemical reaction increases with an increase in temperature. For most reactions, with a $10^\circ\text{C}$ ($10\text{ K}$) rise in temperature, the rate constant nearly doubles (Temperature coefficient $\approx 2$ to $3$).

2. Quantitative Explanation (Arrhenius Equation): $$k = A e^{-E_a / RT}$$ where $A$ is the pre-exponential factor, $E_a$ is the activation energy, $R$ is the gas constant, and $T$ is absolute temperature.

3. Molecular Mechanism:
As temperature increases, the average kinetic energy of reactant molecules increases. The Boltzmann factor $e^{-E_a/RT}$ represents the fraction of molecules possessing energy equal to or greater than activation energy. A small increase in temperature substantially enlarges this fraction, drastically increasing the frequency of effective collisions and hence elevating the rate constant $k$.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
General effect (doubles with 10 K temperature rise)½ Mark
Arrhenius equation $k = A e^{-E_a/RT}$ with variables defined1 Mark
Molecular kinetic explanation (fraction of effective collisions)½ Mark
In-Text 3.8
NCERT In-Text
The rate of the chemical reaction doubles for an increase of $10\text{ K}$ in absolute temperature from $298\text{ K}$. Calculate $E_a$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Arrhenius Equation at Two Temperatures: $$\log \frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303 R} \left[ \frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1 T_2} \right]$$ Given: $T_1 = 298\text{ K}$, $T_2 = 298 + 10 = 308\text{ K}$, $\frac{k_2}{k_1} = 2$, $R = 8.314\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$
$\log 2 = 0.3010$

Substitution: $$0.3010 = \frac{E_a}{2.303 \times 8.314} \left[ \frac{308 - 298}{298 \times 308} \right]$$ $$0.3010 = \frac{E_a}{19.147} \left[ \frac{10}{91784} \right]$$ $$E_a = \frac{0.3010 \times 19.147 \times 91784}{10} = 52897.8\text{ J mol}^{-1} = \mathbf{52.9\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}$$ Answer: The activation energy $E_a$ is $52.9\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$.

Substitution of parameters ($T_1, T_2, \log 2, R$)1 Mark

In-Text 3.9
NCERT In-Text
The activation energy for the reaction $2\text{HI}(g) \rightarrow \text{H}_2 + \text{I}_2(g)$ is $209.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ at $581\text{ K}$. Calculate the fraction of molecules of reactants having energy equal to or greater than activation energy.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Formula for Fraction of Molecules ($x$):
The fraction of molecules having energy $\ge E_a$ is given by the Boltzmann factor: $$x = e^{-E_a / RT} \implies \log x = -\frac{E_a}{2.303 R T}$$ Given: $E_a = 209.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} = 209500\text{ J mol}^{-1}$, $T = 581\text{ K}$, $R = 8.314\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$

Calculation: $$\log x = -\frac{209500}{2.303 \times 8.314 \times 581} = -\frac{209500}{11124.5} = -18.8323$$ $$\log x = \bar{19}.1677 = -19 + 0.1677$$ $$x = \text{Antilog}(0.1677) \times 10^{-19} = \mathbf{1.471 \times 10^{-19}}$$ Answer: The fraction of molecules is $1.471 \times 10^{-19}$.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Boltzmann fraction formula $x = e^{-E_a/RT}$ and log conversion1 Mark
Log calculation and antilog yielding $1.471 \times 10^{-19}$1 Mark
In-Text 3.10
NCERT In-Text
Explain the effect of adding a catalyst on the activation energy and rate of a chemical reaction.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Role and Mechanism of Catalyst:
  1. Lowers Activation Energy: A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway involving a lower potential energy barrier (activation energy, $E_a' < E_a$).
  2. Increases Reaction Rate: According to the Arrhenius equation ($k = A e^{-E_a/RT}$), a lower activation energy exponentially increases the Boltzmann factor $e^{-E_a/RT}$, significantly increasing the rate constant $k$ and thus accelerating the overall reaction.
  3. No Effect on Equilibrium: A catalyst speeds up both forward and backward reactions equally. It does not alter the equilibrium constant ($K_c$) or standard Gibbs free energy change ($\Delta G^\circ$), but simply enables the system to attain equilibrium faster.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Mechanism: provides alternative pathway lowering activation energy ($E_a$)1 Mark
Exponential increase in rate constant $k$ and unaffected equilibrium constant $K$1 Mark

From the rate expression for the following reactions, determine their order of reaction and the dimensions of the rate constants:
(i) $3\text{NO}(g) \rightarrow \text{N}_2\text{O}(g)$; $\text{Rate} = k[\text{NO}]^2$
(ii) $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2(aq) + 3\text{I}^-(aq) + 2\text{H}^+ \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{I}_3^-$; $\text{Rate} = k[\text{H}_2\text{O}_2][\text{I}^-]$
(iii) $\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}(g) \rightarrow \text{CH}_4(g) + \text{CO}(g)$; $\text{Rate} = k[\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}]^{3/2}$
(iv) $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{Cl}(g) \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_4(g) + \text{HCl}(g)$; $\text{Rate} = k[\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{Cl}]$
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
General Dimensional Formula for Rate Constant ($k$):
For an $n^{\text{th}}$ order reaction: $$\text{Units of } k = (\text{mol L}^{-1})^{1-n} \text{ s}^{-1} = \text{mol}^{1-n} \text{L}^{n-1} \text{s}^{-1}$$
  • (i) $\text{Rate} = k[\text{NO}]^2$:
    Order $n = 2$ (Second order).
    Units of $k = (\text{mol L}^{-1})^{1-2} \text{ s}^{-1} = \mathbf{\text{L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}}$.
  • (ii) $\text{Rate} = k[\text{H}_2\text{O}_2][\text{I}^-]$:
    Order $n = 1 + 1 = 2$ (Second order).
    Units of $k = \mathbf{\text{L mol}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}}$.
  • (iii) $\text{Rate} = k[\text{CH}_3\text{CHO}]^{3/2}$:
    Order $n = 3/2 = 1.5$.
    Units of $k = (\text{mol L}^{-1})^{1 - 3/2} \text{ s}^{-1} = \mathbf{\text{mol}^{-1/2}\text{ L}^{1/2}\text{ s}^{-1}}$.
  • (iv) $\text{Rate} = k[\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{Cl}]$:
    Order $n = 1$ (First order).
    Units of $k = (\text{mol L}^{-1})^{1-1} \text{ s}^{-1} = \mathbf{\text{s}^{-1}}$.

Exercise 3.2
NCERT Exercise
For the reaction: $2A + B \rightarrow A_2B$, the rate $= k[A][B]^2$ with $k = 2.0 \times 10^{-6}\text{ mol}^{-2}\text{ L}^2\text{ s}^{-1}$. Calculate the initial rate of the reaction when $[A] = 0.1\text{ mol L}^{-1}$, $[B] = 0.2\text{ mol L}^{-1}$. Calculate the rate of reaction after $[A]$ is reduced to $0.06\text{ mol L}^{-1}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Initial Rate of Reaction: $$\text{Rate}_0 = k [A] [B]^2 = (2.0 \times 10^{-6}) \times (0.1) \times (0.2)^2$$ $$\text{Rate}_0 = 2.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 0.1 \times 0.04 = \mathbf{8.0 \times 10^{-9}\text{ mol L}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}}$$
2. Stoichiometry and Remaining Concentration:
Amount of $A$ reacted: $\Delta [A] = 0.1 - 0.06 = 0.04\text{ mol L}^{-1}$.
According to the reaction $2A + B \rightarrow A_2B$, 2 moles of $A$ consume 1 mole of $B$: $$\text{Amount of } B \text{ reacted} = \frac{1}{2} \times 0.04 = 0.02\text{ mol L}^{-1}$$ Remaining concentration of $B$: $$[B]_{\text{rem}} = 0.2 - 0.02 = 0.18\text{ mol L}^{-1}$$
3. Rate after $[A]$ is reduced to $0.06\text{ M}$: $$\text{Rate} = k [A] [B]^2 = (2.0 \times 10^{-6}) \times (0.06) \times (0.18)^2$$ $$\text{Rate} = 2.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 0.06 \times 0.0324 = \mathbf{3.89 \times 10^{-9}\text{ mol L}^{-1}\text{ s}^{-1}}$$

Exercise 3.5
NCERT Exercise
Mention the factors that affect the rate of a chemical reaction.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Major Factors Affecting the Rate of a Chemical Reaction:
  1. Nature of Reactants: Ionic reactions occur instantaneously, whereas covalent bonds require bond cleavage and have distinct activation energies.
  2. Concentration of Reactants: Greater concentration increases collision frequency per unit volume, increasing the reaction rate.
  3. Temperature: Reaction rates increase exponentially with temperature. As a rule of thumb, every 10 K rise doubles or triples the rate constant.
  4. Presence of Catalyst: A catalyst provides an alternate pathway with lower activation energy, increasing reaction rate without being consumed.
  5. Surface Area of Reactants: In heterogeneous reactions, finely divided powders present larger surface area, leading to faster rates.
  6. Exposure to Light (Radiation): Photochemical reactions require absorption of specific photon energies ($h\nu$) to initiate reaction.

Exercise 3.6
NCERT Exercise
A reaction is second order with respect to a reactant. How is the rate of reaction affected if the concentration of the reactant is:
(i) doubled?
(ii) reduced to half?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Rate Law:
Let the concentration of the reactant be $[A]$. For second order: $$r_1 = k [A]^2$$
(i) When $[A]$ is doubled ($[A'] = 2[A]$): $$r_2 = k [2A]^2 = 4 k [A]^2 = 4 r_1$$ The rate of reaction increases by 4 times.

(ii) When $[A]$ is reduced to half ($[A''] = \frac{1}{2}[A]$): $$r_3 = k \left( \frac{1}{2}[A] \right)^2 = \frac{1}{4} k [A]^2 = \frac{1}{4} r_1$$ The rate of reaction becomes one-fourth ($1/4^{\text{th}}$) of the initial rate.

Exercise 3.7
NCERT Exercise
What is the effect of temperature on the rate constant of a reaction? How can this temperature effect on rate constant be represented quantitatively?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Qualitative Effect:
The rate constant increases significantly with rising temperature. For every $10^\circ\text{C}$ rise, the rate constant doubles or triples because the fraction of molecules with energy exceeding activation energy increases.

2. Quantitative Formulations (Arrhenius Equation): $$k = A e^{-E_a / RT}$$ Taking natural logarithm on both sides: $$\ln k = \ln A - \frac{E_a}{RT}$$ Converting to base-10 logarithm: $$\log k = \log A - \frac{E_a}{2.303 R T}$$ At two different temperatures $T_1$ and $T_2$ with rate constants $k_1$ and $k_2$: $$\log \frac{k_2}{k_1} = \frac{E_a}{2.303 R} \left[ \frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1 T_2} \right]$$

A reaction is first order in $A$ and second order in $B$:

In a reaction between $A$ and $B$, the initial rate of reaction ($r_0$) was measured for different initial concentrations of $A$ and $B$ as given below:
Exp.[A] / mol L⁻¹[B] / mol L⁻¹r₀ / mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹
10.200.305.07 × 10⁻⁵
20.200.105.07 × 10⁻⁵
30.400.051.43 × 10⁻⁴
What is the order of the reaction with respect to $A$ and $B$?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
General Rate Law: $r_0 = k [A]^x [B]^y$

1. Order with respect to $B$ ($y$):
Dividing Exp 1 by Exp 2: $$\frac{5.07 \times 10^{-5}}{5.07 \times 10^{-5}} = \frac{k (0.20)^x (0.30)^y}{k (0.20)^x (0.10)^y} \implies 1 = (3)^y \implies \mathbf{y = 0}$$ The reaction is zero order with respect to $B$.

2. Order with respect to $A$ ($x$):
Since $y = 0$, dividing Exp 3 by Exp 2: $$\frac{1.43 \times 10^{-4}}{5.07 \times 10^{-5}} = \frac{k (0.40)^x}{k (0.20)^x} \implies 2.82 = (2)^x$$ Taking logarithms: $$\log(2.82) = x \log(2) \implies 0.4502 = x (0.3010) \implies x = \frac{0.4502}{0.3010} \approx \mathbf{1.5} \text{ (or } 3/2)$$ Answer: Order with respect to $A$ is 1.5; order with respect to $B$ is 0.

(b) $2\text{ min}^{-1}$

✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Exercise 3.14
NCERT Exercise
The half-life for radioactive decay of $^{14}\text{C}$ is $5730\text{ years}$. An archaeological artifact containing wood had only $80\%$ of the $^{14}\text{C}$ found in a living tree. Estimate the age of the sample.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Radioactive Decay Constant ($k$):
Radioactive decay follows first order kinetics: $$k = \frac{0.693}{t_{1/2}} = \frac{0.693}{5730\text{ years}} = 1.209 \times 10^{-4}\text{ year}^{-1}$$
2. Integrated First Order Equation: $$t = \frac{2.303}{k} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]}$$ Let initial concentration $[R]_0 = 100$. Current concentration $[R] = 80$. $$t = \frac{2.303}{1.209 \times 10^{-4}} \log \left( \frac{100}{80} \right) = \frac{2.303}{1.209 \times 10^{-4}} \log(1.25)$$ $$\log(1.25) = 0.0969$$ $$t = \frac{2.303 \times 0.0969}{1.209 \times 10^{-4}} = \frac{0.22316}{1.209 \times 10^{-4}} = \mathbf{1845.8\text{ years}} \approx \mathbf{1846\text{ years}}$$ Answer: The estimated age of the wood sample is approximately 1846 years.

Exercise 3.16
NCERT Exercise
The rate constant for a first order reaction is $60\text{ s}^{-1}$. How much time will it take to reduce the initial concentration of the reactant to its $1/16^{\text{th}}$ value?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Method 1: Integrated Rate Equation:
Given: $k = 60\text{ s}^{-1}$, $\frac{[R]_0}{[R]} = 16$. $$t = \frac{2.303}{k} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]} = \frac{2.303}{60} \log(16)$$ $$\log(16) = \log(2^4) = 4 \log 2 = 4 \times 0.3010 = 1.2040$$ $$t = \frac{2.303 \times 1.2040}{60} = \frac{2.7728}{60} = \mathbf{4.62 \times 10^{-2}\text{ s}}$$
Method 2: Number of Half-Lives ($n$):
$$\frac{[R]}{[R]_0} = \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^n = \frac{1}{16} = \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^4 \implies n = 4$$ $$t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k} = \frac{0.693}{60} = 0.01155\text{ s}$$ $$t = 4 \times t_{1/2} = 4 \times 0.01155 = \mathbf{4.62 \times 10^{-2}\text{ s}}$$

Exercise 3.18
NCERT Exercise
For a first order reaction, show that time required for $99\%$ completion is twice the time required for the completion of $90\%$ of reaction.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Time for $99\%$ completion ($t_{99\%}$):
When reaction is $99\%$ complete, $[R] = [R]_0 - 0.99[R]_0 = 0.01[R]_0$. $$t_{99\%} = \frac{2.303}{k} \log \frac{[R]_0}{0.01[R]_0} = \frac{2.303}{k} \log(100) = \frac{2.303}{k} \times 2 = \frac{4.606}{k} \quad \text{--- (1)}$$
2. Time for $90\%$ completion ($t_{90\%}$):
When reaction is $90\%$ complete, $[R] = [R]_0 - 0.90[R]_0 = 0.10[R]_0$. $$t_{90\%} = \frac{2.303}{k} \log \frac{[R]_0}{0.10[R]_0} = \frac{2.303}{k} \log(10) = \frac{2.303}{k} \times 1 = \frac{2.303}{k} \quad \text{--- (2)}$$
3. Comparison:
Dividing equation (1) by equation (2): $$\frac{t_{99\%}}{t_{90\%}} = \frac{2 \times \frac{2.303}{k}}{\frac{2.303}{k}} = 2 \implies \mathbf{t_{99\%} = 2 \times t_{90\%}}$$ Hence proved.

A first order reaction takes $40\text{ min}$ for $30\%$ decomposition. Calculate $t_{1/2}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Rate Constant ($k$):
For $30\%$ decomposition, remaining amount is $100 - 30 = 70\%$ at $t = 40\text{ min}$. $$k = \frac{2.303}{t} \log \frac{[R]_0}{[R]} = \frac{2.303}{40} \log \left( \frac{100}{70} \right) = \frac{2.303}{40} \log(1.4286)$$ $$\log(1.4286) = 0.1549$$ $$k = \frac{2.303 \times 0.1549}{40} = \frac{0.3567}{40} = 8.918 \times 10^{-3}\text{ min}^{-1}$$
2. Half-Life ($t_{1/2}$): $$t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k} = \frac{0.693}{8.918 \times 10^{-3}\text{ min}^{-1}} = \mathbf{77.7\text{ minutes}}$$ Answer: The half-life of the reaction is $77.7\text{ minutes}$.

Exercise 3.20
NCERT Exercise
For the decomposition of azoisopropane to hexane and nitrogen at $543\text{ K}$, the following data are obtained:
t / s0360720
P / mm of Hg35.054.063.0
Calculate the rate constant.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
1. Partial Pressure Relations:
Reaction: $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHN}=\text{NCH}(\text{CH}_3)_2(g) \rightarrow \text{N}_2(g) + \text{C}_6\text{H}_{14}(g)$
At $t = 0$: $p_0 = 35.0\text{ mm Hg}$
At time $t$: Partial pressure of azoisopropane $p_A = p_0 - p$
Total pressure $P_t = (p_0 - p) + p + p = p_0 + p \implies p = P_t - p_0$
Therefore, $p_A = p_0 - (P_t - p_0) = 2p_0 - P_t$
Integrated formula: $$k = \frac{2.303}{t} \log \frac{p_0}{2p_0 - P_t}$$
2. Rate constant at $t = 360\text{ s}$ ($P_t = 54.0\text{ mm Hg}$): $$2p_0 - P_t = 2(35.0) - 54.0 = 70.0 - 54.0 = 16.0\text{ mm Hg}$$ $$k_1 = \frac{2.303}{360} \log \left( \frac{35.0}{16.0} \right) = \frac{2.303}{360} \log(2.1875)$$ $$\log(2.1875) = 0.3400 \implies k_1 = \frac{2.303 \times 0.3400}{360} = 2.175 \times 10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}$$
3. Rate constant at $t = 720\text{ s}$ ($P_t = 63.0\text{ mm Hg}$): $$2p_0 - P_t = 2(35.0) - 63.0 = 70.0 - 63.0 = 7.0\text{ mm Hg}$$ $$k_2 = \frac{2.303}{720} \log \left( \frac{35.0}{7.0} \right) = \frac{2.303}{720} \log(5)$$ $$\log(5) = 0.6990 \implies k_2 = \frac{2.303 \times 0.6990}{720} = 2.236 \times 10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}$$
4. Average Rate Constant: $$k_{\text{avg}} = \frac{k_1 + k_2}{2} = \frac{2.175 + 2.236}{2} \times 10^{-3} = \mathbf{2.21 \times 10^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}}$$
