In-Text 7.1
NCERT In-Text
Classify the following as primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols:
(i) $\text{CH}_3-\text{C(CH}_3)_2-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$
(ii) $\text{H}_2\text{C}=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$
(iii) $\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$
(iv) 1-Methylcyclohexanol
(v) 1-Phenylethanol
(vi) 2-Phenylpropan-2-ol
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Classification based on the degree of carbon attached to $-\text{OH}$ group:
  1. $\text{CH}_3-\text{C(CH}_3)_2-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$ (2,2-Dimethylpropan-1-ol): Primary ($1^\circ$) alcohol (attached to a primary carbon).
  2. $\text{H}_2\text{C}=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$ (Prop-2-en-1-ol): Primary ($1^\circ$) alcohol (allylic).
  3. $\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$ (Propan-1-ol): Primary ($1^\circ$) alcohol.
  4. 1-Methylcyclohexanol: Tertiary ($3^\circ$) alcohol (carbon bearing $-\text{OH}$ is attached to three carbons: two in the ring and one methyl).
  5. 1-Phenylethanol [$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH(OH)}-\text{CH}_3$]: Secondary ($2^\circ$) alcohol (benzylic).
  6. 2-Phenylpropan-2-ol [$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{C(OH)(CH}_3)_2$]: Tertiary ($3^\circ$) alcohol (benzylic).

In-Text 7.4
NCERT In-Text
Show how are the following alcohols prepared by the reaction of a suitable Grignard reagent on methanal?
(i) $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCH}_2\text{OH}$
(ii) Cyclohexylmethanol
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Principle: Methanal (formaldehyde, $\text{HCHO}$) reacts with Grignard reagent ($R-\text{MgX}$) followed by hydrolysis to yield a primary alcohol with one additional carbon atom: $$\text{HCHO} + R-\text{MgX} \rightarrow R-\text{CH}_2\text{OMgX} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_3\text{O}^+} R-\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{Mg(OH)X}$$
  1. Preparation of $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCH}_2\text{OH}$ (2-Methylpropan-1-ol):
    The $R$ group required is the isopropyl group ($(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}-$).
    Reagent: Isopropylmagnesium bromide [$(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHMgBr}$]. $$(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHMgBr} + \text{HCHO} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{OMgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O} / \text{H}^+} \mathbf{(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}} + \text{Mg(OH)Br}$$
  2. Preparation of Cyclohexylmethanol:
    The $R$ group required is the cyclohexyl group ($ ext{C}_6 ext{H}_{11}-$).
    Reagent: Cyclohexylmagnesium bromide [$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{11}\text{MgBr}$]. $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{11}\text{MgBr} + \text{HCHO} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{11}-\text{CH}_2\text{OMgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O} / \text{H}^+} \mathbf{\text{C}_6\text{H}_{11}-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}} + \text{Mg(OH)Br}$$

In-Text 7.7
NCERT In-Text
Predict the major product of acid catalysed dehydration of:
(i) 1-methylcyclohexanol and
(ii) butan-1-ol.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Saytzeff Elimination & Carbocation Rearrangement:
  1. 1-Methylcyclohexanol:
    Protonation followed by loss of water generates a stable tertiary carbocation at the ring carbon.
    Loss of a $\beta$-proton from the ring carbon gives the more highly substituted, more stable endocyclic alkene: $$\mathbf{1-\text{Methylcyclohexene}} \quad (\text{Major, Saytzeff product})$$
  2. Butan-1-ol:
    Protonation of butan-1-ol followed by loss of water yields a primary carbocation [$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2^+$], which undergoes a 1,2-hydride shift to form a much more stable secondary carbocation [$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}^+\text{CH}_3$].
    Elimination of a $\beta$-proton from $C_3$ follows Saytzeff's rule to give the more substituted alkene: $$\mathbf{\text{But-2-ene}} \quad (\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_3) \quad (\text{Major})$$

In-Text 7.8
NCERT In-Text
Ortho and para nitrophenols are more acidic than phenol. Draw the resonance structures of the corresponding phenoxide ions.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Reason for Enhanced Acidity:
The nitro group ($-\text{NO}_2$) possesses strong electron-withdrawing inductive ($-I$) and resonance ($-R$) effects.

Resonance Stabilization of $o$- and $p$-Nitrophenoxide Ions:
In the phenoxide ion, the negative charge is delocalized over the ortho and para positions of the aromatic ring.
When the $-\text{NO}_2$ group is located at the ortho or para position, the negative charge is delocalized directly onto the highly electronegative oxygen atoms of the nitro group: $$-\text{C}^- - \text{N}^+(=\text{O})-\text{O}^- \longleftrightarrow -\text{C}=\text{N}^+(-\text{O}^-)-\text{O}^-$$ This additional extended conjugation provides profound stabilization to the conjugate base (phenoxide ion), driving the equilibrium towards dissociation: $$\text{ArOH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{ArO}^- + \text{H}_3\text{O}^+$$ Because the phenoxide ion is substantially more stabilized in $o$- and $p$-nitrophenol than in unsubstituted phenol, $o$- and $p$-nitrophenols are much stronger acids ($pK_a \approx 7.2$) than phenol ($pK_a \approx 10.0$).

Exercise 7.4
NCERT Exercise
Explain why propanol has higher boiling point than that of the hydrocarbon, butane?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Reason:
Both propanol ($ ext{CH}_3 ext{CH}_2 ext{CH}_2 ext{OH}$, $M = 60$) and butane ($ ext{CH}_3 ext{CH}_2 ext{CH}_2 ext{CH}_3$, $M = 58$) have comparable molecular masses.

  • Propanol: Contains a polar $-\text{O}-\text{H}$ bond. The hydrogen atom bonded to electronegative oxygen forms extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds between propanol molecules, creating associated molecular clusters.
  • Butane: A non-polar hydrocarbon whose molecules are held together only by weak van der Waals dispersion forces.
Significantly more thermal energy is required to break the strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds in propanol than the weak van der Waals forces in butane. Hence, propanol boils at a much higher temperature ($370\text{ K}$) than butane ($272.5\text{ K}$).

Exercise 7.5
NCERT Exercise
Alcohols are comparatively more soluble in water than hydrocarbons of similar molecular masses. Explain this fact.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Reason:
Alcohols contain the polar hydroxyl group ($-\text{O}^{\delta-}-\text{H}^{\delta+}$).
This allows alcohol molecules to form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with water molecules: $$R-\text{O}-\text{H} \cdots :\text{O}(\text{H})-\text{H}$$ The energy released during this hydrogen bond formation compensates for breaking the water-water hydrogen bonds and solute-solute interactions, allowing lower alcohols (methanol, ethanol, propanol) to be completely miscible with water.

In contrast, hydrocarbons are non-polar hydrophobic molecules that cannot form hydrogen bonds with water molecules, making them insoluble.

Exercise 7.6

[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Definition:
The addition of diborane ($(\text{BH}_3)_2$) to an alkene to form a trialkylborane, followed by its alkaline oxidation with hydrogen peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2 / \text{OH}^-$) to yield an alcohol, is known as the Hydroboration-Oxidation Reaction.

  2. Nitration of Anisole: $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OCH}_3 + \text{HNO}_3 / \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \mathbf{p\text{-Nitroanisole (major)}} + o\text{-Nitroanisole (minor)} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
  3. Bromination in Ethanoic Acid: $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OCH}_3 + \text{Br}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}} \mathbf{p\text{-Bromoanisole (90%, major)}} + o\text{-Bromoanisole (minor)} + \text{HBr}$$ (No catalyst required due to high activation by $-\text{OCH}_3$).
  4. Friedel-Crafts Acetylation: $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OCH}_3 + \text{CH}_3\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{anhyd. AlCl}_3} \mathbf{4-\text{Methoxyacetophenone (major)}} + 2-\text{Methoxyacetophenone (minor)} + \text{HCl}$$

Exercise 7.26
NCERT Exercise
Some ethers are prepared by Williamson synthesis. Which of the following is not a suitable Williamson synthesis? Explain:
(i) $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-Br} + \text{CH}_3\text{ONa} \rightarrow$
(ii) $\text{CH}_3\text{Br} + (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-ONa} \rightarrow$
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Analysis of Williamson Ether Synthesis:
The reaction proceeds via an $S_N2$ displacement of halide by alkoxide ion.

  1. Route (i): $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-Br} + \text{CH}_3\text{ONa}$: (UNSUITABLE)
    tert-Butyl bromide is a tertiary ($3^\circ$) alkyl halide. Sodium methoxide is not only a nucleophile but also a strong base. Due to severe steric hindrance to $S_N2$ attack, elimination ($E2$) predominates completely, yielding an alkene (2-methylpropene) instead of ether: $$(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-Br} + \text{CH}_3\text{ONa} \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{CH}_2=\text{C(CH}_3)_2} + \text{CH}_3\text{OH} + \text{NaBr}$$
  2. Route (ii): $\text{CH}_3\text{Br} + (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-ONa}$: (SUITABLE)
    Methyl bromide is an unhindered primary halide. The bulky tert-butoxide ion easily attacks the methyl carbon via $S_N2$ to form the desired ether in high yield: $$\text{CH}_3\text{Br} + (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-ONa} \rightarrow \mathbf{(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}-\text{O}-\text{CH}_3} + \text{NaBr}$$
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)

Route (ii) suitable because 1° halide undergoes clean SN2 substitution1 Mark

Exercise 7.28
NCERT Exercise
Give reasons for the higher boiling point of ethanol in comparison to methoxymethane.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Comparison:
Both ethanol ($ ext{C}_2 ext{H}_5 ext{OH}$) and methoxymethane ($ ext{CH}_3 ext{OCH}_3$) are functional isomers with identical molecular formula ($ ext{C}_2 ext{H}_6 ext{O}$) and molecular mass ($46$).

  • Ethanol: Possesses a hydrogen atom bonded to an electronegative oxygen atom ($-\text{O}-\text{H}$). It forms strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds that hold the molecules tightly together in liquid state. Boiling point = $351\text{ K}$.
  • Methoxymethane: Oxygen is bonded only to carbon atoms ($ ext{C}- ext{O}- ext{C}$); it lacks hydrogen bonded to oxygen and cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds. Its molecules are held together only by much weaker dipole-dipole attractions. Boiling point = $248\text{ K}$.

Exercise 7.31
NCERT Exercise
Illustrate with examples the limitations of Williamson synthesis for the preparation of certain types of ethers.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Major Limitations:
  1. Tertiary Alkyl Halides Yield Alkenes: Williamson synthesis is an $S_N2$ reaction. If a tertiary ($3^\circ$) alkyl halide is used with an alkoxide ion, elimination ($E2$) predominates over substitution, producing an alkene instead of an ether: $$(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-Br} + \text{CH}_3\text{ONa} \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{CH}_2=\text{C(CH}_3)_2} + \text{CH}_3\text{OH} + \text{NaBr}$$ Solution: To prepare tert-butyl ethers, use a $3^\circ$ alkoxide and a $1^\circ$ methyl/alkyl halide.
  2. Aryl Halides & Vinyl Halides Cannot Be Used: Aryl halides (e.g., chlorobenzene) and vinyl halides (e.g., chloroethene) cannot be used as substrates because their $\text{C}-\text{X}$ bond has partial double bond character due to resonance and does not undergo $S_N2$ displacement: $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{Cl} + \text{CH}_3\text{ONa} \rightarrow \text{No reaction (under Williamson conditions)}$$ Diaryl ethers (e.g., $ ext{C}_6 ext{H}_5- ext{O}- ext{C}_6 ext{H}_5$) cannot be prepared by Williamson synthesis.

Exercise 7.32
NCERT Exercise
How is ethanal converted into butan-2-ol using Grignard reagent?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Reaction Sequence:
Ethanal ($ ext{CH}_3 ext{CHO}$) contains a 2-carbon carbonyl backbone. To prepare butan-2-ol ($ ext{CH}_3 ext{CH(OH)CH}_2 ext{CH}_3$, 4 carbons), an ethyl group must be added using ethylmagnesium bromide ($ ext{C}_2 ext{H}_5 ext{MgBr}$):

  1. Nucleophilic Addition: The nucleophilic ethyl carbanion of ethylmagnesium bromide attacks the carbonyl carbon of ethanal in dry ether to form a magnesium adduct: $$\text{CH}_3\text{CHO} + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{MgBr} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \text{CH}_3-\text{CH(OMgBr)}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$$
  2. Acid Hydrolysis: Hydrolysis of the adduct with dilute acid decomposes it to yield butan-2-ol: $$\text{CH}_3-\text{CH(OMgBr)}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{O} / \text{H}^+} \mathbf{\text{CH}_3-\text{CH(OH)}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 (\text{Butan-2-ol})} + \text{Mg(OH)Br}$$
