In-Text 9.1
NCERT In-Text
Classify the following amines as primary, secondary or tertiary:
(i) $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}-\text{NH}_2$
(ii) $(\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2)_2\text{NH}$
(iii) $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N}$
(iv) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCH}_3$
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Classification based on number of carbon groups attached to nitrogen:
  1. $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}-\text{NH}_2$ (Propan-2-amine): Primary ($1^\circ$) amine (nitrogen attached to one alkyl group; contains $-\text{NH}_2$ group).
  2. $(\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2)_2\text{NH}$ (Diethylamine): Secondary ($2^\circ$) amine (nitrogen attached to two ethyl groups; contains $-\text{NH}-$ group).
  3. $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N}$ (Trimethylamine): Tertiary ($3^\circ$) amine (nitrogen attached to three methyl groups; contains $-\text{N}<$ group).
  4. $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCH}_3$ (N-Methylaniline): Secondary ($2^\circ$) amine (nitrogen attached to a phenyl ring and a methyl group).

(ii) Write IUPAC names of all the isomers.
(iii) What type of isomerism is exhibited by different pairs of amines?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
All 8 Isomers of $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{11}\text{N}$:

Exhaustive Methylation of Aniline:

NCERT Exercise
Give one chemical test to distinguish between the following pairs of compounds:
(i) Methylamine and dimethylamine
(ii) Secondary and tertiary amines
(iii) Ethylamine and aniline
(iv) Aniline and benzylamine
(v) Aniline and N-methylaniline
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Chemical Tests with Observations:
  1. Methylamine ($1^\circ$) and Dimethylamine ($2^\circ$):
    • Carbylamine Test: Methylamine on heating with alcoholic $\text{KOH}$ and chloroform gives an extremely foul-smelling methyl isocyanide ($ ext{CH}_3 ext{NC}$). Dimethylamine ($2^\circ$) does not give this test.

    • Azo Dye Test: Aniline forms an orange azo dye with alkaline $\beta$-naphthol. Benzylamine (aliphatic) forms an unstable diazonium salt that decomposes with vigorous effervescence of $\text{N}_2$ gas without forming an azo dye.
  5. Aniline and N-Methylaniline:
    • Carbylamine Test: Aniline ($1^\circ$) gives a foul-smelling carbylamine (phenyl isocyanide). N-Methylaniline ($2^\circ$) does not respond.

Exercise 9.3
NCERT Exercise
Account for the following:
(i) $pK_b$ of aniline is more than that of methylamine.
(ii) Ethylamine is soluble in water whereas aniline is not.
(iii) Methylamine in water reacts with $\text{FeCl}_3$ to precipitate hydrated ferric oxide.
(iv) Although amino group is $o,p$-directing in aromatic electrophilic substitution reactions, aniline on nitration gives a substantial amount of $m$-nitroaniline.
(v) Aniline does not undergo Friedel-Crafts reaction.
(vi) Diazonium salts of aromatic amines are more stable than those of aliphatic amines.
(vii) Gabriel phthalimide synthesis is preferred for synthesising primary aliphatic amines.
[7 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Explanations:
  1. $pK_b$ of Aniline > Methylamine: Higher $pK_b$ means weaker base. In aniline, the lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized over the benzene ring via resonance (+R effect), reducing its availability for protonation. In methylamine, the $+I$ effect of $-\text{CH}_3$ increases electron density on nitrogen. Hence, aniline is a weaker base.
  2. Solubility: Ethylamine forms extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds with water molecules due to its small alkyl group. In aniline, the large bulky hydrophobic phenyl ring ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-$) strongly opposes hydration, making it insoluble.
  3. Reaction with $\text{FeCl}_3$: Methylamine is a base; it abstracts a proton from water to produce hydroxide ions: $$\text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{N}^+\text{H}_3 + \text{OH}^-$$ Hydroxide ions react with $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ to precipitate brown hydrated ferric oxide [$\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$ or $\text{Fe(OH)}_3$].
  4. Formation of $m$-Nitroaniline (47%): Nitration is carried out in strongly acidic medium ($ ext{HNO}_3 + ext{H}_2 ext{SO}_4$). Aniline undergoes protonation to form the anilinium ion ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}^+\text{H}_3$). The $-\text{N}^+\text{H}_3$ group has a powerful $-I$ effect and is strongly meta-directing.
  5. Aniline Resists Friedel-Crafts: Aniline is a Lewis base and reacts with the Lewis acid catalyst (anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$) to form an adduct [$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \rightarrow \text{AlCl}_3$]. This places a positive charge on nitrogen, deactivating the benzene ring completely towards electrophiles.
  6. Stability of Arenediazonium Salts: Stabilized by resonance delocalization of positive charge into the benzene ring. Alkanediazonium salts lack resonance and immediately eliminate $\text{N}_2$.
  7. Gabriel Phthalimide Preference: Produces exclusively pure primary aliphatic amines without any contamination by secondary, tertiary amines or quaternary salts.

NCERT Exercise
Give reasons: (i) Primary amines have higher boiling points than tertiary amines of comparable molecular mass. (ii) Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than ammonia.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Explanations:
  1. Boiling Points: $1^\circ$ vs $3^\circ$ Amines:
    Primary amines contain two hydrogen atoms attached to nitrogen ($-N ext{H}_2$). They form extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds ($- ext{N}- ext{H}\cdots\text{N}-$), causing strong molecular association.
    Tertiary amines have no hydrogen atom attached to nitrogen ($-NR_3$); they cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with each other. Consequently, they have much lower boiling points than isomeric primary amines.
  2. Aliphatic Amines Stronger Bases than Ammonia:
    Alkyl groups are electron-releasing due to their positive inductive ($+I$) effect. In aliphatic amines, the $+I$ effect of alkyl groups increases the electron density on the nitrogen atom, enhancing the availability of its lone pair for donation to a proton.
    Additionally, the resulting alkylammonium cation is stabilized by dispersal of the positive charge via $+I$ effect. Hence, aliphatic amines are stronger bases ($pK_b \approx 3-4$) than ammonia ($pK_b = 4.75$).

Write the structure of the Zwitterion of sulphanilic acid. Why does sulphanilic acid not react with sodium bicarbonate?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Zwitterion of Sulphanilic Acid:
Sulphanilic acid ($p$-aminobenzenesulphonic acid) contains both an acidic sulphonic group ($-\text{SO}_3\text{H}$) and a basic amino group ($-\text{NH}_2$) in the same molecule.
The acidic $-\text{SO}_3\text{H}$ transfers its proton to the basic $-\text{NH}_2$ group, forming an internal dipolar salt called a Zwitterion: $$p\text{-H}_2\text{N}-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4-\text{SO}_3\text{H} \rightleftharpoons \mathbf{p\text{-H}_3\text{N}^+-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4-\text{SO}_3^- (\text{Zwitterion})}$$
Why it does not react with $\text{NaHCO}_3$:
In the Zwitterion form, the proton is firmly held by the $-\text{N}^+\text{H}_3$ group and the sulphonate group is present as $-\text{SO}_3^-$. The weakly basic bicarbonate ion ($ ext{HCO}_3^-$) is not strong enough to abstract a proton from the $-\text{N}^+\text{H}_3$ group of the Zwitterion to release carbon dioxide. Hence, it does not give effervescence with $\text{NaHCO}_3$.

Exercise 9.28
NCERT Exercise
Explain why quaternary ammonium salts (such as $[\text{RN}^+(\text{CH}_3)_3]\text{OH}^-$) on heating undergo Hofmann elimination to give the less substituted alkene as the major product.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Hofmann Elimination Mechanism:
When a quaternary ammonium hydroxide is pyrolysed (heated), it undergoes an $E2$ elimination:
$$R-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{N}^+(\text{CH}_3)_3 \; ^-\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} R-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2 + \text{N(CH}_3)_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$ Unlike dehydrohalogenation of alkyl halides which follows Saytzeff's rule, quaternary ammonium elimination follows the Hofmann rule (least substituted alkene is major).

Reasons:
  1. Steric Crowding: The leaving group $-\text{N}^+(\text{CH}_3)_3$ is very bulky. The approaching base ($^-\text{OH}$) cannot easily access sterically hindered internal $eta$-hydrogens. It preferentially attacks the most accessible, unhindered terminal $eta$-hydrogen.
  2. Carbanion-like Transition State: Because the positively charged nitrogen is an extremely strong electron-withdrawing group ($-I$), the transition state has substantial carbanion character. Terminal hydrogens are more acidic than internal ones, stabilizing the transition state leading to the less substituted alkene.
