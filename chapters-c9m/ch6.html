Q1
Find the perimeter of: (i) A rectangle with l = 12 cm, b = 8 cm  (ii) An equilateral triangle with side 9 cm  (iii) A regular hexagon with side 5 cm
3 Marks

(ii) Equilateral triangle: P = 3 × 9 = 27 cm
(iii) Regular hexagon: P = 6 × 5 = 30 cm

Exercise Set 6.2 — Area of Triangles and Quadrilaterals
Q1
Find the area of a triangle with sides 5 cm, 12 cm, and 13 cm using Heron’s formula.
3 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
Figure 6.1: Triangle with Sides a, b, c & Area via Heron's Formula
Step 1: Semi-perimeter s = (5 + 12 + 13)/2 = 30/2 = 15 cm
Step 2: Heron’s formula
Area = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)] = √[15(15−5)(15−12)(15−13)]
= √[15 × 10 × 3 × 2] = √900 = 30 cm²
Suggested Marks Distribution

Q2
A rhombus has diagonals of lengths 16 cm and 12 cm. Find its area and perimeter.
4 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
Figure 6.2: Rhombus with Perpendicular Diagonals d₁ = 16 cm & d₂ = 12 cm
Area = (1/2) × d₁ × d₂ = (1/2) × 16 × 12 = 96 cm²
Side of rhombus: Half-diagonals are 8 and 6.
Side = √(8² + 6²) = √(64+36) = √100 = 10 cm
Perimeter = 4 × 10 = 40 cm

Exercise Set 6.3 — Areas of Composite Figures
Q1
A rectangular park 60 m × 40 m has a semicircular flowerbed of diameter 14 m at one end. Find the area of the park excluding the flowerbed. (π = 22/7)
4 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
Step 1: Rectangle area = 60 × 40 = 2400 m²
Step 2: Semicircle area (r = 7 m) = (1/2)πr² = (1/2) × (22/7) × 49 = 11 × 7 = 77 m²
Step 3: Park area (excluding flowerbed) = 2400 − 77 = 2323 m²

Q3
Find the area of a triangular park ABC with sides 120 m, 80 m, and 50 m using Heron's formula.
3 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
Semi-perimeter s = (a + b + c) / 2 = (120 + 80 + 50) / 2 = 250 / 2 = 125 m.
s − a = 125 − 120 = 5 m
s − b = 125 − 80 = 45 m
s − c = 125 − 50 = 75 m
Area = √[s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)] = √[125 × 5 × 45 × 75]
= √[(5³) × 5 × (3² × 5) × (3 × 5²)] = √[5⁷ × 3³] = 5³ × 3 × √(5 × 3) = 375√15 m² (≈ 1452.37 m²).

Q2
Find the area of a trapezium whose parallel sides are 25 cm and 13 cm, and the distance between them is 8 cm.
2 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
Figure 6.3: Trapezium Area = ½(a + b) × h (a = 25 cm, b = 13 cm, h = 8 cm)
Formula for area of trapezium = ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × height.
Area = ½ × (25 + 13) × 8 = ½ × 38 × 8 = 19 × 8 = 152 cm².

📝 Case Study & Real-Life Modeling
Agricultural Land Surveying (Heron's Formula): A farmer owns a triangular plot of land with sides a = 35 m, b = 53 m, and c = 66 m. The farmer wants to sow wheat which costs ₹12 per square meter for seeds and tilling.
(a) Calculate the semi-perimeter s of the triangular field.
(b) Calculate the total area using Heron's formula.
(c) Determine the total cost of cultivation.
(a) Semi-perimeter: s = (a + b + c)/2 = (35 + 53 + 66)/2 = 154/2 = 77 m.
(b) s - a = 77 - 35 = 42; s - b = 77 - 53 = 24; s - c = 77 - 66 = 11.
Area = √(77 × 42 × 24 × 11) = √(11 × 7 × 7 × 6 × 6 × 4 × 11) = 11 × 7 × 6 × 2 = 924 m².
(c) Cost of cultivation = 924 × ₹12 = ₹11,088.
🧠 HOTS & Assertion-Reasoning
Assertion (A): Among all triangles with a given fixed perimeter, the equilateral triangle encloses the maximum area.
Reason (R): In Heron's formula √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)], the product (s-a)(s-b)(s-c) is maximized when the factors are equal (AM = GM inequality).
Choose: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation. (b) Both A and R are true, but R is not correct explanation. (c) A is true, R is false. (d) A is false, R is true.
Correct Choice: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation.
Explanation: By the AM-GM inequality, the product of terms with constant sum is maximized when all terms are equal: s - a = s - b = s - c ⟹ a = b = c.
