Exercise Set 10.1 — Weighted Averages and Mixtures
Q1
A student scored 72 in Maths (out of 100), 68 in Science (out of 80), and 55 in English (out of 60). Find the weighted average percentage, where the weights are the total marks.
4 Marks
✓ CBSE Standard Answer
Total marks obtained = 72 + 68 + 55 = 195
Total possible marks = 100 + 80 + 60 = 240
Weighted average percentage = (195/240) × 100 = (195 × 100)/240 = 81.25%

This differs from the simple average: (72% + 85% + 91.67%)/3 ≈ 82.9% — illustrating why weights matter.

Q2
20 litres of 30% salt solution is mixed with 30 litres of 50% salt solution. Find the concentration (%) of salt in the mixture.
3 Marks
✓ CBSE Standard Answer
Salt in first solution = 30% of 20 = 6 litres
Salt in second solution = 50% of 30 = 15 litres
Concentration = (6+15)/(20+30) × 100 = 21/50 × 100 = 42%

Q1
The following data shows the number of books read per month by 5 students: 4, 7, 3, 9, 5. Find the mean, median, and mode.
3 Marks
✓ CBSE Standard Answer
Mean = (4+7+3+9+5)/5 = 28/5 = 5.6
Median: Arranged order: 3, 4, 5, 7, 9. Middle value = 5
Mode: All values appear once → No mode

Q2
What is a stacked bar chart? When is it more useful than a simple bar chart? Give one example scenario.
3 Marks
✓ CBSE Standard Answer
Stacked bar chart: A bar chart where each bar is divided into segments representing different sub-categories, stacked on top of each other.
More useful when: You want to compare both the total value and the composition (proportions of sub-categories) across multiple groups simultaneously.
Example: Monthly sales data for a shop selling fruits, vegetables, and dairy — a stacked bar shows both total sales and the contribution of each product category per month.

Exercise Set 10.3 — Interpreting Data
Q1
The marks scored by 8 students in a test are: 45, 67, 89, 34, 72, 67, 56, 89. Find the range and identify the outlier(s), if any.
3 Marks
✓ CBSE Standard Answer
Sorted data: 34, 45, 56, 67, 67, 72, 89, 89
Range = Maximum − Minimum = 89 − 34 = 55
Mean ≈ (34+45+56+67+67+72+89+89)/8 = 519/8 ≈ 64.9. The value 34 is significantly below the mean. It could be considered an outlier (isolated low score).

📝 Case Study & Real-Life Modeling
Environmental Science & Weighted Air Quality Index: An environmental research station records the particulate matter (PM2.5) concentrations at four different monitoring points with weights based on population exposure:
Point 1: 45 µg/m³ (weight 3), Point 2: 60 µg/m³ (weight 2), Point 3: 80 µg/m³ (weight 4), Point 4: 110 µg/m³ (weight 1).
(a) Find the simple arithmetic mean of the four readings.
(b) Calculate the weighted mean concentration of PM2.5.
(c) Why is the weighted mean a more reliable representation than the simple mean?
(a) Simple Mean = (45 + 60 + 80 + 110) / 4 = 295 / 4 = 73.75 µg/m³.
(b) Weighted Mean = Σ(w · x) / Σw = [3(45) + 2(60) + 4(80) + 1(110)] / (3 + 2 + 4 + 1) = [135 + 120 + 320 + 110] / 10 = 685 / 10 = 68.5 µg/m³.
(c) The weighted mean gives greater importance to heavily populated zones (Points 1 & 3), giving a truer measure of actual public health exposure.
🧠 HOTS & Assertion-Reasoning
Assertion (A): The median is a more robust measure of central tendency than the mean when a dataset contains extreme outliers.
Reason (R): The mean utilizes the numerical values of all observations, whereas the median depends only on the positional rank of observations.
Choose: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation. (b) Both A and R are true, but R is not correct explanation. (c) A is true, R is false. (d) A is false, R is true.
Correct Choice: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation.
Explanation: A single extreme value can shift the mean drastically, whereas the middle rank (median) remains stable. Reason (R) correctly explains this structural robustness.
