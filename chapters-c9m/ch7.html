Exercise 7.1 (Criteria for Congruence of Triangles)
Q1
In quadrilateral ACBD, AC = AD and AB bisects ∠A. Show that ΔABC ≅ ΔABD. What can you say about BC and BD?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: In quadrilateral ACBD:
• AC = AD
• Line segment AB bisects ∠A, which implies ∠CAB = ∠DAB.
To Prove: ΔABC ≅ ΔABD and deduce relation between BC and BD.
Proof:
In ΔABC and ΔABD:
1. AC = AD (Given)
2. ∠CAB = ∠DAB (Since AB bisects ∠A)
3. AB = AB (Common side)

Therefore, by SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Congruence Criterion:
ΔABC ≅ ΔABD. Hence Proved.
Relation between BC and BD:
Since corresponding parts of congruent triangles are equal (CPCT):
BC = BD.
Thus, BC and BD are of equal length.

Q3
AD and BC are equal perpendiculars to a line segment AB. Show that CD bisects AB.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given:
• AD ⊥ AB ⟹ ∠OAD = 90°
• BC ⊥ AB ⟹ ∠OBC = 90°
• AD = BC
• Let CD intersect AB at point O.
To Prove: CD bisects AB (i.e. OA = OB).
Proof:
In ΔOAD and ΔOBC:
1. ∠AOD = ∠BOC (Vertically opposite angles)
2. ∠OAD = ∠OBC = 90° (Given perpendiculars)
3. AD = BC (Given)

Therefore, by AAS (Angle-Angle-Side) Congruence Criterion:
ΔOAD ≅ ΔOBC.

By CPCT:
OA = OB.
Since O is the mid-point of AB, CD bisects AB. Hence Proved.

Q4
l and m are two parallel lines intersected by another pair of parallel lines p and q. Show that ΔABC ≅ ΔCDA.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: Lines l || m and lines p || q intersect at vertices A, B, C, D.
AC is the transversal line joining opposite vertices.
To Prove: ΔABC ≅ ΔCDA.
Proof:
1. Since l || m (i.e. AD || BC) and AC is transversal:
∠BCA = ∠DAC (Alternate interior angles)    --- (1)

2. Since p || q (i.e. AB || CD) and AC is transversal:
∠BAC = ∠DCA (Alternate interior angles)    --- (2)

3. In ΔABC and ΔCDA:
• ∠BAC = ∠DCA [from (2)]
• AC = CA (Common side)
• ∠BCA = ∠DAC [from (1)]

By ASA (Angle-Side-Angle) Congruence Rule:
ΔABC ≅ ΔCDA. Hence Proved.

Q8
In right triangle ABC, right angled at C, M is the mid-point of hypotenuse AB. C is joined to M and produced to a point D such that DM = CM. Point D is joined to point B. Show that:
(i) ΔAMC ≅ ΔBMD
(ii) ∠DBC is a right angle
(iii) ΔDBC ≅ ΔACB
(iv) CM = 1/2 AB
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given:
• In ΔABC, ∠ACB = 90°.
• M is mid-point of AB ⟹ AM = BM.
• DM = CM.
(i) To prove ΔAMC ≅ ΔBMD:
In ΔAMC and ΔBMD:
1. AM = BM (M is mid-point of AB)
2. ∠AMC = ∠BMD (Vertically opposite angles)
3. CM = DM (Given)
By SAS Congruence Rule: ΔAMC ≅ ΔBMD. Hence Proved (i).
(ii) To prove ∠DBC is a right angle:
From (i), ΔAMC ≅ ΔBMD, so by CPCT:
∠MAC = ∠MBD (or ∠CAB = ∠DBA).
Since alternate interior angles are equal, line segments DB || AC.
Now, DB || AC and BC is transversal. Consecutive interior angles on same side are supplementary:
∠DBC + ∠ACB = 180°.
Since ∠ACB = 90°:
∠DBC + 90° = 180° ⟹ ∠DBC = 90°. Hence Proved (ii).
(iii) To prove ΔDBC ≅ ΔACB:
In ΔDBC and ΔACB:
1. DB = AC (By CPCT from (i))
2. ∠DBC = ∠ACB = 90°
3. BC = CB (Common side)
By SAS Congruence Rule: ΔDBC ≅ ΔACB. Hence Proved (iii).
(iv) To prove CM = 1/2 AB:
From (iii), ΔDBC ≅ ΔACB, so by CPCT:
CD = AB.
Since M is mid-point of CD (DM = CM):
CD = 2CM.
Therefore, 2CM = AB ⟹ CM = 1/2 AB. Hence Proved (iv).

Q1
In an isosceles triangle ABC, with AB = AC, the bisectors of ∠B and ∠C intersect each other at O. Join A to O. Show that:
(i) OB = OC
(ii) AO bisects ∠A
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: In ΔABC, AB = AC. Rays BO and CO bisect ∠B and ∠C respectively, intersecting at O.
(i) To prove OB = OC:
Since AB = AC, angles opposite to equal sides are equal:
∠ABC = ∠ACB
Multiplying both sides by 1/2:
1/2 ∠ABC = 1/2 ∠ACB
Since BO and CO are angle bisectors:
∠OBC = ∠OCB.
In ΔOBC, since angles opposite to sides OB and OC are equal:
OB = OC (Sides opposite to equal angles of a triangle are equal). Hence Proved (i).
(ii) To prove AO bisects ∠A:
In ΔABO and ΔACO:
1. AB = AC (Given)
2. OB = OC [from (i)]
3. AO = AO (Common side)
By SSS Congruence Rule:
ΔABO ≅ ΔACO.
By CPCT:
∠BAO = ∠CAO.
Therefore, AO bisects ∠A. Hence Proved (ii).

Q6
ΔABC is an isosceles triangle in which AB = AC. Side BA is produced to D such that AD = AB. Show that ∠BCD is a right angle.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: In ΔABC, AB = AC. BA is produced to D such that AD = AB.
To Prove: ∠BCD = 90°.
Proof:
1. In ΔABC, AB = AC:
∠ACB = ∠ABC    --- (1)

2. Given AD = AB and AB = AC ⟹ AD = AC.
In ΔACD, since AC = AD:
∠ACD = ∠ADC    --- (2)

3. Adding (1) and (2):
∠ACB + ∠ACD = ∠ABC + ∠ADC
∠BCD = ∠ABC + ∠ADC    --- (3)

4. In large triangle ΔBCD, by Angle Sum Property:
∠BCD + ∠ABC + ∠ADC = 180°
Substituting ∠ABC + ∠ADC = ∠BCD from (3):
∠BCD + ∠BCD = 180°
2∠BCD = 180° ⟹ ∠BCD = 180° / 2 = 90°.
Hence, ∠BCD is a right angle. Hence Proved.

Q7

2 Marks

1. Since AB = AC, angles opposite to equal sides are equal:
∠B = ∠C

2. By angle sum property of ΔABC:
∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°
90° + ∠B + ∠B = 180°

∠B = 45°.

Since ∠C = ∠B, we also have ∠C = 45°.

Final Answer: ∠B = 45° and ∠C = 45°.

Q1
ΔABC and ΔDBC are two isosceles triangles on the same base BC and vertices A and D on the same side of BC. If AD is extended to intersect BC at P, show that:
(i) ΔABD ≅ ΔACD
(ii) ΔABP ≅ ΔACP
(iii) AP bisects ∠A as well as ∠D
(iv) AP is the perpendicular bisector of BC.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: ΔABC and ΔDBC are isosceles with base BC:
• AB = AC
• DB = DC
• AD is extended to meet BC at P.
(i) In ΔABD and ΔACD:
1. AB = AC (Given)
2. BD = CD (Given)
3. AD = AD (Common side)
By SSS Congruence Rule: ΔABD ≅ ΔACD. Hence Proved (i).
(ii) In ΔABP and ΔACP:
From (i), ΔABD ≅ ΔACD ⟹ ∠BAP = ∠CAP (by CPCT)  --- (1)
Now in ΔABP and ΔACP:
1. AB = AC (Given)
2. ∠BAP = ∠CAP [from (1)]
3. AP = AP (Common side)
By SAS Congruence Rule: ΔABP ≅ ΔACP. Hence Proved (ii).
(iii) AP bisects ∠A as well as ∠D:
• ∠BAP = ∠CAP proves AP bisects ∠A.
• In ΔBDP and ΔCDP:
BD = CD, DP = DP, and BP = CP (by CPCT from (ii)).
By SSS rule, ΔBDP ≅ ΔCDP ⟹ ∠BDP = ∠CDP (by CPCT).
Hence, AP bisects ∠D as well. Hence Proved (iii).
(iv) AP is the perpendicular bisector of BC:
From (ii), ΔABP ≅ ΔACP ⟹ BP = CP (P is mid-point of BC).
Also by CPCT, ∠APB = ∠APC.
Since BPC is a straight line, linear pair sum:
∠APB + ∠APC = 180° ⟹ 2∠APB = 180° ⟹ ∠APB = 90°.
Since AP ⊥ BC and BP = CP, AP is the perpendicular bisector of BC. Hence Proved (iv).

📝 Case Study Question
Bridge Support Triangulation: A railway bridge girder contains triangular frames. In frame ΔPQR, side PQ = PR = 10 m and QR = 12 m. A vertical brace PM is welded perpendicular to QR.
(a) By which congruence criterion is ΔPMQ ≅ ΔPMR?
(b) What is the length of QM?
(c) Find the height PM of the vertical brace.
(a) RHS Congruence Criterion (Hypotenuse PQ = PR = 10, common side PM, right angles at M).
(b) By CPCT, QM = MR = 1/2 QR = 12 / 2 = 6 m.
(c) In right ΔPMQ, by Pythagoras theorem: PM = √(PQ² - QM²) = √(10² - 6²) = √(100 - 36) = √64 = 8 m.

HOTS Problem: Show that of all line segments drawn from a given point not on it, to a given line, the perpendicular line segment is the shortest.
Let line be l and P be point not on l. Draw PM ⊥ l (M on l). Let N be any other point on l.
In right triangle ΔPMN, ∠M = 90°.
Since sum of angles is 180°, ∠N must be acute, so ∠M > ∠N.
In a triangle, the side opposite to the greater angle is longer: PN > PM (or PM < PN).
Hence, perpendicular PM is the shortest of all line segments from P to l.
