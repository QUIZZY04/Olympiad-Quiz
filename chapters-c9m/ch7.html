Exercise Set 7.1 — Basic Concepts of Probability
Q1
Define: (i) Sample space  (ii) Event  (iii) Probability of an event. Write the range of probability values.
4 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
Sample Space (S): The set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment. E.g., for a coin toss: S = {H, T}.
Event (E): Any subset of the sample space. E.g., getting a head: E = {H}.
Probability: P(E) = (Number of favourable outcomes) / (Total number of outcomes) = n(E)/n(S)
Range: 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1. P(impossible event) = 0; P(certain event) = 1.

Q2
A die is rolled once. Find the probability of getting: (i) An even number  (ii) A number greater than 4  (iii) A multiple of 3  (iv) The number 7
4 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution

Sample space S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}; n(S) = 6.

(i) Even numbers: {2, 4, 6}; P = 3/6 = 1/2
(ii) Greater than 4: {5, 6}; P = 2/6 = 1/3
(iii) Multiple of 3: {3, 6}; P = 2/6 = 1/3
(iv) Number 7: Impossible (7 not on die); P = 0

Exercise Set 7.2 — Experimental and Theoretical Probability
Q1
A bag contains 3 red, 5 blue, and 2 green balls. A ball is drawn at random. Find the probability of drawing: (i) A red ball  (ii) A blue ball  (iii) Not a green ball
3 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution

Total balls = 3 + 5 + 2 = 10.

(i) Red: P = 3/10 = 0.3
(ii) Blue: P = 5/10 = 1/2
(iii) Not green: Balls that are not green = 3 + 5 = 8. P = 8/10 = 4/5

Q3
What is the sum of probabilities of all elementary events of an experiment? If P(E) = 0.05, what is the probability of 'not E'?
2 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
The sum of the probabilities of all elementary events of an experiment is always equal to 1.
We know that P(E) + P(not E) = 1.
Therefore, P(not E) = 1 − 0.05 = 0.95.

📝 Case Study & Real-Life Modeling
Quality Control in Electronics (Probability): A batch of 500 LED computer monitors was tested at a manufacturing plant. 465 passed all quality checks on the first attempt, 25 had minor display defects that could be repaired, and 10 were completely discarded.
(a) What is the probability that a randomly chosen monitor passes without defect?
(b) What is the probability that a monitor requires repair?
(c) Verify that the sum of probabilities of all elementary events equals 1.
(a) P(Pass) = 465 / 500 = 93/100 = 0.93.
(b) P(Repair) = 25 / 500 = 1/20 = 0.05.
(c) P(Discard) = 10 / 500 = 0.02. Sum = 0.93 + 0.05 + 0.02 = 1.00. This verifies the fundamental probability law.
🧠 HOTS & Assertion-Reasoning
Assertion (A): If the probability of winning a chess game is 0.68, then the probability of not winning is 0.32.
Reason (R): For any event E, P(E) + P(not E) = 1.
Choose: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation. (b) Both A and R are true, but R is not correct explanation. (c) A is true, R is false. (d) A is false, R is true.
Correct Choice: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation.
Explanation: The complementary event of winning is 'not winning'. By rule P(not E) = 1 - P(E) = 1 - 0.68 = 0.32, which directly validates both statement and reason.
