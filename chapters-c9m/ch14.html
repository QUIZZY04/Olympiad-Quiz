Exercise Set 14.1 — Surface Area of Cuboids and Cubes
Q1
Find the total surface area and lateral surface area of a cuboid with length 12 cm, breadth 8 cm, and height 5 cm.
3 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution

Given: l = 12 cm, b = 8 cm, h = 5 cm

Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2(lb + bh + hl)
= 2(12×8 + 8×5 + 5×12) = 2(96 + 40 + 60) = 2 × 196 = 392 cm²
Lateral Surface Area (LSA) = 2(l + b) × h
= 2(12 + 8) × 5 = 2 × 20 × 5 = 200 cm²
Suggested Marks Distribution
Correct formula for TSA½ Mark

LSA = 200 cm²1½ Marks

A room is 10 m long, 7 m wide, and 4 m high. Find the cost of whitewashing the four walls and ceiling at ₹12 per m². (Ignore doors and windows.)

Exercise Set 14.2 — Surface Area of Cylinders and Cones
Q1
Find the curved surface area and total surface area of a cylinder with radius 7 cm and height 20 cm. (Use π = 22/7)
3 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
CSA = 2πrh = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × 20 = 2 × 22 × 20 = 880 cm²
TSA = 2πr(r + h) = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × (7 + 20) = 44 × 27 = 1188 cm²

Exercise Set 14.4 — Mixed Applications and Word Problems
Q1
A metallic sphere of radius 4.2 cm is melted and recast into a cylinder of radius 6 cm. Find the height of the cylinder. (π = 22/7)
4 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
Volume of sphere = (4/3)πr³ = (4/3) × (22/7) × (4.2)³
= (4/3) × (22/7) × 74.088 = (4 × 22 × 74.088)/(3 × 7) = 6511.75/21 ≈ 310.27 cm³
Volume of cylinder = πR²H = (22/7) × 36 × H
Equating: (22/7) × 36 × H = (4/3) × (22/7) × (4.2)³
36H = (4/3) × (4.2)³ = (4 × 74.088)/3 = 98.784
H = 98.784/36 = 2.744 cm ≈ 2.74 cm

📝 Case Study & Real-Life Modeling
Sustainable Packaging Optimization (Surface Area & Volume): A juice manufacturer needs to pack 1 litre (1,000 cm³) of fruit juice. They are comparing two options: Option A is a cuboidal tetra-pack of base 10 cm × 5 cm and height 20 cm. Option B is a cylindrical tin can of radius r = 5 cm and height h = 12.73 cm.
(a) Verify that both containers have approximately 1,000 cm³ volume.
(b) Calculate the total surface area (packaging material required) for Option A.
(c) Calculate the total surface area for Option B and determine which packaging is more eco-friendly (uses less material).
(a) Volume A = 10 × 5 × 20 = 1,000 cm³. Volume B = πr²h = (22/7) × 25 × 12.73 ≈ 1,000 cm³.
(b) Surface Area A = 2(lb + bh + hl) = 2(10×5 + 5×20 + 20×10) = 2(50 + 100 + 200) = 2(350) = 700 cm².
(c) Surface Area B = 2πr(r + h) = 2 × (22/7) × 5 × (5 + 12.73) = (220/7) × 17.73 ≈ 557.2 cm².
Conclusion: The cylindrical can requires 142.8 cm² less material (≈ 20.4% less), making Option B significantly more eco-friendly and resource-efficient.
🧠 HOTS & Assertion-Reasoning
Assertion (A): When all linear dimensions of a cube are tripled, its surface area increases 9-fold, while its volume increases 27-fold.
Reason (R): Surface area scales proportionally with the square of the linear scale factor (k²), while volume scales with the cube of the linear factor (k³).
Choose: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation. (b) Both A and R are true, but R is not correct explanation. (c) A is true, R is false. (d) A is false, R is true.
Correct Choice: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation.
Explanation: For scaling factor k = 3: Area' = 6(3a)² = 9(6a²) = 9 × Area. Volume' = (3a)³ = 27a³ = 27 × Volume. The geometric square-cube scaling law perfectly explains this.
