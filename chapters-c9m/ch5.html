Chapter 5: I’m Up and Down, and Round and Round

Ganita Manjari (Part I) — Exercise Sets 5.1, 5.2 & 5.3 — Suggested Marks Distribution

About this chapter: This chapter explores oscillatory and circular motion through mathematics — including sinusoidal behaviour, angles, and periodic patterns. It connects geometry with real-world motion.

Exercise Set 5.1 — Angles and Circular Motion
Q1
Convert the following angles from degrees to radians: (i) 90°  (ii) 180°  (iii) 270°  (iv) 360°
2 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution

Formula: Radians = Degrees × π/180

(i) 90° = 90 × π/180 = π/2 radians
(ii) 180° = 180 × π/180 = π radians
(iii) 270° = 270 × π/180 = 3π/2 radians
(iv) 360° = 360 × π/180 = 2π radians
Suggested Marks Distribution
Correct formula stated½ Mark
All four correct conversions1½ Marks
Q2
A wheel rotates 5 times per second. Find the angle swept per second in radians and the arc length swept by a point 30 cm from the centre.
4 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
Figure 5.1: Circular Rotation, Angles in Radians & Arc Length s = rθ
Step 1: Angle per second
One full rotation = 2π radians.
5 rotations per second → Angle = 5 × 2π = 10π radians/second
Step 2: Arc length
Arc length = radius × angle = 30 × 10π = 300π cm/second

≈ 942.5 cm/second

Suggested Marks Distribution
Angle = 10π radians/s2 Marks
Arc length = 300π cm/s2 Marks
Exercise Set 5.2 — Periodic and Oscillatory Patterns
Q1
The height (in cm) of a child on a swing is given by h(t) = 80 + 30 sin(t), where t is time in seconds. Find the maximum and minimum heights and the height at t = 0.
3 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
Figure 5.2: Periodic Height Function h(t) = 80 + 30 sin(t) (Amplitude = 30 cm)
Maximum: sin(t) reaches max value 1. h_max = 80 + 30(1) = 110 cm
Minimum: sin(t) reaches min value −1. h_min = 80 + 30(−1) = 50 cm
At t = 0: sin(0) = 0. h(0) = 80 + 30(0) = 80 cm
Suggested Marks Distribution
Maximum height 110 cm1 Mark
Minimum height 50 cm1 Mark
Height at t=0 is 80 cm1 Mark
Q2
What is a periodic function? Give two real-life examples and state one mathematical example.
3 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution

Periodic function: A function f(x) is periodic if there exists a positive number T such that f(x + T) = f(x) for all x. T is called the period.

Real-life examples:
(1) Motion of a pendulum — it swings back and forth with equal time periods.
(2) Heart beats — the pattern of contraction and relaxation repeats regularly.
Mathematical example: f(x) = sin(x) has period 2π; sin(x + 2π) = sin(x).
Suggested Marks Distribution
Definition with T stated1 Mark
Two real-life examples1 Mark
Mathematical example (sin x)1 Mark
Exercise Set 5.3 — Applications
Q1
A Ferris wheel of radius 20 m completes one revolution in 40 seconds. Find: (i) Angular speed in radians per second (ii) Speed of a passenger on the rim.
4 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
(i) Angular speed: ω = 2π/T = 2π/40 = π/20 radians per second
(ii) Linear speed: v = rω = 20 × π/20 = π metres per second ≈ 3.14 m/s
Suggested Marks Distribution
Angular speed = π/20 rad/s2 Marks
Linear speed = π m/s2 Marks
Q3
Find the angle in degrees swept by the minute hand of a clock in 25 minutes.
2 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
A minute hand completes 360° in 60 minutes.
Rate of rotation = 360° / 60 = 6° per minute.
Angle swept in 25 minutes = 25 × 6° = 150°.
Suggested Marks Distribution
Rate per minute (6 degrees/min)1 Mark
Total angle calculation = 150 degrees1 Mark
Q3
The period of oscillation of a simple pendulum is T = 2 seconds. How many complete oscillations does it make in 5 minutes?
2 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
Total time t = 5 minutes = 5 × 60 = 300 seconds.
Time period for 1 oscillation T = 2 s.
Number of oscillations = Total time / T = 300 / 2 = 150 complete oscillations.
Suggested Marks Distribution
Time conversion to seconds1 Mark
Calculating 150 oscillations1 Mark
Q2
A bicycle wheel has diameter 70 cm. How many revolutions will it make to travel a distance of 1.1 km?
2 Marks
✓ Suggested Step-by-Step Solution
Radius r = 70 / 2 = 35 cm = 0.35 m.
Circumference C = 2πr = 2 × (22/7) × 35 cm = 220 cm = 2.2 m.
Total distance = 1.1 km = 1,100 m.
Number of revolutions = 1,100 / 2.2 = 500 revolutions.
Suggested Marks Distribution
Circumference = 2.2 m1 Mark
Revolutions = 5001 Mark

📝 Case Study & Real-Life Modeling
Clockwork Mechanisms & Rotational Angles: In an antique clock tower, the minute hand has length 14 cm and the hour hand has length 10.5 cm.
(a) Find the angle swept by the minute hand between 3:00 PM and 3:25 PM.
(b) What angle does the hour hand rotate through in 20 minutes?
(c) At 3:00 PM, what is the measure of the angle between the two hands?
(a) In 60 minutes, the minute hand covers 360°. In 25 minutes: θ = (360°/60) × 25 = 6° × 25 = 150°.
(b) The hour hand rotates 360° in 12 hours (720 minutes), which is 0.5° per minute. In 20 minutes: (0.5°) × 20 = 10°.
(c) At 3:00 PM, the hour hand points at 3 (90°) and the minute hand points at 12 (0°). The angle between them is 90° (right angle).
🧠 HOTS & Assertion-Reasoning
Assertion (A): A pendulum bob that oscillates through an angle of 30° on either side of the vertical sweeps a total angular span of 60° during each swing.
Reason (R): The total angular range is the sum of displacements from the central equilibrium position to both extreme positions.
Choose: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation. (b) Both A and R are true, but R is not correct explanation. (c) A is true, R is false. (d) A is false, R is true.
Correct Choice: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation.
Explanation: From -30° to +30° the angular displacement is 30° + 30° = 60°, which matches the physical span from one amplitude to the other.
