Exercise 5.1
Q1
Which of the following statements are true and which are false? Give reasons for your answers:
(i) Only one line can pass through a single point.
(ii) There are an infinite number of lines which pass through two distinct points.
(iii) A terminated line can be produced indefinitely on both sides.
(iv) If two circles are equal, then their radii are equal.
(v) In figure, if AB = PQ and PQ = XY, then AB = XY.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) Only one line can pass through a single point.
State: False.
Reason: Through a single given point, infinitely many lines can be drawn passing through it in all possible directions. (Euclid's basic postulate observation).
(ii) There are an infinite number of lines which pass through two distinct points.
State: False.
Reason: According to Euclid's Axiom 5.1 (or Postulate 1): Given two distinct points, there is one and only one unique line that passes through them.
(iii) A terminated line can be produced indefinitely on both sides.
State: True.
Reason: This is precisely Euclid's Postulate 2: A terminated line (what we call a line segment today) can be produced indefinitely on either side to form a continuous line.
(iv) If two circles are equal, then their radii are equal.
State: True.
Reason: Two circles are equal if and only if their boundaries coincide when superimposed on each other (Euclid's Axiom 4: Things which coincide with one another are equal to one another). Hence their centres and boundaries coincide, meaning their radii must be identical.
(v) If AB = PQ and PQ = XY, then AB = XY.
State: True.
Reason: By Euclid's Axiom 1: Things which are equal to the same thing are equal to one another. Since both AB and XY are equal to the same segment PQ, AB = XY.

Q2
Give a definition for each of the following terms. Are there other terms that need to be defined first? What are they, and how might you define them?
(i) Parallel lines
(ii) Perpendicular lines
(iii) Line segment
(iv) Radius of a circle
(v) Square
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Prerequisite undefined/defined terms: Point, Line, Plane, Ray, Angle, Distance, Circle. Euclid took Point, Line, and Plane as primitive undefined notions.

(i) Parallel lines:
Two coplanar straight lines which do not intersect each other however far they are extended in both directions are called parallel lines. The perpendicular distance between them remains constant throughout.
Prerequisite terms: Lines, Plane, Intersection, Distance.
(ii) Perpendicular lines:
Two intersecting straight lines that form a right angle (an angle of 90°) at their point of intersection are called perpendicular lines.
Prerequisite terms: Line, Point of intersection, Right angle.
(iii) Line segment:
A straight path terminating at two definite end points A and B, consisting of all points on the line lying between A and B, is called a line segment.
Prerequisite terms: Point, Line, Betweenness.
(iv) Radius of a circle:
The fixed distance from the centre of a circle to any point lying on the circumference of the circle (or the line segment joining the centre to any point on the boundary).
Prerequisite terms: Circle, Centre, Distance, Point.
(v) Square:
A quadrilateral in which all four sides are equal in length and all four interior angles are right angles (90°).
Prerequisite terms: Quadrilateral, Side, Length, Right angle.

Q3
Consider two 'postulates' given below:
(i) Given any two distinct points A and B, there exists a third point C which is in between A and B.
(ii) There exist at least three points that are not on the same line.
Do these postulates contain any undefined terms? Are these postulates consistent? Do they follow from Euclid's postulates? Explain.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Undefined terms:
Yes, these postulates contain several undefined terms such as 'point', 'line', and the relational concept of 'between' (a point lying between two other points).
2. Consistency:
Yes, the system of these two statements is consistent because they deal with two completely different non-contradictory geometric situations:
• Statement (i) asserts that a line segment contains infinitely many points between any two of its points (property of density/continuity of a line).
• Statement (ii) asserts that not all points in the universe lie on a single straight line (points determine a two-dimensional plane).
Neither statement contradicts the other, nor does either contradict any established geometric fact.
3. Do they follow from Euclid's postulates?
No, they do not directly follow from Euclid's postulates. Rather, they follow from Euclid's axioms and the modern formal axioms of Euclidean plane geometry established by David Hilbert.

Q4
If a point C lies between two points A and B such that AC = BC, then prove that AC = 1/2 AB. Explain by drawing the figure.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: Point C lies on line segment AB such that AC = BC.
To Prove: AC = 1/2 AB.
Figure Description:
Draw a line segment AB with endpoints A and B. Mark point C between A and B such that segment AC equals segment BC.
A ─────── C ─────── B
Proof:
Given: AC = BC    --- (1)

Adding AC to both sides of equation (1):
AC + AC = BC + AC    --- (2)
(Reason: Euclid's Axiom 2 — If equals are added to equals, the wholes are equal.)

Now, from the figure, AC + BC coincides with line segment AB.
By Euclid's Axiom 4 (Things which coincide with one another are equal to one another):
BC + AC = AB

Substituting this into (2):
2AC = AB
Dividing by 2 (or by Euclid's Axiom 7 — Things which are halves of the same things are equal to one another):
AC = 1/2 AB. Hence Proved.

Q5
In Question 4, point C is called a mid-point of line segment AB. Prove that every line segment has one and only one mid-point.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
To Prove: A line segment has a unique mid-point.
Proof by Contradiction:
Let if possible, line segment AB have two distinct mid-points, say C and D.
Since C is a mid-point of AB:
AC = 1/2 AB    --- (1)  [from Q4]

Since D is also a mid-point of AB:
AD = 1/2 AB    --- (2)  [from Q4]

From equations (1) and (2):
Both AC and AD are equal to the same quantity 1/2 AB.
By Euclid's Axiom 1 (Things which are equal to the same thing are equal to one another):
AC = AD

This equality is only possible if point C and point D coincide with each other.
Hence, our assumption that C and D are distinct mid-points is false.

Conclusion: Every line segment has one and only one mid-point.

Q6
In figure, if AC = BD, then prove that AB = CD.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: Points A, B, C, D lie in order on a straight line, and AC = BD.
To Prove: AB = CD.
A ─────── B ─────── C ─────── D
Proof:
From the figure, point B lies between A and C:
AC = AB + BC    --- (1)

Also, point C lies between B and D:
BD = BC + CD    --- (2)

Given that AC = BD, we substitute from (1) and (2):
AB + BC = BC + CD

Subtracting BC from both sides:
By Euclid's Axiom 3 (If equals are subtracted from equals, the remainders are equal):
(AB + BC) - BC = (BC + CD) - BC
AB = CD. Hence Proved.

Q7
Why is Axiom 5, in the list of Euclid's axioms, considered a 'universal truth'? (Note that the question is not about the fifth postulate).
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Euclid's Axiom 5:
"The whole is greater than the part."
Reason why it is a Universal Truth:
1. An axiom is a fundamental assumption used throughout mathematics (not just geometry) that is self-evident and does not require a formal proof.
2. By definition, if a quantity B is a non-empty, proper part of a whole quantity A, there exists some positive remainder C > 0 such that A = B + C.
3. Since C > 0, it mathematically follows that A > B.
4. This principle holds universally across all domains of science, physical nature, arithmetic, and daily human experience — a piece of bread is smaller than the loaf, a state is smaller than the country, a subset is smaller than the proper universal set. Hence, it is regarded as a universal truth.

Exercise 5.2
Q1
How would you rewrite Euclid's fifth postulate so that it would be easier to understand?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Original Statement of Euclid's Fifth Postulate: If a straight line falling on two straight lines makes the interior angles on the same side of it taken together less than two right angles, then the two straight lines, if produced indefinitely, meet on that side on which the sum of angles is less than two right angles.

Simpler and Equivalent Form (Playfair's Axiom):
"For every line l and for every point P not lying on l, there exists a unique line m passing through P and parallel to l."

Alternative Everyday Language:
If two straight lines are intersected by a third transversal line such that the sum of the interior angles on one side is strictly less than 180°, the two lines will definitely intersect each other if extended on that side. If the sum of interior angles on one side equals 180°, the lines will never intersect (they are parallel).

Q2
Does Euclid's fifth postulate imply the existence of parallel lines? Explain.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Yes, Euclid's fifth postulate directly implies the existence of parallel lines.

Detailed Explanation:
Let straight line n fall on two straight lines l and m such that the sum of the interior angles on one side of n is denoted by ∠1 + ∠2, and on the other side by ∠3 + ∠4.
Since the sum of all four interior angles on both sides is 360° (two straight angles):
(∠1 + ∠2) + (∠3 + ∠4) = 360°
Case Analysis:
• If ∠1 + ∠2 < 180°, then by the 5th Postulate, lines l and m intersect on that side.
• But if ∠1 + ∠2 = 180° (equal to two right angles), then:
∠3 + ∠4 = 360° - 180° = 180°.
• Therefore, the sum of interior angles is not less than 180° on either side of the transversal line n.
• Consequently, by Euclid's fifth postulate, the lines l and m will never meet or intersect on either side, no matter how far they are produced indefinitely.

Conclusion: Lines that never meet are parallel lines. Thus, Euclid's fifth postulate establishes that parallel lines exist whenever the sum of interior angles on one side of a transversal equals 180°.

📝 Case Study Question
Axiomatic Systems in Architecture: A civil engineer lays out two parallel railway tracks crossed by an access road at angle 90°.
(a) Which of Euclid's axioms guarantees that if track 1 is straight and track 2 is straight, their parallel distance is maintained?
(b) If segment AB on track 1 has length 50 m and segment CD on track 2 has length 50 m, which axiom implies they have identical span?
(c) State Euclid's Postulate 4.
(a) Playfair's equivalent of Euclid's 5th Postulate (unique parallel line through a point).
(b) Euclid's Axiom 1 (Things which are equal to the same thing are equal to one another).
(c) Postulate 4: 'All right angles are equal to one another.'
⚡ Assertion & Reason
Assertion (A): Two distinct intersecting lines cannot both be parallel to the same line.
Reason (R): Through a given point not on a line, only one line can be drawn parallel to the given line (Playfair's axiom).
Choose: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A. (b) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A. (c) A is true, R is false. (d) A is false, R is true.
Answer: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
Explanation: If two intersecting lines were both parallel to the same line, then through their common point of intersection there would be two lines parallel to the same line, which directly violates Playfair's axiom (R). Thus R is the precise logical reason for A.
