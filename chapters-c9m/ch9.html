Exercise 9.1 (Chords & Congruent Circles)
Q1
Recall that two circles are congruent if they have the same radii. Prove that equal chords of congruent circles subtend equal angles at their centres.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: Two congruent circles C(O, r) and C'(O', r) with equal chords AB = CD.
To Prove: ∠AOB = ∠CO'D.
Proof:
Join OA, OB and O'C, O'D.
In ΔAOB and ΔCO'D:
1. OA = O'C (Radii of congruent circles = r)
2. OB = O'D (Radii of congruent circles = r)
3. AB = CD (Given equal chords)

By SSS Congruence Rule:
ΔAOB ≅ ΔCO'D.

By CPCT (Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles):
∠AOB = ∠CO'D. Hence Proved.

Exercise 9.2 (Chords & Distance from Centre)
Q1
Two circles of radii 5 cm and 3 cm intersect at two points and the distance between their centres is 4 cm. Find the length of the common chord.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given:
• Circle 1 with centre O and radius r₁ = 5 cm.
• Circle 2 with centre O' and radius r₂ = 3 cm.
• Distance between centres OO' = 4 cm.
• Let common chord be AB.
Pythagorean Triple Observation:
In ΔOAO':
OA = 5 cm, O'A = 3 cm, OO' = 4 cm.
Notice that: 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5².
O'A² + OO'² = OA².
By Converse of Pythagoras Theorem, ΔOAO' is a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse OA, right-angled at O':
∠AO'O = 90°.
Length of Common Chord:
Since ∠AO'O = 90°, the common chord AB is perpendicular to the line of centres OO' passing through the centre O' of the smaller circle.
Therefore, O' is the mid-point of chord AB, and O'A is the perpendicular distance.
Radius of smaller circle is O'A = 3 cm.
Total length of common chord AB = 2 × O'A = 2 × 3 = 6 cm.

Final Answer: The length of the common chord is 6 cm.

Q2
If two equal chords of a circle intersect within the circle, prove that the segments of one chord are equal to corresponding segments of the other chord.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: A circle with centre O and two equal chords AB and CD (AB = CD) intersecting at point X within the circle.
To Prove: AX = CX and XB = XD (or AX = XD and XB = XC).
Construction:
Draw perpendiculars from centre O to chords: OL ⊥ AB and OM ⊥ CD. Join OX.
Proof:
1. Perpendicular from centre bisects the chord:
AL = LB = 1/2 AB and CM = MD = 1/2 CD.
Since AB = CD ⟹ AL = CM and LB = MD  --- (1)

2. Equal chords are equidistant from the centre: OL = OM.

3. In right-angled triangles ΔOLX and ΔOMX:
• ∠OLX = ∠OMX = 90°
• Hypotenuse OX = OX (Common)
• OL = OM (Equal chords equidistant from centre)
By RHS Congruence Rule: ΔOLX ≅ ΔOMX.
By CPCT: LX = MX  --- (2)

4. Adding (1) and (2):
AL + LX = CM + MX ⟹ AX = CX.

5. Subtracting from equal chords (AB = CD):
AB - AX = CD - CX ⟹ XB = XD. Hence Proved.

Q4
If a line intersects two concentric circles (circles with the same centre) with centre O at A, B, C and D, prove that AB = CD.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: Two concentric circles with common centre O. A secant line intersects outer circle at A and D, and inner circle at B and C.
To Prove: AB = CD.
Construction: Draw perpendicular OM ⊥ AD from centre O to the line.
Proof:
1. AD is a chord of the outer circle. The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord:
AM = MD    --- (1)

2. BC is a chord of the inner circle. The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord:
BM = MC    --- (2)

3. Subtracting equation (2) from equation (1):
AM - BM = MD - MC
From the figure:
AM - BM = AB and MD - MC = CD.

Therefore, AB = CD. Hence Proved.

Q5
Three girls Reshma, Salma and Mandip are playing a game by standing on a circle of radius 5 m drawn in a park. Reshma throws a ball to Salma, Salma to Mandip, Mandip to Reshma. If the distance between Reshma and Salma and between Salma and Mandip is 6 m each, what is the distance between Reshma and Mandip?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given:
• Circle with centre O and radius R = 5 m.
• Reshma (R), Salma (S), Mandip (M) stand on circle, so OR = OS = OM = 5 m.
• Chords RS = SM = 6 m.
• To find length of chord RM.
Step 1: Geometry & Symmetry:
Join OS, intersecting RM at K. In kite ORSM, since OR = OM = 5 and RS = SM = 6, the diagonal OS is the perpendicular bisector of chord RM.
Therefore, OK ⊥ RM and RK = KM ⟹ RM = 2 RK.
Step 2: Area of ΔORS:
In isosceles ΔORS with sides OR = OS = 5 m and RS = 6 m:
Draw altitude OL from O to RS. Since OL bisects RS: RL = 6/2 = 3 m.
In right ΔOLR: OL = √(OR² - RL²) = √(5² - 3²) = √(25 - 9) = √16 = 4 m.
Area of ΔORS = 1/2 × Base × Height = 1/2 × RS × OL = 1/2 × 6 × 4 = 12 m².
Step 3: Equate Area using Base OS:
Taking OS as base, altitude is RK (since RK ⊥ OS):
Area of ΔORS = 1/2 × OS × RK
12 = 1/2 × 5 × RK ⟹ 5 × RK = 24 ⟹ RK = 24/5 = 4.8 m.

Total distance between Reshma and Mandip:
RM = 2 × RK = 2 × 4.8 = 9.6 m.

Final Answer: The distance between Reshma and Mandip is 9.6 m.

Q6
A circular park of radius 20 m is situated in a colony. Three boys Ankur, Syed and David are sitting at equal distance on its boundary each having a toy telephone in his hands to talk each other. Find the length of the string of each phone.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given:
• Circle with centre O and radius R = 20 m.
• Ankur (A), Syed (S), David (D) are at equal distances, so ΔASD is an equilateral triangle inscribed in the circle.
• Let the side length of equilateral triangle ΔASD be 2x meters (so SD = 2x).
Properties of Equilateral Triangle & Circumcentre:
In an equilateral triangle, the circumcentre O coincides with the centroid, orthocentre, and in-centre.
Draw median/altitude AM from A to SD. M is the mid-point of SD, so SM = MD = x.
Since O is the centroid, it divides median AM in ratio 2 : 1:
AO / OM = 2 / 1 ⟹ OM = 1/2 AO.
Given circumradius AO = 20 m:
OM = 20 / 2 = 10 m.
Total median length AM = AO + OM = 20 + 10 = 30 m.
In right triangle ΔAMD:
By Pythagoras theorem:
AD² = AM² + MD²
(2x)² = 30² + x²
4x² = 900 + x²
3x² = 900 ⟹ x² = 300 ⟹ x = √300 = 10√3 m.

Side length of string = 2x = 2 × 10√3 = 20√3 m.

Final Answer: The length of the string of each phone is 20√3 ≈ 34.64 m.

Q1
In the figure, A, B and C are three points on a circle with centre O such that ∠BOC = 30° and ∠AOB = 60°. If D is a point on the circle other than the arc ABC, find ∠ADC.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Step 1: Total angle subtended by arc ABC at centre O:
∠AOC = ∠AOB + ∠BOC = 60° + 30° = 90°.
Step 2: Angle subtended at circumference:
Theorem: The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is double the angle subtended by it at any point on the remaining part of the circle.
∠AOC = 2 ∠ADC
90° = 2 ∠ADC ⟹ ∠ADC = 90° / 2 = 45°.

Final Answer: ∠ADC = 45°.

Q2
A chord of a circle is equal to the radius of the circle. Find the angle subtended by the chord at a point on the minor arc and also at a point on the major arc.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: In circle with centre O, chord AB = OA = OB = r.
Step 1: Central Angle:
In ΔOAB, all three sides are equal (OA = OB = AB = r).
Therefore, ΔOAB is an equilateral triangle:
∠AOB = 60°.
Step 2: Angle in the Major Arc:
Let C be any point on the major arc. By the arc angle theorem:
∠ACB = 1/2 ∠AOB = 1/2 × 60° = 30°.
Angle subtended at major arc = 30°.
Step 3: Angle in the Minor Arc:
Let D be any point on the minor arc. Quadrilateral ACBD is a cyclic quadrilateral.
The opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary:
∠ACB + ∠ADB = 180°
30° + ∠ADB = 180° ⟹ ∠ADB = 180° - 30° = 150°.
Angle subtended at minor arc = 150°.

In the figure, ∠PQR = 100°, where P, Q and R are points on a circle with centre O. Find ∠OPR.

✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Chord PR divides circle into major and minor arcs. Point Q lies on the circle with ∠PQR = 100°.
The reflex angle subtended by major arc PR at centre O is double the angle subtended at Q:
Reflex ∠POR = 2 × ∠PQR = 2 × 100° = 200°.
Step 2: Interior ∠POR in ΔOPR:
∠POR = 360° - Reflex ∠POR = 360° - 200° = 160°.
Step 3: Find ∠OPR:
In ΔOPR, sides OP = OR (radii of the same circle).

By angle sum property in ΔOPR:
∠POR + ∠OPR + ∠ORP = 180°
160° + 2∠OPR = 180°
2∠OPR = 180° - 160° = 20° ⟹ ∠OPR = 10°.

Final Answer: ∠OPR = 10°.

If diagonals of a cyclic quadrilateral are diameters of the circle through the vertices of the quadrilateral, prove that it is a rectangle.

Proof:

2. BD is also a diameter.

3. All four interior angles of quadrilateral ABCD are 90°:
∠A = ∠B = ∠C = ∠D = 90°.

4. Opposite angles are equal (90° = 90°), so ABCD is a parallelogram. A parallelogram with right angles is a rectangle.
Hence, ABCD is a rectangle. Hence Proved.

Q8
If the non-parallel sides of a trapezium are equal, prove that it is cyclic.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: Trapezium ABCD with AB || CD and non-parallel sides AD = BC.
To Prove: ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral (i.e. ∠A + ∠C = 180° or ∠B + ∠D = 180°).
Construction: Draw perpendiculars AM ⊥ CD and BN ⊥ CD.
Proof:
1. Since AB || CD, the perpendicular distance between two parallel lines is constant throughout:
AM = BN.

2. In right-angled triangles ΔAMD and ΔBNC:
• ∠AMD = ∠BNC = 90°
• Hypotenuse AD = Hypotenuse BC (Given)
• Side AM = BN (Distance between parallel lines)
By RHS Congruence Rule:
ΔAMD ≅ ΔBNC.

3. By CPCT:
∠D = ∠C    --- (1)

4. Since AB || CD and AD is transversal, consecutive interior angles are supplementary:
∠A + ∠D = 180°.
Substituting ∠D = ∠C from (1):
∠A + ∠C = 180°.

5. By the Converse of Cyclic Quadrilateral Theorem: If the sum of a pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral is 180°, the quadrilateral is cyclic.
Hence, ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. Hence Proved.

Q12
Prove that a cyclic parallelogram is a rectangle.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given: ABCD is a cyclic parallelogram.
To Prove: ABCD is a rectangle (each interior angle = 90°).
Proof:
1. Since ABCD is a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal:
∠A = ∠C    --- (1)

2. Since ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral, the sum of opposite angles is 180°:
∠A + ∠C = 180°    --- (2)

3. Substituting (1) into (2):
∠A + ∠A = 180°
2∠A = 180° ⟹ ∠A = 90°.

4. Similarly, ∠B = ∠D and ∠B + ∠D = 180° ⟹ ∠B = 90°.
Since all interior angles of the parallelogram are 90°, ABCD is a rectangle. Hence Proved.

📝 Case Study Question
Roundabout Traffic Island: A circular traffic rotary of radius 14 m has three entry points P, Q, R. Arc PQ subtends 120° at the rotary centre O, and arc QR subtends 100° at O.
(a) What angle does remaining arc RP subtend at the centre O?
(b) What is the angle ∠PQR subtended at the entry point Q on the circumference?
(c) What is the length of chord PQ in terms of trigonometric ratios?
(a) Total angle around centre O = 360°. ∠POR = 360° - (120° + 100°) = 360° - 220° = 140°.
(b) By arc angle theorem, ∠PQR = 1/2 ∠POR = 1/2 × 140° = 70°.
(c) In isosceles ΔOPQ (OP=OQ=14, ∠POQ=120°): Chord PQ = 2 × 14 × sin(60°) = 28 × (√3/2) = 14√3 m.
🧠 HOTS
HOTS: If two circles intersect at two points, prove that their centres lie on the perpendicular bisector of the common chord.
Let circles with centres O and O' intersect at A and B (common chord AB).
In ΔOAO' and ΔOBO': OA = OB (radii of circle O), O'A = O'B (radii of circle O'), OO' = OO' (common).
By SSS, ΔOAO' ≅ ΔOBO' ⟹ ∠AOO' = ∠BOO'.
Now in ΔAOM and ΔBOM (M is intersection of OO' and AB): OA = OB, ∠AOM = ∠BOM, OM = OM (common).
By SAS, ΔAOM ≅ ΔBOM ⟹ AM = BM and ∠OMA = ∠OMB = 90°.
Hence OO' is the perpendicular bisector of common chord AB.
