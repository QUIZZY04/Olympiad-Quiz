Write a paragraph in your own words on each of the following: (a) Preparation of soil (b) Sowing (c) Weeding (d) Threshing
5 Marks (80–120 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Word Count: ~120 words)

(a) Preparation of Soil: Soil preparation is the first and most vital agricultural step. Farmers use ploughs, harrows, and cultivators to till, loosen, and aerate the topsoil, enabling deep root penetration and benefiting earthworms and soil microbes.

Q5
Explain how fertilisers are different from manure.
3 Marks (50–80 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Word Count: ~80 words)

Key Differences Between Fertilisers and Manure:

  • 1. Nature: Fertilisers are chemically synthesized inorganic salt compounds (e.g. urea, NPK); Manure is a natural organic substance derived from decomposition of animal dung, urine, and agricultural plant waste.
  • 2. Preparation: Fertilisers are manufactured in industrial chemical factories; Manure is prepared in open fields or compost pits through microbial decomposition process.
  • 3. Effect on Soil: Fertilisers are nutrient-rich but do not add humus or improve soil texture; Manure replenishes soil humus, improves water-holding capacity, and enriches microbial activity.

Q6
What is irrigation? Describe two methods of irrigation which conserve water.
3 Marks (50–80 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Word Count: ~80 words)

Definition of Irrigation: The process of artificially supplying water to crops through canals, wells, tube wells, or reservoirs at regular intervals to ensure proper germination and growth is called irrigation.

Water-Conserving Irrigation Methods:

  • 1. Sprinkler System: Water pumped through perpendicular pipes emerges from rotating nozzles as fine droplets simulating rainfall, highly efficient for uneven terrain and sandy porous soil, minimizing evaporation losses.
  • 2. Drip Irrigation System: Water drips slowly drop by drop directly at root zone through narrow perforated tubes, eliminating surface evaporation and runoff, ideal for fruit orchards and vegetable gardens.

Q8
Explain how soil gets affected by the continuous plantation of crops in a field.
2 Marks (30–50 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Word Count: ~50 words)

When crops are cultivated repeatedly in the same field without rotation, they selectively absorb specific essential mineral nutrients like Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Potassium (NPK) from the topsoil. This continuous depletion causes severe soil infertility over time, significantly reducing crop yield. Farmers must restore soil health through crop rotation with legumes or by applying organic manure regularly.

Q9
What are weeds? How can we control them?
3 Marks (50–80 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Word Count: ~80 words)

Definition of Weeds: Unwanted, undesirable wild plants that grow spontaneously alongside cultivated crops in a field are called weeds (e.g., Amaranthus, wild oat, wild mustard). They actively compete with main crop plants for sunlight, water, space, and essential soil nutrients, reducing crop productivity significantly.

Methods of Weed Control:

  • 1. Tilling before Sowing: Ploughing uproots and exposes weeds to sunlight, causing them to dry and decompose as humus.
  • 2. Manual Removal: Uprooting or cutting weeds using a khurpi close to ground level between crop rows.
  • 3. Chemical Weedicides: Spraying selective chemical herbicides like 2,4-D dissolved in water kills weeds without harming crops.

Disease-causing microorganisms are called pathogens. They cause immense harm in the following ways:

Q9

3 Marks (50–80 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Word Count: ~80 words)

Definition: Antibiotics are chemical medicines derived from or produced by living microorganisms (bacteria/fungi) that kill or inhibit the growth of harmful pathogenic bacteria. Examples include Penicillin (from Penicillium) and Streptomycin.

Precautions While Taking Antibiotics:

  • 1. Always take antibiotics strictly as prescribed by a registered medical doctor only.
  • 2. Complete the entire prescribed course fully to prevent development of antibiotic resistance.
  • 3. Do not take antibiotics unnecessarily as they destroy beneficial gut microflora.
  • 4. Antibiotics are completely ineffective against viral infections like common cold and influenza.

Q6
Explain the difference between thermoplastic and thermosetting plastics.
3 Marks (50–80 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Word Count: ~80 words)

Thermoplastic vs Thermosetting Plastic:

  • 1. Thermoplastics: These soften easily upon heating and can be remoulded repeatedly into new shapes without any change in chemical composition. Examples include PVC (Poly Vinyl Chloride) used in pipes and Polythene used in bags.

Q13
What is the angle of incidence of a ray if the reflected ray is at an angle of 90° to the incident ray?
2 Marks (Numerical Proof)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Given: Total angle between the incident ray and the reflected ray = 90°.

By the First Law of Reflection: Angle of Incidence (∠i) = Angle of Reflection (∠r).

Since the total angle between incident and reflected ray = ∠i + ∠r = 90°:

∠i + ∠i = 90°   ➔   2∠i = 90°   ➔   ∠i = 45°

Therefore, the angle of incidence is 45°.
