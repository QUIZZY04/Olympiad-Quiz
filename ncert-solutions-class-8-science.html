Crop Production and Management

Agricultural practices — sowing, irrigation, weeding, harvesting and storage.

Biology Agriculture 12 Questions
Objective Fill in the Blanks & MCQ 1 Mark Each

(c) Damaged seeds would _______ on top of water. (d) For growing a crop, sufficient sunlight and _______ and _______ from the soil are essential.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) crop
(b) preparation (ploughing/tilling)
(c) float
(d) water and nutrients

Match items in Column A with those in Column B: Kharif crops | Rabi crops | Chemical fertilisers | Organic manure — with: Animal excreta & crop residue | Urea and super phosphate | Paddy and maize | Wheat and gram

(b) Rabi crops (November–March): Wheat, Gram, Pea, Mustard

Q5
What is irrigation? Describe two methods of irrigation which conserve water.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Irrigation: The supply of water to crops at appropriate intervals is called irrigation. (1 mark)

Two water-conserving methods:
  • Drip Irrigation (1 mark): Water is supplied drop by drop directly at the roots of plants through pipes/nozzles. Minimum water wastage. Ideal for fruit gardens, orchards and nurseries.
  • Sprinkler Irrigation (1 mark): Water is distributed through pipes and sprinkled over crops like rain through rotating nozzles. Suitable for uneven land and sandy soils where water infiltrates quickly.

Q6
What are weeds? How can we control them?
2 marks
✅ Answer
Weeds: Undesirable plants that grow along with the crop plants in the field are called weeds. Example: Amaranthus, Xanthium. They compete with crops for nutrients, water and sunlight. (1 mark)

Control methods: The process of removing weeds is called weeding. It can be done by:
  • Manual removal using a khurpi (trowel)
  • Weedicides (herbicides) such as 2,4-D — sprayed on the field
  • Tilling before sowing uproots and dries up weeds
(1 mark)
CBSE Marking Scheme
Definition of weeds with example1 mark
Any two control methods1 mark
Long Answer 4–5 Marks Each
Q7
Write a paragraph on each: (a) Preparation of soil (b) Sowing (c) Weeding (d) Threshing
5 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Preparation of Soil: The first step before growing a crop is preparation of the soil. The soil is loosened and turned to allow roots to penetrate easily, provide better aeration, and enable water to percolate. Ploughing (tilling) is done using a plough (hala). After ploughing, clods are broken into fine particles by levelling with a leveller. Sometimes manure/fertiliser is added at this stage. (1 mark)

(b) Sowing: Sowing is the process of scattering seeds in the soil. It is the most important step. Seeds must be healthy and good-quality (tested by floating in water — good seeds sink). Sowing is done using traditional funnel-shaped tools or seed drills. Seed drills ensure proper spacing and uniform depth. (1 mark)

(c) Weeding: Removal of unwanted plants (weeds) from the crop field is called weeding. It is done manually with a khurpi or by using weedicides. Weeding is important because weeds compete with crop plants for nutrients, water, space and sunlight, and also harbour insects and pests. Weeding is done before the crop flowers. (1 mark)

(d) Threshing: Threshing is the separation of grain seeds from the harvested crop stalks (chaff). After harvesting, the crop is dried and then stalks are beaten against a hard surface or a threshing machine is used. Winnowing separates lighter husk from heavier grains using wind. Today combine machines perform both harvesting and threshing simultaneously. (1 mark)

Introductory overview of agricultural practices: (1 mark)
CBSE Marking Scheme
Each paragraph (a), (b), (c), (d)1 mark each
Overall presentation/coherence1 mark
Q8
Explain how soil gets affected by the continuous plantation of crops in a field.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Continuous cultivation of the same crop in the same field leads to: (1 mark)
  • Depletion of specific nutrients: The same crop takes up the same nutrients repeatedly, making the soil deficient in those minerals. (1 mark)
  • Reduced soil fertility: Humus content decreases as no new organic matter is added. Microbial activity reduces. Soil becomes less fertile over time.
  • Solution — Crop rotation: Growing different crops alternately (e.g., wheat after legumes) restores nutrients. Leguminous crops fix atmospheric nitrogen back into the soil. (1 mark)
CBSE Marking Scheme
Effect: depletion of nutrients1 mark
Effect: reduced fertility / humus1 mark
Solution: crop rotation / adding manure1 mark

✅ Answer
(a) microscope
(b) nitrogen

Tick the correct answer — (a) Yeast is used in the production of: (i) sugar (ii) alcohol (iii) hydrochloric acid (iv) oxygen. Q3
Match the organisms in Column A with their action in Column B: Bacteria, Rhizobium, Lactobacillus, Yeast, A protozoan, A virus — with: Causing malaria, Causing cholera, Producing curd, Producing bread, Fixing nitrogen, Causing AIDS

Short Answer 2–3 Marks Each
Q4
Write a short paragraph on the harms caused by microorganisms.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Microorganisms can be harmful in many ways:
  • Disease in humans: Bacteria cause tuberculosis, cholera, typhoid. Viruses cause cold, influenza, COVID-19, polio. Protozoa cause malaria, dysentery. Fungi cause ringworm, athlete's foot. (1 mark)
  • Disease in plants: Several plant diseases are caused by fungi, bacteria and viruses — e.g., wheat rust (fungus), citrus canker (bacteria). (1 mark)
  • Food spoilage: Microorganisms spoil food by decomposing it, producing toxins and bad odour, making it unfit for consumption. (1 mark)

Disease in plants1 mark

Q5
What are antibiotics? What precautions must be taken while taking antibiotics?
3 marks
✅ Answer
Antibiotics: Medicines that kill or stop the growth of disease-causing microorganisms (bacteria) are called antibiotics. Examples: Streptomycin, Penicillin, Erythromycin. They are produced from fungi or bacteria. (1 mark)

Precautions:
  • Take antibiotics only on a doctor's advice. (½ mark)
  • Complete the full course — stopping early can make bacteria resistant. (½ mark)
  • Antibiotics are not effective against viral infections like cold or flu. (½ mark)
  • Do not take antibiotics unnecessarily or in excess. (½ mark)
CBSE Marking Scheme
Definition with examples1 mark
Any 4 precautions2 marks (½ each)
Q6
Name the process by which Rhizobium bacteria help increase the fertility of soil. Also explain briefly.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Process: Nitrogen Fixation (1 mark)

Rhizobium bacteria live in the root nodules of leguminous plants (e.g., pea, groundnut, bean). These bacteria fix atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) gas and convert it into nitrogenous compounds (ammonia/nitrates) that can be used by plants. This enriches the soil with nitrogen, increasing its fertility. This is called biological nitrogen fixation. (1 mark)

1 mark each
✅ Answer

Q2
Mark 'T' if the statement is True and 'F' if it is False: (a) Rayon is a synthetic fibre. (b) Safety match industry uses melamine as a coating material. (c) Polythene (a type of plastic) is used for making computer disks. (d) Thermoplastics are used in manufacturing car parts and electric switches.

(a) False — Rayon is a semi-synthetic (artificial silk) fibre made from cellulose (wood pulp).
(b) False — Melamine is used as a floor covering, not for safety matches.
(c) False — Polythene is used for making polythene bags; computer disks use other polymers.
(d) False — Thermosetting plastics (not thermoplastics) are used in car parts and electric switches because they do not melt on heating.
Short Answer 2–3 Marks
Q3
Explain why plastic containers are favoured for storing food.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Plastic containers are preferred for storing food because:
  • Non-reactive (chemically inert): Plastic does not react with the food inside. It does not corrode like metals. (1 mark)
  • Lightweight and durable: Plastic containers are light, strong and unbreakable, making them easy to carry and handle. They are also cheap and available in various shapes. (1 mark)
  • They are good insulators and resist moisture.

Give examples to show that plastics are non-corrosive in nature.
2 marks

Plastics do not corrode (rust/react) with water, air, or chemicals. Examples: (1 mark each)
  • Chemicals such as acids, alkalis and other substances are stored in plastic containers because plastic does not react with them.
  • Pipes for water supply are made of PVC (polyvinyl chloride) — plastic — because they do not corrode like iron pipes.
  • Teflon-coated non-stick cookware — Teflon is a plastic that is not affected by soap water, oils or food acids.

Materials: Metals and Non-Metals

Physical & chemical properties of metals and non-metals, uses and reactions.

ChemistryMaterials
Objective MCQ & True/False 1 Mark Each
Q1
Which of the following can be beaten into thin sheets? (a) Zinc (b) Phosphorus (c) Sulphur (d) Oxygen
1 mark
✅ Answer
(a) Zinc
Zinc is a metal. The property of metals to be beaten into thin sheets is called malleability. Phosphorus, Sulphur, and Oxygen are non-metals and are brittle (solid non-metals) — they cannot be beaten into sheets.
Q2
Mark T/F: (a) Phosphorus is a very reactive non-metal. (b) Sodium is a very reactive metal. (c) Copper displaces zinc from zinc sulphate solution. (d) Coal can be drawn into wires.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) True — Phosphorus is highly reactive; white phosphorus catches fire spontaneously in air.
(b) True — Sodium reacts vigorously with water and air; stored in kerosene.
(c) False — Copper is less reactive than zinc and cannot displace zinc from its salt solution. (Zinc can displace copper.)
(d) False — Coal is a non-metal. It is brittle and cannot be drawn into wires. Only metals have ductility.
Short Answer 2–3 Marks
Q3
What happens when (a) dilute sulphuric acid is poured on a copper plate? (b) iron nails are placed in copper sulphate solution? Write word equations for the reactions.
3 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Dilute H₂SO₄ reacts with copper to form copper sulphate and hydrogen gas is released. (1 mark)
Word equation: Copper + Dilute sulphuric acid → Copper sulphate + Hydrogen gas

(b) Iron is more reactive than copper. Iron displaces copper from copper sulphate solution. The blue colour of copper sulphate fades and a reddish-brown deposit of copper forms on iron nails. (1 mark)
Word equation: Iron + Copper sulphate → Iron sulphate + Copper

This is a displacement reaction. (1 mark)
CBSE Marking Scheme
Observation + equation for (a)1 mark
Observation + equation for (b)1 mark
Identifying as displacement reaction1 mark

Coal and Petroleum

Natural resources — fossil fuels, their formation, uses and conservation.

ChemistryEnergy
Objective 1 Mark Each
Q1
What are the advantages of using CNG and LPG as fuels?
2 marks
✅ Answer
CNG (Compressed Natural Gas) and LPG (Liquefied Petroleum Gas):
  • Clean fuels: They produce less pollution on burning compared to petrol, diesel and coal. (1 mark)
  • High calorific value: They release more heat energy per unit mass. Economical and efficient. (½ mark)
  • Easy transportation: They can be transported through pipelines (CNG) or in cylinders (LPG), making distribution easier. (½ mark)

Describe characteristics and uses of coke.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Characteristics of Coke: (1½ marks)
  • Tough, porous and black substance
  • Almost pure form of carbon (~90% carbon)
  • Burns without smoke and at very high temperatures
Uses of Coke: (1½ marks)
  • Used as a reducing agent in the extraction/smelting of metals (e.g., iron from iron ore in blast furnace)
  • Used as a fuel in industries
  • Used in the manufacture of steel and many other products

Combustion and Flame

Types of combustion, conditions for combustion, flames, fire extinguishers.

ChemistryEnergy
Objective 1 Mark Each
Q1
List conditions under which combustion can take place.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Three conditions necessary for combustion:
  • Presence of a combustible substance (fuel) — e.g., wood, LPG, coal. (1 mark)
  • Presence of a supporter of combustion (oxygen/air) — Without air, fire is extinguished. (½ mark)
  • Attainment of ignition temperature — The minimum temperature required for a substance to catch fire. (½ mark)
Q2
Explain the role of a fire extinguisher in fighting fire. Which fire extinguisher is NOT suitable for electrical fires?
3 marks
✅ Answer
Role of fire extinguisher: A fire extinguisher cuts off the supply of air or lowers the temperature of the fuel below its ignition temperature, or both. This removes one or both conditions needed for combustion and the fire is extinguished. (2 marks)

Extinguisher not suitable for electrical fires: Water-based extinguisher should NOT be used for electrical fires because water conducts electricity and can cause electrocution. CO₂ extinguishers are used instead. (1 mark)
CBSE Marking Scheme
Removing air supply / lowering temperature2 marks
Water extinguisher not for electrical fires1 mark

Reaching the Age of Adolescence

Puberty, hormones, reproductive health, nutrition during adolescence.

BiologyHealth
Short Answer
Q1
What is menstruation? Explain why it occurs.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Menstruation: The monthly shedding of the uterine lining (endometrium) along with blood in females is called menstruation. It typically lasts 2–8 days and occurs every 28–30 days. (1 mark)

Reason it occurs: Every month, the uterus prepares itself (builds a thick lining) for a fertilised egg to implant. If fertilisation does not occur, the unfertilised egg (ovum) and the uterine lining are shed. This shedding of blood and tissue through the vagina is menstruation. (2 marks)
Q2
Name the hormones responsible for changes during puberty in (a) boys and (b) girls.
2 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Boys: Testosterone — secreted by testes. Responsible for voice deepening, facial hair, muscle development. (1 mark)
(b) Girls: Oestrogen — secreted by ovaries. Responsible for breast development, widening of hips, onset of menstruation. (1 mark)

Force and Pressure

Contact and non-contact forces, pressure, atmospheric pressure, applications.

PhysicsForces
Objective 1 Mark Each
Q1
Give two examples each of situations in which you push or pull to change the state of motion of objects.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Push examples: (i) Pushing a football to kick it (stationary → motion). (ii) Pushing brakes to stop a moving bicycle (motion → rest). (1 mark)

Pull examples: (i) Pulling a door to open it. (ii) Pulling a cart by a horse. (1 mark)
Q2
A bag of cotton and an iron bar are having equal mass (weight). Which is heavier and why?
2 marks
✅ Answer
Both are equally heavy since they have the same mass (weight). However, the iron bar appears heavier in practice because: The iron bar exerts more pressure on the hand since it has a smaller contact area, while cotton exerts less pressure due to its larger surface area. Pressure = Force / Area. So, same force with smaller area (iron bar) gives a feeling of being heavier. (2 marks)

Sound

Production and propagation of sound, characteristics — loudness, pitch, frequency, amplitude, echoes.

PhysicsWaves
Objective 1 Mark Each
Q1
Choose the correct answer: Sound can travel through (a) gases only (b) solids only (c) liquids only (d) solids, liquids, and gases
1 mark
✅ Answer
(d) Solids, liquids, and gases
Sound is a mechanical wave and can travel through any material medium. It cannot travel through vacuum. Sound travels fastest in solids and slowest in gases.
Q2
What is an echo? What is the minimum distance required to hear an echo?
3 marks
✅ Answer
Echo: A reflected sound heard after the original sound is called an echo. It is produced when sound reflects off a hard, large surface. (1 mark)

Condition: The reflected sound must reach the ear at least 0.1 seconds after the original sound (since the ear can distinguish two sounds only if they are separated by ≥ 0.1 s). (1 mark)

Minimum distance: If speed of sound = 344 m/s in air (at ~25°C): Distance = (344 × 0.1)/2 = 17.2 m (≈ 17 m). The obstacle must be at least 17 m away. (1 mark)
CBSE Marking Scheme
Definition of echo1 mark

Minimum distance ~17 m1 mark

Chemical Effects of Electric Current

Conductors, insulators, electrolysis, electroplating, applications.

Physics/Chemistry
Short Answer
Q1
What is electroplating? Explain with an example why it is used.
4 marks
✅ Answer
Electroplating: The process of depositing a thin layer of one metal over another metal using electric current is called electroplating. (1 mark)

Process: The object to be plated is made the cathode (negative electrode) and the plating metal is made the anode. The electrolyte is a solution of the plating metal's salt. When current passes, metal ions from the anode dissolve in solution and deposit on the cathode. (2 marks)

Example and Use: Chromium plating on iron car parts — iron is protected from corrosion (rust) and looks shiny and attractive. Iron taps, bicycle handlebars are chromium plated. (1 mark)

Some Natural Phenomena

Lightning, earthquakes — causes, effects, safety measures.

PhysicsEarth Science
Short Answer
Q1
Describe the function of a lightning conductor in protecting tall buildings from lightning damage.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Lightning Conductor: A device to protect buildings from damage by lightning. (½ mark)

Structure: A pointed metal rod fixed at the top of the building, connected by a thick copper wire running down to a metal plate buried deep in the ground (earth). (1 mark)

Function: During a thunderstorm, if lightning strikes, it is attracted by the pointed tip of the rod. The charge flows down through the wire into the ground harmlessly, protecting the building. (1½ marks)
CBSE Marking Scheme
Definition / purpose½ mark
Structure description1 mark
Working / function1½ marks
Q2
Explain what causes earthquakes. State any four precautions to be taken during an earthquake.
4 marks
✅ Answer
Cause of Earthquakes: The Earth's crust is made of several large pieces called tectonic plates that float on the semi-liquid mantle below. When these plates suddenly move, slip or collide, the resulting vibrations produce seismic waves that we feel as earthquakes. The point inside the Earth where the disturbance occurs is the focus; the point on the surface directly above is the epicentre. (2 marks)

Precautions: (2 marks)
  • Take shelter under a strong table or stand in a doorway during shaking.
  • Do not use lifts during an earthquake.
  • Move away from tall buildings, trees and power lines if outdoors.
  • Lie face down, covering head and neck, until shaking stops.

Light

Reflection, laws of reflection, multiple images, periscope, dispersion of light, human eye.

PhysicsOptics
Objective 1 Mark Each
Q1
State the laws of reflection of light.
2 marks
✅ Answer
The two laws of reflection of light are:
  • First Law: The angle of incidence (∠i) is always equal to the angle of reflection (∠r). (1 mark)
  • Second Law: The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal to the reflecting surface at the point of incidence — all three lie in the same plane. (1 mark)
Q2
What is dispersion of light? Explain why a prism splits white light into seven colours.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Dispersion of light: The splitting of white light into its seven constituent colours (VIBGYOR) when it passes through a prism is called dispersion of light. (1 mark)

Why it happens: White light is a mixture of seven colours (Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red). When white light enters a glass prism, each colour bends (refracts) by a different amount because each has a slightly different wavelength. Violet bends the most and Red bends the least. This separates them into a band of colours called the spectrum. (2 marks)
CBSE Marking Scheme
Definition of dispersion1 mark
White light = 7 colours (VIBGYOR)1 mark
Each colour refracts differently1 mark
Q3
Describe the construction and working of a periscope.
4 marks
✅ Answer
Construction: (2 marks)
A periscope is an optical device used to see objects that are not in the direct line of sight. It consists of a long tube with two plane mirrors (or reflecting prisms) fixed at each end at an angle of 45° to the horizontal.

Working: (2 marks)
Light from the distant object enters the top mirror at 45° and reflects downward. It travels through the tube and strikes the second (lower) mirror, also at 45°, and reflects horizontally to the observer's eye. This way, an observer can see objects above walls or water surfaces (used in submarines).

Construction (diagram/description)2 marks
Working principle2 marks
