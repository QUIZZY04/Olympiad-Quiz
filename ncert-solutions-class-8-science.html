Chapter 1: Crop Production and Management

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 11 (Complete)

Q1
Fill in the blanks: (a) Same kind of plants grown on large scale is called _______. (b) First step before growing crops is _______ of soil. (c) Damaged seeds _______ on top of water. (d) For growing crop, sunlight and _______ and _______ from soil are essential.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) crop
(b) preparation
(c) float
(d) water and nutrients
Q2
Match items in Column A with Column B: (i) Kharif crops (ii) Rabi crops (iii) Chemical fertilisers (iv) Organic manure — with: (a) Food for cattle (b) Urea & super phosphate (c) Animal excreta, cow dung, plant waste (d) Wheat, gram, pea (e) Paddy and maize
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(i) Kharif → (e) Paddy & maize
(ii) Rabi → (d) Wheat, gram, pea
(iii) Fertilisers → (b) Urea & super phosphate
(iv) Manure → (c) Animal excreta, cow dung, plant waste
Q3
Give two examples of each: (a) Kharif crop (b) Rabi crop
1 mark
✅ Answer
(a) Kharif: Paddy (rice) and Maize.
(b) Rabi: Wheat and Gram.
Q4
Write a paragraph on: (a) Preparation of soil (b) Sowing (c) Weeding (d) Threshing
4 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Soil Preparation: Loosening and turning soil using plough so roots penetrate deep and breathe.
(b) Sowing: Selecting healthy seeds and placing them in soil at proper depth using seed drill.
(c) Weeding: Removing unwanted plants (weeds) manually with khurpi or using weedicides (2,4-D).
(d) Threshing: Separating grain seeds from harvested crop stalks/chaff.
Q5
Explain how fertilisers are different from manure.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Fertilisers are factory-made inorganic salts rich in specific N, P, K nutrients but add no humus. Manure is natural organic decomposed waste providing humus and improving soil texture.
Q6
What is irrigation? Describe two water-conserving irrigation methods.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Irrigation is water supply to crops at regular intervals. Methods: (1) Sprinkler System: Rotating nozzles spray water like rain (best for uneven/sandy land). (2) Drip System: Water drops directly at roots (zero wastage).
Q7
If wheat is sown in Kharif season, what would happen?
2 marks
✅ Answer
Wheat requires cold dry winter weather. In Kharif (monsoon), excess rain causes waterlogging, seed rot, and pest attacks, destroying crop yield.
Q8
Explain how soil is affected by continuous crop plantation.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Depletes soil nutrients, reducing fertility. Restored by crop rotation (legumes) or applying manure.
Q9
What are weeds? How can we control them?
3 marks
✅ Answer
Undesirable wild plants in field. Controlled by tilling before sowing, manual uprooting (khurpi), or spraying weedicides (2,4-D).
Q10
Arrange in proper order: (1) Sugar factory (2) Irrigation (3) Harvesting (4) Sowing (5) Preparation of soil (6) Ploughing (7) Manuring
2 marks
✅ Answer
5 ➔ 6 ➔ 4 ➔ 7 ➔ 2 ➔ 3 ➔ 1
Q11
Crossword puzzle: Down: 1. Irrigation 2. Storage 5. Crop. Across: 3. Harvester 4. Gram 6. Winnowing
3 marks
✅ Answer
Down: 1. IRRIGATION, 2. STORAGE, 5. CROP. Across: 3. HARVESTER, 4. GRAM, 6. WINNOWING.

Chapter 2: Microorganisms: Friend and Foe

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 9 (Complete)

Q1
Fill in blanks: (a) Microorganisms seen with _______. (b) Blue-green algae fix _______ directly. (c) Alcohol produced by _______. (d) Cholera caused by _______.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) microscope (b) nitrogen (c) yeast (d) bacteria
Q2
Tick correct answer: (a) Yeast in production of: (ii) alcohol (b) Antibiotic is: (ii) Streptomycin (c) Carrier of malaria protozoan: (i) female Anopheles (d) Common carrier: (ii) housefly (e) Bread dough rises: (iii) growth of yeast cells (f) Sugar to alcohol: (iii) fermentation
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) (ii) alcohol (b) (ii) Streptomycin (c) (i) female Anopheles mosquito (d) (ii) housefly (e) (iii) growth of yeast cells (f) (iii) fermentation
Q3
Match Column A & B: Bacteria ➔ Cholera, Rhizobium ➔ Nitrogen fixation, Lactobacillus ➔ Setting curd, Yeast ➔ Baking bread, Protozoan ➔ Malaria, Virus ➔ AIDS
1 mark each
✅ Answer
Bacteria: Cholera; Rhizobium: Nitrogen fixation; Lactobacillus: Setting curd; Yeast: Baking bread; Protozoan: Malaria; Virus: AIDS.
Q4
Can microorganisms be seen with naked eye? How?
2 marks
✅ Answer
No. Observed under microscope or magnifying glass (bread mould).
Q5
What are major groups of microorganisms?
2 marks
✅ Answer
1. Bacteria 2. Fungi 3. Protozoa 4. Algae (and Viruses).
Q6
Name microorganisms which fix atmospheric nitrogen.
1 mark
✅ Answer
Rhizobium bacteria, Azotobacter, and Blue-green algae.
Q7
Write 10 lines on usefulness of microorganisms.
5 marks
✅ Answer
1. Curd making 2. Bread baking 3. Alcohol production 4. Antibiotics 5. Vaccines 6. Nitrogen fixation 7. Environmental cleanup (decomposition) 8. Vinegar production 9. Gut digestion 10. Sewage treatment.
Q8
Short paragraph on harmful effects of microorganisms.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Cause human diseases (TB, cholera, malaria, flu), animal diseases (anthrax), plant rusts, and spoil food/leather.
Q9
What are antibiotics? Precautions while taking them.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Medicines killing bacteria (e.g. Streptomycin). Precautions: Doctor's prescription, complete full course, avoid unnecessary use, ineffective on viral cold.

Chapter 3: Synthetic Fibres and Plastics

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 15 (Complete)

Q1
Explain why some fibres are called synthetic.
1 mark
✅ Answer
Made by humans using chemical polymers from petrochemicals.
Q2
Rayon is different from synthetic fibres because: (b) it is obtained from wood pulp.
1 mark
✅ Answer
(b) Obtained from natural wood pulp chemically treated.
Q3
Fill in blanks: (a) artificial / man-made (b) petrochemicals (c) polymer.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) artificial/man-made (b) petrochemicals (c) polymer
Q4
Examples showing nylon is very strong.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Parachutes, rock climbing ropes, seat belts (stronger than steel wire).
Q5
Why plastic containers favoured for storing food.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Non-reactive with food, light weight, strong, cheap, durable.
Q6
Thermoplastic vs Thermosetting plastic.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Thermoplastics soften on heating & remolded (PVC, Polythene). Thermosetting set permanently & cannot be remolded (Bakelite, Melamine).
Q7
Why made of thermosetting: (a) Saucepan handles (b) Electric plugs/switches
2 marks
✅ Answer
Bakelite is poor conductor of heat (saucepan handle) and electricity (switches) and does not melt.
Q8
Recycled vs Non-recycled plastics list.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Recycled: Plastic toys, carry bags, bowls, wire cover. Non-recycled: Telephone, cooker handles, switches.
Q9
Cotton vs Synthetic shirt for Rana in summer.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Cotton shirt because it absorbs sweat and evaporates it to cool the body.
Q10
Examples showing plastics non-corrosive.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Storing acids/cleaners in plastic bottles; PVC water pipes.
Q11
Toothbrush handle & bristles same material?
2 marks
✅ Answer
No. Bristles soft/flexible (nylon), handle rigid/firm (hard plastic).
Q12
'Avoid plastics as far as possible' comment.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Plastics non-biodegradable, cause pollution and clog drains. Follow 5 R rule.
Q13
Match Polyester ➔ wrinkle free, Teflon ➔ non-stick cookware, Rayon ➔ wood pulp, Nylon ➔ parachutes.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
Polyester: Wrinkle-free; Teflon: Non-stick; Rayon: Wood pulp; Nylon: Parachutes.
Q14
'Synthetic fibres help conservation of forests' comment.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Synthetic fibres made chemically from oil, saving trees used for rayon/wood pulp.
Q15
Activity showing thermoplastic poor conductor of electricity.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Insert plastic scale in cell-bulb circuit; bulb does not glow.

Chapter 4: Materials — Metals and Non-Metals

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 11 (Complete)

Q1
Which can be beaten into thin sheets? (a) Zinc (b) Phosphorus (c) Sulphur (d) Oxygen
1 mark
✅ Answer
(a) Zinc
Q2
Which statement is correct? (c) Generally, metals are ductile.
1 mark
✅ Answer
(c) Generally, metals are ductile
Q3
Fill in blanks: (a) reactive (b) good, electricity (c) more (d) hydrogen.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) reactive (b) good, electricity (c) more (d) hydrogen
Q4
Mark T/F: (a) F (b) T (c) F (d) F
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) False (b) True (c) False (d) False
Q5
Distinguish metals and non-metals on properties: Appearance, Hardness, Malleability, Ductility, Heat conduction, Electricity conduction.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Metals: Lustrous, hard, malleable, ductile, good heat & electricity conductors. Non-metals: Dull, soft/brittle, non-malleable, non-ductile, poor conductors.
Q6
Reasons: (a) Aluminium foil for food (b) Immersion rods metallic (c) Copper cannot displace zinc (d) Na & K stored in kerosene.
4 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Malleable & non-reactive. (b) Metals conduct heat & electricity. (c) Cu is less reactive than Zn. (d) Na & K react violently with air/water.
Q7
Can lemon pickle be stored in aluminium utensil? Explain.
2 marks
✅ Answer
No. Lemon contains citric acid which reacts with aluminium metal to produce toxic hydrogen salts, spoiling the pickle.
Q8
Match: Gold ➔ Jewellery, Iron ➔ Machinery, Aluminium ➔ Wrapping food, Carbon ➔ Fuel, Copper ➔ Electric wire, Mercury ➔ Thermometers.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Gold: Jewellery; Iron: Machinery; Aluminium: Wrapping food; Carbon: Fuel; Copper: Electric wire; Mercury: Thermometers.
Q9
What happens when: (a) Dilute H2SO4 on copper plate (b) Iron nails in copper sulphate solution + word equations.
3 marks
✅ Answer
(a) No reaction with cold dil H2SO4; on heating releases H2 gas.
(b) Iron displaces copper: Iron + Copper sulphate ➔ Iron sulphate + Copper (blue turns green).
Q10
Saloni burning charcoal gas test & equations.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Gas is CO2. Test: Add water to gas, test with blue litmus ➔ turns red (acidic carbonic acid). C + O2 ➔ CO2; CO2 + H2O ➔ H2CO3.
Q11
Gold jewellery polished weight loss explanation.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Jeweller uses Aqua Regia (acid mixture) which dissolves outer dull gold layer. The inner shiny gold is exposed, causing slight loss of total gold weight.

Chapter 5: Coal and Petroleum

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 9 (Complete)

Q1
Advantages of CNG and LPG as fuels.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Clean burning, high calorific value, low smoke, easy pipe transport.
Q2
Petroleum product used for surfacing roads.
1 mark
✅ Answer
Bitumen (replaces coal tar).
Q3
Coal formation from dead vegetation & carbonisation.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Dead trees buried in soil under high temp & pressure over millions of years convert to coal via carbonisation.
Q4
Fill in blanks: (a) Fossil fuels: Coal, Petroleum, Natural gas (b) Separation of petroleum: Refining (c) Least polluting vehicle fuel: CNG.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) Coal, Petroleum, Natural gas (b) Refining (c) CNG
Q5
Mark T/F: (a) Fossil fuels lab made (F) (b) CNG more polluting than petrol (F) (c) Coke pure form of carbon (T) (d) Kerosene not fossil fuel (F).
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) False (b) False (c) True (d) False
Q6
Why fossil fuels are exhaustible natural resources.
2 marks
✅ Answer
They take millions of years to form and existing reserves are limited while consumption is very fast.
Q7
Characteristics and uses of coke.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Tough, porous, black, almost pure carbon (~90%). Used in steel manufacturing and metal extraction.
Q8
Process of petroleum formation.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Organisms living in sea died and settled at sea bed, covered by sand/clay. High temp/pressure and absence of air converted them to petroleum & natural gas.
Q9
Power shortage graph analysis.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Shows steady increase in power shortage % over years; highlights need for energy conservation & renewable resources.

Chapter 6: Combustion and Flame

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 13 (Complete)

Q1
List conditions under which combustion takes place.
2 marks
✅ Answer
1. Fuel 2. Air (Oxygen) 3. Ignition temperature.
Q2
Fill in blanks: (a) Burning of wood causes pollution. (b) Liquid fuel in homes is LPG. (c) Fuel must be heated to ignition temp before burning. (d) Fire produced by oil cannot be controlled by water.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) pollution (b) LPG (c) ignition temperature (d) water
Q3
CNG in automobiles reduced urban pollution. Explain.
2 marks
✅ Answer
CNG burns completely leaving no unburnt carbon residue or harmful sulphur/nitrogen oxides.
Q4
Compare LPG and wood as fuels.
3 marks
✅ Answer
LPG has higher calorific value (55,000 kJ/kg vs 17,000 kJ/kg), produces no smoke/ash, and easy to store. Wood cuts trees and causes smoke.
Q5
Give reasons: (a) Water not for electrical fires (b) LPG better than wood (c) Paper on aluminium pipe doesn't burn easily.
3 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Water conducts electricity risk electrocution. (b) LPG high heat, no smoke. (c) Aluminium pipe conducts heat away from paper so paper doesn't reach ignition temperature.
Q6
Labelled diagram of candle flame.
3 marks
✅ Answer
1. Outermost zone (Blue, complete combustion, hottest). 2. Middle zone (Yellow, luminous, partial combustion). 3. Innermost zone (Black, unburnt wax vapour, least hot).
Q7
Unit of calorific value.
1 mark
✅ Answer
kJ/kg (kilojoules per kilogram).
Q8
Explain how CO2 controls fire.
2 marks
✅ Answer
CO2 is heavier than oxygen, blankets the fire cutting off oxygen supply, and expands rapidly cooling the fuel.
Q9
Difficult to burn heap of green leaves vs dry leaves.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Green leaves contain moisture which absorbs heat, raising ignition temp. Dry leaves have no water so catch fire easily.
Q10
Which zone of flame goldsmith uses & why.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Outermost non-luminous zone (blue) because it is the hottest part with complete combustion.
Q11
Numerical: 4.5 kg fuel burnt, 180,000 kJ heat produced. Calculate calorific value.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Calorific value = Heat produced / Fuel mass = 180,000 kJ / 4.5 kg = 40,000 kJ/kg.
Q12
Can rusting be called combustion? Discuss.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Yes, rusting is a slow oxidation reaction producing heat, but no light/flame is produced unlike rapid combustion.
Q13
Abida (outer zone) vs Ramesh (yellow inner zone) water heating.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Abida's water will heat faster because outermost blue zone is hottest.

Chapter 7: Conservation of Plants and Animals

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 11 (Complete)

Q1
Fill in blanks: (a) Place where animals protected: Sanctuary. (b) Species found only in particular area: Endemic. (c) Migratory birds fly due to climate changes.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) Wildlife sanctuary (b) Endemic (c) Climatic
Q2
Differentiate: (a) Sanctuary vs Biosphere Reserve (b) Zoo vs Sanctuary (c) Endangered vs Extinct (d) Flora vs Fauna.
4 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Sanctuary protects animals; Biosphere reserve conserves total ecosystem + culture. (b) Zoo is artificial human setup; Sanctuary is natural habitat. (c) Endangered facing extinction; Extinct no longer exist. (d) Flora = Plants; Fauna = Animals.
Q3
Deforestation effects on animals, environment, villages, cities, earth, next generation.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Destroys animal habitat, increases CO2 causing global warming, leads to soil erosion/floods, lowers groundwater, harms future life.
Q4
What happens if: (a) keep cutting trees (b) habitat disturbed (c) top soil exposed.
3 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Desertification & global warming. (b) Animal species become endangered/extinct. (c) Loss of humus turning fertile land into desert.
Q5
Short answers: (a) Why conserve biodiversity (b) Protected forests not 100% safe (c) Tribals depend on jungle (d) Causes/consequences of deforestation (e) Red Data Book (f) Migration.
4 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Maintain ecological balance. (b) Poachers encroach. (c) Food, firewood, herbs. (e) Red Data Book keeps record of endangered species. (f) Seasonal bird movement for breeding.
Q6
Is cutting trees for factory projects justified?
2 marks
✅ Answer
No. Unchecked deforestation damages environment. Mandatory reforestation must equal cut trees.
Q7
How contribute to green wealth of locality?
2 marks
✅ Answer
Planting saplings, watering plants, encouraging community gardening, preventing tree cutting.
Q8
Deforestation leading to reduced rainfall.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Trees release moisture via transpiration aiding rain clouds. Fewer trees = less water vapor & higher global temperatures.
Q9
National parks in state.
1 mark
✅ Answer
Jim Corbett (Uttarakhand), Gir (Gujarat), Kaziranga (Assam), Ranthambore (Rajasthan).
Q10
Why save paper & ways to save paper.
2 marks
✅ Answer
17 full grown trees cut to make 1 tonne paper. Ways: Recycle paper, use both sides, go digital.
Q11
Complete word puzzle on conservation.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Species: ENDANGERED; Extinct: DINOSAUR; Record: RED DATA BOOK.

Chapter 8: Cell — Structure and Functions

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 10 (Complete)

Q1
True/False: (a) T (b) F (Nerve cells branched) (c) F (Cell is basic unit) (d) T.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) True (b) False (c) False (d) True
Q2
Sketch human nerve cell & function.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Long branched neuron. Function: Transmit electrical message signals controlling body actions.
Q3
Short notes: (a) Cytoplasm (b) Nucleus.
3 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Jelly-like substance between membrane & nucleus containing organelles. (b) Spherical central control center containing nucleolus & chromosomes.
Q4
Which part of cell contains organelles?
1 mark
✅ Answer
Cytoplasm
Q5
Plant vs Animal cell differences (sketches & 3 points).
3 marks
✅ Answer
Plant cell has cell wall, chloroplasts, large vacuole. Animal cell lacks cell wall & chloroplasts, has small vacuoles.
Q6
Eukaryotes vs Prokaryotes.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Prokaryotes lack well-defined nuclear membrane (Bacteria, Blue-green algae). Eukaryotes have true membrane-bound nucleus (Onion cell, Cheek cell).
Q7
Where are chromosomes found & function?
2 marks
✅ Answer
Found in nucleus. Carry genes that transfer hereditary characters from parents to offspring.
Q8
'Cells are basic structural units of living organisms' explain.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Just like bricks form a building, cells assemble to form tissues ➔ organs ➔ organisms.
Q9
Why chloroplasts found only in plant cells?
2 marks
✅ Answer
Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll required for photosynthesis (making food from sunlight). Animals do not synthesize food.
Q10
Cell crossword puzzle.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Across: 1. NUCLEUS, 3. PLASTID, 4. CYTOPLASM. Down: 1. NERVE, 2. CELL WALL.

Chapter 9: Reproduction in Animals

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 10 (Complete)

Q1
Importance of reproduction in organisms.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Ensures continuation of species generation after generation, preventing extinction.
Q2
Process of fertilisation in human beings.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Sperm from father fuses with ovum (egg) from mother inside fallopian tube to form single-celled zygote (Internal Fertilisation).
Q3
Choose correct answer: (a) Internal fertilisation in female body (b) Tadpole develops by metamorphosis (c) Number of nuclei in zygote is ONE.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) inside female body (b) metamorphosis (c) one
Q4
Mark T/F: (a) Oviparous lay eggs (T) (b) Sperm single cell (T) (c) External fertilisation in frogs (T) (d) Amoeba reproduces by budding (F - binary fission) (e) Zygote formed as result of fertilisation (T) (f) Embryo made of single cell (F).
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) True (b) True (c) True (d) False (e) True (f) False
Q5
Differences between zygote and foetus.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Zygote is single-celled initial fertilised egg. Foetus is multicellular stage where body parts (hands, legs, head) are clearly identifiable.
Q6
Define asexual reproduction & 2 methods.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Reproduction involving single parent without gamete fusion. Methods: (1) Binary Fission in Amoeba (2) Budding in Hydra.
Q7
Female organ where embryo gets embedded.
1 mark
✅ Answer
Uterus (uterine wall).
Q8
What is metamorphosis? Examples.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Drastic transformation of larva/tadpole into adult through drastic biological changes. Ex: Frog (tadpole ➔ frog), Silkworm (caterpillar ➔ moth).
Q9
Internal vs External fertilisation.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Internal occurs inside female body (humans, cows, hens). External occurs outside female body in water (frogs, fish).
Q10
Reproduction crossword puzzle.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Across: 1. FERTILISATION 3. ZYGOTE. Down: 2. BINARY 4. OVUM.

Chapter 10: Reaching the Age of Adolescence

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 10 (Complete)

Q1
Term for chemical secretions of endocrine glands.
1 mark
✅ Answer
Hormones
Q2
What is adolescence?
2 marks
✅ Answer
Period of life between 11 and 19 years when body undergoes changes leading to reproductive maturity.
Q3
What is menstruation? Explain.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Monthly breakdown and discharge of thick inner uterine lining along with blood vessels in females when egg is not fertilised (28-30 day cycle).
Q4
List body changes at puberty.
3 marks
✅ Answer
1. Sudden increase in height. 2. Voice change (Adam's apple in boys). 3. Sweat & acne. 4. Maturation of reproductive organs.
Q5
Table of Endocrine Glands & Hormones.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Pituitary ➔ Growth Hormone; Thyroid ➔ Thyroxine; Pancreas ➔ Insulin; Adrenal ➔ Adrenalin; Testes ➔ Testosterone; Ovaries ➔ Oestrogen.
Q6
Sex hormones definition & functions.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Hormones controlling male/female secondary sexual characteristics (Testosterone in boys, Oestrogen in girls). Controlled by Pituitary gland.
Q7
Choose correct option: (a) Balanced diet for adolescents includes Chapati, dal, vegetables.
1 mark
✅ Answer
(ii) Chapati, dal, vegetables.
Q8
Notes on: (a) Adam's apple (b) Secondary sexual characters (c) Sex determination.
4 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Protruding voice box in throat of boys at puberty. (b) External features distinguishing male and female. (c) Male sperm carries X or Y; female egg has X. XX = Girl, XY = Boy.
Q9
Word grid puzzle on hormones.
2 marks
✅ Answer
INSULIN, THYROXINE, ADRENALIN, TESTOSTERONE, ESTROGEN, PITUITARY.
Q10
Height growth calculation table.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Calculation: (Present height (cm) / % of full height at that age) × 100.

Chapter 11: Force and Pressure

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 10 (Complete)

Q1
Two examples of push or pull changing motion.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Pushing a football (rest to motion); Pulling brakes on bicycle (motion to rest).
Q2
Two examples of force changing shape of object.
2 marks
✅ Answer
1. Pressing dough ball with hands. 2. Squeezing a rubber ball.
Q3
Fill in blanks: (a) pull (b) friction (c) contact (d) electrostatic.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) pull (b) friction (c) contact (d) electrostatic
Q4
Archer shooting arrow fill in blanks.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Muscular force, shape change, contact force, gravity.
Q5
Identify agent exerting force & object acted on: (a) Squeezing lemon (b) Toothpaste tube (c) Load suspended from spring (d) High jumper.
4 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Fingers on lemon (b) Hand on tube (c) Load on spring (d) Jumper's muscles on ground.
Q6
Blacksmith hammering hot iron... effect of force.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Muscular force changes shape of hot iron into desired tool.
Q7
Inflated balloon pressed against wall after rubbing with synthetic cloth.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Electrostatic force attracts charged balloon to wall.
Q8
Forces acting on plastic bucket of water held above ground.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Gravitational force (downwards) & Muscular force (upwards). Equal and opposite forces cancel out, so state of motion doesn't change.
Q9
Rocket fired upwards... two forces acting.
2 marks
