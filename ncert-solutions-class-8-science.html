Q4
Write a paragraph on: (a) Preparation of soil (b) Sowing (c) Weeding (d) Threshing
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

(a) Preparation of Soil: Soil loosening allows roots to breathe easily and penetrates deep. Microbes and earthworms multiply, turning soil into nutrient-rich humus.

(b) Sowing: Placing healthy clean seeds into soil at proper depth using seed drills.

(c) Weeding: Removing wild competitive plants (weeds) using khurpi or spraying 2,4-D weedicides.

(d) Threshing: Process of separating grain seeds from harvested crop stalks/chaff using combine harvester.

CBSE Marking Scheme
Each point fully explained with technical terms1 Mark each

Q12
'Avoid plastics as far as possible'. Comment on this advice.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Plastics pose severe environmental threats:

  • Non-Biodegradable Nature: Plastics take several hundred years to decompose naturally, accumulating in landfills.
  • Environmental & Air Pollution: Burning plastic releases toxic poisonous fumes into atmosphere causing severe air pollution.
  • Drain Clogging & Animal Hazard: Discarded plastic bags block municipal drainage lines and choke digestive tracts of cows and marine animals, causing death.
  • 5 R Principle: We should practice Refuse, Reduce, Reuse, Recycle, and Recover to minimize plastic hazard.

Q9
What happens when: (a) Dilute H2SO4 poured on copper plate (b) Iron nails placed in copper sulphate solution. Write word equations.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

(a) Dilute Sulphuric acid on Copper plate: Copper does not react with dilute sulphuric acid at room temperature. However, upon heating, copper reacts with concentrated sulphuric acid to release Hydrogen gas and form Copper sulphate.

Word Equation: Copper + Sulphuric acid (conc.) ➔ Copper sulphate + Water + Sulphur dioxide

(b) Iron nails in Copper sulphate solution: Iron is more reactive than copper in the reactivity series. Iron displaces copper from copper sulphate solution. The blue colour of copper sulphate fades to light green (iron sulphate), and reddish-brown copper deposits on iron nails.

Word Equation: Iron (Fe) + Copper sulphate (CuSO₄) ➔ Iron sulphate (FeSO₄) + Copper (Cu)

(a) Explanation + Word equation1½ Marks

Chapter 6: Combustion and Flame

Complete NCERT Exercise Solutions — CBSE Marking Scheme Format

Chemistry13 Questions
Q6
Make a labelled diagram of a candle flame.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

A candle flame consists of three distinct zones based on temperature and oxygen availability:

  • 1. Outermost Zone (Blue): Zone of complete combustion. High supply of oxygen. Hottest part of flame. Non-luminous.
  • 2. Middle Zone (Yellow): Zone of partial/incomplete combustion. Moderate heat. Luminous yellow zone containing unburnt glowing carbon particles.
  • 3. Innermost Zone (Black): Zone of unburnt wax vapours around the wick. Least hot zone.
CBSE Marking Scheme
Outermost blue zone (complete combustion, hottest)1 Mark
Middle yellow zone (partial combustion)1 Mark
Innermost dark zone (unburnt wax, least hot)1 Mark

Chapter 13: Sound

Complete NCERT Exercise Solutions — CBSE Marking Scheme Format

Physics13 Questions
Q5
A pendulum oscillates 40 times in 4 seconds. Find its time period and frequency.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Given:

  • Number of oscillations = 40
  • Total time taken = 4 seconds

1. Frequency (f): Frequency is the number of oscillations per second.

Frequency = Total Oscillations / Total Time = 40 / 4 = 10 Hz

2. Time Period (T): Time period is the time taken to complete 1 oscillation.

Time Period = 1 / Frequency = 1 / 10 = 0.1 seconds
CBSE Marking Scheme
Frequency formula & calculation (10 Hz)1½ Marks
Time period formula & calculation (0.1 s)1½ Marks

Chapter 16: Light

Complete NCERT Exercise Solutions — CBSE Marking Scheme Format

Physics17 Questions
Q4
State the laws of reflection of light.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

The two fundamental Laws of Reflection of light are:

  • First Law of Reflection: The angle of incidence (∠i) is always equal to the angle of reflection (∠r). (∠i = ∠r)
  • Second Law of Reflection: The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal to the reflecting surface at the point of incidence — all three lie in the same plane.
CBSE Marking Scheme
First Law statement (∠i = ∠r)1 Mark
