Chapter 1: Crop Production and Management

Complete NCERT Exercise Solutions — Questions 1 to 11

BiologyAgriculture11 Questions
Q1
Select the correct word from the list and fill in the blanks: (a) The same kind of plants grown and cultivated on a large scale at a place is called _______. (b) The first step before growing crops is _______ of the soil. (c) Damaged seeds would _______ on top of water. (d) For growing a crop, sufficient sunlight and _______ and _______ from the soil are essential.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) crop
(b) preparation
(c) float
(d) water and nutrients
Q2
Match items in Column A with those in Column B: (i) Kharif crops (ii) Rabi crops (iii) Chemical fertilisers (iv) Organic manure — with: (a) Food for cattle (b) Urea and super phosphate (c) Animal excreta, cow dung, urine and plant waste (d) Wheat, gram, pea (e) Paddy and maize
1 mark each
✅ Answer
  • (i) Kharif crops → (e) Paddy and maize
  • (ii) Rabi crops → (d) Wheat, gram, pea
  • (iii) Chemical fertilisers → (b) Urea and super phosphate
  • (iv) Organic manure → (c) Animal excreta, cow dung, urine and plant waste
Q3
Give two examples of each: (a) Kharif crop (b) Rabi crop
1 mark
✅ Answer
(a) Kharif crops: Paddy (rice) and Maize (also Cotton, Soybean).
(b) Rabi crops: Wheat and Gram (also Pea, Mustard).
Q4
Write a paragraph in your own words on each of the following: (a) Preparation of soil (b) Sowing (c) Weeding (d) Threshing
4 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Preparation of Soil: It is the first step in crop production. Soil is loosened and turned using a plough (tilling/ploughing). Loosening allows plant roots to penetrate deep, breathe easily, and promotes growth of earthworms and microbes that add humus. Large soil clods are broken using a leveller. (1 mark)

(b) Sowing: Process of placing clean, healthy, disease-free seeds in soil. High-yielding healthy seeds are selected (good seeds sink in water while damaged hollow seeds float). Sowing is done manually or using seed drills to ensure proper depth and distance between seeds. (1 mark)

(c) Weeding: Removal of unwanted wild plants (weeds) growing alongside crop plants. Weeds compete for water, nutrients, space, and light. Weeding is done manually using a khurpi or by spraying weedicides like 2,4-D before flowering. (1 mark)

(d) Threshing: Process of separating grain seeds from harvested crop chaff/stalks. Done manually by beating or mechanically using a thresher or combine machine. (1 mark)
CBSE Marking Scheme
Each paragraph (a)-(d)1 mark each

Explain how fertilisers are different from manure.

Q6
What is irrigation? Describe two methods of irrigation which conserve water.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Irrigation: Supply of water to crops at regular intervals. (1 mark)

Modern Water-Conserving Methods:
  • Sprinkler System: Perpendicular pipes with rotating nozzles spray water like rain. Best for uneven land and sandy soil. (1 mark)
  • Drip System: Water falls drop by drop directly near plant roots through narrow tubing. No water wastage; ideal for fruit plants and water-scarce regions. (1 mark)
Q7
If wheat is sown in the kharif season, what would happen? Discuss.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Wheat is a Rabi crop requiring cold weather, mild temperature, and moderate moisture. If sown in Kharif season (June–October):
1. Excessive rain water during monsoon will cause waterlogging, rotting the wheat seeds and roots.
2. High temperature and humidity will hinder growth and attract fungal infections/pests, leading to zero or poor yield.
Q8
Explain how soil gets affected by the continuous plantation of crops in a field.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Continuous cultivation depletes soil of specific essential nutrients (like N, P, K), leading to poor fertility, reduced crop yield, and degraded soil structure. To replenish nutrients, farmers must practice crop rotation (alternating with leguminous crops) or apply organic manure/fertilisers.
Q9
What are weeds? How can we control them?
3 marks
✅ Answer
Weeds: Undesirable plants that grow naturally along with main crops in a field (e.g. Amaranthus/Chaulai, Wild Oat/Javi). (1 mark)

Control Methods: (2 marks)
1. Tilling before sowing: Uproots and kills weeds, turning them into humus.
2. Manual removal: Uprooting or cutting close to ground using a khurpi or seed drill.
3. Chemical control: Spraying weedicides like 2,4-D diluted in water before weeds produce flowers/seeds.
Q10

✅ Answer
Correct Sequence:
(5) Preparation of soil ➔ (6) Ploughing the field ➔ (4) Sowing ➔ (7) Manuring ➔ (2) Irrigation ➔ (3) Harvesting ➔ (1) Sending crop to sugar factory

Chapter 2: Microorganisms: Friend and Foe

Complete NCERT Exercise Solutions — Questions 1 to 9

Q1
Fill in the blanks: (a) Microorganisms can be seen with the help of a _______. (b) Blue-green algae fix _______ directly from air to enhance fertility of soil. (c) Alcohol is produced with the help of _______. (d) Cholera is caused by _______.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) microscope
(b) nitrogen
(c) yeast
(d) bacteria (Vibrio cholerae)
Q2
Tick the correct answer: (a) Yeast is used in production of: (i) sugar (ii) alcohol (iii) hydrochloric acid (iv) oxygen. (b) The following is an antibiotic: (i) Sodium bicarbonate (ii) Streptomycin (iii) Alcohol (iv) Yeast. (c) Carrier of malaria-causing protozoan: (i) female Anopheles mosquito (ii) cockroach (iii) housefly (iv) butterfly. (d) The most common carrier of communicable diseases is: (i) ant (ii) housefly (iii) dragonfly (iv) spider. (e) The bread or idli dough rises because of: (i) heat (ii) grinding (iii) growth of yeast cells (iv) kneading. (f) The process of conversion of sugar into alcohol is called: (i) nitrogen fixation (ii) moulding (iii) fermentation (iv) infection.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) (ii) alcohol
(b) (ii) Streptomycin
(c) (i) female Anopheles mosquito
(d) (ii) housefly
(e) (iii) growth of yeast cells

Q3
Match the organisms in Column A with their action in Column B: (i) Bacteria (ii) Rhizobium (iii) Lactobacillus (iv) Yeast (v) A protozoan (vi) A virus — with: (a) Fixing nitrogen (b) Setting of curd (c) Baking of bread (d) Causing malaria (e) Causing cholera (f) Causing AIDS
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(i) Bacteria → (e) Causing cholera
(ii) Rhizobium → (a) Fixing nitrogen
(iii) Lactobacillus → (b) Setting of curd
(iv) Yeast → (c) Baking of bread
(v) A protozoan → (d) Causing malaria

Q4
Can microorganisms be seen with the naked eye? If not, how can they be seen?
2 marks
✅ Answer
No, microorganisms are microscopic and cannot be seen with the naked eye because of their extremely tiny size. They can be observed using a microscope (or a magnifying glass in case of bread mould fungi).
Q5
What are the major groups of microorganisms?
2 marks
✅ Answer
The four major groups of microorganisms are:
1. Bacteria (e.g. Lactobacillus, E. coli)
2. Fungi (e.g. Yeast, Bread mould, Penicillium)
3. Protozoa (e.g. Amoeba, Paramecium, Plasmodium)
4. Algae (e.g. Spirogyra, Chlamydomonas)
(Note: Viruses are also microscopic agents that reproduce inside host cells.)
Q6
Name the microorganisms which can fix atmospheric nitrogen in the soil.
1 mark
✅ Answer
Rhizobium bacteria (living in root nodules of legumes), Azotobacter, and Blue-green algae (cyanobacteria like Anabaena, Nostoc).
Q7
Write 10 lines on the usefulness of microorganisms in our lives.
5 marks
✅ Answer
1. Lactobacillus converts milk into curd.
2. Yeast ferments sugar into alcohol and wine.
3. Yeast produces CO₂ gas to make bread, cakes, and bhaturas soft and fluffy.
4. Antibiotics like Penicillin and Streptomycin are produced from fungi and bacteria to treat diseases.
5. Vaccines made from dead/weakened microbes protect humans from diseases like polio and smallpox.
6. Rhizobium and blue-green algae fix atmospheric nitrogen, enhancing soil fertility.
7. Decomposers break down dead organic waste into plant nutrients, keeping the environment clean.
8. Bacteria are used in the commercial production of acetic acid (vinegar).
9. Some bacteria live in human intestine aiding digestion and producing Vitamin K.
10. Microbes are used in sewage treatment and bio-fertiliser production.
Q8
Write a short paragraph on the harmful effects of microorganisms.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Harmful microorganisms (pathogens) cause diseases in humans, animals, and plants. In humans, bacteria cause tuberculosis, typhoid, and cholera; viruses cause cold, influenza, and polio; protozoa cause malaria and dysentery. In animals, anthrax is caused by bacteria. In plants, citrus canker and rust of wheat reduce crop yield. Additionally, microbes spoil food, milk, clothes, and leather by releasing toxic substances (food poisoning).
Q9
What are antibiotics? What precautions must be taken while taking antibiotics?
3 marks
✅ Answer
Antibiotics: Medicines manufactured from bacteria or fungi that kill or stop the growth of disease-causing bacteria. Example: Streptomycin, Tetracycline. (1 mark)

Precautions: (2 marks)
1. Take only on prescription by a qualified doctor.
2. Complete the full prescribed course; stopping early makes bacteria resistant.
3. Avoid taking antibiotics when not needed, as it kills beneficial gut bacteria.
4. Antibiotics are ineffective against viral infections like cold and flu.

Chapter 3: Synthetic Fibres and Plastics

Complete NCERT Exercise Solutions — Questions 1 to 15

ChemistryMaterials15 Questions
Q1
Explain why some fibres are called synthetic.
1 mark
✅ Answer
Fibres like nylon, polyester, and acrylic are called synthetic because they are man-made chemical polymers synthesised by human beings using petrochemicals, unlike natural fibres (cotton, wool, silk) obtained from plants or animals.
Q2
Mark the correct answer: Rayon is different from synthetic fibres because: (a) it has a silk-like appearance (b) it is obtained from wood pulp (c) its fibres can also be woven like those of natural fibres
1 mark
✅ Answer
(b) it is obtained from wood pulp (natural source cellulose chemically treated).
Q3
Fill in the blanks: (a) Synthetic fibres are also called _______ or _______ fibres. (b) Synthetic fibres are synthesised from raw material called _______. (c) Like synthetic fibres, plastic is also a _______.
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(a) artificial or man-made
(b) petrochemicals
(c) polymer
Q4
Give examples which indicate that nylon fibres are very strong.
2 marks
✅ Answer
A nylon thread is stronger than a steel wire of comparable thickness. Examples of high-strength uses:
1. Parachutes and ropes for rock climbing.
2. Fishing nets, seat belts, and tire cords.
Q5
Explain why plastic containers are favoured for storing food.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Plastic containers do not react with food, water, or air (non-corrosive). They are lightweight, strong, durable, inexpensive, and easily molded into various shapes and sizes.
Q6
Explain the difference between thermoplastic and thermosetting plastic.
3 marks
✅ Answer
ThermoplasticThermosetting Plastic
Deforms easily on heating and can be bent/re-molded repeatedly.Once molded, cannot be softened or re-molded by heating.
Ex: Polythene, PVCEx: Bakelite, Melamine
Q7
Explain why the following are made of thermosetting plastics: (a) Saucepan handles (b) Electric plugs/switches/plug boards
2 marks
✅ Answer
(a) Saucepan handles: Made of Bakelite because it is a poor conductor of heat and does not melt when exposed to high flames.
(b) Electric switches/plugs: Made of Bakelite/Melamine because it is a poor conductor of electricity (insulator) and fire resistant.
Q8
Categorise materials into 'can be recycled' and 'cannot be recycled': Telephone instruments, plastic toys, cooker handles, carry bags, ball point pens, plastic bowls, plastic covering on electric wires, plastic chairs, electrical switches.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Can be recycled (Thermoplastics): Plastic toys, carry bags, ball point pens, plastic bowls, plastic wire coverings, plastic chairs.
Cannot be recycled (Thermosetting): Telephone instruments, cooker handles, electrical switches.
Q9
Rana wants to buy shirts for summer. Should he buy cotton shirts or shirts made from synthetic material? Advise Rana, giving your reason.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Rana should buy cotton shirts. Cotton absorbs sweat well and allows air circulation through pores, evaporating sweat to cool the body. Synthetic shirts do not absorb sweat or let heat escape, making the wearer sticky and uncomfortable.
Q10
Give examples to show that plastics are non-corrosive in nature.
2 marks
✅ Answer
1. Corrosive chemicals like acids and cleaning agents (harpic/bleach) are stored in plastic bottles.
2. Water pipes are made of PVC because plastic does not rust when exposed to moisture and air.
Q11
Should the handle and bristles of a toothbrush be made of the same material? Explain your answer.
2 marks
✅ Answer
No. The bristles must be soft, flexible, and elastic (nylon) so they do not injure gums. The handle must be hard, rigid, and firm (hard plastic) to provide a comfortable grip.
Q12
'Avoid plastics as far as possible'. Comment on this advice.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Plastics are non-biodegradable and take hundreds of years to decompose. Burning plastic releases toxic fumes causing air pollution. Discarded plastic clogs drains, chokes soil, and kills animals that swallow it. We should follow the 5 Rs: Refuse, Reduce, Reuse, Recycle, Recover.
Q13
Match terms of Column A with phrases in Column B: (i) Polyester (ii) Teflon (iii) Rayon (iv) Nylon — with: (a) Prepared by using wood pulp (b) Used for making parachutes and stockings (c) Fabrics do not wrinkle easily (d) Used to make non-stick cookware
1 mark each
✅ Answer
(i) Polyester → (c) Fabrics do not wrinkle easily
(ii) Teflon → (d) Used to make non-stick cookware
(iii) Rayon → (a) Prepared by using wood pulp
(iv) Nylon → (b) Used for making parachutes and stockings
Q14
'Manufacturing synthetic fibres is actually helping conservation of forests'. Comment.
2 marks
✅ Answer
Natural fibres like cotton and rayon require cutting trees (wood pulp) or clear-cutting land. Synthetic fibres are made chemically from petrochemicals, reducing timber harvesting and forest destruction.
Q15
Describe an activity to show that thermoplastic is a poor conductor of electricity.
3 marks
✅ Answer
Connect a electric cell, an LED bulb, and copper wires to form a circuit. Leave a gap between wire ends. Insert a metal key in gap — bulb glows. Replace key with a plastic scale (thermoplastic) — bulb does NOT glow. This proves plastic does not conduct electric current.

Chapter 5: Coal and Petroleum

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 9

Q1
What are the advantages of using CNG and LPG as fuels?
2 marks
✅ Answer
Clean burning with minimal smoke/pollution, high calorific value, transported easily through cylinders/pipes.
Q3
Describe how coal is formed from dead vegetation. What is this process called?
3 marks
✅ Answer
300 million years ago, dense forests got buried under soil. Under high pressure and high temperature inside Earth, dead plants slowly converted to coal. This slow process is called carbonisation.

What is angle of incidence if reflected ray is at 90° to incident ray?
