Chapter 1: Crop Production and Management

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 11 (Complete)

Q1
Fill in the blanks: (a) Crop (b) Preparation (c) Float (d) Water, Nutrients
1 Mark Each
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) crop (b) preparation (c) float (d) water and nutrients
CBSE Marking Scheme
Each correct blank1 Mark each
Q2
Match items in Column A with Column B: (i) Kharif crops ➔ Paddy & maize, (ii) Rabi crops ➔ Wheat, gram, pea, (iii) Chemical fertilisers ➔ Urea & super phosphate, (iv) Organic manure ➔ Animal excreta, cow dung, plant waste.
1 Mark Each
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) Kharif crops ➔ Paddy and maize; (ii) Rabi crops ➔ Wheat, gram, pea; (iii) Chemical fertilisers ➔ Urea and super phosphate; (iv) Organic manure ➔ Animal excreta, cow dung, urine and plant waste.
Q3
Give two examples of each: (a) Kharif crop (b) Rabi crop
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) Kharif crops: Paddy (Rice) and Maize.
(b) Rabi crops: Wheat and Gram.
Q4
Write a paragraph on: (a) Preparation of soil (b) Sowing (c) Weeding (d) Threshing
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

(a) Soil Preparation: Loosening and turning soil using plough so roots penetrate deep and breathe. Promotes earthworms and friendly microbes adding humus.

(b) Sowing: Selection of clean, healthy seeds using water flotation test, then planting at proper depth using seed drills.

(c) Weeding: Removing wild competitive plants (weeds) manually with khurpi or spraying 2,4-D weedicides.

(d) Threshing: Separating harvested grain seeds from chaff/stalks using a thresher or combine harvester.

CBSE Marking Scheme
Each paragraph with technical terms1 Mark each
Q5
Explain how fertilisers are different from manure.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Fertilisers are factory-made inorganic chemical salts rich in specific N, P, K nutrients but add no humus. Manure is natural organic decomposed plant/animal waste providing rich humus and improving soil texture without causing water pollution.

Q6
What is irrigation? Describe two water-conserving irrigation methods.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Irrigation is artificial water supply to crops at regular intervals. Methods: (1) Drip System: Water drops directly at root zone via pipes (zero wastage). (2) Sprinkler System: Rotating nozzles spray water like rain (best for uneven land).

Q7
If wheat is sown in Kharif season, what would happen?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Wheat requires cold dry winter weather. In Kharif (monsoon), excess rain causes waterlogging, seed rot, and severe fungal pest attacks, destroying crop yield.
Q8
Explain how soil is affected by continuous crop plantation.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Depletes soil nutrients (N, P, K), rendering soil infertile. Restored by crop rotation with leguminous plants or applying manure.
Q9
What are weeds? How can we control them?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Undesirable wild plants in field. Controlled by tilling before sowing, manual uprooting (khurpi), or spraying weedicides (2,4-D).
Q10
Arrange in proper order: (1) Sugar factory (2) Irrigation (3) Harvesting (4) Sowing (5) Preparation of soil (6) Ploughing (7) Manuring
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
5 (Preparation of soil) ➔ 6 (Ploughing) ➔ 4 (Sowing) ➔ 7 (Manuring) ➔ 2 (Irrigation) ➔ 3 (Harvesting) ➔ 1 (Sugar factory)
Q11
Crossword puzzle: Down: 1. Irrigation 2. Storage 5. Crop. Across: 3. Harvester 4. Gram 6. Winnowing
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Down: 1. IRRIGATION, 2. STORAGE, 5. CROP. Across: 3. HARVESTER, 4. GRAM, 6. WINNOWING.

Chapter 6: Combustion and Flame

NCERT Exercise Questions 1 to 13 (Complete)

Q1
List conditions under which combustion takes place.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Fuel 2. Air/Oxygen 3. Ignition temperature.
Q2
Fill in blanks: (a) pollution (b) LPG (c) ignition temperature (d) water.
1 Mark Each
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) pollution (b) LPG (c) ignition temperature (d) water
Q3
CNG in automobiles reduced pollution. Explain.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
CNG burns cleanly leaving no unburnt carbon soot or harmful sulphur/nitrogen oxides.
Q4
Compare LPG and wood as fuels.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
LPG has higher calorific value (55,000 vs 17,000 kJ/kg), produces zero smoke/ash, and doesn't cause deforestation.
Q5
Give reasons: (a) Water not for electrical fires (b) LPG better fuel (c) Paper on aluminium pipe doesn't burn easily.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) Water conducts electricity risking electrocution. (b) LPG high heat, smoke-free. (c) Aluminium metal conducts heat away preventing paper reaching ignition temperature.
Q6
Labelled diagram of candle flame.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Outermost blue (complete, hottest) 2. Middle yellow (luminous, partial) 3. Innermost dark (unburnt wax).
Q7
Unit of calorific value.
1 Mark
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
kJ/kg (kilojoules per kilogram).
Q8
Explain how CO2 controls fire.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
CO2 is heavier than O2, blankets fire cutting off air supply, and cools fuel temperature.
Q9
Difficult to burn green leaves vs dry leaves.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Green leaves contain moisture absorbing heat and raising ignition temp. Dry leaves have no water catching fire instantly.
Q10
Zone of flame goldsmith uses & why.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Outermost blue non-luminous zone (hottest part with complete combustion).
Q11
Numerical: 4.5 kg fuel burnt, 180,000 kJ heat. Calculate calorific value.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Calorific value = Heat / Mass = 180,000 / 4.5 = 40,000 kJ/kg.
Q12
Can rusting be called combustion? Discuss.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Yes, slow oxidation producing heat, but no light/flame produced.
Q13
Abida (outer zone) vs Ramesh (yellow inner zone) water heating.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Abida's water heats faster because outermost blue zone is hottest.
