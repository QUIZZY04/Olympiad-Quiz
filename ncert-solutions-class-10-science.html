Chapter 1: Chemical Reactions and Equations

Balancing Equations, Types of Reactions (Combination, Decomposition, Displacement, Redox) & Everyday Effects — CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27

NCERT In-Text Questions (Page 6, 10, 13)
Question 1
Why should a magnesium ribbon be cleaned before burning in air?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Scientific Reason:

Magnesium is a highly reactive metal. When exposed to atmospheric air, it slowly reacts with atmospheric oxygen to form a thin, stable, and protective inert layer of basic magnesium oxide ($MgO$) and basic magnesium carbonate on its surface:

$$2\text{Mg}(s) + \text{O}_2(g) \longrightarrow 2\text{MgO}(s)$$

This protective oxide coating prevents further contact of the underlying magnesium metal with oxygen and inhibits ignition. Therefore, before burning in air, the ribbon must be thoroughly rubbed and cleaned with sandpaper to remove this inert oxide layer so that pure magnesium metal comes into direct contact with atmospheric oxygen, allowing it to ignite smoothly with a dazzling white flame.

Question 2
Write the balanced equation for the following chemical reactions: (i) Hydrogen + Chlorine → Hydrogen chloride, (ii) Barium chloride + Aluminium sulphate → Barium sulphate + Aluminium chloride, (iii) Sodium + Water → Sodium hydroxide + Hydrogen.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Balanced Chemical Equations with Physical State Symbols:

  1. (i) Hydrogen + Chlorine $\longrightarrow$ Hydrogen chloride:
    $$\text{H}_2(g) + \text{Cl}_2(g) \longrightarrow 2\text{HCl}(g)$$
  2. (ii) Barium chloride + Aluminium sulphate $\longrightarrow$ Barium sulphate + Aluminium chloride:
    $$3\text{BaCl}_2(aq) + \text{Al}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3(aq) \longrightarrow 3\text{BaSO}_4(s)\downarrow + 2\text{AlCl}_3(aq)$$ Note: Barium sulphate forms a dense white precipitate.
  3. (iii) Sodium + Water $\longrightarrow$ Sodium hydroxide + Hydrogen:
    $$2\text{Na}(s) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \longrightarrow 2\text{NaOH}(aq) + \text{H}_2(g)\uparrow + \text{Heat}$$

Question 3
A solution of a substance 'X' is used for white-washing. (i) Name the substance 'X' and write its formula. (ii) Write the reaction of the substance 'X' named in (i) above with water.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Identification and Reaction of Substance 'X':

  1. (i) Name and Formula:
    The substance 'X' used for white-washing is Calcium oxide (commonly called Quicklime).
    Chemical Formula: $\text{CaO}$.
  2. (ii) Chemical Reaction with Water:
    Quicklime reacts vigorously with liquid water in a highly exothermic reaction to produce slaked lime (calcium hydroxide) with the evolution of a large amount of heat and hissing sound: $$\text{CaO}(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \longrightarrow \text{Ca(OH)}_2(aq) + \text{Heat}$$
  3. Application Note:
    When slaked lime solution is applied to walls, it slowly reacts with carbon dioxide present in the air to form a thin, shiny layer of calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) over 2 to 3 days: $$\text{Ca(OH)}_2(aq) + \text{CO}_2(g) \longrightarrow \text{CaCO}_3(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$

Question 4
Why is the amount of gas collected in one of the test tubes in the electrolysis of water double of the amount collected in the other? Name this gas.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Scientific Explanation:

In the electrolysis of water, water molecules ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) are decomposed into hydrogen gas and oxygen gas by passing an electric current through acidulated water:

$$2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \xrightarrow{\text{Electric current}} 2\text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$
  1. Mole Ratio: According to the balanced chemical equation and Avogadro's Law (equal volumes of gases contain equal numbers of molecules at the same temperature and pressure), 2 moles (or 2 volumes) of hydrogen gas are liberated for every 1 mole (or 1 volume) of oxygen gas.
  2. Electrode Reactions:
    • At Cathode (negative electrode): Hydrogen ions gain electrons: $2\text{H}^+ + 2e^- \longrightarrow \text{H}_2(g)$ [Volume = 2 units].
    • At Anode (positive electrode): Oxygen gas is collected: $2\text{O}^{2-} \longrightarrow \text{O}_2(g) + 4e^-$ [Volume = 1 unit].
  3. Conclusion: The gas collected in double the volume is Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$) because water contains hydrogen and oxygen atoms chemically combined in a fixed $2 : 1$ atomic ratio by volume.

Question 5
Why does the colour of copper sulphate solution change when an iron nail is dipped in it?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Observation and Chemical Mechanism:

When an iron nail is placed in blue-coloured copper sulphate solution, a single displacement reaction occurs because Iron ($\text{Fe}$) is more reactive than Copper ($\text{Cu}$) in the electrochemical reactivity series.

Chemical Reaction:

$$\text{Fe}(s) + \text{CuSO}_4(aq) \longrightarrow \text{FeSO}_4(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$$ $$\text{(Grey iron nail)} + \text{(Blue solution)} \longrightarrow \text{(Pale green solution)} + \text{(Reddish-brown deposit)}$$

Observable Changes:

  1. The characteristic deep blue colour of copper sulphate fades gradually and transforms into a light pale green solution due to the formation of ferrous sulphate ($\text{FeSO}_4$).
  2. A shining reddish-brown coating of displaced metallic copper gets deposited on the surface of the iron nail.

Question 6
Why is respiration considered an exothermic reaction? Explain with balanced chemical equation.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Detailed Biochemical Explanation:

Respiration is considered an exothermic reaction because energy is released during the metabolic breakdown of food inside living cells.

Mechanistic Steps:

  1. During digestion, carbohydrates consumed in our diet (such as rice, potatoes, bread) are broken down into simpler sugars, predominantly glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$).
  2. This glucose is absorbed into the bloodstream and delivered to tissue cells where it combines with inhaled oxygen in the presence of cellular enzymes.
  3. The glucose undergoes slow, controlled combustion (cellular respiration) to form carbon dioxide, water, and releases metabolic energy in the form of ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate): $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6(aq) + 6\text{O}_2(aq) \longrightarrow 6\text{CO}_2(aq) + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{Energy (38 ATP)}$$
  4. Since substantial energy is liberated to maintain vital cellular functions and body temperature, cellular respiration is classified as an exothermic process.

Question 7
Why are decomposition reactions called the opposite of combination reactions? Write equations for decomposition reactions where energy is supplied in the form of heat, light and electricity.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

1. Fundamental Comparison:

  • A Combination reaction is a chemical reaction in which two or more simple reactants (elements or compounds) combine together to form a single, more complex product: $$A + B \longrightarrow AB + \text{Heat (usually exothermic)}$$
  • A Decomposition reaction is a chemical reaction in which a single complex reactant breaks down into two or more simpler substances upon the absorption of external energy (heat, light, or electricity): $$AB + \text{Energy} \longrightarrow A + B \text{ (always endothermic)}$$
  • Thus, decomposition reactions are exact chemical and thermodynamic opposites of combination reactions.

2. Three Modes of Decomposition Reactions with Balanced Equations:

  1. Thermal Decomposition (Energy supplied as Heat $\Delta$):
    Heating green crystals of ferrous sulphate breaks it down into ferric oxide, sulphur dioxide, and sulphur trioxide: $$2\text{FeSO}_4(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3(s) + \text{SO}_2(g) + \text{SO}_3(g)$$ Opposite combination example: $2\text{Mg}(s) + \text{O}_2(g) \longrightarrow 2\text{MgO}(s)$.
  2. Electrolytic Decomposition (Energy supplied as Electricity):
    Passing electric current through molten sodium chloride or acidulated water: $$2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \xrightarrow{\text{Electric current}} 2\text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$ Opposite combination example: $2\text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \longrightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$.
  3. Photolytic Decomposition (Energy supplied as Sunlight $h\nu$):
    White silver chloride turns grey in sunlight due to decomposition into silver and chlorine gas: $$2\text{AgCl}(s) \xrightarrow{\text{Sunlight}} 2\text{Ag}(s) + \text{Cl}_2(g)$$ Opposite combination example: $2\text{Ag}(s) + \text{Cl}_2(g) \longrightarrow 2\text{AgCl}(s)$.

Question 8
In the reaction: $\text{CuO} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Cu} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$, identify the substance oxidised, substance reduced, oxidising agent and reducing agent.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Concept of Redox (Reduction-Oxidation) Reactions:

  • Oxidation: Gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen (or loss of electrons).
  • Reduction: Loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen (or gain of electrons).
  • Oxidising Agent: The substance that gives oxygen or removes hydrogen (gets reduced).
  • Reducing Agent: The substance that removes oxygen or adds hydrogen (gets oxidised).

Analysis of the Reaction:

$$\text{CuO}(s) + \text{H}_2(g) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Cu}(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$
  1. Substance Oxidised: $\text{H}_2$ gains oxygen to form $\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Therefore, Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$) is oxidised.
  2. Substance Reduced: $\text{CuO}$ loses oxygen to form metallic $\text{Cu}$. Therefore, Copper(II) oxide ($\text{CuO}$) is reduced.
  3. Oxidising Agent: $\text{CuO}$ provides the oxygen and causes oxidation of $\text{H}_2$. Hence, $\text{CuO}$ is the oxidising agent.
  4. Reducing Agent: $\text{H}_2$ abstracts oxygen from $\text{CuO}$. Hence, $\text{H}_2$ is the reducing agent.

Case Study Question • Rancidity & Food Preservation
A food technologist observed that potato chips fried in edible vegetable oil develop an unpleasant foul smell and rancid taste when left in open containers for 3 weeks, whereas chips sealed in commercial bags remain crisp and fresh for 6 months. (i) Name the chemical phenomenon responsible for spoilage. (ii) Which gas is flushed into chips packets to prevent this change and why? (iii) Name two antioxidant additives used in fatty food preservation.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Chemical Phenomenon: Rancidity (specifically oxidative rancidity). Fats and oils in open air undergo slow oxidation by atmospheric oxygen to form volatile carboxylic acids, aldehydes, and ketones with rancid odor.
  2. (ii) Gas Used: Nitrogen gas ($\text{N}_2$). Nitrogen is an unreactive, inert gas at room temperature due to its strong triple covalent bond ($N \equiv N$). Flushing with nitrogen expels atmospheric oxygen and provides an inert atmosphere that prevents oxidation of fats.
  3. (iii) Antioxidant Additives:
    • BHA: Butylated Hydroxyanisole
    • BHT: Butylated Hydroxytoluene
