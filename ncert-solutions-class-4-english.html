Chapter 1: Together We Can

Inspirational Poem on Teamwork, Unity, Community Cooperation & National Progress

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • The power of collective effort: how small individual actions combine to achieve great things.
  • Metaphor of drops of water forming a mighty ocean and grains of sand creating pleasant land.
  • Appreciating cultural diversity and working hand-in-hand for societal welfare.
  • Poetic devices: rhyming words, rhythm, and motivational theme.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 What makes the mighty ocean according to the poem 'Together We Can'? How does the poet compare small acts of kindness to nature's elements? Let us Think • Comprehension 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
According to the poem, little drops of water come together to form the mighty ocean, and tiny grains of sand combine to make the pleasant, fertile land.

The poet uses these natural examples to illustrate that no deed of kindness or cooperation is ever too small. Just as countless tiny drops create an immense ocean, small individual acts of love, honesty, and helpfulness by citizens join together to make our communities happy and our country great and prosperous.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Mentions little drops of water and grains of sand 1.5 M
Explains metaphor of individual kindness building a mighty society 1.5 M
Q2 What makes the earth like heaven above according to the poem? Let us Think • Comprehension 2 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
According to the poem, little deeds of kindness and little words of love make the earth happy and peaceful, transforming our world to resemble heaven above.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (2 Marks):
Identification of little deeds of kindness 1.0 M
Identification of words of love creating harmony 1.0 M
Q3 Find pairs of rhyming words from the poem and use each in an original sentence: (a) Sand - Land, (b) Make - Take, (c) Love - Above. Let us Learn Words • Rhyming Words 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
(a) Sand - Land:
• Sand: The children built high castles with wet sand on the beach.
• Land: India is a blessed land of rivers, mountains, and colorful traditions.

(b) Make - Take:
• Make: Hard work and patience make all our dreams come true.
• Take: We should always take care of our grandparents when they are unwell.

(c) Love - Above:
• Love: A mother's unconditional love brings comfort to the family.
• Above: The bright morning sun shone high above the green hills.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Correct pairs of rhyming words identified 1.5 M
Original and grammatically correct sentences 1.5 M
Q4 In the lesson, children from different states share their cultural greetings and festivals. Name any three traditional festivals celebrated across different regions of India and state one common value they all share. Let us Speak • Unity in Diversity 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
1. Bihu (Assam): Celebrates the harvest season, nature's bounty, and gratitude through community dancing and feasts.
2. Pongal / Makar Sankranti (Tamil Nadu & North India): Honors the Sun God and farmers for nourishing crops.
3. Onam (Kerala): Commemorates King Mahabali with floral rangolis (Pookkalam) and community solidarity.

Common Value: All Indian festivals foster brotherhood, sharing food with the needy, respecting nature, and rejoicing in unity despite diverse languages.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Three diverse regional harvest festivals named 1.5 M
Explanation of shared universal values of unity and gratitude 1.5 M
Q5 Design a classroom 'Kindness Wall' activity. List three small acts of helpfulness that Class 4 students can carry out every day to support their classmates. Let us Do • Teamwork Activity 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Classroom Kindness Wall Procedure:
1. Sharing Learning Materials: Lending an extra pencil, eraser, or ruler to a benchmate who forgot theirs.
2. Welcoming & Assisting: Helping a classmate who missed school due to illness by sharing notebook notes and explaining missed lessons.
3. Keeping Surroundings Clean: Picking up paper scraps together after lunch and keeping classroom desks tidy without waiting for the helper.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Three practical classroom cooperation deeds described 1.5 M
Clear emphasis on mutual empathy and inclusivity 1.5 M

Community Cleanliness Drive in Shanti Nagar

Real-World Case Scenario: The residents of Shanti Nagar noticed that their neighborhood park was littered with plastic wrappers and dry branches after a storm. Instead of waiting for municipality workers, 15 school children formed 'Team Eco-Warriors' and spent two hours on Sunday morning cleaning the flowerbeds and jogging tracks.

Analytical Task: How does the initiative of Team Eco-Warriors reflect the core message of 'Together We Can'? State two benefits of collaborative community work over individual efforts.

Reflection of Core Message: Just as 'little drops of water make a mighty ocean,' individual children joining hands turned an overwhelming clean-up task into an enjoyable two-hour triumph.

Two Benefits:
1. Division of Labor & Speed: Tasks that take one person several days are accomplished swiftly when divided among motivated team members.
2. Community Ownership: Working together instills pride in shared public spaces and inspires adults and neighbors to keep the environment clean.

Chapter 2: The Tinkling Bells

Heartwarming Story on Honesty, Moral Integrity, Empathy & the Joy of Righteous Living

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • Story of young Chinna, who loses his grandfather's money meant for buying cattle bells.
  • The moral dilemma: An honest mistake by a fruit seller gives Chinna extra change.
  • The golden virtue of honesty: Returning what does not belong to you brings genuine peace and self-respect.
  • Parental guidance and how positive reinforcement nurtures upright character in children.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 Answer the following from the story: (a) Why was Chinna upset when he returned home? (b) How did his mother Kamala comfort him? Let us Think • Comprehension 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
(a) Chinna was deeply upset and crying because he had lost the money that his beloved Dadaji (grandfather) had given him to buy brass tinkling bells for their cow.

(b) His mother Kamala gently wiped away his tears, spoke comforting words, and promised that she would give him money to buy the bells so he wouldn't feel sorrowful.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Explains loss of Dadaji's bell money 1.5 M
Describes mother Kamala's comforting reaction and promise 1.5 M
Q2 What mistake did the fruit seller make, and how did Chinna react when he realized it? Let us Think • Comprehension 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
The fruit seller accidentally handed Chinna ten rupees extra while returning his change after buying fruit.

Although Chinna was tempted for a moment because he needed money for the bells, his conscience reminded him that keeping another person's hard-earned money was dishonest. He immediately walked back and returned the ten-rupee coin to the fruit seller.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Mentions fruit seller giving ₹10 extra by mistake 1.5 M
Explains Chinna's moral decision to return the money 1.5 M
Q3 Write 'T' for True and 'F' for False: (1) Chinna wanted to buy bells to attach to his bicycle. (2) Kamala comforted Chinna when he was crying. (3) The fruit seller gave Chinna twenty rupees extra. (4) Chinna returned the extra money to the fruit seller. (5) The fruit seller was angry when Chinna returned the money. Let us Think • True or False 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
(1) False — Chinna wanted bells for the family cow, not a bicycle.
(2) True — His mother Kamala lovingly consoled him.
(3) False — The fruit seller gave ten rupees extra, not twenty.
(4) True — Chinna honestly returned the ten-rupee coin.
(5) False — The fruit seller was very happy, blessed Chinna, and praised his integrity.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
All 5 statements evaluated with correct factual justifications 3.0 M
Q4 Why did Chinna decide to return the extra money instead of keeping it? What would have happened if he had kept it? Let us Think • Think & Discuss 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Chinna decided to return the money because he remembered his own deep sadness when he lost his coins. He understood that the fruit seller had worked hard all day, and losing ten rupees would cause him financial distress.

If Chinna had kept the money, he would have suffered from guilt and a troubled conscience, knowing that he had acquired bells through dishonesty rather than righteous effort.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Relates Chinna's empathy to his own experience of loss 1.5 M
Explains consequence of guilt versus peace of honest conduct 1.5 M

Sentence: Rohan was filled with immense joy when he found his missing geometry box under his desk.

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Lost Wallet on the School Playground

Real-World Case Scenario: During recess, 9-year-old Kabir found a leather wallet containing ₹500 and an identity card near the basketball court. His friends advised him to spend the money at the school canteen. Instead, Kabir immediately marched to the Principal's office and deposited the wallet.

Analytical Task: Which character from 'The Tinkling Bells' does Kabir's behavior parallel? State two character strengths demonstrated by Kabir in this situation.

Character Parallel: Kabir's upright conduct directly mirrors Chinna, who refused to keep the fruit seller's extra ₹10 despite needing money for bells.

Two Character Strengths:
1. Integrity under Peer Pressure: Kabir rejected the temptation of instant gratification and peer encouragement to spend the money.
2. Empathy & Responsibility: He realized the owner of the wallet would be worried and took immediate administrative action to restore the lost property.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: 1 Mark for identifying parallel to Chinna; 1 Mark for two character strengths (integrity and empathy).

Chapter 3: Be Smart, Be Safe

Essential Civic Awareness, Road Safety Rules, Pedestrian Guidelines & Emergency Preparedness

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • The four cardinal rules of pedestrian street crossing: Stop, Look Right, Look Left, Look Right Again.
  • Importance of zebra crossings, pedestrian footpaths, and pedestrian traffic signals.
  • Safety on public roads: Holding an adult's hand, avoiding running across traffic, and avoiding blind corners.
  • Traffic signal lights: Red (Stop), Amber/Yellow (Wait/Get Ready), Green (Go).
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 What four essential steps must you always follow before crossing any busy road? Let us Think • Comprehension 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
1. Stop: Stand safely on the footpath or curb at the edge of the zebra crossing.
2. Look Right: Check for incoming traffic from your right side.
3. Look Left: Check for vehicles approaching from the left.
4. Look Right Again: Take a final look to the right to verify that all vehicles have stopped or the road is completely clear before walking across.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Correct sequence of Stop, Look Right, Look Left, Look Right Again 2.0 M
Mentions crossing only when traffic is stopped or road is clear 1.0 M
Q2 Why should children hold an adult's hand while crossing roads, and why is playing on the footpath prohibited? Let us Think • Comprehension 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Holding an Adult's Hand: Adults have better judgment of vehicle speeds, higher visual vantage points, and can shield children from sudden maneuvers by reckless drivers.

Not Playing on Footpaths: Footpaths are meant exclusively for safe walking. Playing tag or ball games on footpaths creates distractions, risking a child or ball rolling into oncoming traffic and causing severe accidents.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Scientific explanation of adult traffic assessment and guidance 1.5 M
Safety hazards of playing near live road traffic 1.5 M
Q3 Match each traffic light color with its official rule: (a) Red, (b) Amber / Yellow, (c) Green. What does a green pedestrian walking figure indicate? Let us Learn Words • Traffic Signs 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
(a) Red: Stop completely behind the stop line.
(b) Amber / Yellow: Slow down and prepare to stop; do not accelerate to beat the light.
(c) Green: Go forward if the intersection is clear.

Green Pedestrian Figure: Indicates that vehicular traffic is halted and pedestrians may safely cross the road using the zebra crossing.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Correct meanings of Red, Amber, and Green signals 1.5 M
Explanation of green pedestrian walking symbol 1.5 M
Q4 What should you do if there is no zebra crossing or traffic signal nearby when you need to cross the road? Let us Think • Think & Discuss 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
If no zebra crossing exists nearby:
1. Choose a straight stretch of road where you can see vehicles clearly in both directions (never cross at sharp turns or behind parked buses).
2. Wait patiently on the pavement until there is a wide gap in traffic.
3. Check right, left, and right again.
4. Walk straight across quickly and alertly without running or looking at mobile devices.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Avoids blind spots / sharp bends / parked vehicles 1.5 M
Calm, vigilant walking procedure described 1.5 M

School Gate Traffic Congestion

Real-World Case Scenario: At 2:00 PM, several auto-rickshaws and parents' two-wheelers crowd right outside the school gate. A 4th-grade student, Aryan, drops his water bottle on the road and rushes into the middle of the street between parked vans to retrieve it.

Two Safety Mistakes:

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Exam Preparation vs. Mobile Notifications

Real-World Case Scenario: While revising science definitions for tomorrow's unit test, Rohan keeps his mother's smartphone next to his textbook. Every three minutes, he glances at notification chimes, replies to chat messages, and re-reads the same paragraph five times without remembering it.

Analytical Task: Which advice from M.A. Stodart's poem does Rohan fail to practice? What specific advice would you give Rohan to improve his test recall?

Poem Rule Violated: Rohan violates the fundamental rule: 'Work while you work, play while you play; that is the way to be cheerful and gay.' By trying to study and chat simultaneously, his work is being 'done by halves.'

Actionable Advice: Rohan should switch off the smartphone or place it in another room for 45 minutes, focus entirely on revising one science topic with full 'might,' and only check messages after completing his revision goals.

Chapter 5: The Old Stag

Touching Forest Fable on Empathy, Ecological Harmony, Repentance & Selfless Care

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • The wise and affectionate old stag who lived peacefully on a green hillock.
  • The stag falls sick and cannot walk down to meet his woodland friends.
  • Thoughtless grazing: Visiting animals eat up the hillock's tender grass, leaving the stag without food.
  • Moral awakening: Realizing their thoughtlessness, the animals nurse the hillock back to life and feed the stag.
  • Environmental message: Caring for community resources and protecting the green cover of nature.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 Why was the old stag so popular among all the forest animals and birds? Why could he not go down the hillock to meet them? Let us Think • Comprehension 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Popularity: The old stag was popular because of his kind, gentle, and loving nature. Every morning, he warmly greeted every animal, bird, and insect, showing genuine affection to all creatures.

Inability to Walk: In his advanced age, the stag fell severely ill and grew too weak to walk down the steep hillock to the river and meadows.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Describes kind, welcoming nature of the stag 1.5 M
Explains sickness and physical weakness preventing descent 1.5 M
Q2 Why did the lush green cover of the hillock slowly disappear? What tragedy did this cause for the sick stag? Let us Think • Comprehension 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
When the animals heard of the stag's illness, they flocked to visit him on the hillock. While standing and chatting, each visiting animal thoughtlessly nibbled on the fresh, tender grass.

Because hundreds of animals visited day after day, the entire green cover of the hillock was completely eaten away. Consequently, the weak stag had no food left near his resting place and began starving.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Explains visitors thoughtlessly grazing during visits 1.5 M
Identifies severe consequence of starvation for the stag 1.5 M
Q3 How did the forest animals realize their mistake, and what constructive steps did they take to help the stag recover? Let us Think • Comprehension 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
The animals realized their mistake when they saw the barren, brown hillock and noticed that the sick stag was shivering and weak from hunger. Overcome with deep remorse, they took immediate action:
1. They stopped all grazing on the hillock to let the soil rest and the grass regrow.
2. Every visiting animal started bringing fresh tender leaves, juicy fruits, and clear water from the valley to feed the stag until he regained his strength.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Identification of animals' guilt upon seeing barren hillock 1.5 M
Two restorative actions: halting grazing and bringing fresh food 1.5 M
Q4 What would happen to the natural environment around us if each human thought only of their immediate selfish needs? Let us Think • Think & Discuss 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
If every person considered only their immediate personal desires without thinking of others, our forests would be cut down, clean rivers would be polluted with plastic waste, and green pastures would turn into barren deserts.

Just like the animals on the hillock, selfish consumption destroys the ecosystem that sustains all life, leaving no resources for future generations or vulnerable living beings.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Relates animal parable to human environmental degradation 1.5 M
Highlights long-term ecological balance and intergenerational equity 1.5 M

Chapter 6: Braille

Inspiring Biography of Louis Braille, Tactile Literacy, Inclusion & Overcoming Adversity

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • The early childhood of Louis Braille in Coupvray, France, and his tragic eye injury in his father's workshop.
  • The burning desire of visually impaired students to read and learn independently.
  • Captain Charles Barbier's military code ('Night Writing') and its shortcomings.
  • Louis Braille's breakthrough: Creating a 6-dot tactile code representing letters, numbers, and music.
  • Empathy, disability awareness, and the universal symbol of the white cane.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 Describe the Braille system of reading and writing. How do visually impaired individuals use it to read books? Let us Think • Comprehension 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
The Braille system is a tactile reading and writing method based on an embossed code of raised dots arranged inside rectangular cells.

Each cell has six dot positions arranged in two vertical columns of three dots each. Different combinations of raised dots represent letters of the alphabet, punctuation marks, numbers, and musical notes. Visually impaired individuals lightly glide their sensitive fingertips across the raised dots from left to right to read fluently.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Explains matrix of 6 raised dots per cell 1.5 M
Describes fingertip touch reading from left to right 1.5 M
Q2 Why was Captain Charles Barbier's 'Night Writing' system not successful among blind children? Let us Think • Comprehension 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Captain Barbier's 'Night Writing' was originally developed for soldiers in battle to read messages in the dark without lighting lamps. However, it was not successful for general reading because:
1. It was based on complex sound codes rather than letters of the alphabet.
2. It used 12 raised dots per cell, which was too large to fit underneath the pad of a single finger without moving up and down, making reading slow and clumsy.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Explains military origins of Night Writing 1.0 M
Cites 12-dot complexity and inability to cover with one fingertip 2.0 M
Q3 What major improvement did young Louis Braille introduce to make reading simple, elegant, and efficient? Let us Think • Comprehension 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Louis Braille simplified the 12-dot military system down to a compact 6-dot cell. This revolutionary improvement meant that every single letter could be felt and recognized instantly under the tip of a single finger in one touch, drastically speeding up reading and allowing blind students to write easily using a slate and stylus.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Reduction from 12 dots to 6 dots 1.5 M
Enables instant recognition under a single fingertip 1.5 M
Q4 Why is the mobility cane used by visually challenged individuals always white in color? What difficulties would a person face if the world had no light? Let us Think • Think & Discuss 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
White Cane Purpose: The cane is painted bright white (often with a red reflective stripe) so that motorists, cyclists, and pedestrians can spot it clearly from afar, immediately recognizing that the traveler is visually challenged and according them safety and right-of-way.

World Without Light: Without light, people would struggle with spatial navigation, reading printed books, identifying family members by appearance, avoiding physical obstacles, and recognizing distant hazards.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Explains high visibility and international recognition of white cane 1.5 M
Reflects thoughtfully on sensory reliance in absence of sight 1.5 M
Q5 Conduct a tactile identification experiment: Blindfold yourself and describe how you can distinguish a coin, a key, an eraser, and a pencil solely through touch. Let us Do • Tactile Sensory Activity 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Psychological Metaphor of Pinocchio's Nose

Real-World Case Scenario: Modern behavioral scientists observe that when people tell deliberate lies, micro-expressions, increased blink rates, vocal pitch changes, and autonomic nervous system stress reactions involuntarily reveal the deceit.

Analytical Task: Why is Pinocchio's growing nose one of the greatest moral metaphors in children's literature? How does one lie inevitably lead to another?

The Metaphor: Pinocchio's nose externalizes an internal psychological truth: deceit cannot remain secret. Even if invisible physically, lying distorts a person's dignity and character visibly to those around them.

The Trap of Lies: To protect the first lie, a person must fabricate three more lies. Soon, the web of deception becomes so cumbersome and absurd (just like an enormous wooden nose) that confession and honest apology are the only way back to freedom.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: 1 Mark for explaining externalization of guilt; 1 Mark for compound snowball effect of continuous deceit.
