Unit 1: My Land

Chapter 1: Together We Can

Inspirational Poem on Teamwork, Unity, Community Cooperation & National Progress

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • The power of collective effort: how small individual actions combine to achieve great things.
  • Metaphor of drops of water forming a mighty ocean and grains of sand creating pleasant land.
  • Appreciating cultural diversity and working hand-in-hand for societal welfare.
  • Poetic devices: rhyming words, rhythm, and motivational theme.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 What makes the mighty ocean according to the poem 'Together We Can'? How does the poet compare small acts of kindness to nature's elements? Reading Comprehension • Poetic Analysis 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
According to the poem, little drops of water come together to form the mighty ocean, and tiny grains of sand combine to make the pleasant, fertile land.

The poet uses these natural examples to illustrate that no deed of kindness or cooperation is ever too small. Just as countless tiny drops create an immense ocean, small individual acts of love, honesty, and helpfulness by citizens join together to make our communities happy and our country great and prosperous.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Mentions little drops of water and grains of sand 1.5 M
Explains metaphor of individual kindness building a mighty society 1.5 M
Q2 Find pairs of rhyming words from the poem and use each in an original sentence: (a) Sand - Land, (b) Make - Take, (c) Love - Above. Vocabulary & Rhyming Words 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
(a) Sand - Land:
• Sand: The children built high castles with wet sand on the beach.
• Land: India is a blessed land of rivers, mountains, and colorful traditions.

(b) Make - Take:
• Make: Hard work and patience make all our dreams come true.
• Take: We should always take care of our grandparents when they are unwell.

(c) Love - Above:
• Love: A mother's unconditional love brings comfort to the family.
• Above: The bright morning sun shone high above the green hills.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Identifies correct rhyming pairs 1.5 M
Constructs grammatically accurate contextual sentences 1.5 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Classroom Cleanliness Drive Cooperation

Real-World Case Scenario: During the Swachh Bharat hour, some students refuse to pick up dry paper wrappers because 'someone else dropped them'. The class monitor recites lines from 'Together We Can'.

Analytical Task: How does the message of the poem help resolve this conflict? What should the students do?

The poem teaches that when everyone contributes a tiny bit of effort, massive tasks become effortless. Instead of arguing about who dropped the waste, every student should pick up two pieces of paper. Within two minutes, the entire classroom becomes spotless. This proves that cooperation transforms community spaces.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: 1.5 Marks for connecting poem's theme; 1.5 Marks for practical cooperative resolution.

Chapter 2: The Tinkling Bells

Heartwarming Village Story of Rural Cattle Care, Traditional Bells & Animal Empathy

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • The role of domestic cattle in Indian agricultural life and rural traditions.
  • Traditional brass cattle bells (ghungroos) used by farmers to locate their animals in meadows.
  • Empathy and affection between a village boy and his pet calf.
  • Understanding that caring for animals is an integral aspect of Indian cultural ethos.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 Why did the farmer tie tinkling brass bells around the neck of his cattle? How did the sound of the bell help Ramu find his lost calf? Story Comprehension 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
The farmer tied sweet-sounding brass bells around the neck of his cattle for two important reasons:
1. To easily identify and track their location when they graze in deep village pastures and forested hills.
2. As a mark of affection and traditional celebration during harvest festivals.

When Ramu's calf strayed away at sunset, darkness had begun to fall. Ramu stopped and listened attentively. In the quiet evening breeze, he recognized the familiar rhythmic 'ting-a-ling' sound of his calf's special bell near the stream, which guided him directly to where the calf was standing safely under a banyan tree.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
States reasons for tying bells to cattle 1.5 M
Explains how the distinctive sound guided Ramu in the dusk 1.5 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Animal Welfare and Compassion

Real-World Case Scenario: During hot summer afternoons, stray cattle wander around concrete roads searching for drinking water and shade.

Analytical Task: Based on the values of 'The Tinkling Bells', what two thoughtful steps can school children take in their neighborhood to help domestic animals?

1. Children can place clean earthen or cement water troughs outside their houses and refill them daily with cool fresh water.
2. They can organize leftover fresh vegetable peels and clean green grass to feed stray cattle rather than dumping food inside plastic bags.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: 1.5 Marks for water bowl suggestion; 1.5 Marks for safe green fodder practice.

Chapter 3: Be Smart, Be Safe

Essential Road Safety Rules, Pedestrian Zebra Crossings, Traffic Signals & Personal Safety

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • The meaning of traffic signal colors: Red (Stop), Amber/Yellow (Wait/Get Ready), Green (Go).
  • Using designated zebra crossings, footbridges, and pedestrian subways to cross busy city roads.
  • The safety drill: 'Stop, Look Left, Look Right, and Look Left Again' before stepping onto the tarmac.
  • Wearing helmets while riding two-wheelers and fastening seat belts in cars.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 What is the four-step safety drill that students must follow before crossing a street? Why is it dangerous to run across the road suddenly? Safety Rules & Drill 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
The Four-Step Safety Drill:
1. STOP: Stand firmly on the pavement curb well away from the moving traffic edge.
2. LOOK RIGHT: Check for oncoming vehicles from the driver's approaching side.
3. LOOK LEFT: Check for vehicles approaching from the opposite lane.
4. LOOK RIGHT AGAIN: Confirm the road is still clear, and walk straight across calmly.

Why running across is dangerous:
Running can cause a student to trip and fall onto the roadway. Furthermore, drivers cannot predict sudden darting movements and may not have sufficient braking distance to stop their vehicles in time. Always walk steadily and watchfully.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Accurately lists the 4 steps (Stop, Look Right, Look Left, Look Right Again) 2.0 M
Explains braking distance and tripping risks of running 1.0 M

Chapter 4: One Thing at a Time

Classic Poem by M.A. Stodart on Concentration, Diligence & Meaningful Living

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • The principle of deep focus: doing one task with full heart and mind before moving to the next.
  • Balancing work and play: 'Work while you work, play while you play, that is the way to be happy and gay.'
  • Avoiding half-done tasks: 'Things done by halves are never done right.'
  • Valuing fleeting moments: making good use of every golden minute.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 What does the poet mean by 'Things done by halves are never done right'? How can a student apply this rule during home study? Poem Explanation & Moral Insight 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
The phrase means that if we attempt a task with half-hearted effort or split attention (like studying while watching television or checking a phone), the work will always remain incomplete, full of mistakes, and lacking quality.

Application to Home Study:
A student should establish a quiet study zone without gadgets. When doing maths homework, dedicate full mental focus for 45 uninterrupted minutes. Once the homework is finished to perfection, the student can go out to play sports freely with a relaxed and satisfied mind.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Explains meaning of 'done by halves' 1.5 M
Gives realistic study habit application 1.5 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Overcoming Distraction During Exam Preparation

Real-World Case Scenario: Aman sits with his science book open but constantly chats with his brother and glances at his mobile screen every two minutes. After two hours, he cannot recall a single definition.

Analytical Task: Identify Aman's mistake using the message of 'One Thing at a Time' and suggest a disciplined remedy.

Aman is guilty of divided attention ('doing things by halves'). Remedy: Put the mobile in another room, close the door, and set a timer for 30 minutes of silent reading. Focusing entirely on one page at a time will allow him to learn thoroughly in a fraction of the time.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: 1.5 Marks for identifying divided focus; 1.5 Marks for single-task study strategy.

Chapter 5: The Old Stag

Aesop's Fable on False Vanity, True Utility, Humility & Life Values

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • The classic moral tale: a stag admires his magnificent branching antlers but despises his thin, spindly legs.
  • The crisis: a pack of hunting hounds chases the stag through the forest.
  • His slender legs carry him swiftly to safety, but his grand antlers get entangled in thick overhead tree branches.
  • Moral lesson: We often value superficial beauty that can harm us, while despising practical strengths that protect our lives.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 Why was the stag proud of his horns but ashamed of his legs? How did his feelings change after escaping from the hunters? Story Analysis & Character Evolution 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
When the stag looked at his reflection in a clear forest pool, he was enchanted by his grand, branching antlers, comparing them to a royal crown. However, when he looked down at his long, thin, spindly legs, he felt deeply disappointed and ashamed, thinking they ruined his majestic appearance.

When a pack of hunting dogs attacked, his swift legs carried him across the open plain like the wind. But as he rushed into the dense woods, his grand antlers got tangled in the low hanging branches, trapping him until he struggled free. He realized with deep humility that the slender legs he despised had saved his life, while the ornate horns he worshipped almost caused his death.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Describes reflection at the pool and vanity 1.5 M
Explains realization in the woods (legs saved him, horns trapped him) 1.5 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Appreciating Inner Skills over Outer Appearance

Real-World Case Scenario: In a school debate, a student wears ordinary worn-out shoes but presents extraordinary arguments and scientific facts, winning the gold trophy.

Analytical Task: How does the story of 'The Old Stag' relate to this real-life school event?

The fable teaches that external glamour is superficial. Just as the stag's plain legs held real speed and power, the student's plain shoes did not diminish their brilliant intellect. True merit lies in competence, character, and usefulness, not in expensive showy attire.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: 1.5 Marks for relating fable to real-life merit; 1.5 Marks for articulation of core value.

Chapter 6: Braille

Inspirational Biography of Louis Braille & the Revolutionary Tactile Alphabet

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • The life of Louis Braille: accidental loss of sight in his father's workshop at age 3 in Coupvray, France.
  • His brilliant determination at the Royal Institute for Blind Youth in Paris.
  • Adapting military 'night writing' into an intuitive 6-dot embossed cell system.
  • How Braille empowers millions of visually challenged people globally to read, write, study music, and code.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 Who was Louis Braille? How did he invent the Braille script, and how do visually impaired persons read books using it? Biographical Comprehension & System Details 4 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Louis Braille was a French educator and inventor who lost his eyesight during early childhood due to an eye injury in his father's workshop.

Invention of Braille Script:
At the age of 15, Louis learned about a military code invented by Captain Charles Barbier called 'night writing', used by soldiers to read orders silently in darkness. Louis simplified this complex system into an elegant grid of 6 raised dots arranged in two columns of three dots each.

How Persons Read Braille:
Different combinations of raised dots represent different letters of the alphabet, punctuation marks, and numbers. Readers lightly glide their index fingertips across the embossed paper sheets to perceive the dot arrangements rapidly through touch.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (4 Marks):
Background of Louis Braille and age/origin 1.5 M
Description of the 6-dot grid system 1.5 M
Explanation of fingertip touch reading 1.0 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study (CBQ)

Inclusive Public Architecture

Real-World Case Scenario: Many modern metro train stations and lift buttons in India now feature raised Braille dots alongside standard printed numbers.

Analytical Task: Why is installing Braille in public elevators and train stations an essential requirement for a civilized society?

Installing Braille grants independence, dignity, and safety to visually impaired individuals. It allows them to navigate public transport, select correct floors in hospitals, and read emergency instructions without having to rely on strangers, fulfilling their right to equal accessibility.
CBSE Assessment Rubric: 1.5 Marks for highlighting independence and dignity; 1.5 Marks for public accessibility rights.

📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 Why is outdoor play superior to playing mobile games? List three benefits of practicing daily yoga for school students. Health & Lifestyle Analysis 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Why outdoor play is superior to mobile screens:
Outdoor sports engage the entire muscular and cardiovascular system, increasing lung capacity, heart strength, and bone density. Furthermore, running under sunlight provides Vitamin D and fosters face-to-face social bonds, whereas prolonged mobile gaming strains eyes, promotes a sedentary lifestyle, and causes mental fatigue.

Three Benefits of Daily Yoga:
1. Improves Concentration: Pranayama (breath control) calms nervous tension and sharpens memory.
2. Better Posture: Asanas like Tadasana and Bhujangasana correct slouching from sitting long hours at school desks.
3. Builds Flexibility & Resilience: Keeps muscles supple, preventing sports injuries.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):

Chapter 8: The Lagori Champions

Exciting Traditional Indian Sport: Pithu / Seven Stones, Rules & Team Camaraderie

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • Lagori (also known as Pithu or Lingorchya): a beloved traditional team game played across India.
  • Game mechanics: seven flat stones stacked in a tower, a rubber ball, two competing teams (Attackers and Defenders).
  • Tactics: knocking the pile down, dodging the ball while rebuilding the seven-stone tower, shouting 'LAGORI!'
  • Values of coordination, agility, honest sportsmanship, and celebrating traditional games.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 Describe the step-by-step rules of playing Lagori. How does a team score a win, and how can the defending team stop them? Game Rules & Strategy 4 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Rules of Lagori (Seven Stones):
1. Setup: Seven flat stones of decreasing sizes are stacked in a pyramid in the center of a circle. Two teams with equal players take positions.
2. Striking: An attacker throws a soft rubber ball from a fixed crease to topple the stone pyramid.
3. Rebuilding & Dodging: If the stones are knocked down, the attacking team rushes forward to restack all seven stones in proper size order. Meanwhile, the defending team retrieves the ball and tries to hit ('tag') any attacker below the waist with the ball.
4. Winning the Round: If the attackers rebuild the entire stack and shout 'LAGORI!' before any teammate is hit, they win 1 point. If the defenders hit an attacker before the stack is completed, the attackers are 'out' and teams switch roles.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (4 Marks):
Explains setup with 7 flat stones 1.0 M
Describes attacking vs defending roles accurately 2.0 M
Defines winning condition ('LAGORI!') and tagging out 1.0 M

Chapter 9: Hekko

Traditional Indigenous Game of Nagaland, Physical Agility & Cultural Heritage

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • Exploring traditional games from Northeast India: Hekko of Nagaland.
  • Focus on balance, single-leg hopping, agility, and upper-body control.
  • How indigenous games reflect geographical lifestyle and physical endurance.
  • Promoting unity in diversity by appreciating indigenous sporting cultures.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 What is Hekko, and which state of India does it originate from? How is the game played, and what physical skills does it test? Cultural Sports Appreciation 3 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Origin: Hekko is an indigenous traditional game originating from the vibrant hill state of Nagaland in Northeast India.

Gameplay:
The game is played inside a marked boundary or circular ring. Players hold one of their legs behind them with their hand and hop strictly on the other foot. Competitors attempt to nudge or push their opponent out of the boundary ring using only shoulder and body momentum, without dropping their raised foot to the ground.

Skills Tested:
It rigorously tests single-leg balance, muscular stamina, core strength, agility, and spatial alertness.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (3 Marks):
Correct state of origin (Nagaland) 1.0 M
Description of one-legged hopping and ring boundary 1.0 M
Lists skills tested (balance, stamina, agility) 1.0 M

Analytical Task: Why is plastic litter especially dangerous in high mountains, and what eco-friendly pledge should every visitor take?

Plastic takes hundreds of years to decompose and chokes delicate alpine streams, threatening wild animals and polluting the pure water sources that flow down to millions of people. Every visitor must take the pledge: 'Take nothing but memories, leave nothing but footprints, and bring all plastic waste back down to the plains.'

Chapter 12: Maheshwar

Historic Heritage Town on the Narmada, Ahilyabai Holkar & Handloom Weaving

Chapter Highlights, Theme, Vocabulary & Grammar Focus
  • Historical overview of Maheshwar: ancient capital situated on the sacred banks of the Narmada River in Madhya Pradesh.
  • The visionary leadership of Queen Ahilyabai Holkar: building stone ghats, temples, rest houses, and patronizing artisans.
  • The world-renowned heritage of Maheshwari sarees: unique geometric border designs inspired by temple carvings, silk-cotton weaving.
  • Appreciating Indian architectural monuments and traditional handloom crafts.
📖 NCERT Textbook Exercise Solutions, Comprehension & Grammar (100% Coverage)
Q1 Who was Rani Ahilyabai Holkar, and what great contributions did she make to the town of Maheshwar? What is unique about Maheshwari sarees? Historical & Cultural Heritage 4 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Rani Ahilyabai Holkar:
Rani Ahilyabai Holkar was the noble, pious, and courageous 18th-century ruler of the Malwa kingdom. She made Maheshwar her royal capital and turned it into a flourishing center of culture, spirituality, and trade.

Contributions to Maheshwar:
• She constructed the majestic stone Ahilya Fort and grand stepped bathing ghats along the sacred Narmada River.
• She invited master weavers from Surat, Malwa, and South India, giving them royal patronage to create the unique Maheshwari textile craft.

Unique features of Maheshwari Sarees:
Maheshwari sarees are woven from fine silk and cotton threads, making them lightweight and comfortable. Their distinctive reversible borders feature delicate geometric patterns (like checks, stripes, and floral triangles) inspired by the historic stone carvings on the temple walls of Maheshwar Fort.
📋 CBSE Official Step Marking Rubric (4 Marks):
Identifies Rani Ahilyabai Holkar and royal capital 1.5 M
Lists architectural and artisan patronage contributions 1.5 M

Q1 How did Miss Sullivan help Helen Keller understand that words have meaning? Describe the famous incident at the water pump. Biographical Comprehension 4 Marks
Model Answer & Step-by-Step Explanation:
Miss Sullivan was a patient and loving teacher. She gave Helen a doll and finger-spelled D-O-L-L into Helen's hand using the tactile manual alphabet. At first, Helen only imitated the finger movements without realizing words were names for objects.

The Water Pump Breakthrough:
One morning, Miss Sullivan took Helen to the garden water pump. As she pumped, a stream of icy cool water gushed over Helen's hand. At the exact same instant, Miss Sullivan spelled W-A-T-E-R into Helen's other palm. Suddenly, a spark of awareness ignited in Helen's mind. She realized that W-A-T-E-R was the name of the wonderful, cool, living substance flowing over her skin. That day, language entered Helen's world, unlocking her mind forever.

At every stage of the boy's life, the tree gave selflessly without asking for anything in return:
1. Childhood: The tree gave shade, leaves for crowns, and apples to eat; the boy climbed its trunk and swung from branches.
2. Youth: When the young man needed money, the tree urged: 'Pluck my apples and sell them in the market.'

The tree was happy because its love was completely pure and unconditional—its greatest joy was being useful and bringing comfort to the one it loved.
