8.1
NCERT Exercise
What are the hybridisation states of each carbon atom in the following compounds?
(a) $\text{CH}_2=\text{C}=\text{O}$
(b) $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2$
(c) $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CO}$
(d) $\text{CH}_2=\text{CHCN}$
(e) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Rule:
• Carbon with 4 $\sigma$ bonds (0 $\pi$ bonds) $\rightarrow \mathbf{sp^3}$ (tetrahedral, $109.5^\circ$)
• Carbon with 3 $\sigma$ bonds (1 $\pi$ bond) $\rightarrow \mathbf{sp^2}$ (trigonal planar, $120^\circ$)
• Carbon with 2 $\sigma$ bonds (2 $\pi$ bonds) $\rightarrow \mathbf{sp}$ (linear, $180^\circ$)

(a) Ketene ($\overset{1}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2=\overset{2}{\text{C}}=\text{O}$):
• $\text{C}_1$ forms $3\ \sigma$ bonds ($2$ with $\text{H}$, $1$ with $\text{C}$) and $1\ \pi$ bond $\implies \mathbf{sp^2}$
• $\text{C}_2$ forms $2\ \sigma$ bonds ($1$ with $\text{C}$, $1$ with $\text{O}$) and $2\ \pi$ bonds $\implies \mathbf{sp}$

(b) Propene ($\overset{3}{\text{C}}\text{H}_3-\overset{2}{\text{C}}\text{H}=\overset{1}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2$):
• $\text{C}_3$: $4\ \sigma$ bonds $\implies \mathbf{sp^3}$
• $\text{C}_2$: $3\ \sigma$ bonds, $1\ \pi$ bond $\implies \mathbf{sp^2}$
• $\text{C}_1$: $3\ \sigma$ bonds, $1\ \pi$ bond $\implies \mathbf{sp^2}$

(c) Acetone ($(\overset{1,3}{\text{C}}\text{H}_3)_2\overset{2}{\text{C}}=\text{O}$):
• The two methyl carbons ($\text{C}_1$ and $\text{C}_3$) form $4\ \sigma$ bonds each $\implies \mathbf{sp^3}$
• Carbonyl carbon ($\text{C}_2$) forms $3\ \sigma$ bonds and $1\ \pi$ bond ($=\text{O}$) $\implies \mathbf{sp^2}$

(d) Acrylonitrile ($\overset{3}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2=\overset{2}{\text{C}}\text{H}-\overset{1}{\text{C}}\equiv\text{N}$):
• $\text{C}_3$: $3\ \sigma$ bonds, $1\ \pi$ bond $\implies \mathbf{sp^2}$
• $\text{C}_2$: $3\ \sigma$ bonds, $1\ \pi$ bond $\implies \mathbf{sp^2}$
• $\text{C}_1$ (nitrile carbon): $2\ \sigma$ bonds ($1$ to $\text{C}$, $1$ to $\text{N}$) and $2\ \pi$ bonds $\implies \mathbf{sp}$

(e) Benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$):
All 6 ring carbon atoms form $3\ \sigma$ bonds ($2$ with adjacent carbons, $1$ with hydrogen) and share delocalised $\pi$ bonds $\implies$ All 6 carbons are $\mathbf{sp^2}$ hybridised.

8.2
NCERT Exercise
Indicate the $\sigma$ and $\pi$ bonds in the following molecules:
(a) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$
(b) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}$
(c) $\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2$
(d) $\text{CH}_2=\text{C}=\text{CH}_2$
(e) $\text{CH}_3\text{NO}_2$
(f) $\text{HCONHCH}_3$.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Rule: Every single bond is $1\ \sigma$ bond; every double bond has $1\ \sigma + 1\ \pi$ bond; every triple bond has $1\ \sigma + 2\ \pi$ bonds.

(a) Benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$):
$6\ \text{C}-\text{C}\ \sigma$ bonds $+ 6\ \text{C}-\text{H}\ \sigma$ bonds $= \mathbf{12\ \sigma\text{ bonds}}$, and $\mathbf{3\ \pi\text{ bonds}}$ (delocalised).

(b) Cyclohexane ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}$):
$6\ \text{C}-\text{C}\ \sigma$ bonds $+ 12\ \text{C}-\text{H}\ \sigma$ bonds $= \mathbf{18\ \sigma\text{ bonds}}$, and $\mathbf{0\ \pi\text{ bonds}}$.

(c) Dichloromethane ($\text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2$):
$2\ \text{C}-\text{H}\ \sigma$ bonds $+ 2\ \text{C}-\text{Cl}\ \sigma$ bonds $= \mathbf{4\ \sigma\text{ bonds}}$, and $\mathbf{0\ \pi\text{ bonds}}$.

(d) Allene ($\text{CH}_2=\text{C}=\text{CH}_2$):
$4\ \text{C}-\text{H}\ \sigma$ bonds $+ 2\ \text{C}-\text{C}\ \sigma$ bonds $= \mathbf{6\ \sigma\text{ bonds}}$, and $2\ \text{C}=\text{C}\ \pi$ bonds $= \mathbf{2\ \pi\text{ bonds}}$.

(e) Nitromethane ($\text{CH}_3\text{NO}_2$):
$3\ \text{C}-\text{H}\ \sigma$ bonds $+ 1\ \text{C}-\text{N}\ \sigma$ bond $+ 2\ \text{N}-\text{O}\ \sigma$ bonds $= \mathbf{6\ \sigma\text{ bonds}}$, and $1\ \text{N}=\text{O}\ \pi$ bond $= \mathbf{1\ \pi\text{ bond}}$.

(f) N-Methylformamide ($\text{HCONHCH}_3$):
$1\ \text{C}-\text{H} + 1\ \text{C}=\text{O}(\sigma) + 1\ \text{C}-\text{N} + 1\ \text{N}-\text{H} + 1\ \text{N}-\text{C} + 3\ \text{C}-\text{H} = \mathbf{8\ \sigma\text{ bonds}}$, and $1\ \text{C}=\text{O}\ \pi$ bond $= \mathbf{1\ \pi\text{ bond}}$.

8.4
NCERT Exercise
Give the IUPAC names of the following compounds:
(a) $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{Cl})\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$
(b) $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}=\text{CHCHO}$
(c) $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{OH})\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{COOH}$
(d) $\text{CH}_3\text{COCH}_2\text{COCH}_3$
(e) $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)\text{CH}_2\text{CH}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)\text{CH}_3$
(f) $\text{Cl}_2\text{CHCH}_2\text{OH}$.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

(a) $\overset{1}{\text{CH}_3}\overset{2}{\text{CH}}(\text{Cl})\overset{3}{\text{CH}_2}\overset{4}{\text{CH}_3}$:
Longest chain = 4 carbons (butane); chlorine substituent at carbon 2 $\implies$ 2-Chlorobutane.

(b) $\overset{5}{\text{CH}_3}\overset{4}{\text{CH}_2}\overset{3}{\text{CH}}=\overset{2}{\text{CH}}\overset{1}{\text{C}}\text{HO}$:
Principal functional group is aldehyde ($-\text{CHO}$ at $\text{C}_1$); double bond between $\text{C}_2$ and $\text{C}_3$; 5-carbon chain $\implies$ Pent-2-en-1-al (or Pent-2-enal).

(c) $\overset{5}{\text{CH}_3}\overset{4}{\text{CH}}(\text{OH})\overset{3}{\text{CH}_2}\overset{2}{\text{CH}_2}\overset{1}{\text{C}}\text{OOH}$:
Carboxylic acid has highest priority ($-\text{COOH}$ at $\text{C}_1$); $-\text{OH}$ acts as substituent (hydroxy) at $\text{C}_4$; 5 carbons $\implies$ 4-Hydroxypentanoic acid.

(d) $\overset{1}{\text{CH}_3}\overset{2}{\text{C}}\text{O}\overset{3}{\text{CH}_2}\overset{4}{\text{C}}\text{O}\overset{5}{\text{CH}_3}$:
Symmetrical 5-carbon chain with two ketone groups at carbons 2 and 4 $\implies$ Pentane-2,4-dione.

(e) $\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)-\text{CH}_3$:
Expanding $-\text{C}_2\text{H}_5$ as $-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_3$ gives the longest chain of 7 carbons (heptane):
$$\overset{7}{\text{CH}_3}-\overset{6}{\text{CH}_2}-\overset{5}{\text{CH}}(\text{CH}_3)-\overset{4}{\text{CH}_2}-\overset{3}{\text{CH}}(\text{CH}_3)-\overset{2}{\text{CH}_2}-\overset{1}{\text{CH}_3}$$ Numbering from right gives substituents at $\text{C}_3$ and $\text{C}_5$ (locant set 3,5 from both sides) $\implies$ 3,5-Dimethylheptane.

(f) $\overset{2}{\text{Cl}_2\text{CH}}-\overset{1}{\text{CH}_2}\text{OH}$:
Principal functional group is alcohol ($-\text{OH}$ at $\text{C}_1$); two chlorine atoms at $\text{C}_2$ $\implies$ 2,2-Dichloroethan-1-ol (or 2,2-Dichloroethanol).

NCERT Exercise
Which of the following represents the correct IUPAC name for the compounds concerned?
(a) 2,2-Dimethylpentane or 2-Dimethylpentane
(b) 2,4,7-Trimethyloctane or 2,5,7-Trimethyloctane
(c) 2-Chloro-4-methylpentane or 4-Chloro-2-methylpentane
(d) But-3-yn-1-ol or But-4-ol-1-yne.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

(a) 2,2-Dimethylpentane:
Correct: 2,2-Dimethylpentane.
Reason: Every substituent must have a separate locant number. Since there are two methyl groups at carbon 2, both locants must be specified ('2,2-' rather than '2-').

(b) 2,4,7-Trimethyloctane:
Correct: 2,4,7-Trimethyloctane.
Reason: Lowest locant rule. Comparing the two numbering sets from both ends: $(2, 4, 7)$ vs $(2, 5, 7)$. At the first point of difference, $4 < 5$. Therefore, the set $(2, 4, 7)$ is preferred.

(c) 2-Chloro-4-methylpentane:
Correct: 2-Chloro-4-methylpentane.
Reason: Numbering from both ends gives the locant set $(2, 4)$. When locant sets are identical from both ends, the substituent that comes first alphabetically (Chloro before Methyl) gets the lower number (position 2).

(d) But-3-yn-1-ol:
Correct: But-3-yn-1-ol.
Reason: The principal functional group is alcohol ($-\text{OH}$), which has higher priority than an alkyne (triple bond). The chain must be numbered to give $-\text{OH}$ the lowest locant ($ ext{C}_1$).

NCERT Exercise
Which of the two: $\text{O}_2\text{NCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{O}^-$ or $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{O}^-$ is expected to be more stable and why?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Answer: $\mathbf{O_2NCH_2CH_2O^-}$ is much more stable than $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{O}^-$.

Reason:
1. In $\text{O}_2\text{NCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{O}^-$, the nitro group ($-\text{NO}_2$) possesses a powerful electron-withdrawing inductive effect ($-I$ effect). It pulls electron density away from the negatively charged oxygen atom, dispersing the negative charge over a larger molecular volume and greatly stabilizing the alkoxide anion.
2. In contrast, in ethoxide ion ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{O}^-$), the ethyl group has an electron-donating inductive effect ($+I$ effect). It pushes electrons toward the oxygen atom, intensifying the negative charge and destabilizing the ion.

8.10
NCERT Exercise
Explain why alkyl groups act as electron donors when attached to a $\pi$ system.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Alkyl groups attached to an unsaturated $\pi$ system (e.g., an alkene or benzene ring) donate electron density through two distinct mechanisms:

1. Hyperconjugation (No-Bond Resonance / Baker-Nathan Effect):
The $\sigma$ electrons of the $\text{C}-\text{H}$ bond of the alkyl group (specifically at the $\alpha$-carbon) overlap with the adjacent empty/partially filled $p$-orbital or the $\pi^*$ antibonding orbital of the unsaturated system:
$$\text{H}-\text{C}-\text{C}=\text{C} \longleftrightarrow \text{H}^+\ \text{C}=\text{C}-\text{C}^-$$ This delocalisation of $\sigma$ electrons directly increases the electron density of the $\pi$ system.

2. Inductive Effect ($+I$ effect):
$sp^3$ hybridised alkyl carbons are less electronegative than the $sp^2$ hybridised carbons of the $\pi$ system, creating an electron push toward the double bond or aromatic ring.

8.11
NCERT Exercise
Draw the resonance structures for the following compounds. Show the electron shift using curved-arrow notation:
(a) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$
(b) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2$
(c) $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CHCHO}$
(d) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CHO}$
(e) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}_2^+$
(f) $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}\text{CH}_2^+$.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

(a) Phenol ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH}$):
The lone pair on oxygen delocalises into the ring ($+R$ effect), creating negative charge density at ortho and para positions:
I (Neutral) $\longleftrightarrow$ II (Ortho negative, $\text{O}^+$) $\longleftrightarrow$ III (Para negative, $\text{O}^+$) $\longleftrightarrow$ IV (Ortho' negative, $\text{O}^+$) $\longleftrightarrow$ V (Kekulé form).

(b) Nitrobenzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2$):
The nitro group withdraws electron density from the ring ($-R$ effect), creating positive charge at ortho and para positions:
I $\longleftrightarrow$ II (Ortho positive) $\longleftrightarrow$ III (Para positive) $\longleftrightarrow$ IV (Ortho' positive) $\longleftrightarrow$ V.

(c) Crotonaldehyde ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{CH}=\text{O}$):
$$\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{CH}=\text{O} \longleftrightarrow \text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{O}^-$$

(d) Benzaldehyde ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CHO}$):
Aldehyde group is electron-withdrawing by $-R$ effect; positive charges appear at ortho and para positions of the benzene ring.

(e) Benzyl carbocation ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_2^+$):
The empty $p$-orbital on the exocyclic carbon accepts $\pi$ electrons from the ring; positive charge is delocalised over the ortho and para positions (4 resonance contributors).

(f) Allylic carbocation ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\text{CH}_2^+$):
$$\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}=\text{CH}-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}_2 \longleftrightarrow \text{CH}_3-\overset{+}{\text{C}}\text{H}-\text{CH}=\text{CH}_2$$

8.12
NCERT Exercise
What are electrophiles and nucleophiles? Explain with examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

1. Electrophiles (Electron-seeking / Lewis acids):
• Definition: Reagents that are electron-deficient and seek an electron pair from electron-rich centres to form a covalent bond.
• Positively charged electrophiles: $\text{H}^+, \text{Cl}^+, \text{Br}^+, \text{NO}_2^+, \text{CH}_3^+, \text{R}_3\text{C}^+$
• Neutral electrophiles (incomplete octet or polar centre): $\text{BF}_3, \text{AlCl}_3, \text{SO}_3, \text{FeCl}_3, :\text{CCl}_2$ (carbene), carbonyl carbon ($>\overset{\delta+}{\text{C}}=\text{O}$).

2. Nucleophiles (Nucleus-seeking / Lewis bases):
• Definition: Reagents that are electron-rich, possessing at least one unshared lone pair of electrons or a $\pi$ bond, which they donate to an electron-deficient centre.

Explain, why an organic liquid vaporises at a temperature below its normal boiling point in steam distillation.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Principle of Steam Distillation:
Steam distillation is employed for purifying water-immiscible organic liquids having significant vapour pressure at $100^\circ\text{C}$.

In a mixture of two mutually immiscible liquids (water and organic liquid), each liquid exerts its own vapour pressure independently of the other:
$$P_{\text{total}} = p_{\text{water}} + p_{\text{organic liquid}}$$ The mixture boils at a temperature ($T$) at which the total vapour pressure equals the external atmospheric pressure ($P_{\text{atm}} = 1\text{ atm} = 760\text{ mm Hg}$):
$$p_{\text{water}} + p_{\text{organic liquid}} = P_{\text{atm}}$$ Since water contributes a significant partial vapour pressure ($p_{\text{water}} > 0$), the organic liquid needs to exert only a partial pressure equal to $(P_{\text{atm}} - p_{\text{water}})$ for the combined system to boil.
Because the required vapour pressure is much less than $1\text{ atm}$, the mixture boils at a temperature lower than the normal boiling point of both water ($100^\circ\text{C}$) and the organic liquid, preventing thermal decomposition of sensitive organic compounds.

Fractional distillation is used to separate two or more miscible volatile liquids whose boiling points are very close to each other (boiling point difference $\Delta T_b < 25^\circ\text{C}$ or $25\text{ K}$).

What is thin layer chromatography (TLC)? Explain $R_f$ value.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Thin Layer Chromatography (TLC):
TLC is an effective micro-analytical type of adsorption chromatography.
A thin, uniform layer (about $0.2\text{ mm}$ thick) of an adsorbent (silica gel or alumina) is coated on an inert glass plate or plastic sheet (TLC plate), representing the stationary phase.
The sample spot is applied near the bottom, and the plate is placed in an elution chamber containing an appropriate developing solvent (mobile phase). As the solvent ascends by capillary action, components separate based on their differential adsorption affinities.

$R_f$ Value (Retention Factor):
The relative rate of migration of each component is quantified by its $R_f$ value, defined as:
$$\mathbf{R_f = \frac{\text{Distance travelled by the substance from baseline}}{\text{Distance travelled by the solvent front from baseline}}}$$ • $R_f$ is a dimensionless number ranging between $0$ and $1$.
• Under identical experimental conditions (adsorbent, solvent, temperature), $R_f$ is a constant characteristic of a given compound, used for identification.

Principle & Procedure of Carius Sulphur Estimation:

1. Digestion: A known mass ($w\text{ g}$) of the organic sulphur compound is heated with concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ (or fuming nitric acid) in a sealed Carius tube.
The sulphur present is oxidised quantitatively to sulphuric acid:
$$\text{S} + 2\text{HNO}_3 \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + 2\text{NO}$$

2. Precipitation: The contents of the tube are washed into a beaker and an excess of barium chloride solution ($\text{BaCl}_2$) is added to precipitate all sulphate as insoluble barium sulphate:
$$\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{BaCl}_2 \rightarrow \mathbf{\text{BaSO}_4}\downarrow \text{ (white ppt)} + 2\text{HCl}$$ The precipitate is filtered, washed thoroughly with hot distilled water, dried, ignited, and weighed accurately as $w_1\text{ g}$ of $\text{BaSO}_4$.

3. Calculation Formula:
Molar mass of $\text{BaSO}_4 = 137.3 + 32 + 4(16) = 233.3\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
$$233.3\text{ g of } \text{BaSO}_4 \text{ contains } 32\text{ g of sulphur}$$ $$\mathbf{\%\text{ of Sulphur} = \frac{32}{233} \times \frac{\text{Mass of } \text{BaSO}_4\text{ formed } (w_1)}{\text{Mass of organic compound } (w)} \times 100}$$

• $\text{C}_2$ ($-\text{CH}=$): $3\ \sigma$ bonds, $1\ \pi$ bond $\implies \mathbf{sp^2}$
