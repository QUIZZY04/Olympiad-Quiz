6.1
NCERT Exercise
A liquid is in equilibrium with its vapour in a sealed container at a fixed temperature. The volume of the container is suddenly increased.
(a) What is the initial effect of the change on vapour pressure?
(b) How do rates of evaporation and condensation change initially?
(c) What happens when equilibrium is restored and what will be the final vapour pressure?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

(a) Initial effect on vapour pressure:
When the volume of the container is suddenly increased, the same number of vapour molecules now occupies a larger volume. Therefore, the number of vapour molecules per unit volume decreases, causing the vapour pressure to decrease initially.

(b) Initial effect on rates of evaporation and condensation:
• Rate of evaporation depends primarily on surface area of the liquid and temperature. Since temperature is constant and the surface area remains essentially unchanged, the rate of evaporation remains constant initially.
• Rate of condensation depends directly on the concentration (vapour pressure) of vapour molecules colliding with the liquid surface. Since vapour density decreases, the rate of condensation decreases initially ($r_{\text{cond}} < r_{\text{evap}}$).

(c) Restoration of equilibrium & final vapour pressure:
Since rate of evaporation exceeds rate of condensation, more liquid evaporates to replenish vapour molecules. Gradually, the number of vapour molecules per unit volume increases until rate of condensation equals rate of evaporation once again. At constant temperature, equilibrium vapour pressure depends solely on temperature: hence the final vapour pressure will be equal to the initial equilibrium vapour pressure.

NCERT Exercise
What is $K_c$ for the following equilibrium when the equilibrium concentration of each substance is: $[\text{SO}_2] = 0.60\text{ M}$, $[\text{O}_2] = 0.82\text{ M}$ and $[\text{SO}_3] = 1.90\text{ M}$?
$$2\text{SO}_2(\text{g}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g}) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{SO}_3(\text{g})$$
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Write the equilibrium constant expression ($K_c$):
$$K_c = \frac{[\text{SO}_3]^2}{[\text{SO}_2]^2 [\text{O}_2]}$$

Step 2: Substitute the equilibrium concentrations:
$$K_c = \frac{(1.90)^2}{(0.60)^2 \times (0.82)}$$ $$K_c = \frac{3.61}{0.36 \times 0.82} = \frac{3.61}{0.2952} \approx 12.2289\text{ M}^{-1} \approx 12.23\text{ L mol}^{-1}$$

Final Answer: The equilibrium constant $K_c = 12.23\text{ L mol}^{-1}$ (or $12.23$).

At $700\text{ K}$, equilibrium constant for the reaction:
$$\text{H}_2(\text{g}) + \text{I}_2(\text{g}) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI}(\text{g})$$
is $54.8$. If $0.5\text{ mol L}^{-1}$ of $\text{HI}(\text{g})$ is present at equilibrium at $700\text{ K}$, what are the concentrations of $\text{H}_2(\text{g})$ and $\text{I}_2(\text{g})$ assuming that we initially started with $\text{HI}(\text{g})$ and allowed it to reach equilibrium at $700\text{ K}$?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Reaction stoichiometry and equilibrium relation:
$$\text{H}_2(\text{g}) + \text{I}_2(\text{g}) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI}(\text{g}), \quad K_c = 54.8$$ Since we started with pure $\text{HI}(\text{g})$, the decomposition produces equimolar amounts of $\text{H}_2(\text{g})$ and $\text{I}_2(\text{g})$: $$[\text{H}_2] = [\text{I}_2] = x$$

Step 2: Express $K_c$:
$$K_c = \frac{[\text{HI}]^2}{[\text{H}_2][\text{I}_2]} = \frac{(0.5)^2}{x \times x} = \frac{0.25}{x^2}$$

Step 3: Solve for $x$:
$$x^2 = \frac{0.25}{K_c} = \frac{0.25}{54.8} \approx 4.562 \times 10^{-3}$$ $$x = \sqrt{4.562 \times 10^{-3}} = 0.0675\text{ mol L}^{-1} = 0.068\text{ M}$$

Final Answer: $[\text{H}_2] = [\text{I}_2] = 0.068\text{ mol L}^{-1}$.

6.5
NCERT Exercise
One of the reactions that takes place in producing steel from iron ore is the reduction of $\text{FeO}(\text{s})$ by carbon monoxide to give iron and $\text{CO}_2$:
$$\text{FeO}(\text{s}) + \text{CO}(\text{g}) \rightleftharpoons \text{Fe}(\text{s}) + \text{CO}_2(\text{g}), \quad K_p = 0.265 \text{ at } 1050\text{ K}$$
What are the equilibrium partial pressures of $\text{CO}$ and $\text{CO}_2$ at $1050\text{ K}$ if the initial partial pressures are: $p_{\text{CO}} = 1.4\text{ atm}$ and $p_{\text{CO}_2} = 0.80\text{ atm}$?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Determine initial reaction quotient $Q_p$:
Since solids $\text{FeO}(\text{s})$ and $\text{Fe}(\text{s})$ have activity $= 1$: $$Q_p = \frac{p_{\text{CO}_2}}{p_{\text{CO}}} = \frac{0.80}{1.4} \approx 0.571$$ Since $Q_p (0.571) > K_p (0.265)$, the reaction proceeds in the reverse direction to reach equilibrium.

Step 2: Set up partial pressure changes:
Let $p$ be the decrease in partial pressure of $\text{CO}_2$ (and increase in $\text{CO}$): $$p_{\text{CO}_2} = 0.80 - p, \quad p_{\text{CO}} = 1.4 + p$$

Step 3: Solve for $p$ using $K_p$:
$$K_p = \frac{p_{\text{CO}_2}}{p_{\text{CO}}} = \frac{0.80 - p}{1.4 + p} = 0.265$$ $$0.80 - p = 0.265(1.4 + p) = 0.371 + 0.265 p$$ $$0.80 - 0.371 = 1.265 p \implies 0.429 = 1.265 p \implies p = \frac{0.429}{1.265} \approx 0.339\text{ atm}$$

Step 4: Equilibrium partial pressures:
$$p_{\text{CO}} = 1.4 + 0.339 = 1.739\text{ atm} \approx 1.74\text{ atm}$$ $$p_{\text{CO}_2} = 0.80 - 0.339 = 0.461\text{ atm} \approx 0.46\text{ atm}$$

Final Answer: $p_{\text{CO}} = 1.74\text{ atm}$ and $p_{\text{CO}_2} = 0.46\text{ atm}$.

NCERT Exercise
For the equilibrium:
$$2\text{NOCl}(\text{g}) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NO}(\text{g}) + \text{Cl}_2(\text{g})$$
the value of the equilibrium constant, $K_c$ is $3.75 \times 10^{-6}$ at $1069\text{ K}$. Calculate $K_p$ for the reaction at this temperature.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Formula connecting $K_p$ and $K_c$:
$$K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n_g}$$ Here, $\Delta n_g = n_p - n_r = (2 + 1) - 2 = 3 - 2 = 1$.

Step 2: Values given:
$K_c = 3.75 \times 10^{-6}$, $R = 0.0831\text{ bar L K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$, $T = 1069\text{ K}$.

Step 3: Calculation:
$$K_p = (3.75 \times 10^{-6}) \times (0.0831 \times 1069)^1$$ $$0.0831 \times 1069 = 88.8339$$ $$K_p = 3.75 \times 10^{-6} \times 88.8339 = 3.331 \times 10^{-4}\text{ bar}$$

Final Answer: $K_p = 3.33 \times 10^{-4}\text{ bar}$.

6.8
NCERT Exercise
A sample of $\text{HI}(\text{g})$ is placed in flask at a pressure of $0.2\text{ atm}$. At equilibrium the partial pressure of $\text{HI}(\text{g})$ is $0.04\text{ atm}$. What is $K_p$ for the given equilibrium?
$$2\text{HI}(\text{g}) \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2(\text{g}) + \text{I}_2(\text{g})$$
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Set up equilibrium partial pressures:
Initial pressure: $p_{\text{HI}} = 0.2\text{ atm}$, $p_{\text{H}_2} = 0$, $p_{\text{I}_2} = 0$.
Let $2x$ be the decrease in $p_{\text{HI}}$.
At equilibrium: $p_{\text{HI}} = 0.2 - 2x = 0.04\text{ atm}$.
$$2x = 0.2 - 0.04 = 0.16 \implies x = 0.08\text{ atm}$$ Therefore: $$p_{\text{H}_2} = x = 0.08\text{ atm}, \quad p_{\text{I}_2} = x = 0.08\text{ atm}$$

Step 2: Calculate $K_p$:
$$K_p = \frac{p_{\text{H}_2} \cdot p_{\text{I}_2}}{(p_{\text{HI}})^2} = \frac{0.08 \times 0.08}{(0.04)^2} = \frac{0.0064}{0.0016} = 4.0$$

Final Answer: $K_p = 4.0$.

NCERT Exercise
A mixture of $1.57\text{ mol}$ of $\text{N}_2$, $1.92\text{ mol}$ of $\text{H}_2$ and $8.13\text{ mol}$ of $\text{NH}_3$ is introduced into a $20\text{ L}$ reaction vessel at $500\text{ K}$. At this temperature, the equilibrium constant, $K_c$ for the reaction $\text{N}_2(\text{g}) + 3\text{H}_2(\text{g}) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3(\text{g})$ is $1.7 \times 10^2$. Is the reaction mixture at equilibrium? If not, what is the direction of the net reaction?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Calculate molar concentrations:
$$[\text{N}_2] = \frac{1.57\text{ mol}}{20\text{ L}} = 0.0785\text{ M}$$ $$[\text{H}_2] = \frac{1.92\text{ mol}}{20\text{ L}} = 0.096\text{ M}$$ $$[\text{NH}_3] = \frac{8.13\text{ mol}}{20\text{ L}} = 0.4065\text{ M}$$

Step 2: Calculate Reaction Quotient $Q_c$:
$$Q_c = \frac{[\text{NH}_3]^2}{[\text{N}_2][\text{H}_2]^3} = \frac{(0.4065)^2}{(0.0785) \times (0.096)^3}$$ $$(0.4065)^2 = 0.16524$$ $$(0.096)^3 = 8.847 \times 10^{-4}$$ Denominator $= 0.0785 \times 8.847 \times 10^{-4} = 6.945 \times 10^{-5}$ $$Q_c = \frac{0.16524}{6.945 \times 10^{-5}} \approx 2379.3 = 2.38 \times 10^3$$

Step 3: Compare $Q_c$ with $K_c$:
Since $Q_c (2.38 \times 10^3) > K_c (1.7 \times 10^2)$, the mixture is not at equilibrium.
To reach equilibrium, $Q_c$ must decrease, meaning products must convert into reactants: the net reaction will proceed in the reverse (backward) direction (decomposition of $\text{NH}_3$).

6.10
NCERT Exercise
The equilibrium constant for the reaction:
$$\text{N}_2(\text{g}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g}) \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NO}(\text{g})$$
at $2000\text{ K}$ is $4 \times 10^{-4}$. In the presence of a catalyst the equilibrium is attained $10$ times faster. What will be the equilibrium constant in the presence of the catalyst at $2000\text{ K}$?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Concept:
A catalyst increases the speed at which equilibrium is attained by providing an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy. Crucially, it lowers the activation energy of both the forward and reverse reactions by the exact same amount.

Therefore, a catalyst increases the rate of both forward and reverse reactions equally ($r_f$ and $r_b$ increase by the same factor):
$$K_c = \frac{k_f}{k_b}$$ The ratio $k_f / k_b$ remains unchanged. Equilibrium constant depends only on temperature.

Final Answer: The equilibrium constant in the presence of the catalyst at $2000\text{ K}$ remains unchanged: $K = 4 \times 10^{-4}$.

6.16
NCERT Exercise
Explain why pure liquids and solids can be ignored while writing the equilibrium constant expression.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Explanation:
The molar concentration (or density in moles per volume) of a pure solid or pure liquid is defined as:
$$\text{Molar Concentration} = \frac{\text{Moles}}{\text{Volume}} = \frac{\text{Mass} / M}{V} = \frac{\text{Mass} / V}{M} = \frac{\text{Density}}{M}$$ Since the density of a pure solid or liquid is an intensive property that remains constant at a fixed temperature, and its molar mass $M$ is a constant, its molar concentration is constant throughout the reaction.
In chemical thermodynamics, the thermodynamic activity of pure solids and pure liquids in their standard states is conventionally taken as unity ($a = 1$). Consequently, this constant concentration is subsumed into the equilibrium constant $K$, and omitted from the equilibrium expression.

$$V = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Solubility}} = \frac{1.0\text{ g}}{0.4103\text{ g L}^{-1}} \approx 2.437\text{ L} \approx 2.44\text{ L}$$

Final Answer: The minimum volume of water required is $2.44\text{ L}$.

The concentration of sulphide ion in $0.1\text{ M HCl}$ solution saturated with hydrogen sulphide is $1.0 \times 10^{-19}\text{ M}$. If $10\text{ mL}$ of this is added to $5\text{ mL}$ of $0.04\text{ M}$ solution of the following: $\text{FeSO}_4$, $\text{MnCl}_2$, $\text{ZnCl}_2$ and $\text{CdCl}_2$, in which of these solutions will precipitation occur?
($K_{sp}$ values: $\text{FeS} = 6.3 \times 10^{-18}$, $\text{MnS} = 2.5 \times 10^{-13}$, $\text{ZnS} = 1.6 \times 10^{-24}$, $\text{CdS} = 8.0 \times 10^{-27}$).
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Calculate concentrations in the mixed solution:
Total Volume $= 10\text{ mL} + 5\text{ mL} = 15\text{ mL}$.
$$[\text{S}^{2-}] = \frac{10\text{ mL} \times 1.0 \times 10^{-19}\text{ M}}{15\text{ mL}} = \frac{2}{3} \times 10^{-19}\text{ M} = 6.67 \times 10^{-20}\text{ M}$$ For each metal cation ($M^{2+} = \text{Fe}^{2+}, \text{Mn}^{2+}, \text{Zn}^{2+}, \text{Cd}^{2+}$):
$$[M^{2+}] = \frac{5\text{ mL} \times 0.04\text{ M}}{15\text{ mL}} = \frac{0.20}{15} = 1.33 \times 10^{-2}\text{ M}$$

Step 2: Calculate Ionic Product ($Q_{sp}$):
$$Q_{sp} = [M^{2+}][\text{S}^{2-}] = (1.33 \times 10^{-2}) \times (6.67 \times 10^{-20}) = 8.87 \times 10^{-22}$$

Step 3: Compare $Q_{sp}$ with $K_{sp}$ of each sulphide:
• FeS: $K_{sp} = 6.3 \times 10^{-18} > Q_{sp} (8.87 \times 10^{-22}) \implies$ No precipitation.
• MnS: $K_{sp} = 2.5 \times 10^{-13} > Q_{sp} (8.87 \times 10^{-22}) \implies$ No precipitation.
• ZnS: $K_{sp} = 1.6 \times 10^{-24} < Q_{sp} (8.87 \times 10^{-22}) \implies$ Precipitation occurs ($Q_{sp} > K_{sp}$).
• CdS: $K_{sp} = 8.0 \times 10^{-27} < Q_{sp} (8.87 \times 10^{-22}) \implies$ Precipitation occurs ($Q_{sp} > K_{sp}$).

Final Answer: Precipitation will occur only in solutions of $\text{ZnCl}_2$ and $\text{CdCl}_2$ (yielding precipitates of $\text{ZnS}$ and $\text{CdS}$).
