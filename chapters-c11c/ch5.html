Question 5.1
NCERT Exercise
Choose the correct answer. A thermodynamic state function is a quantity
(i) used to determine heat changes
(ii) whose value is independent of path
(iii) used to determine pressure volume work
(iv) whose value depends on path.
[1 Mark]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Correct Option: (ii) whose value is independent of path

Scientific Definition & Explanation:
• A **state function** (or state variable) is a macroscopic thermodynamic property whose value depends solely on the **current state of the system** (its initial and final states, specified by temperature, pressure, volume, and composition), and is **completely independent of the path or mechanism** taken to reach that state.
• Examples of state functions include: Internal energy ($U$), Enthalpy ($H$), Entropy ($S$), Gibbs free energy ($G$), Pressure ($P$), Volume ($V$), and Temperature ($T$).
• In contrast, heat ($q$) and work ($w$) are **path functions** because their magnitudes depend directly on the specific path followed.

Question 5.2
NCERT Exercise
For the process to occur under adiabatic conditions, the correct condition is:
(i) $\Delta T = 0$
(ii) $\Delta p = 0$
(iii) $q = 0$
(iv) $w = 0$.
[1 Mark]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Correct Option: (iii) $q = 0$

Thermodynamic Classification of Processes:
• In an **adiabatic process**, the system is thermally insulated from its surroundings by adiabatic walls so that **no heat enters or leaves the system** across the boundary.
• Therefore, the heat exchanged is zero: $\mathbf{q = 0}$.

*(Comparison with other processes: $\Delta T = 0$ is isothermal; $\Delta p = 0$ is isobaric; $\Delta V = 0$ is isochoric).*

Question 5.3
NCERT Exercise
The enthalpies of all elements in their standard states are:
(i) unity
(ii) zero
(iii) $< 0$
(iv) different for each element.
[1 Mark]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Correct Option: (ii) zero

Thermodynamic Convention:
• By international thermodynamic convention (IUPAC), the **standard enthalpy of formation ($\Delta_f H^ominus$)** of every pure element in its most stable reference physical state of aggregation at $298.15\text{ K}$ and $1\text{ bar}$ pressure is arbitrarily defined as **zero**:
$$\mathbf{\Delta_f H^ominus [\text{pure element in standard state}] = 0}$$
• Examples: $\Delta_f H^ominus [\text{C}(\text{graphite})] = 0$, $\Delta_f H^ominus [\text{O}_2(g)] = 0$, $\Delta_f H^ominus [\text{H}_2(g)] = 0$, $\Delta_f H^ominus [\text{Br}_2(l)] = 0$, $\Delta_f H^ominus [\text{Na}(s)] = 0$.

Question 5.4
NCERT Exercise
$\Delta U^ominus$ of combustion of methane is $-X\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$. The value of $\Delta H^ominus$ is
(i) $= \Delta U^ominus$
(ii) $> \Delta U^ominus$
(iii) $< \Delta U^ominus$
(iv) $= 0$.
[1.5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Correct Option: (iii) $< \Delta U^ominus$

Mathematical & Thermodynamic Proof:

1. Write the balanced thermochemical combustion equation for methane at $298\text{ K}$:
$$\text{CH}_4(g) + 2\text{O}_2(g) \longrightarrow \text{CO}_2(g) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$
2. Calculate change in gaseous moles ($\Delta n_g$):
$$\Delta n_g = n_g(\text{products}) - n_g(\text{reactants})$$
$$\Delta n_g = 1 - (1 + 2) = 1 - 3 = \mathbf{-2}$$
*(Note: Liquid water $\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$ is not counted in $\Delta n_g$).*

3. Apply the fundamental relation between $\Delta H$ and $\Delta U$:
$$\Delta H^ominus = \Delta U^ominus + \Delta n_g R T$$
$$\Delta H^ominus = \Delta U^ominus + (-2) R T = \Delta U^ominus - 2 R T$$
Since $R$ and $T$ are strictly positive quantities ($2RT > 0$), subtracting $2RT$ from $\Delta U^ominus$ makes $\Delta H^ominus$ more negative than $\Delta U^ominus$:
$$\mathbf{\Delta H^ominus < \Delta U^ominus \quad (\text{Option iii})}$$

Question 5.5
NCERT Exercise
The enthalpy of combustion of methane, graphite and dihydrogen at $298\text{ K}$ are $-890.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$, $-393.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$, and $-285.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ respectively. Enthalpy of formation of $\text{CH}_4(g)$ will be
(i) $-74.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
(ii) $-52.27\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
(iii) $+74.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
(iv) $+52.26\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Correct Option: (i) $-74.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

Step-by-Step Calculation using Hess's Law:

Target Formation Equation for Methane:
$$\text{C}(\text{graphite}) + 2\text{H}_2(g) \longrightarrow \text{CH}_4(g) \quad \Delta_f H^ominus = ?$$

Given Thermochemical Combustion Equations:
1. $\text{CH}_4(g) + 2\text{O}_2(g) \to \text{CO}_2(g) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \quad \Delta H_1 = -890.3\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
2. $\text{C}(\text{graphite}) + \text{O}_2(g) \to \text{CO}_2(g) \quad \Delta H_2 = -393.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
3. $\text{H}_2(g) + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2(g) \to \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \quad \Delta H_3 = -285.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

$$\Delta_f H^ominus = \Delta H_2 + 2(\Delta H_3) - \Delta H_1$$
$$\Delta_f H^ominus = -393.5 + 2(-285.8) - (-890.3)$$
$$\Delta_f H^ominus = -393.5 - 571.6 + 890.3 = -965.1 + 890.3 = \mathbf{-74.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}$$

$$\mathbf{\text{The enthalpy of formation of methane is } -74.8\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} \quad (\text{Option i}).}$$

Question 5.6
NCERT Exercise
A reaction, $A + B \to C + D + q$ is found to have a positive entropy change. The reaction will be
(i) possible at high temperature
(ii) possible only at low temperature
(iii) not possible at any temperature
(iv) possible at any temperature.
[1.5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Correct Option: (iv) possible at any temperature

Thermodynamic Justification using Gibbs Equation:

1. The reaction evolves heat ($+q$), so it is **exothermic**: $\mathbf{\Delta H < 0}$ (negative).
2. The entropy change is positive: $\mathbf{\Delta S > 0}$ (positive).
3. Apply the Gibbs-Helmholtz spontaneity equation:
$$\Delta G = \Delta H - T \Delta S$$
• Since $\Delta H$ is negative ($-$) and $\Delta S$ is positive ($+$), the term $-T\Delta S$ is strictly negative ($-$) at all absolute temperatures ($T > 0\text{ K}$).
• Adding two negative terms guarantees that **$\Delta G$ is strictly negative ($\Delta G < 0$) at ALL temperatures**.

$$\mathbf{\text{The reaction is spontaneous and possible at any temperature } (\text{Option iv}).}$$

Identifying Delta H < 0 (exothermic) and Delta S > 00.5 Mark
Proving Delta G = Delta H - T*Delta S < 0 at all temperatures (Option iv)1 Mark

$$\Delta H = -742.7 + 1.2388 = \mathbf{-741.46\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} \approx -741.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}$$

Question 5.10
NCERT Exercise
Calculate the enthalpy change on freezing of $1.0\text{ mol}$ of water at $10.0^\circ\text{C}$ to ice at $-10.0^\circ\text{C}$. $\Delta_{\text{fus}} H = 6.03\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ at $0^\circ\text{C}$.
$C_p[\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)] = 75.3\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1},\; C_p[\text{H}_2\text{O}(s)] = 36.8\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
The freezing process occurs in three reversible sequential steps:

1. **Cooling liquid water from $10.0^circ\text{C}$ ($283.15\text{ K}$) to $0^circ\text{C}$ ($273.15\text{ K}$):**
$$\Delta H_1 = n \times C_p(l) \times \Delta T = (1.0\text{ mol}) \times (75.3\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}) \times (0 - 10)\text{ K}$$
$$\Delta H_1 = -753\text{ J} = \mathbf{-0.753\text{ kJ}}$$

2. **Freezing liquid water to ice at $0^circ\text{C}$ ($273.15\text{ K}$):**
$$\Delta H_2 = -\Delta_{\text{fus}} H = -(1.0\text{ mol} \times 6.03\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}) = \mathbf{-6.030\text{ kJ}}$$

3. **Cooling ice from $0^circ\text{C}$ ($273.15\text{ K}$) to $-10.0^circ\text{C}$ ($263.15\text{ K}$):**
$$\Delta H_3 = n \times C_p(s) \times \Delta T = (1.0\text{ mol}) \times (36.8\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}) \times (-10 - 0)\text{ K}$$
$$\Delta H_3 = -368\text{ J} = \mathbf{-0.368\text{ kJ}}$$

Total Enthalpy Change ($\Delta H_{\text{total}}$):
$$\Delta H_{\text{total}} = \Delta H_1 + \Delta H_2 + \Delta H_3 = (-0.753) + (-6.030) + (-0.368) = \mathbf{-7.151\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}$$

$$\mathbf{\text{The enthalpy change on freezing is } -7.151\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}.}$$

Question 5.16
NCERT Exercise
For an isolated system, $\Delta U = 0$, what will be $\Delta S$?
[1.5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Answer: For an isolated system, **$\Delta S > 0$ (positive)** for any spontaneous (natural) process, and **$\Delta S = 0$** at equilibrium.

Thermodynamic Explanation:
• An **isolated system** cannot exchange matter or energy (heat or work) with its surroundings ($q = 0, w = 0 \implies \Delta U = 0$).
• According to the **Second Law of Thermodynamics**, any natural or spontaneous process occurring in an isolated system is accompanied by an **increase in entropy (randomness or disorder)**.
• Hence, for any spontaneous change in an isolated system: $\mathbf{\Delta S > 0}$.
• When the isolated system reaches thermodynamic equilibrium, entropy attains its maximum value, and $\mathbf{\Delta S = 0}$.

Delta S > 0 for spontaneous process; Delta S = 0 at equilibrium1 Mark

Question 5.17
NCERT Exercise
For the reaction at $298\text{ K}$, $2A + B \to C$, $\Delta H = 400\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ and $\Delta S = 0.2\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$. At what temperature will the reaction become spontaneous considering $\Delta H$ and $\Delta S$ to be constant over the temperature range?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
• $\Delta H = 400\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
• $\Delta S = 0.2\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$

Criterion for Spontaneity:
$$\Delta G = \Delta H - T \Delta S < 0$$
$$\Delta H - T \Delta S < 0 \implies T \Delta S > \Delta H \implies T > \frac{\Delta H}{\Delta S}$$

Equilibrium Threshold Temperature ($T_{\text{eq}}$):
$$T_{\text{eq}} = \frac{\Delta H}{\Delta S} = \frac{400\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}{0.2\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}} = \mathbf{2000\text{ K}}$$

$$\mathbf{\text{The reaction will become spontaneous at temperatures above } 2000\text{ K (i.e., } T > 2000\text{ K}\text{).}}$$

✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

• The process involves the **formation of a stable covalent chemical bond** ($\text{Cl}-\text{Cl}$) from two isolated chlorine atoms.
• Bond formation always releases energy (exothermic).

2. Sign of Entropy Change ($\Delta S$):
• Two moles of gaseous atoms ($2\text{Cl}$) combine to form only one mole of diatomic molecules ($\text{Cl}_2$).

For the reaction $2A(g) + B(g) \to 2D(g)$, $\Delta U^\ominus = -10.5\text{ kJ}$ and $\Delta S^\ominus = -44.1\text{ J K}^{-1}$ at $298\text{ K}$. Calculate $\Delta G^\ominus$ for the reaction, and predict whether the reaction may occur spontaneously.
[2.5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data at $T = 298\text{ K}$:
• $\Delta U^\ominus = -10.5\text{ kJ}$
• $\Delta S^\ominus = -44.1\text{ J K}^{-1} = -0.0441\text{ kJ K}^{-1}$
• $R = 8.314 \times 10^{-3}\text{ kJ K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$

1. Calculate $\Delta n_g$:
$$\Delta n_g = 2 - (2 + 1) = 2 - 3 = \mathbf{-1}$$

2. Calculate $\Delta H^\ominus$:
$$\Delta H^\ominus = \Delta U^\ominus + \Delta n_g R T = -10.5 + (-1)(8.314 \times 10^{-3})(298)$$
$$\Delta H^\ominus = -10.5 - 2.478 = \mathbf{-12.978\text{ kJ}}$$

3. Calculate $\Delta G^\ominus$:
$$\Delta G^\ominus = \Delta H^\ominus - T \Delta S^\ominus = -12.978 - (298)(-0.0441)$$
$$\Delta G^\ominus = -12.978 + 13.142 = \mathbf{+0.164\text{ kJ} = +164\text{ J}}$$

Spontaneity Prediction:
Since $\Delta G^\ominus > 0$ (positive), the reaction **will NOT occur spontaneously** at $298\text{ K}$.

The equilibrium constant for a reaction is $10$. What will be the value of $\Delta G^\ominus$? $R = 8.314\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1},\; T = 300\text{ K}$.

✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given Data:
• Equilibrium constant $K = 10$
• Gas constant $R = 8.314\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$
• Temperature $T = 300\text{ K}$

Standard Gibbs Free Energy Formula:
$$\Delta G^\ominus = -2.303 R T \log K$$
$$\Delta G^\ominus = -2.303 \times (8.314\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}) \times (300\text{ K}) \times \log(10)$$

$$\Delta G^\ominus = -2.303 \times 8.314 \times 300 \times 1 = \mathbf{-5744.14\text{ J mol}^{-1} \approx -5.744\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}}$$

$$\mathbf{\text{The standard Gibbs free energy change is } -5.744\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}.}$$

Question 5.21
NCERT Exercise
Comment on the thermodynamic stability of $\text{NO}(g)$, given:
1. $\frac{1}{2}\text{N}_2(g) + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2(g) \to \text{NO}(g);\; \Delta_r H^\ominus = 90\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$
2. $\text{NO}(g) + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2(g) \to \text{NO}_2(g);\; \Delta_r H^\ominus = -74\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$.
[2.5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Thermodynamic Stability Analysis:

1. **Stability of $\text{NO}(g)$ relative to its elements ($\text{N}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$):**
• The formation of $\text{NO}(g)$ from $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$ is **endothermic** ($\Delta_r H^\ominus = +90\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$).
• Heat is absorbed during its formation, meaning $\text{NO}(g)$ resides at a higher energy state than its constituent elements.
• Hence, $\text{NO}(g)$ is **thermodynamically unstable** with respect to decomposition into $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$.

2. **Stability of $\text{NO}(g)$ relative to $\text{NO}_2(g)$:**
• The oxidation of $\text{NO}(g)$ to $\text{NO}_2(g)$ is **strongly exothermic** ($\Delta_r H^\ominus = -74\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$).
• This means $\text{NO}(g)$ readily and spontaneously oxidizes to $\text{NO}_2(g)$ in the presence of oxygen, making it unstable in air.

$$\mathbf{\text{Conclusion: } \text{NO}(g) \text{ is thermodynamically unstable relative to both its elements and } \text{NO}_2(g).}$$

✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

2. Entropy change of the surroundings ($\Delta S_{\text{surr}}$):
$$\Delta S_{\text{surr}} = \frac{q_{\text{surr}}}{T} = \frac{+286000\text{ J mol}^{-1}}{298\text{ K}} = \mathbf{+959.73\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1} \approx +960\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}}$$

$$\mathbf{\text{The entropy change in the surroundings is } +959.73\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1} \text{ (or } +960\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}\text{).}}$$
