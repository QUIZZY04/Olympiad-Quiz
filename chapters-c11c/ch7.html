7.1
NCERT Exercise
Assign oxidation number to the underlined elements in each of the following species:
(a) $\text{NaH}_2\underline{\text{P}}\text{O}_4$
(b) $\text{NaH}\underline{\text{S}}\text{O}_4$
(c) $\text{H}_4\underline{\text{P}}_2\text{O}_7$
(d) $\text{K}_2\underline{\text{Mn}}\text{O}_4$
(e) $\text{Ca}\underline{\text{O}}_2$
(f) $\text{Na}\underline{\text{B}}\text{H}_4$
(g) $\text{H}_2\underline{\text{S}}_2\text{O}_7$
(h) $\text{KAl}(\underline{\text{S}}\text{O}_4)_2 \cdot 12\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Rule of Oxidation Numbers: Sum of oxidation numbers in a neutral compound is zero ($0$). Standard values: $\text{Na} = +1, \text{K} = +1, \text{Ca} = +2, \text{Al} = +3, \text{H} = +1$ (with non-metals), $\text{O} = -2$ (in oxides).

(a) $\text{NaH}_2\underline{\text{P}}\text{O}_4$:
$(+1) + 2(+1) + x + 4(-2) = 0 \implies 1 + 2 + x - 8 = 0 \implies x - 5 = 0 \implies \mathbf{x = +5}$.

(b) $\text{NaH}\underline{\text{S}}\text{O}_4$:
$(+1) + (+1) + x + 4(-2) = 0 \implies 2 + x - 8 = 0 \implies \mathbf{x = +6}$.

(c) $\text{H}_4\underline{\text{P}}_2\text{O}_7$:
$4(+1) + 2x + 7(-2) = 0 \implies 4 + 2x - 14 = 0 \implies 2x = 10 \implies \mathbf{x = +5}$.

(d) $\text{K}_2\underline{\text{Mn}}\text{O}_4$:
$2(+1) + x + 4(-2) = 0 \implies 2 + x - 8 = 0 \implies \mathbf{x = +6}$.

(e) $\text{Ca}\underline{\text{O}}_2$ (Calcium Peroxide):
$(+2) + 2x = 0 \implies 2x = -2 \implies \mathbf{x = -1}$ (peroxide linkage $-\text{O}-\text{O}-$).

(f) $\text{Na}\underline{\text{B}}\text{H}_4$:
In borohydrides, $\text{H}$ is hydride ($-1$), $\text{Na} = +1$:
$(+1) + x + 4(-1) = 0 \implies 1 + x - 4 = 0 \implies \mathbf{x = +3}$.

(g) $\text{H}_2\underline{\text{S}}_2\text{O}_7$ (Oleum / Pyrosulphuric acid):
$2(+1) + 2x + 7(-2) = 0 \implies 2 + 2x - 14 = 0 \implies 2x = 12 \implies \mathbf{x = +6}$.

(h) $\text{KAl}(\underline{\text{S}}\text{O}_4)_2 \cdot 12\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (Potash Alum):
$\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is neutral ($0$). For sulphate radical $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$:
$x + 4(-2) = -2 \implies x - 8 = -2 \implies \mathbf{x = +6}$.

7.2
NCERT Exercise
What are the oxidation numbers of the underlined elements in each of the following and how do you rationalise your results?
(a) $\underline{\text{K}}\text{I}_3$
(b) $\text{H}_2\underline{\text{S}}_4\text{O}_6$
(c) $\underline{\text{Fe}}_3\text{O}_4$
(d) $\underline{\text{C}}\text{H}_3\underline{\text{C}}\text{H}_2\text{OH}$
(e) $\underline{\text{C}}\text{H}_3\underline{\text{C}}\text{OOH}$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

(a) $\text{K}\underline{\text{I}}_3$:
Conventional formula gives average oxidation number of $\text{I} = -1/3$.
Rationalisation: $\text{KI}_3$ consists of $\text{K}^+$ and triiodide ion $\text{I}_3^-$. The triiodide ion is formed by coordination of $\text{I}_2$ with iodide ion: $[ ext{I}^- \leftarrow \text{I}-\text{I}]$. Thus, one iodine atom has oxidation number $-1$ and the other two iodine atoms have oxidation number $0$. Average $= (-1 + 0 + 0)/3 = -1/3$.

(b) $\text{H}_2\underline{\text{S}}_4\text{O}_6$ (Tetrathionate ion):
$2(+1) + 4x + 6(-2) = 0 \implies 4x = 10 \implies x = +2.5$ (average).
Rationalisation: Structure is $\text{O}_3\text{S}-\text{S}-\text{S}-\text{SO}_3^{2-}$. The two terminal sulphur atoms are bonded to three oxygen atoms and have oxidation number $+5$ each. The two central bridging sulphur atoms are bonded only to sulphur atoms (homonuclear bond) and have oxidation number $0$ each. Average $= (5 + 0 + 0 + 5)/4 = +2.5$.

(c) $\underline{\text{Fe}}_3\text{O}_4$ (Magnetite):
$3x + 4(-2) = 0 \implies x = +8/3$.
Rationalisation: $\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4$ is a mixed oxide: $\text{FeO} \cdot \text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$. It contains one $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ ion (oxidation number $+2$) and two $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ ions (oxidation number $+3$ each). Average $= (2 + 2 \times 3)/3 = +8/3$.

(d) $\underline{\text{C}}_2\text{H}_6\text{O}$ (Ethanol, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$):
For $\text{C}-2$ ($-\text{CH}_3$): bonded to $3\text{ H}$ ($+1$ each) and one $\text{C}$ ($0$): $x + 3(+1) + 0 = 0 \implies \mathbf{x = -3}$.
For $\text{C}-1$ ($-\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$): bonded to $2\text{ H}$ ($+1$ each), one $\text{OH}$ ($-1$), and one $\text{C}$ ($0$): $x + 2(+1) + (-1) = 0 \implies x + 1 = 0 \implies \mathbf{x = -1}$.
Average oxidation number of carbon $= (-3 + -1)/2 = -2$.

(e) $\underline{\text{C}}_2\text{H}_4\text{O}_2$ (Ethanoic acid, $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$):
For $\text{C}-2$ ($-\text{CH}_3$): $x + 3(+1) = 0 \implies \mathbf{x = -3}$.
For $\text{C}-1$ ($-\text{COOH}$): bonded to $=\text{O}$ ($-2$) and $-\text{OH}$ ($-1$): $x + (-2) + (-1) = 0 \implies \mathbf{x = +3}$.
Average oxidation number of carbon $= (-3 + +3)/2 = 0$.

7.3
NCERT Exercise
Justify that the following reactions are redox reactions:
(a) $\text{CuO}(\text{s}) + \text{H}_2(\text{g}) \rightarrow \text{Cu}(\text{s}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{g})$
(b) $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3(\text{s}) + 3\text{CO}(\text{g}) \rightarrow 2\text{Fe}(\text{s}) + 3\text{CO}_2(\text{g})$
(c) $4\text{BCl}_3(\text{g}) + 3\text{LiAlH}_4(\text{s}) \rightarrow 2\text{B}_2\text{H}_6(\text{g}) + 3\text{LiCl}(\text{s}) + 3\text{AlCl}_3(\text{s})$
(d) $2\text{K}(\text{s}) + \text{F}_2(\text{g}) \rightarrow 2\text{KF}(\text{s})$
(e) $4\text{NH}_3(\text{g}) + 5\text{O}_2(\text{g}) \rightarrow 4\text{NO}(\text{g}) + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{g})$.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

A reaction is a redox reaction if it involves simultaneous oxidation (increase in oxidation number) and reduction (decrease in oxidation number).

(a) $\text{CuO} + \text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{Cu} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$:
• $\text{Cu}$: $+2 \rightarrow 0$ (Decrease $\implies$ Reduction; $\text{CuO}$ is oxidising agent).
• $\text{H}$: $0 \rightarrow +1$ (Increase $\implies$ Oxidation; $\text{H}_2$ is reducing agent). $\implies$ Redox reaction.

(b) $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{CO} \rightarrow 2\text{Fe} + 3\text{CO}_2$:
• $\text{Fe}$: $+3 \rightarrow 0$ (Decrease $\implies$ Reduction; $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ is oxidising agent).
• $\text{C}$: $+2 \rightarrow +4$ (Increase $\implies$ Oxidation; $\text{CO}$ is reducing agent). $\implies$ Redox reaction.

(c) $4\text{BCl}_3 + 3\text{LiAlH}_4 \rightarrow 2\text{B}_2\text{H}_6 + 3\text{LiCl} + 3\text{AlCl}_3$:
• $\text{B}$: $+3 \rightarrow -3$ in $\text{B}_2\text{H}_6$ (Decrease $\implies$ Reduction; $\text{BCl}_3$ is oxidant).
• $\text{H}$ in $\text{LiAlH}_4$ (hydride, $-1$) $\rightarrow +1$ in $\text{B}_2\text{H}_6$ (Increase $\implies$ Oxidation; $\text{LiAlH}_4$ is reductant). $\implies$ Redox reaction.

(d) $2\text{K} + \text{F}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{KF}$:
• $\text{K}$: $0 \rightarrow +1$ (Increase $\implies$ Oxidation; $\text{K}$ is reducing agent).
• $\text{F}$: $0 \rightarrow -1$ (Decrease $\implies$ Reduction; $\text{F}_2$ is oxidising agent). $\implies$ Redox reaction.

(e) $4\text{NH}_3 + 5\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 4\text{NO} + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$:
• $\text{N}$: $-3 \rightarrow +2$ in $\text{NO}$ (Increase $\implies$ Oxidation; $\text{NH}_3$ is reducing agent).
• $\text{O}$: $0 \rightarrow -2$ in $\text{NO}$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (Decrease $\implies$ Reduction; $\text{O}_2$ is oxidising agent). $\implies$ Redox reaction.

NCERT Exercise
Fluorine reacts with dilute $\text{NaOH}$ and concentrated $\text{NaOH}$ differently:
(a) $2\text{F}_2(\text{g}) + 2\text{OH}^-(\text{aq}) \rightarrow 2\text{F}^-(\text{aq}) + \text{OF}_2(\text{g}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l})$
(b) $2\text{F}_2(\text{g}) + 4\text{OH}^-(\text{aq}) \rightarrow 4\text{F}^-(\text{aq}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g}) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l})$
Show that these are redox reactions.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

(a) Reaction with dilute $\text{NaOH}$:
$$2\overset{0}{\text{F}_2} + 2\text{OH}^- \rightarrow 2\overset{-1}{\text{F}^-} + \overset{+2}{\text{O}}\overset{-1}{\text{F}_2} + \text{H}_2\overset{-2}{\text{O}}$$ • $\text{F}_2$: Oxidation number decreases from $0$ to $-1$ in $\text{F}^-$ and $\text{OF}_2$ $\implies$ Reduction.
• Oxygen in $\text{OH}^-$: Increases from $-2$ to $+2$ in $\text{OF}_2$ $\implies$ Oxidation.
Since oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously, it is a redox reaction.

(b) Reaction with concentrated $\text{NaOH}$:
$$2\overset{0}{\text{F}_2} + 4\text{OH}^- \rightarrow 4\overset{-1}{\text{F}^-} + \overset{0}{\text{O}_2} + 2\text{H}_2\overset{-2}{\text{O}}$$ • $\text{F}_2$: Oxidation number decreases from $0$ to $-1$ $\implies$ Reduction.
• Oxygen in $\text{OH}^-$: Oxidation number increases from $-2$ to $0$ in $\text{O}_2$ $\implies$ Oxidation.
Therefore, this is also a redox reaction.

NCERT Exercise
Calculate the oxidation number of sulphur, chromium and nitrogen in $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_5$, $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$ and $\text{NO}_3^-$. Suggest structure of these compounds. Account for the fallacy.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

1. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_5$ (Caro's acid / Peroxomonosulphuric acid):
• Fallacy from conventional rule: $2(+1) + x + 5(-2) = 0 \implies x - 8 = 0 \implies x = +8$. But sulphur has only 6 valence electrons, so its maximum oxidation number cannot exceed $+6$.
• Structural rationalisation: Structure contains one peroxo group ($-\text{O}-\text{O}-$ with O.N. $-1$ each) and three normal oxide oxygens ($-2$ each):
$$\text{HO}-\text{SO}_2-\text{O}-\text{OH}$$ $$2(+1) + x + 3(-2) + 2(-1) = 0 \implies 2 + x - 6 - 2 = 0 \implies \mathbf{x = +6}$.

2. $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$ (Dichromate ion):
$$2x + 7(-2) = -2 \implies 2x - 14 = -2 \implies 2x = 12 \implies \mathbf{x = +6}$$ Structure: Two tetrahedral $\text{CrO}_4$ units sharing one bridging oxygen atom ($\text{Cr}-\text{O}-\text{Cr}$ bond angle $\approx 126^\circ$). Both chromium atoms are in their maximum $+6$ oxidation state; no peroxo linkage, so conventional calculation is correct.

3. $\text{NO}_3^-$ (Nitrate ion):
$$x + 3(-2) = -1 \implies x - 6 = -1 \implies \mathbf{x = +5}$$ Structure: Trigonal planar ($sp^2$) with nitrogen forming one single bond to $-\text{O}^-$, one double bond to $=\text{O}$, and one coordinate/dative covalent bond to $\rightarrow \text{O}$. Maximum oxidation state of nitrogen is $+5$ (all 5 valence electrons involved in bonding). No fallacy.

7.8
NCERT Exercise
While sulphur dioxide and hydrogen peroxide can act as oxidising as well as reducing agents in their reactions, ozone and nitric acid act only as oxidants. Why?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Concept: An element can act as an oxidising agent if its oxidation state can decrease, and as a reducing agent if its oxidation state can increase. If it is already in an intermediate oxidation state, it can undergo both.

1. $\text{SO}_2$: Sulphur is in the $+4$ oxidation state. The allowable range for sulphur (Group 16) is $-2$ to $+6$. Since $+4$ is an intermediate state, sulphur can be oxidised to $+6$ (acting as reductant) or reduced to $0$ or $-2$ (acting as oxidant).

2. $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$: Oxygen is in the $-1$ oxidation state (peroxide). Allowable states for oxygen range from $-2$ to $+2$. Oxygen can be oxidised to $0$ in $\text{O}_2$ (acting as reductant) or reduced to $-2$ in $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (acting as oxidant).

3. $\text{HNO}_3$: Nitrogen is in its maximum oxidation state of $+5$ (Group 15 has 5 valence electrons). Nitrogen cannot lose any more electrons to increase its oxidation state. It can only accept electrons and reduce its oxidation number (e.g., to $+4, +2, +1, 0, -3$). Hence, $\text{HNO}_3$ acts only as an oxidant.

4. $\text{O}_3$: Ozone easily decomposes to liberate nascent oxygen: $\text{O}_3 \rightarrow \text{O}_2 + [\text{O}]$, which has an exceptionally high electron affinity to reduce oxygen to $-2$. Therefore, ozone acts exclusively as a powerful oxidant.

Consider the reactions:
(a) $6\text{CO}_2(\text{g}) + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6(\text{aq}) + 6\text{O}_2(\text{g})$
(b) $\text{O}_3(\text{g}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2(\text{l}) \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) + 2\text{O}_2(\text{g})$
Why is it more appropriate to write these reactions as:
(a) $6\text{CO}_2(\text{g}) + 12\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) \rightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6(\text{aq}) + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) + 6\text{O}_2(\text{g})$
(b) $\text{O}_3(\text{g}) + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2(\text{l}) \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g})$?
Suggest a technique to investigate the path of the above (a) and (b) redox reactions.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

(a) Photosynthesis Reaction:
In photosynthesis, isotopic tracer studies using $^{18}\text{O}$-labelled water ($\text{H}_2^{18}\text{O}$) proved that the entire $6\text{ moles}$ of oxygen gas produced ($6\text{O}_2$) originates exclusively from water, not from carbon dioxide.
Since $6\text{ molecules}$ of $\text{O}_2$ contain $12\text{ oxygen atoms}$, at least $12\text{ molecules}$ of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ must be consumed. The $6\text{ molecules}$ of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ produced on the right-hand side are regenerated during subsequent biochemical cycles (light-independent reactions). Hence writing $12\text{H}_2\text{O}$ on the reactant side reflects the true reaction mechanism.

(b) Ozone and Hydrogen Peroxide Reaction:
When isotopic $^{18}\text{O}$-labelled $\text{H}_2^{18}\text{O}_2$ reacts with normal ozone $\text{O}_3$, the evolved oxygen consists of two distinct pools:
• One mole of $^{18}\text{O}_2$ originates purely from $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ oxidation.
• One mole of ordinary $\text{O}_2$ originates from ozone decomposition.
Writing $\text{O}_2 + \text{O}_2$ emphasises that the two dioxygen molecules arise from two different mechanistic pathways.

Investigative Technique:
The path and mechanism of these reactions are investigated using Isotopic Tracer Technique (using heavy isotope $^{18}\text{O}$ followed by mass spectrometric analysis).

7.10
NCERT Exercise
The compound $\text{AgF}_2$ is an unstable compound. However, if formed, the compound acts as a very strong oxidising agent. Why?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Reason:
In $\text{AgF}_2$, silver is present in the $+2$ oxidation state ($\text{Ag}^{2+}$).
Silver normally exists in the exceptionally stable $+1$ oxidation state ($\text{Ag}^+$) with a completely filled, stable $4d^{10}$ electronic configuration.
The $\text{Ag}^{2+}$ ion has an incomplete $4d^9$ configuration and has an extraordinarily high standard reduction potential:
$$\text{Ag}^{2+}(\text{aq}) + e^- \rightarrow \text{Ag}^+(\text{aq}), \quad E^\circ = +1.98\text{ V}$$ Because of this very large positive electrode potential, $\text{Ag}^{2+}$ has an immense tendency to gain an electron to attain the stable $4d^{10}$ state, making $\text{AgF}_2$ a very strong oxidising agent.

7.11
NCERT Exercise
Whenever a reaction between an oxidising agent and a reducing agent is carried out, a compound of lower oxidation state is formed if the reducing agent is in excess and a compound of higher oxidation state is formed if the oxidising agent is in excess. Justify this statement giving three illustrations.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Illustration 1: Carbon and Oxygen:
• Excess Reducing Agent ($\text{C}$): Carbon burns in limited oxygen to form carbon monoxide (lower oxidation state $+2$):
$$2\text{C}(\text{s, excess}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g}) \rightarrow 2\overset{+2}{\text{CO}}(\text{g})$$
• Excess Oxidising Agent ($\text{O}_2$): In excess oxygen, carbon is oxidised to carbon dioxide (higher oxidation state $+4$):
$$\text{C}(\text{s}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g, excess}) \rightarrow \overset{+4}{\text{CO}_2}(\text{g})$$

Illustration 2: Phosphorus and Chlorine:
• Excess Reducing Agent ($\text{P}_4$): Reacts to form phosphorus trichloride (lower state $+3$):
$$\text{P}_4(\text{s, excess}) + 6\text{Cl}_2(\text{g}) \rightarrow 4\overset{+3}{\text{PCl}_3}(\text{l})$$
• Excess Oxidising Agent ($\text{Cl}_2$): Forms phosphorus pentachloride (higher state $+5$):
$$\text{P}_4(\text{s}) + 10\text{Cl}_2(\text{g, excess}) \rightarrow 4\overset{+5}{\text{PCl}_5}(\text{s})$$

Illustration 3: Sodium and Oxygen:
• Excess Reducing Agent ($\text{Na}$): Limited oxygen forms sodium oxide (lower oxidation state for oxygen, oxide $-2$):
$$4\text{Na}(\text{s, excess}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g}) \rightarrow 2\text{Na}_2\overset{-2}{\text{O}}(\text{s})$$
• Excess Oxidising Agent ($\text{O}_2$): Forms sodium peroxide (peroxide $-1$):

✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Balanced chemical equation:
$$4\text{NH}_3(\text{g}) + 5\text{O}_2(\text{g}) \rightarrow 4\text{NO}(\text{g}) + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{g})$$ Molar masses: $\text{NH}_3 = 17\text{ g mol}^{-1}$, $\text{O}_2 = 32\text{ g mol}^{-1}$, $\text{NO} = 30\text{ g mol}^{-1}$.
$4 \times 17 = 68\text{ g of } \text{NH}_3$ reacts with $5 \times 32 = 160\text{ g of } \text{O}_2$ to give $4 \times 30 = 120\text{ g of } \text{NO}$.

Step 2: Determine Limiting Reactant:
Available moles of $\text{NH}_3 = \frac{10.0}{17} = 0.588\text{ mol}$.
Available moles of $\text{O}_2 = \frac{20.0}{32} = 0.625\text{ mol}$.
From stoichiometry, $0.588\text{ mol of } \text{NH}_3$ requires $\frac{5}{4} \times 0.588 = 0.735\text{ mol of } \text{O}_2$.
Since only $0.625\text{ mol of } \text{O}_2$ is available, $\text{O}_2$ is the limiting reactant.

From $160\text{ g of } \text{O}_2$, $\text{NO}$ produced $= 120\text{ g}$.

NCERT Exercise
Why does an electrochemical cell stop working after some time?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution

Thermodynamic and Kinetic Explanation:
As the electrochemical cell operates, the spontaneous cell reaction consumes reactants and produces products:

1. Change in Ionic Concentrations:
At the anode, metal dissolves into solution increasing anode cation concentration ($[\text{Zn}^{2+}]$ rises). At the cathode, metal ions discharge, lowering cathode cation concentration ($[\text{Cu}^{2+}]$ falls).

2. Application of Nernst Equation:
$$E_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cell}} - \frac{2.303 RT}{nF} \log\frac{[\text{Anode Cation}]}{[\text{Cathode Cation}]}$$ As the concentration ratio $Q$ increases, the term $\frac{2.303 RT}{nF}\log Q$ increases, causing $E_{\text{cell}}$ to decrease continuously.

3. Equilibrium State ($E_{\text{cell}} = 0$):
Eventually, the system reaches dynamic chemical equilibrium where the electrode potential of the cathode equals the electrode potential of the anode ($E_{\text{cathode}} = E_{\text{anode}}$). At this point, $\Delta G = 0$ and $E_{\text{cell}} = 0$. No net electron flow occurs, and the cell stops working.
