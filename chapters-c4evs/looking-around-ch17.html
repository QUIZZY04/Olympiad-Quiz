Chapter 17: Nandita in Mumbai

Rural-Urban Contrast, Slum Tenements (Chawls), High-Rise Skyscrapers & Water Woes | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Nandita comes from a village to Mumbai for her mother's hospital treatment.
  • Contrasts in living conditions:
  • 1. Mama's Chawl room: Single room where family sleeps, cooks, sits, and washes; public toilet shared by the whole lane (filthy and foul-smelling).
  • 2. Village House: One room but separate open courtyard, separate cooking area, and separate outdoor bathing space.
  • Water struggles: In the chawl, waking at 4:00 am to fight in long queues at the public street tap. In the high-rise skyscraper (Babita's flat), water flows continuously from shiny taps.
  • Experiencing elevators for the first time; Chowpatty beach and Mumbai sea view.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (16 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Sociological Contrast • Urban Living
What differences did Nandita find between getting water in her village and getting water in Mumbai's chawl? Why was she afraid of taking the high-rise lift initially?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Difference in Water Collection:
• In the Village: There were no water taps, but Nandita walked 20 minutes to a clean village pond or well. There were never any fights or shouting; water was available throughout the day.
• In Mumbai's Chawl: Water arrived at a single public tap only from 4:00 am to 5:00 am. If you didn't reach on time, there was no water for the whole day. People pushed, fought, and screamed in long bucket queues.

2. Fear of the Lift:
Nandita had never seen a multi-story iron cage moving vertically. When she entered the lift with multiple people and the doors slid shut, the sudden upward acceleration made her stomach churn, causing her to hold onto the metal railing in panic.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Detailed contrast between village pond and Mumbai public tap queues 2.0 M
Psychological explanation of first-time lift vertigo and fear 1.0 M
Q2 Urban Sociology • Chawl Life
Compare Nandita's village house with her Mama's house in Mumbai regarding space, cooking, and water supply.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Village House vs. Mumbai Chawl Room:
1. Space: Village house has an open courtyard, separate veranda, and distinct rooms; Mama's chawl is a single cramped room where the family sits, sleeps, and cooks.
2. Cooking: Village house has a separate outdoor cooking chulha; in Mumbai, cooking is done in a corner of the same small room.
3. Water Supply: Village has a pond or well 20 minutes away where water is fetched peacefully; in Mumbai, a public street tap opens at 4 AM for only a short time, leading to fierce shouting and fighting among residents.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Three distinct points of contrast (space, cooking, water access) 3.0 M
Q3 Social Reality • High-Rise Apartments
How did Nandita feel when she visited the tall multistory building where her Mami worked as a domestic helper?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Nandita's Experience in the High-Rise:
Nandita was amazed to see an elevator (lift) that zoomed up like a big flying iron cage with lights and fans. Inside Babloo's flat on the 12th floor, she was stunned to see separate rooms for sitting, sleeping, and dining, and a bathroom with running taps where water flowed continuously while people let it run wastefully.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Lift experience and amazement at indoor plumbing contrast 2.0 M
Q4 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'Nandita in Mumbai', describe how students can conduct an observational study in their school or residential community. What three parameters should they record in their field notebook?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Field Observation Protocol:
1. Direct Inquiry: Students observe the environment, community infrastructure, or natural ecosystem related to Nandita in Mumbai during a guided campus walk.
2. Three Field Log Parameters:
• (a) Physical features, adaptations, or materials observed in nature/daily life.
• (b) Ecological dependencies (availability of clean water, sunlight, soil quality, or habitat safety).
• (c) Human actions observed (cleanliness practices, waste disposal, or conservation efforts).
3. Conclusion: Maintaining a nature journal fosters environmental sensitivity and scientific curiosity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Observation methodology clearly outlined 1.5 M
Three tangible field parameters detailed 1.5 M
Q5 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles in 'Nandita in Mumbai', classify living organisms or natural resources into distinct categories and state the scientific rationale behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Renewable / Biodegradable Resources): Elements that naturally replenish and decompose safely without releasing toxins into the soil or waterways.
• Category 2 (Non-Renewable / Non-Biodegradable Elements): Materials that persist in the environment for decades, requiring active recycling and mindful consumption.
• Scientific Rationale: Differentiating between biodegradable and non-biodegradable items empowers students to adopt zero-waste and eco-friendly lifestyles.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q6 Community Living & Cultural Diversity • Social Harmony
How does 'Nandita in Mumbai' illustrate the importance of community helpers, traditional skills, or regional diversity across India? Give two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Helpers & Regional Diversity:
1. Traditional Craftsmanship & Occupations: Across Indian states, local artisans, farmers, weavers, and sanitation workers contribute essential services that sustain healthy communities.
2. Mutual Interdependence: Every profession deserves dignity and respect. When people of diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds cooperate, villages and cities flourish in peace.
3. Examples: From weavers crafting heritage textiles to transport workers navigating rugged terrains, every occupation shapes our shared national heritage.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of community interdependence 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting dignity of labor 1.5 M
Q7 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 4 student can implement at home and school inspired by the teachings of 'Nandita in Mumbai'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Conservation: Turn off taps while brushing teeth and reuse rinse-water from washed vegetables to water garden pots.
2. Waste Segregation: Separate organic wet kitchen peelings into compost bins while sending clean paper, cardboard, and plastics for recycling.
3. Protecting Green Cover: Plant a native sapling on birthdays and set up a bird bath on balconies during warm summer months.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Water saving technique explained 1.0 M
Waste segregation practice detailed 1.0 M
Flora/fauna protection action listed 1.0 M
Q8 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze a real-world scenario from 'Nandita in Mumbai': If a local natural waterbody or forest area is polluted by garbage, what steps should the local community take to restore it?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Restoration Action Plan:
1. Immediate Cleanup Drive: Organize student and resident volunteer drives equipped with gloves to safely collect floating plastics and litter.
2. Preventive Regulations: Install prominent awareness signboards prohibiting dumping, accompanied by secure covered dustbins.
3. Biological Rejuvenation: Plant native bank-stabilizing trees and reeds along water edges to prevent soil erosion and encourage aquatic life.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate cleanup measures proposed 1.5 M
Long-term preventive community measures 1.5 M
Q9 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate empathy towards stray animals, birds, and elderly citizens in their daily lives, as highlighted in 'Nandita in Mumbai'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Everyday Empathy in Practice:
1. Care for Living Creatures: Never throw stones or tease stray animals; keep a bowl of clean drinking water in shady spots.
2. Respect for Elders: Offer seats in public transport, speak politely, listen patiently to grandparents' stories, and assist them in carrying light groceries.
3. Core Principle: Compassion for all living beings builds a humane, peaceful, and responsible society.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Kindness towards animals and birds 1.5 M
Respectful support towards senior citizens 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Water Wastage Awareness in Cities

While cleaning utensils at the high-rise flat, Nandita noticed the housemaid left the kitchen tap running continuously while gossiping.

Analytical Task: Why did this upset Nandita, and what should every citizen do to prevent water wastage?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Nandita was heartbroken because she knew how desperately her Mama and aunties fought at 4:00 am for just a single bucket of water, while here precious drinking water was poured down the drain carelessly. Citizens must turn off taps while brushing or soaping dishes, repair leaking valves, and cherish every drop.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for emotional contrast with slum water crisis; 1.5 Marks for actionable conservation practices.
