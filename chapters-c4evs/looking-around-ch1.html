Chapter 1: Going to School

Transport Modes Across India: Bamboo Bridges, Trolleys, Vallam, Camel Carts & Jugaad | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Different ways children travel to school across geographical terrains in India.
  • Assam: Walking on elevated bamboo and rope bridges across flooded streams.
  • Ladakh: Crossing deep canyons using a wooden trolley pulling along a fixed iron rope with pulleys.
  • Kerala: Traveling in small wooden boats called 'Vallam' to cross backwater canals.
  • Rajasthan (Camel carts), Gujarat (Jugaad vehicle powered by motorcycle engine), and Uttarakhand (rocky mountain paths).

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (16 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Geographical Adaptation • Transport
How do children in Ladakh reach their school across wide and deep rivers? What is a 'Vallam', and in which state of India is it used?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. School Travel in Ladakh:
In Ladakh, rivers are very wide and deep. Children cross them using a strong wooden trolley (an open box with seats). A thick, strong iron rope is tied securely to giant rocks or sturdy trees on both river banks. A pulley rolls over the iron rope, pulling the trolley carrying four to five children smoothly across the canyon.

2. Vallam:
A 'Vallam' is a small traditional wooden boat used in parts of Kerala to help children cross backwater lagoons, rivers, and canals to reach school.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Detailed explanation of Ladakh trolley and pulley system 1.5 M
Definition of Vallam and Kerala state reference 1.5 M
Q2 Regional Transport • Bamboo Bridges & Jugaad
How do children in Assam use bamboo bridges during the monsoon? What is a 'Jugaad' vehicle in Gujarat?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Bamboo Bridges in Assam:
During the heavy monsoon in Assam, water often rises knee-deep or waist-deep on village roads. Children and villagers tie long, sturdy bamboo poles and ropes together across streams to create narrow, elevated footbridges. Children hold their books in one hand and grasp the bamboo railing with the other to cross safely.

2. The 'Jugaad' Vehicle in Gujarat:
A 'Jugaad' is an ingenious locally-assembled vehicle made by attaching the front engine and handlebar of a motorcycle or scooter to a wooden cart chassis with wheels at the back. It carries several children together to school, making a characteristic 'phut-phut-phut' sound.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Assam bamboo bridge construction and crossing technique 1.5 M
Gujarat Jugaad mechanical structure and purpose 1.5 M
Q3 Terrain Adaptation • Jungles and Snow
What difficulties do children face while walking through thick jungles or deep snow to reach school?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Walking Through Thick Jungles:
In dense forests, sunlight barely penetrates, creating darkness even during the day. The paths are overgrown with thorns, and children must stay alert to snake bites, insects, and wild animals, guiding themselves by listening to bird calls and animal sounds.

2. Walking on Snow (Northern Hills):
Children must hold hands carefully. If the snow is fresh and soft, their feet sink deep into it, making walking exhausting. If the snow is frozen hard, the surface becomes icy and dangerously slippery, causing children to slip and fall.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Jungle navigation risks and sensory awareness 1.5 M
Soft snow sinking vs frozen ice slipping hazards 1.5 M

Q5 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles in 'Going to School', classify living organisms or natural resources into distinct categories and state the scientific rationale behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Renewable / Biodegradable Resources): Elements that naturally replenish and decompose safely without releasing toxins into the soil or waterways.
• Category 2 (Non-Renewable / Non-Biodegradable Elements): Materials that persist in the environment for decades, requiring active recycling and mindful consumption.
• Scientific Rationale: Differentiating between biodegradable and non-biodegradable items empowers students to adopt zero-waste and eco-friendly lifestyles.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q6 Community Living & Cultural Diversity • Social Harmony
How does 'Going to School' illustrate the importance of community helpers, traditional skills, or regional diversity across India? Give two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Helpers & Regional Diversity:
1. Traditional Craftsmanship & Occupations: Across Indian states, local artisans, farmers, weavers, and sanitation workers contribute essential services that sustain healthy communities.
2. Mutual Interdependence: Every profession deserves dignity and respect. When people of diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds cooperate, villages and cities flourish in peace.
3. Examples: From weavers crafting heritage textiles to transport workers navigating rugged terrains, every occupation shapes our shared national heritage.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of community interdependence 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting dignity of labor 1.5 M
Q7 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 4 student can implement at home and school inspired by the teachings of 'Going to School'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Conservation: Turn off taps while brushing teeth and reuse rinse-water from washed vegetables to water garden pots.
2. Waste Segregation: Separate organic wet kitchen peelings into compost bins while sending clean paper, cardboard, and plastics for recycling.
3. Protecting Green Cover: Plant a native sapling on birthdays and set up a bird bath on balconies during warm summer months.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Water saving technique explained 1.0 M
Waste segregation practice detailed 1.0 M
Flora/fauna protection action listed 1.0 M
Q8 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze a real-world scenario from 'Going to School': If a local natural waterbody or forest area is polluted by garbage, what steps should the local community take to restore it?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Restoration Action Plan:
1. Immediate Cleanup Drive: Organize student and resident volunteer drives equipped with gloves to safely collect floating plastics and litter.
2. Preventive Regulations: Install prominent awareness signboards prohibiting dumping, accompanied by secure covered dustbins.
3. Biological Rejuvenation: Plant native bank-stabilizing trees and reeds along water edges to prevent soil erosion and encourage aquatic life.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate cleanup measures proposed 1.5 M
Long-term preventive community measures 1.5 M
Q9 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate empathy towards stray animals, birds, and elderly citizens in their daily lives, as highlighted in 'Going to School'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Everyday Empathy in Practice:
1. Care for Living Creatures: Never throw stones or tease stray animals; keep a bowl of clean drinking water in shady spots.
2. Respect for Elders: Offer seats in public transport, speak politely, listen patiently to grandparents' stories, and assist them in carrying light groceries.
3. Core Principle: Compassion for all living beings builds a humane, peaceful, and responsible society.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Kindness towards animals and birds 1.5 M
Respectful support towards senior citizens 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Universal Right to Safe Education

In many hilly villages of Uttarakhand, the path to school is strewn with steep, jagged rocks and wild bushes.

Analytical Task: What physical qualities do children develop traveling daily on mountain paths, and how should local panchayats assist them?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Children develop extraordinary balance, foot agility, strong leg muscles, and fearless resilience. Panchayats must pave safe all-weather stone pathways, erect safety railings near steep precipices, and build small footbridges over mountain torrents to ensure zero dropouts.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for physical resilience and agility; 1.5 Marks for civic infrastructure improvements.
