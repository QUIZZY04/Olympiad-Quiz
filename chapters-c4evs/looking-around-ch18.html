Chapter 18: Too Much Water, Too Little Water

Water Crises: Water Parks in Maharashtra, Diarrhea in Nalgonda & Village Water Committees (Holgundi) | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • The paradox of water distribution: Massive water parks wasting millions of litres on joy slides in Bazar Gaon, Maharashtra, while neighboring farmers suffer severe drinking water shortages.
  • Waterborne diseases: Drinking contaminated water causing severe diarrhea and vomiting.
  • Life-saving home remedy: Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) — 1 teaspoon sugar + a pinch of salt dissolved in 1 glass of boiled, cooled water.
  • Empowerment: Children's Water Panchayat (Bheema Sangh) in Holgundi, Karnataka, rejuvenating dried village stepwells and restoring water security.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (16 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Public Health & First Aid Science
What causes severe dehydration during diarrhea and vomiting? How do you prepare a homemade Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) step-by-step?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Cause of Dehydration:
During severe diarrhea and vomiting, the body rapidly loses critical water and essential mineral salts (electrolytes). If these fluids are not replaced immediately, vital organs can fail, which is especially fatal for young children.

2. Preparation of Homemade ORS:
• Step 1: Boil one glass of clean drinking water and let it cool completely.
• Step 2: Add one level teaspoon of sugar (provides instant energy and aids electrolyte absorption).
• Step 3: Add one small pinch of salt (restores sodium electrolytes; taste should not be saltier than tears).
• Step 4: Stir thoroughly until completely dissolved. Sip slowly throughout the day.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Biological danger of water and electrolyte loss 1.0 M
Exact 4-step preparation recipe (boiled water, 1 tsp sugar, pinch salt) 2.0 M
Q2 Public Health • Waterborne Illness & ORS
What causes diarrhea and vomiting? How can you prepare an Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) at home in an emergency?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Causes of Diarrhea and Vomiting:
Drinking water contaminated with sewage bacteria, amoebae, or eating unwashed, fly-infested food causes intestinal infections, leading to rapid loss of body water and vital salts (dehydration).

2. Home-Made ORS Preparation:
• Boil one glass (approx. 250 ml) of clean drinking water and let it cool.
• Add one level teaspoon of sugar and a tiny pinch of salt.
• Stir until completely dissolved. Sip slowly to replenish lost fluids and electrolytes.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Contaminated water/food etiology explained 1.5 M
Accurate water, sugar, and salt proportions for home ORS 1.5 M
Q3 Civic Equity • Water Commercialization
In the chapter, a luxurious water park operated with splashing fountains while neighbouring villagers had no drinking water. What moral lesson does this highlight?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Lesson on Water Justice:
Water is a fundamental human right essential for survival. Wasting millions of litres of precious potable water on recreational slides and water fountains while neighboring poor citizens queue for hours for a single bucket of dirty water represents gross civic inequality. Clean drinking water for survival must always take priority over commercial entertainment.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Articulating right to clean water over luxury recreational waste 2.0 M

Q5 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles in 'Too Much Water, Too Little Water', classify living organisms or natural resources into distinct categories and state the scientific rationale behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Renewable / Biodegradable Resources): Elements that naturally replenish and decompose safely without releasing toxins into the soil or waterways.
• Category 2 (Non-Renewable / Non-Biodegradable Elements): Materials that persist in the environment for decades, requiring active recycling and mindful consumption.
• Scientific Rationale: Differentiating between biodegradable and non-biodegradable items empowers students to adopt zero-waste and eco-friendly lifestyles.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q6 Community Living & Cultural Diversity • Social Harmony
How does 'Too Much Water, Too Little Water' illustrate the importance of community helpers, traditional skills, or regional diversity across India? Give two concrete examples.

Community Helpers & Regional Diversity:

Q7 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 4 student can implement at home and school inspired by the teachings of 'Too Much Water, Too Little Water'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Conservation: Turn off taps while brushing teeth and reuse rinse-water from washed vegetables to water garden pots.
2. Waste Segregation: Separate organic wet kitchen peelings into compost bins while sending clean paper, cardboard, and plastics for recycling.
3. Protecting Green Cover: Plant a native sapling on birthdays and set up a bird bath on balconies during warm summer months.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Water saving technique explained 1.0 M
Waste segregation practice detailed 1.0 M
Flora/fauna protection action listed 1.0 M
Q8 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze a real-world scenario from 'Too Much Water, Too Little Water': If a local natural waterbody or forest area is polluted by garbage, what steps should the local community take to restore it?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Restoration Action Plan:
1. Immediate Cleanup Drive: Organize student and resident volunteer drives equipped with gloves to safely collect floating plastics and litter.
2. Preventive Regulations: Install prominent awareness signboards prohibiting dumping, accompanied by secure covered dustbins.
3. Biological Rejuvenation: Plant native bank-stabilizing trees and reeds along water edges to prevent soil erosion and encourage aquatic life.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate cleanup measures proposed 1.5 M
Long-term preventive community measures 1.5 M
Q9 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate empathy towards stray animals, birds, and elderly citizens in their daily lives, as highlighted in 'Too Much Water, Too Little Water'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Everyday Empathy in Practice:
1. Care for Living Creatures: Never throw stones or tease stray animals; keep a bowl of clean drinking water in shady spots.
2. Respect for Elders: Offer seats in public transport, speak politely, listen patiently to grandparents' stories, and assist them in carrying light groceries.
3. Core Principle: Compassion for all living beings builds a humane, peaceful, and responsible society.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Kindness towards animals and birds 1.5 M
Respectful support towards senior citizens 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Bheema Sangh (Children's Water Panchayat)

In Holgundi village (Karnataka), adults gave up farming because village wells had dried up. The children's panchayat (Bheema Sangh) took charge.

Analytical Task: How did the children of Bheema Sangh restore water security to their village?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
The children surveyed the hill slopes and discovered the ancient community tank was choked with mud, weeds, and cracked walls. They convinced elders, cleaned the silt, repaired stone bunds, and planted trees on the slopes. When monsoon rains arrived, the tank held water, recharging underground wells so farming resumed.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for identifying choked tank and desilting; 1.5 Marks for bund repair and groundwater recharge.
