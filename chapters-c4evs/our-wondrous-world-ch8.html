Chapter 8: How Things are Made

Artisanal Crafts: Pottery, Handloom Weaving, Carpentry, Metal Casting & Indian Guilds | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Traditional Indian craft traditions: Terracotta pottery, handloom textiles, wood carving, and metal casting.
  • The stages of pottery: Digging clay, kneading to remove air bubbles, shaping on the potter's wheel, sun-drying, firing in a kiln (bhatti), and painting.
  • Handloom weaving: The interlacing of lengthwise threads (warp) and crosswise threads (weft) on a wooden loom.
  • Carpentry tools and techniques: Sawing, chiselling, planing, joinery, and sandpaper finishing.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (14 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Traditional Crafts • Pottery Process
Describe the step-by-step process of making an earthen pitcher (matka) on a potter's wheel. Why must the pot be baked in a kiln?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Steps in Making a Matka:
1. Clay Preparation: Fine, sticky riverbed clay is dug up, stones and grit are sifted out, and water is mixed in.
2. Kneading: The wet clay is kneaded vigorously with hands and feet to remove air bubbles and create uniform plasticity.
3. Shaping on Wheel: A lump of clay is centered on the spinning potter's wheel. The potter uses skilled fingers and water to hollow and pull up the walls into a round pot shape.
4. Beating & Sun-Drying: The bottom is beaten with a wooden mallet (thapa) and stone anvil (pindi) to make the base round and strong, then dried in the shade and sun.

2. Why Kiln Baking is Essential:
Unbaked sun-dried clay pots dissolve back into mud as soon as water is poured into them. Baking pots inside a high-temperature brick kiln (bhatti) causes irreversible chemical fusion of silica particles, turning the clay into hard, permanent, porous, and waterproof terracotta.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Sequential four-step pottery making process 2.5 M
Scientific reason for kiln baking and terracotta transformation 1.5 M
Q2 Textile Crafts • Handloom Weaving
What is the difference between 'warp' and 'weft' in weaving cloth on a loom?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Warp vs. Weft:
• Warp (तानी): The set of long, parallel threads stretched lengthwise tightly on the wooden loom frame from front to back.
• Weft (भरनी): The crosswise thread wound on a bobbin inside a flying shuttle that is passed back and forth horizontally through the separated warp threads.
The tight interlocking of warp and weft threads produces the continuous fabric of a saree, shawl, or cloth sheet.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear, correct definition of lengthwise warp threads 1.5 M
Clear, correct definition of crosswise weft threads and shuttle 1.5 M
Q3 Woodwork & Carpentry • Tools and Skills
Name three essential tools used by a carpenter and state the specific purpose of each tool.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Essential Carpentry Tools:
1. Hand Saw (Aari): A steel blade with sharp teeth used to cut timber and planks across or along the grain.
2. Chisel (Rookhani / Chheeni): A beveled steel blade used with a wooden mallet to carve mortises, grooves, and artistic designs into wood.
3. Hand Plane (Randa): A wooden or metal tool with an adjustable blade used to shave off rough bumps and make wooden planks completely flat and smooth.

Q4 Cultural Heritage • Supporting Artisans
Why are traditional handmade crafts losing ground to factory-made goods? How can we support local artisans?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Reasons for Decline:
Machine-made factory goods are produced rapidly in huge volumes at lower costs using plastic and synthetic fibers, making it hard for handloom weavers and potters who spend days on a single piece to compete in price.

2. Ways to Support Artisans:
• Buy authentic handicraft products (like khadi, terracotta tableware, and wooden toys) directly at artisan fairs (Saras Mela / Dilli Haat).
• Value the artistic uniqueness of handmade goods and avoid excessive bargaining with poor craftspeople.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Economic reasons for factory competition 1.0 M
Practical consumer choices to support indigenous craftsmen 1.0 M
Q5 Traditional Crafts • Pottery Making
Describe the four main steps involved in making an earthen pot on a potter's wheel.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Steps in Pottery:
1. Clay Preparation: Kneading clay with water and sand to remove air bubbles and make it pliable.
2. Spinning & Shaping: Placing clay on the rotating potter's wheel and shaping with wet thumbs into pots.
3. Sun-Drying: Leaving raw pots in the shade to dry slowly without cracking.
4. Firing in Kiln (Bhatti): Baking pots at high heat in a furnace to make them hard, waterproof, and durable.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Four sequential steps of pottery explained 4 M
Q6 Textile Crafts • Handloom Weaving
What is weaving? What is the difference between 'warp' and 'weft' threads on a handloom?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Weaving & Threads:
• Weaving: The process of interlacing two sets of yarn at right angles to create fabric.
• Warp: Long vertical threads stretched taut lengthwise along the loom frame.
• Weft: Horizontal threads carried back and forth across the warp using a sliding shuttle.

Q7 Paper Recycling • Eco-Craft
How can used old newspapers and notebook pages be recycled into handmade paper at school?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Handmade Paper Recycling:
1. Tear waste paper into small shreds and soak in water overnight.
2. Mash into a smooth wet pulp using a mortar or blender.
3. Spread the pulp evenly over a wire mesh screen frame.
4. Press out excess water with a sponge, gently lift the wet paper sheet, and let it dry under the sun.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Four steps of paper recycling explained 3 M

Q9 Metal Crafts • Blacksmith & Coppersmith
How does a blacksmith shape hard iron into farming tools like sickles, axes, and ploughshares?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Blacksmithing:
The blacksmith heats the iron bar in a red-hot charcoal furnace until it glows soft and malleable. Then, holding it with tongs, he beats it forcefully on a heavy iron anvil with a hammer to forge the desired sharp cutting shape.

Q13 Glass Bangle Making • Firozabad
Which city in India is world-famous for making colourful glass bangles? What raw material is melted?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Glass Bangles of Firozabad:
Firozabad in Uttar Pradesh is famous for bangle making. High-temperature furnaces melt silica sand, soda ash, and coloring chemicals, which artisans blow and spiral onto rotating metal rods.

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Revitalizing Eco-Friendly Terracotta Over Single-Use Plastics

Tea stalls outside railway stations discarded plastic cups that littered tracks and choked city drains.

Analytical Task: Evaluate why the Indian Railways' decision to serve hot tea in unglazed clay 'kulhads' is an ecological and economic masterstroke.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Kulhads are 100% biodegradable and break down back into natural soil without releasing toxic microplastics. Clay is naturally porous and enhances the earthy aroma of tea while keeping it insulated. Economically, mass procurement of kulhads directly pumps millions of rupees into rural potter families and preserves indigenous pottery skills across Indian villages.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for environmental biodegradability and zero plastic waste; 1.5 Marks for rural livelihood generation and craft preservation.
