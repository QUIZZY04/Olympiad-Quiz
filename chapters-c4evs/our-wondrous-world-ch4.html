Chapter 4: Growing up with Nature

Plant Germination, Animal Life Cycles, Pet Care & Behavioral Adaptations | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Conditions for seed germination: Water, oxygen/air, suitable temperature, and viable cotyledons.
  • The developmental stages of a plant: Seed coat rupture, radicle (root) emergence, plumule (shoot) growth, and true leaves.
  • Life cycles in animals: Complete metamorphosis in butterflies (egg, larva, pupa, adult) vs. direct development in mammals.
  • Compassionate pet care, responsible feeding, hygienic housing, and recognizing distress signals in animals.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (14 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Scientific Inquiry • Seed Germination
What conditions are necessary for a bean seed to germinate? Explain the role of the radicle and plumule.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Necessary Conditions for Germination:
• Water (Moisture): Softens the tough seed coat and activates dormant growth enzymes.
• Air (Oxygen): Needed for cellular respiration to generate energy for cell division.
• Warmth (Suitable Temperature): Optimum warmth allows biochemical reactions to proceed smoothly. (Light is generally not required until leaves appear).

2. Role of Radicle and Plumule:
• Radicle: The embryonic root that grows downward into the soil (positive geotropism) to anchor the seedling and absorb water and dissolved minerals.
• Plumule: The embryonic shoot that grows upward toward sunlight (positive phototropism) to develop stems, leaves, and flowers.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Three vital environmental factors for germination 1.5 M
Correct biological functions of radicle and plumule 1.5 M
Q2 Life Cycles • Metamorphosis
Describe the four stages in the life cycle of a butterfly. Why does the caterpillar eat voraciously?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Four Stages of Butterfly Life Cycle (Metamorphosis):
1. Egg: Laid by the adult female butterfly on the underside of a host plant's leaf.
2. Larva (Caterpillar): Hatches from the egg; worm-like, with chewing mouthparts.
3. Pupa (Chrysalis): The caterpillar spins a protective cocoon or shell around itself; inside, dramatic organ reorganization takes place.
4. Adult Butterfly: Emerges with wings and siphoning mouthparts to feed on flower nectar and reproduce.

2. Why Caterpillars Eat Voraciously:
The caterpillar needs to store immense energy and nutrients because during the pupa stage, it cannot eat at all. All the energy required to grow wings, legs, and antennae comes from the food consumed during the larval stage.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate description of all four stages 2.5 M
Biological reasoning for the caterpillar's intense feeding 1.5 M
Q3 Animal Welfare • Responsible Pet Care
What responsibilities must a family take when adopting a domestic pet (like a dog or cat)?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Responsibilities of Pet Ownership:
1. Nutritious Food & Clean Water: Providing species-appropriate, balanced meals at fixed intervals and 24/7 access to fresh drinking water.
2. Veterinary Health: Timely vaccinations (especially anti-rabies), regular deworming, and prompt medical check-ups when sick.
3. Hygienic Living Space: A clean, dry, well-ventilated shelter with protection from extreme cold or summer heat.
4. Exercise & Affection: Daily walking, playtime, and gentle, loving interaction without teasing or physical cruelty.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Four comprehensive dimensions of pet care (food, health, shelter, affection) 3.0 M
Q4 Comparative Biology • Plant vs. Animal Growth
How does the growth of a mango tree differ from the growth of a puppy? State two key differences.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Differences in Growth:
1. Locational vs. Proportionate Growth: A plant grows only at specific growing tips (meristems at root tips and stem tips), whereas a puppy grows uniformly across all body parts simultaneously.
2. Duration of Growth: A tree can continue to grow new branches, leaves, and rings of wood throughout its life (indeterminate growth), while a puppy stops growing in height and length once it reaches adult maturity (determinate growth).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Two accurate, scientifically valid differences in growth patterns 2.0 M
Q5 Life Cycle • Butterfly Metamorphosis
Describe the four distinct stages in the life cycle of a butterfly. What is metamorphosis?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Stages of Butterfly Life Cycle:
1. Egg: Laid on the underside of green leaves.
2. Larva (Caterpillar): Hatches and eats leaves hungrily, growing rapidly.
3. Pupa (Chrysalis): Caterpillar spins a protective cocoon around itself and rests while undergoing internal transformation.
4. Adult Butterfly: Emerges with colourful wings.
• Metamorphosis: The dramatic transformation of an immature form into an adult in distinct stages.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Four stages correctly sequenced and described 3 M
Scientific definition of metamorphosis 1 M
Q6 Life Cycle • Frog Development
How does a tadpole differ from an adult frog in where it lives and how it breathes?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Tadpole vs Adult Frog:
• Tadpole: Lives entirely in water, has a swimming tail, no legs, and breathes dissolved oxygen through gills.
• Adult Frog: Has strong jumping legs, no tail, and breathes through lungs on land and moist skin underwater.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Tadpole features (tail, gills, water) 1.5 M
Adult frog features (legs, lungs/skin, amphibian) 1.5 M

Q8 Summer Empathy • Bird Baths
Why is it important to set up shallow earthen bowls of fresh water for birds on our balconies during hot summer months?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Bird Baths:
In peak summer, natural puddles and ponds dry up. Birds suffer from dehydration and heat stress. Keeping shallow earthen bowls filled with clean water gives them drinking water and helps them splash to cool their feathers.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Dehydration prevention and heat relief explained 2 M
Q9 Social Insects • Ant Colonies
What can human beings learn from the social organization and discipline of ants?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Lessons from Ants:
1. Teamwork: Ants work together harmoniously without fighting.
2. Hard Work & Discipline: They march in straight lines, sharing food and carrying loads much heavier than their own weight.
3. Planning Ahead: They store grain in underground tunnels before winter or rainy seasons.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Three clear character lessons from ants 3 M
Q10 Animal Parenting • Protective Instincts
How do mother birds protect their fragile eggs and feed their newly hatched chicks?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Bird Parenting:
Mother birds build nests in hidden leafy heights, sit on eggs to keep them warm (incubation), and defend them aggressively against predators. Once chicks hatch, parents fly repeatedly all day to bring soft worms and mash food into their open beaks.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Incubation and nest defense 1 M
Feeding helpless chicks described 1 M
Q11 Camouflage • Survival Mechanism
What is camouflage? How does it help a chameleon or green grasshopper survive in the wild?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Camouflage:
Camouflage is an animal's ability to blend its body colour and texture with surrounding leaves, bark, or sand. A green grasshopper hides from hungry birds by matching grass, and a chameleon shifts colour to surprise prey and avoid predators.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Definition of camouflage 1 M
Grasshopper/chameleon survival role 1 M
Q12 Aquatic Life • Fish Adaptations
Mention two physical adaptations that help fish swim and breathe easily underwater.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Fish Adaptations:
1. Streamlined Body & Fins: A pointed torpedo shape reduces water resistance, and fins provide propulsion and steering.
2. Gills: Feather-like gills absorb dissolved oxygen from water directly into bloodstream.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Streamlined shape/fins explained 1 M
Gills breathing mechanism explained 1 M
Q13 Coexistence • Animal Welfare
Why should we avoid bursting noisy firecrackers near dogs, cats, and birds during festivals?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Protecting Animals from Noise:
Animals have far more sensitive hearing than humans. Loud explosive firecrackers terrify pets and street animals, disorient roosting birds, cause panic injuries, and fill the air with suffocating toxic fumes.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Sensitive hearing and distress explained 2 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Saving Dehydrated Migratory Birds in Peak Summer

During blistering summer heatwaves, small birds often collapse on residential balconies from severe dehydration.

Analytical Task: Design a simple, hygienic bird watering and feeding station that Class 4 students can install.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Students can place shallow earthen bowls (sakoras) filled with clean, cool water in shaded balcony spots or tree branches, changing the water twice daily to prevent mosquito breeding. Adding grain trays with millets and sunflower seeds provides birds with quick energy without exposing them to predatory stray cats.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for earthen bowl setup and daily hygienic water change; 1.5 Marks for shade placement and predator protection.
