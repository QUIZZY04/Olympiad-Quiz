Chapter 16: A Busy Month

Gijubhai Badheka's Letter: Bird Nests, Beak Adaptations, Animal Teeth & Avian Parental Care | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Letter written in April 1936 by pioneering educator Gijubhai Badheka from Bal Mandir, Bhavnagar, Gujarat.
  • Diverse bird nests and architecture:
  • 1. Indian Robin: Lays eggs among roadside stones; nest lined with grass, soft wool, roots, and hair.
  • 2. Crow: Builds messy nests high on trees using twigs and even iron wire.
  • 3. Koel (Cuckoo): Does NOT build a nest; lays eggs slyly in crow's nest; crow hatches them.
  • 4. Tailor Bird: Stitches two broad leaves together with sharp beak; lays eggs in the leafy pouch.
  • 5. Weaver Bird (Baya): Male weaves beautiful hanging bottleneck nests; female inspects all and chooses the best.
  • Bird beaks and animal teeth adaptations (cow, cat, snake, squirrel teeth that never stop growing).

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (16 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Ornithology • Bird Behavior
How does the Weaver Bird (Baya) choose its mate? Why doesn't the Koel build its own nest? Why do squirrels gnaw continuously on hard objects?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Weaver Bird Mate Selection:
The male Weaver Bird weaves intricate, beautiful, hanging woven bottle-shaped nests from tall grass fibers. The female bird inspects every single nest in the tree colony and chooses the one that is strongest, best ventilated, and most secure to lay her eggs.

2. Koel's Nest Parasitism:
The Koel (cuckoo) does not build its own nest. It slyly lays its eggs in an unsuspecting crow's nest. Because the eggs look very similar, the crow faithfully incubates and hatches the Koel's chicks alongside its own.

3. Why Squirrels Gnaw Continuously:
A squirrel's front teeth (incisors) grow continuously throughout its entire life. If a squirrel does not constantly gnaw on hard nuts and wood to grind them down, its teeth will grow so long that it won't be able to close its mouth or eat, leading to death.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Weaver bird male nest building and female inspection 1.5 M
Koel laying eggs in crow's nest (brood parasitism) 1.0 M
Squirrel incisors growing continuously requiring grinding 1.5 M
Q2 Ornithology • Bird Nests
Describe how the following birds construct their nests: (a) Indian Robin, (b) Tailorbird, (c) Weaver Bird.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Nest Construction Styles:
• (a) Indian Robin: Builds soft cup nests in low stone crevices or bush forks using dry grass, roots, animal hair, wool, and soft cotton swabs.
• (b) Tailorbird (दर्जिन चिड़िया): Uses its needle-sharp beak like a sewing needle to pierce and stitch two broad green leaves together with plant fibers, creating a cozy pocket nest.
• (c) Male Weaver Bird (बया): Intricately weaves a beautiful pendant-shaped pouch nest with a hanging entrance tunnel using grass strips. The female inspects all nests and chooses the best one to lay eggs.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate architectural style described for all three birds 3.0 M
Q3 Functional Morphology • Bird Beaks & Claws
How are the beaks of an Eagle and a Sunbird adapted to their specific food habits?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Beak Adaptations:
• Eagle: Has a short, heavy, hooked, razor-sharp beak designed to tear tough meat and flesh of hunted prey (rats, snakes, fish).
• Sunbird: Has a long, slender, curved, straw-like beak perfectly designed to probe deep inside tubular flowers to suck sweet nectar without damaging blossoms.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Hooked meat-tearing beak of eagle vs slender nectar-sucking beak of sunbird 2.0 M
Q4 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'A Busy Month', describe how students can conduct an observational study in their school or residential community. What three parameters should they record in their field notebook?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Field Observation Protocol:
1. Direct Inquiry: Students observe the environment, community infrastructure, or natural ecosystem related to A Busy Month during a guided campus walk.
2. Three Field Log Parameters:
• (a) Physical features, adaptations, or materials observed in nature/daily life.
• (b) Ecological dependencies (availability of clean water, sunlight, soil quality, or habitat safety).
• (c) Human actions observed (cleanliness practices, waste disposal, or conservation efforts).
3. Conclusion: Maintaining a nature journal fosters environmental sensitivity and scientific curiosity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Observation methodology clearly outlined 1.5 M
Three tangible field parameters detailed 1.5 M
Q5 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles in 'A Busy Month', classify living organisms or natural resources into distinct categories and state the scientific rationale behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Renewable / Biodegradable Resources): Elements that naturally replenish and decompose safely without releasing toxins into the soil or waterways.
• Category 2 (Non-Renewable / Non-Biodegradable Elements): Materials that persist in the environment for decades, requiring active recycling and mindful consumption.
• Scientific Rationale: Differentiating between biodegradable and non-biodegradable items empowers students to adopt zero-waste and eco-friendly lifestyles.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q6 Community Living & Cultural Diversity • Social Harmony
How does 'A Busy Month' illustrate the importance of community helpers, traditional skills, or regional diversity across India? Give two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Helpers & Regional Diversity:
1. Traditional Craftsmanship & Occupations: Across Indian states, local artisans, farmers, weavers, and sanitation workers contribute essential services that sustain healthy communities.
2. Mutual Interdependence: Every profession deserves dignity and respect. When people of diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds cooperate, villages and cities flourish in peace.
3. Examples: From weavers crafting heritage textiles to transport workers navigating rugged terrains, every occupation shapes our shared national heritage.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of community interdependence 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting dignity of labor 1.5 M
Q7 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 4 student can implement at home and school inspired by the teachings of 'A Busy Month'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Conservation: Turn off taps while brushing teeth and reuse rinse-water from washed vegetables to water garden pots.
2. Waste Segregation: Separate organic wet kitchen peelings into compost bins while sending clean paper, cardboard, and plastics for recycling.
3. Protecting Green Cover: Plant a native sapling on birthdays and set up a bird bath on balconies during warm summer months.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Water saving technique explained 1.0 M
Waste segregation practice detailed 1.0 M
Flora/fauna protection action listed 1.0 M
Q8 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze a real-world scenario from 'A Busy Month': If a local natural waterbody or forest area is polluted by garbage, what steps should the local community take to restore it?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Restoration Action Plan:
1. Immediate Cleanup Drive: Organize student and resident volunteer drives equipped with gloves to safely collect floating plastics and litter.
2. Preventive Regulations: Install prominent awareness signboards prohibiting dumping, accompanied by secure covered dustbins.
3. Biological Rejuvenation: Plant native bank-stabilizing trees and reeds along water edges to prevent soil erosion and encourage aquatic life.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate cleanup measures proposed 1.5 M
Long-term preventive community measures 1.5 M
Q9 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate empathy towards stray animals, birds, and elderly citizens in their daily lives, as highlighted in 'A Busy Month'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Everyday Empathy in Practice:
1. Care for Living Creatures: Never throw stones or tease stray animals; keep a bowl of clean drinking water in shady spots.
2. Respect for Elders: Offer seats in public transport, speak politely, listen patiently to grandparents' stories, and assist them in carrying light groceries.
3. Core Principle: Compassion for all living beings builds a humane, peaceful, and responsible society.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Kindness towards animals and birds 1.5 M
Respectful support towards senior citizens 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Bird Beak Diversity and Feeding Habits

Compare a woodpecker's long chisel-like beak with a sparrow's short conical beak and an eagle's curved hooked beak.

Analytical Task: How does each beak design match the bird's specific food source?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Woodpecker: Strong chisel-like beak hammers into tree bark to pull out hidden woodboring insects.
2. Sparrow: Short, thick, conical beak exerts crushing force to crack hard grain shells and seeds.
3. Eagle / Hawk: Sharp, curved hooked beak tears raw meat of prey animals.
Rubric: 1.0 Mark for Woodpecker insect drilling; 1.0 Mark for Sparrow seed crushing; 1.0 Mark for Eagle meat tearing.
