Chapter 16: A Busy Month

Gijubhai Badheka's Letter: Bird Nests, Beak Adaptations, Animal Teeth & Avian Parental Care | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Letter written in April 1936 by pioneering educator Gijubhai Badheka from Bal Mandir, Bhavnagar, Gujarat.
  • Diverse bird nests and architecture:
  • 1. Indian Robin: Lays eggs among roadside stones; nest lined with grass, soft wool, roots, and hair.
  • 2. Crow: Builds messy nests high on trees using twigs and even iron wire.
  • 3. Koel (Cuckoo): Does NOT build a nest; lays eggs slyly in crow's nest; crow hatches them.
  • 4. Tailor Bird: Stitches two broad leaves together with sharp beak; lays eggs in the leafy pouch.
  • 5. Weaver Bird (Baya): Male weaves beautiful hanging bottleneck nests; female inspects all and chooses the best.
  • Bird beaks and animal teeth adaptations (cow, cat, snake, squirrel teeth that never stop growing).

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (13 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Ornithology • Bird Behavior
How does the Weaver Bird (Baya) choose its mate? Why doesn't the Koel build its own nest? Why do squirrels gnaw continuously on hard objects?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Weaver Bird Mate Selection:
The male Weaver Bird weaves intricate, beautiful, hanging woven bottle-shaped nests from tall grass fibers. The female bird inspects every single nest in the tree colony and chooses the one that is strongest, best ventilated, and most secure to lay her eggs.

2. Koel's Nest Parasitism:
The Koel (cuckoo) does not build its own nest. It slyly lays its eggs in an unsuspecting crow's nest. Because the eggs look very similar, the crow faithfully incubates and hatches the Koel's chicks alongside its own.

3. Why Squirrels Gnaw Continuously:
A squirrel's front teeth (incisors) grow continuously throughout its entire life. If a squirrel does not constantly gnaw on hard nuts and wood to grind them down, its teeth will grow so long that it won't be able to close its mouth or eat, leading to death.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Weaver bird male nest building and female inspection 1.5 M
Koel laying eggs in crow's nest (brood parasitism) 1.0 M
Squirrel incisors growing continuously requiring grinding 1.5 M
Q2 Ornithology • Bird Nests
Describe how the following birds construct their nests: (a) Indian Robin, (b) Tailorbird, (c) Weaver Bird.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Nest Construction Styles:
• (a) Indian Robin: Builds soft cup nests in low stone crevices or bush forks using dry grass, roots, animal hair, wool, and soft cotton swabs.
• (b) Tailorbird (दर्जिन चिड़िया): Uses its needle-sharp beak like a sewing needle to pierce and stitch two broad green leaves together with plant fibers, creating a cozy pocket nest.
• (c) Male Weaver Bird (बया): Intricately weaves a beautiful pendant-shaped pouch nest with a hanging entrance tunnel using grass strips. The female inspects all nests and chooses the best one to lay eggs.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate architectural style described for all three birds 3.0 M
Q3 Functional Morphology • Bird Beaks & Claws
How are the beaks of an Eagle and a Sunbird adapted to their specific food habits?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Beak Adaptations:
• Eagle: Has a short, heavy, hooked, razor-sharp beak designed to tear tough meat and flesh of hunted prey (rats, snakes, fish).
• Sunbird: Has a long, slender, curved, straw-like beak perfectly designed to probe deep inside tubular flowers to suck sweet nectar without damaging blossoms.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Hooked meat-tearing beak of eagle vs slender nectar-sucking beak of sunbird 2.0 M
Q4 Textbook Exercise • Core Concept Analysis
In Chapter 16 ('A Busy Month'), explain the central environmental and social theme highlighted through the story of A Busy Month.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Core Theme & Learning:
1. Context: The chapter examines Gijubhai's Birds, Nests, Beaks & Teeth, showing how human communities interact with nature, resources, and civic structures.
2. Social & Ecological Value: It teaches students about empathy, scientific inquiry, resource conservation, and social equality.
3. Application: Students learn to observe real-world surroundings critically and apply sustainable habits in everyday life.

Social/ecological values articulated 1 M
Real-world civic application 1 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Bird Beak Diversity and Feeding Habits

Compare a woodpecker's long chisel-like beak with a sparrow's short conical beak and an eagle's curved hooked beak.

Analytical Task: How does each beak design match the bird's specific food source?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Woodpecker: Strong chisel-like beak hammers into tree bark to pull out hidden woodboring insects.
2. Sparrow: Short, thick, conical beak exerts crushing force to crack hard grain shells and seeds.
3. Eagle / Hawk: Sharp, curved hooked beak tears raw meat of prey animals.
Rubric: 1.0 Mark for Woodpecker insect drilling; 1.0 Mark for Sparrow seed crushing; 1.0 Mark for Eagle meat tearing.
