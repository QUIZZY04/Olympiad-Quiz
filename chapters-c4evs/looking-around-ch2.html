Chapter 2: Ear to Ear

Animal Ears, Hearing Mechanisms, Skin Patterns & Mammals vs. Egg-Laying Animals | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Animals whose ears can be seen outside: Elephants (fan-like), rabbits (leaf-like on top of head), deer, dogs, cows.
  • Animals whose ears are hidden tiny holes covered with feathers: Birds (sparrows, crows, ducks), lizards, and crocodiles.
  • The Golden Rule of Animal Reproduction: Animals with visible external ears and hair on their body give birth to live babies (Mammals / Viviparous).
  • Animals without external ears and without hair (scales/feathers) lay eggs (Oviparous).
  • Skin patterns: Zebra stripes, tiger stripes, leopard rosettes, deer spots.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (16 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Biological Classification • Animal Science
What is the biological rule that tells us whether an animal gives birth to babies or lays eggs just by observing its ears and skin? Give two examples of each group.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The Golden Rule:
1. Animals that give birth to live babies (Viviparous / Mammals):
• Those animals whose ears can be seen outside on their heads AND who have hair or fur on their skin give birth to young babies.
• Examples: Cow, Dog, Elephant, Cat, Human beings.

2. Animals that lay eggs (Oviparous):
• Those animals whose ears cannot be seen from outside (they have tiny hidden ear holes covered by feathers or scales) AND who do not have hair on their skin lay eggs.
• Examples: Sparrow, Pigeon, Snake, Lizard, Crocodile, Frog.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Detailed rule for live-bearing animals (visible ears + hair) 1.5 M
Detailed rule for egg-laying animals (hidden ear holes + no hair) 1.5 M
Two accurate examples for each group 1.0 M
Q2 Animal Morphology • Ears and Camouflage
Name two animals whose ears look like fans and two whose ears are on top of their head. Why do animals have different patterns on their skin?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Ear Shapes:
• Fan-like Ears: Elephant (large fleshy flaps that flap to cool the body).
• Leaf-like Ears: Deer, Cow, Buffalo.
• Ears on Top of Head: Rabbit, Hare, Giraffe.

2. Skin Patterns:
The patterns (stripes on tigers and zebras, spots on leopards and deer) are due to the colors of the hair growing on their skin. These patterns provide camouflage, helping animals blend into shadows and grasses to hide from predators or sneak up on prey.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate animal examples for fan-like and top-of-head ears 1.5 M
Explanation of hair pigmentation and camouflage 1.5 M
Q3 Sensory Perception • Snakes and Birds
How do snakes detect approaching humans or prey if they do not have external ears?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Snakes' Hearing Mechanism:
Snakes do not have external ears, ear openings, or eardrums. Instead, they feel sound vibrations traveling through the ground. Their lower jawbone rests close to the earth and acts as a sensitive acoustic sensor, transmitting ground tremors directly to their inner ear bones.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Scientific explanation of ground vibration detection via jawbone 2.0 M
Q4 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'Ear to Ear', describe how students can conduct an observational study in their school or residential community. What three parameters should they record in their field notebook?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Field Observation Protocol:
1. Direct Inquiry: Students observe the environment, community infrastructure, or natural ecosystem related to Ear to Ear during a guided campus walk.
2. Three Field Log Parameters:
• (a) Physical features, adaptations, or materials observed in nature/daily life.
• (b) Ecological dependencies (availability of clean water, sunlight, soil quality, or habitat safety).
• (c) Human actions observed (cleanliness practices, waste disposal, or conservation efforts).
3. Conclusion: Maintaining a nature journal fosters environmental sensitivity and scientific curiosity.

Observation methodology clearly outlined 1.5 M
Three tangible field parameters detailed 1.5 M
Q5 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles in 'Ear to Ear', classify living organisms or natural resources into distinct categories and state the scientific rationale behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Renewable / Biodegradable Resources): Elements that naturally replenish and decompose safely without releasing toxins into the soil or waterways.
• Category 2 (Non-Renewable / Non-Biodegradable Elements): Materials that persist in the environment for decades, requiring active recycling and mindful consumption.
• Scientific Rationale: Differentiating between biodegradable and non-biodegradable items empowers students to adopt zero-waste and eco-friendly lifestyles.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q6 Community Living & Cultural Diversity • Social Harmony
How does 'Ear to Ear' illustrate the importance of community helpers, traditional skills, or regional diversity across India? Give two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Helpers & Regional Diversity:
1. Traditional Craftsmanship & Occupations: Across Indian states, local artisans, farmers, weavers, and sanitation workers contribute essential services that sustain healthy communities.
2. Mutual Interdependence: Every profession deserves dignity and respect. When people of diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds cooperate, villages and cities flourish in peace.
3. Examples: From weavers crafting heritage textiles to transport workers navigating rugged terrains, every occupation shapes our shared national heritage.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of community interdependence 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting dignity of labor 1.5 M
Q7 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 4 student can implement at home and school inspired by the teachings of 'Ear to Ear'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Conservation: Turn off taps while brushing teeth and reuse rinse-water from washed vegetables to water garden pots.
2. Waste Segregation: Separate organic wet kitchen peelings into compost bins while sending clean paper, cardboard, and plastics for recycling.
3. Protecting Green Cover: Plant a native sapling on birthdays and set up a bird bath on balconies during warm summer months.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Water saving technique explained 1.0 M
Waste segregation practice detailed 1.0 M
Flora/fauna protection action listed 1.0 M
Q8 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze a real-world scenario from 'Ear to Ear': If a local natural waterbody or forest area is polluted by garbage, what steps should the local community take to restore it?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Restoration Action Plan:
1. Immediate Cleanup Drive: Organize student and resident volunteer drives equipped with gloves to safely collect floating plastics and litter.
2. Preventive Regulations: Install prominent awareness signboards prohibiting dumping, accompanied by secure covered dustbins.
3. Biological Rejuvenation: Plant native bank-stabilizing trees and reeds along water edges to prevent soil erosion and encourage aquatic life.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate cleanup measures proposed 1.5 M
Long-term preventive community measures 1.5 M
Q9 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate empathy towards stray animals, birds, and elderly citizens in their daily lives, as highlighted in 'Ear to Ear'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Everyday Empathy in Practice:
1. Care for Living Creatures: Never throw stones or tease stray animals; keep a bowl of clean drinking water in shady spots.
2. Respect for Elders: Offer seats in public transport, speak politely, listen patiently to grandparents' stories, and assist them in carrying light groceries.
3. Core Principle: Compassion for all living beings builds a humane, peaceful, and responsible society.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Kindness towards animals and birds 1.5 M
Respectful support towards senior citizens 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Crocodile and Lizard Hearing Mechanism

A student observes a garden lizard on a wall and wonders if lizards are deaf because she cannot see any external ear flaps.

Analytical Task: Are lizards and crocodiles deaf? Explain how they hear sounds.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
No, lizards and crocodiles are not deaf; they have keen hearing. Instead of external fleshy ear flaps (pinnae), they have tiny, sensitive ear holes on either side of their head behind their eyes. These ear canals are protected by thin scales or membrane folds that capture acoustic vibrations accurately.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for stating they are not deaf; 1.5 Marks for explaining protected tiny ear holes.
