Chapter 3: A Day with Nandu

Baby Elephant Nandu, Elephant Herd Dynamics, Matriarchal Leadership & Wildlife Facts | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Nandu: A three-month-old baby elephant weighing 200 kilograms.
  • An adult elephant can eat more than 100 kilograms of green leaves and twigs in a single day.
  • Elephants sleep for only 2 to 4 hours in a day; mud and water keep their thick skin cool; large fan-like ears flap to release body heat.
  • Herd structure: An elephant herd has only female elephants and their young calves. The oldest female is the Matriarch (leader) of the herd.
  • Male elephants stay in the herd only until they are 14 to 15 years old, then leave to wander alone.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (16 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Wildlife Biology • Elephant Herd Structure
Who is the leader of an elephant herd? What role do male elephants play in the herd, and why do elephants love playing in mud and water?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Leader of the Herd:
The oldest female elephant (the Matriarch / Nani ma) is the supreme leader of the herd. She decides when to move, finds waterholes during droughts, and directs the herd's defense.

2. Role of Male Elephants:
Male elephant calves live in the herd only until they are 14 to 15 years old. Once they reach maturity, they leave the herd to wander and forage independently alone in the forest.

3. Why Elephants Love Mud and Water:
Elephants do not have sweat glands across their thick skin. Rolling in cool mud coats their skin with a protective layer that shields them from scorching tropical sun and insect bites. Splashing water and flapping their giant ears acts like a natural air conditioner, cooling the blood vessels running through the ear flaps.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identifies oldest female (matriarch) as leader 1.0 M
Explains male calves leaving at age 14-15 1.5 M
Scientific cooling function of mud coating and ear flapping 1.5 M
Q2 Wildlife Ecology • Elephant Herd Behavior
Who is the leader of an elephant herd? Why do male elephants leave the herd when they grow up?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Leader of the Herd:
The oldest female elephant is the Matriarch (leader) of the herd. She decides when to move, where to find waterholes, and protects the young calves.

2. Why Male Elephants Leave:
An elephant herd consists exclusively of female elephants and their babies. Young male calves live in the herd only until they reach 14 to 15 years of age. Once they mature, they leave the maternal herd to live independently or form small bachelor groups.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Matriarch role and duties explained 1.5 M
Male elephants leaving at age 14-15 explained 1.5 M
Q3 Animal Welfare • Freedom vs. Captivity
How does the life of an elephant in a wild forest differ from that of an elephant tied in chains in a circus?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Wild vs. Captive Elephant:
• In the Wild: Elephants roam freely across vast jungle corridors, bathe in natural rivers, feed on fresh bamboo and grasses, play socially with herd members, and express natural maternal instincts.
• In Captivity: Captive elephants are often tied with heavy metal chains on concrete floors, kept in solitary isolation, beaten with bullhooks (ankush) to perform unnatural circus tricks, and deprived of natural foraging and social herds.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Three natural conditions in the wild described 1.5 M
Cruelty and confinement in captivity contrasted 1.5 M
Q4 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'A Day with Nandu', describe how students can conduct an observational study in their school or residential community. What three parameters should they record in their field notebook?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Field Observation Protocol:
1. Direct Inquiry: Students observe the environment, community infrastructure, or natural ecosystem related to A Day with Nandu during a guided campus walk.
2. Three Field Log Parameters:
• (a) Physical features, adaptations, or materials observed in nature/daily life.
• (b) Ecological dependencies (availability of clean water, sunlight, soil quality, or habitat safety).
• (c) Human actions observed (cleanliness practices, waste disposal, or conservation efforts).
3. Conclusion: Maintaining a nature journal fosters environmental sensitivity and scientific curiosity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Observation methodology clearly outlined 1.5 M
Three tangible field parameters detailed 1.5 M
Q5 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles in 'A Day with Nandu', classify living organisms or natural resources into distinct categories and state the scientific rationale behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Renewable / Biodegradable Resources): Elements that naturally replenish and decompose safely without releasing toxins into the soil or waterways.
• Category 2 (Non-Renewable / Non-Biodegradable Elements): Materials that persist in the environment for decades, requiring active recycling and mindful consumption.
• Scientific Rationale: Differentiating between biodegradable and non-biodegradable items empowers students to adopt zero-waste and eco-friendly lifestyles.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q6 Community Living & Cultural Diversity • Social Harmony
How does 'A Day with Nandu' illustrate the importance of community helpers, traditional skills, or regional diversity across India? Give two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Helpers & Regional Diversity:
1. Traditional Craftsmanship & Occupations: Across Indian states, local artisans, farmers, weavers, and sanitation workers contribute essential services that sustain healthy communities.
2. Mutual Interdependence: Every profession deserves dignity and respect. When people of diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds cooperate, villages and cities flourish in peace.
3. Examples: From weavers crafting heritage textiles to transport workers navigating rugged terrains, every occupation shapes our shared national heritage.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of community interdependence 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting dignity of labor 1.5 M
Q7 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 4 student can implement at home and school inspired by the teachings of 'A Day with Nandu'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Conservation: Turn off taps while brushing teeth and reuse rinse-water from washed vegetables to water garden pots.
2. Waste Segregation: Separate organic wet kitchen peelings into compost bins while sending clean paper, cardboard, and plastics for recycling.
3. Protecting Green Cover: Plant a native sapling on birthdays and set up a bird bath on balconies during warm summer months.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Water saving technique explained 1.0 M
Waste segregation practice detailed 1.0 M
Flora/fauna protection action listed 1.0 M
Q8 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze a real-world scenario from 'A Day with Nandu': If a local natural waterbody or forest area is polluted by garbage, what steps should the local community take to restore it?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Restoration Action Plan:
1. Immediate Cleanup Drive: Organize student and resident volunteer drives equipped with gloves to safely collect floating plastics and litter.
2. Preventive Regulations: Install prominent awareness signboards prohibiting dumping, accompanied by secure covered dustbins.
3. Biological Rejuvenation: Plant native bank-stabilizing trees and reeds along water edges to prevent soil erosion and encourage aquatic life.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate cleanup measures proposed 1.5 M
Long-term preventive community measures 1.5 M
Q9 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate empathy towards stray animals, birds, and elderly citizens in their daily lives, as highlighted in 'A Day with Nandu'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Everyday Empathy in Practice:
1. Care for Living Creatures: Never throw stones or tease stray animals; keep a bowl of clean drinking water in shady spots.
2. Respect for Elders: Offer seats in public transport, speak politely, listen patiently to grandparents' stories, and assist them in carrying light groceries.
3. Core Principle: Compassion for all living beings builds a humane, peaceful, and responsible society.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Kindness towards animals and birds 1.5 M
Respectful support towards senior citizens 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Animal Empathy and Captivity Ethics

Circuses and tourist sites keep elephants tied tightly with iron chains on concrete floors, forcing them to carry heavy tourist howdahs.

Analytical Task: Based on 'A Day with Nandu', why is chaining an elephant deeply cruel, and how does it violate their natural social life?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Elephants are highly intelligent, emotional, and social beings who thrive by walking up to 20 km daily in cooperative family herds. Solitary confinement with heavy iron chains causes severe joint arthritis, psychological trauma, and deprives them of essential mud baths and family bonding.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for social herd nature and need for daily movement; 1.5 Marks for physical and mental cruelty of chains.
