Chapter 7: From the Window

Changing Landscapes: Red Soil of Goa, Konkan Railway Bridges, Tunnels & Level Crossings | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • The Konkan Railway journey: Rolling past green paddy fields, coconut groves, and reddish laterite soil of Goa.
  • The sheer engineering feat of the Konkan route: 2,000 bridges and 92 tunnels carved through the Western Ghats (Sahyadri mountains).
  • Level crossings: Understanding safety barriers, waiting for the train to pass, and turning off vehicle engines to save fuel.
  • Crossing the massive bridge over the Zuari/Mandovi river: Hearing the hollow, echoing sound of train wheels on open steel bridge girders.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (16 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Railway Geography & Sound Physics
Why did the sound of the train wheels change dramatically when crossing the long river bridge? How many tunnels and bridges exist on the route from Goa to Kerala?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Why the wheel sound changed:
When running over ordinary ground, sound waves bounce off the solid earthen embankment and ballast stones. But when the train moved onto the steel river bridge, there was no solid ground under the tracks—only open steel girders and deep river water below. The steel metal reverberated, creating a loud, hollow, echoing 'khad-khad-khad' rumbling roar.

2. Engineering statistics:
Along the scenic Western Ghats route from Goa to Kerala, the train passes through 92 mountain tunnels and crosses 2,000 river and ravine bridges.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Hollow echoing sound physics of open steel girders over water 1.5 M
Exact statistics: 92 tunnels and 2,000 bridges 1.5 M
Q2 Railway Engineering • Bridges and Tunnels
Why are there 2000 bridges and 92 tunnels on the Konkan Railway route between Goa and Kerala?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Reasons for Numerous Bridges and Tunnels:
The Konkan Railway traverses the rugged, magnificent Western Ghats mountain range and coastal river systems. The train route must cross numerous deep river estuaries, backwater creeks, and gorges (requiring 2000 bridges) and pierce through solid granite mountain ridges (requiring 92 excavated tunnels) to maintain a smooth, level track without steep gradients.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Geographical topography of Western Ghats and rivers 1.5 M
Engineering necessity of bridges and tunnels explained 1.5 M
Q3 Road Safety • Level Crossings
Why is it dangerous to cross a railway track under a closed level crossing barrier? What precautions must drivers take?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Danger of Crossing Under Closed Gates:
Level crossing gates are closed when a high-speed express train is approaching. Modern trains travel at over 100 km/h and take hundreds of metres to stop even after braking. Crossing under the gate can lead to fatal collisions.

2. Precautions:
Drivers and pedestrians must wait patiently behind the white stop line, switch off vehicle engines to save fuel and reduce noise, and proceed only after the barrier is fully raised.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Speed of trains and fatal collision danger 1.0 M
Turning off engines and waiting behind line 1.0 M
Q4 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'From the Window', describe how students can conduct an observational study in their school or residential community. What three parameters should they record in their field notebook?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Field Observation Protocol:
1. Direct Inquiry: Students observe the environment, community infrastructure, or natural ecosystem related to From the Window during a guided campus walk.
2. Three Field Log Parameters:
• (a) Physical features, adaptations, or materials observed in nature/daily life.
• (b) Ecological dependencies (availability of clean water, sunlight, soil quality, or habitat safety).
• (c) Human actions observed (cleanliness practices, waste disposal, or conservation efforts).
3. Conclusion: Maintaining a nature journal fosters environmental sensitivity and scientific curiosity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Observation methodology clearly outlined 1.5 M
Three tangible field parameters detailed 1.5 M
Q5 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles in 'From the Window', classify living organisms or natural resources into distinct categories and state the scientific rationale behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Renewable / Biodegradable Resources): Elements that naturally replenish and decompose safely without releasing toxins into the soil or waterways.
• Category 2 (Non-Renewable / Non-Biodegradable Elements): Materials that persist in the environment for decades, requiring active recycling and mindful consumption.
• Scientific Rationale: Differentiating between biodegradable and non-biodegradable items empowers students to adopt zero-waste and eco-friendly lifestyles.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q6 Community Living & Cultural Diversity • Social Harmony
How does 'From the Window' illustrate the importance of community helpers, traditional skills, or regional diversity across India? Give two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Helpers & Regional Diversity:
1. Traditional Craftsmanship & Occupations: Across Indian states, local artisans, farmers, weavers, and sanitation workers contribute essential services that sustain healthy communities.
2. Mutual Interdependence: Every profession deserves dignity and respect. When people of diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds cooperate, villages and cities flourish in peace.
3. Examples: From weavers crafting heritage textiles to transport workers navigating rugged terrains, every occupation shapes our shared national heritage.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of community interdependence 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting dignity of labor 1.5 M
Q7 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 4 student can implement at home and school inspired by the teachings of 'From the Window'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Conservation: Turn off taps while brushing teeth and reuse rinse-water from washed vegetables to water garden pots.
2. Waste Segregation: Separate organic wet kitchen peelings into compost bins while sending clean paper, cardboard, and plastics for recycling.
3. Protecting Green Cover: Plant a native sapling on birthdays and set up a bird bath on balconies during warm summer months.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Water saving technique explained 1.0 M
Waste segregation practice detailed 1.0 M
Flora/fauna protection action listed 1.0 M
Q8 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze a real-world scenario from 'From the Window': If a local natural waterbody or forest area is polluted by garbage, what steps should the local community take to restore it?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Restoration Action Plan:
1. Immediate Cleanup Drive: Organize student and resident volunteer drives equipped with gloves to safely collect floating plastics and litter.
2. Preventive Regulations: Install prominent awareness signboards prohibiting dumping, accompanied by secure covered dustbins.
3. Biological Rejuvenation: Plant native bank-stabilizing trees and reeds along water edges to prevent soil erosion and encourage aquatic life.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate cleanup measures proposed 1.5 M
Long-term preventive community measures 1.5 M
Q9 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate empathy towards stray animals, birds, and elderly citizens in their daily lives, as highlighted in 'From the Window'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Everyday Empathy in Practice:
1. Care for Living Creatures: Never throw stones or tease stray animals; keep a bowl of clean drinking water in shady spots.
2. Respect for Elders: Offer seats in public transport, speak politely, listen patiently to grandparents' stories, and assist them in carrying light groceries.
3. Core Principle: Compassion for all living beings builds a humane, peaceful, and responsible society.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Kindness towards animals and birds 1.5 M
Respectful support towards senior citizens 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Fuel Conservation at Railway Level Crossings

At a closed railway crossing gate, dozens of motorists keep their car, bus, and scooter engines idling, filling the air with thick smoke and deafening horn noise.

Analytical Task: Why should motorists switch off vehicle engines while waiting at a closed railway gate?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
A railway gate remains closed for 5 to 10 minutes. Turning off engines saves costly petrol and diesel, cuts carbon emissions, prevents air pollution from choking waiting pedestrians, and eliminates unbearable noise, making the environment cleaner.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for fuel savings and emission reduction; 1.5 Marks for air and noise pollution prevention.
