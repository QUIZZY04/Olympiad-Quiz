Chapter 9: Different Lands, Different Lives

Geographical Diversity of India: Mountains, Deserts, Coastal Plains & River Valleys | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • The major physical divisions of India: Northern Mountains (Himalayas), Great Indian Desert (Thar), Coastal Plains, and Deccan Plateau.
  • Climate adaptations in shelter: Sloping roofs in snow and rain zones; thick mud walls and flat roofs in arid deserts; stilt houses in flood-prone river plains.
  • Traditional livelihoods shaped by local topography: Terrace farming, sheep rearing, coastal fishing, date-palm cultivation, and river transport.
  • Rich cultural unity amidst vast linguistic, dietary, and architectural diversity across India.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (14 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Geographical Adaptation • Architecture
Why do houses in the hilly regions of Himachal Pradesh and Kashmir have steeply sloping roofs, while houses in the Thar Desert of Rajasthan have thick mud walls and flat roofs?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Houses in Hilly Regions (Himachal Pradesh & Kashmir):
These areas experience heavy monsoon rains and freezing winter snowfall. Sloping roofs made of corrugated iron sheets or slate tiles allow rainwater and heavy snow to slide off immediately without accumulating on the roof. This prevents roof collapse, water leakage, and dampness.

2. Houses in the Thar Desert (Rajasthan):
The desert climate experiences scorching daytime temperatures exceeding 45°C. Thick mud walls act as excellent natural insulators, preventing outside heat from penetrating into living rooms during the day. Flat roofs are used to sleep under open starlit skies at night when desert sands cool down, and for drying grains and chillies.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Detailed reasoning for sloping roofs in snow/rain hill zones 2.0 M
Detailed reasoning for thick mud insulation and flat roofs in deserts 2.0 M
Q2 Regional Livelihoods • Terrace Farming
What is 'terrace farming' (step farming)? Why is it practiced predominantly in mountainous regions?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Terrace Farming:
Terrace farming is an agricultural method where steep mountain slopes are cut into a series of broad, flat horizontal step-like terraces supported by stone retaining walls.

2. Why Practiced in Mountains:
• On steep mountain slopes, heavy rainfall rushes down rapidly, washing away the fertile topsoil (soil erosion).
• By creating flat step terraces, the velocity of runoff water is slowed down, allowing water to soak in and retain fertile soil so crops like tea, rice, and maize can grow successfully.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate physical definition of step terraces 1.5 M
Prevention of soil erosion and water retention explained 1.5 M
Q3 Flood Adaptation • Stilt Houses
Why are houses in rural Assam built on tall bamboo stilts 10 to 12 feet above the ground?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Reasons for Stilt Houses in Assam:
1. Severe Monsoon Floods: Assam receives torrential monsoon rainfall causing the mighty Brahmaputra river and its tributaries to flood vast plains every year. Raising houses 10–12 feet on strong bamboo or timber stilts keeps floodwaters from entering living rooms.
2. Protection from Dangerous Animals: Stilts prevent venomous snakes, leeches, scorpions, and wild animals displaced by floodwaters from entering the home.
3. Abundant Bamboo: Bamboo grows abundantly in northeastern forests and is durable, lightweight, resilient, and water-resistant.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Floodwater prevention and elevation reasoning 1.5 M
Safety against reptiles and ecological abundance of bamboo 1.5 M
Q4 Cultural Geography • Unity in Diversity
What does 'Unity in Diversity' mean in the context of India? Give two examples of how diverse cultures celebrate together.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Meaning of Unity in Diversity:
It means that despite having thousands of languages, diverse cuisines, distinct traditional dresses, and varied geographical landscapes, all Indians share a deep common bond of national identity, mutual respect, and shared constitutional brotherhood.

2. Examples of Shared Celebrations:
• Harvest festivals celebrated nationwide under different regional names during the same week of January: Makar Sankranti (North & West), Pongal (Tamil Nadu), Bihu (Assam), and Lohri (Punjab).
• National festivals like Independence Day and Republic Day celebrated with equal patriotism by citizens across all states.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate definition of Unity in Diversity 1.0 M
Harvest and national festivals cited as concrete evidence 1.0 M
Q5 Climate Architecture • Sloping vs Flat Roofs
Why do houses in hilly areas like Himachal Pradesh and Kashmir have steeply sloping roofs, while houses in Rajasthan have flat roofs?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Roof Design Adaptations:
• Hilly Regions (Himachal/Kashmir): Steep sloping roofs allow heavy rainwater and falling snow to slide down quickly without accumulating and damaging the structure.
• Hot Deserts (Rajasthan): Flat roofs are used to sleep under open night skies, dry food grains, and collect scarce rainwater.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Sloping roof snow/rain drainage explained 1.5 M
Flat roof multi-purpose use in dry deserts 1.5 M
Q6 Flood Adaptations • Stilt Houses
Why are houses in Assam built on thick bamboo stilts 10 to 12 feet above the ground?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Stilt Houses in Assam:
Assam experiences torrential monsoon rainfall and seasonal flooding from the Brahmaputra River. Raising houses on strong bamboo or wooden stilts prevents floodwaters from entering living rooms and keeps wild animals and snakes away.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Floodwater prevention explained 1.5 M
Protection from snakes and wild animals 1.5 M
Q7 Desert Living • Mud Insulation
How do thick mud walls and thatched roofs keep houses in rural Rajasthan naturally cool during extreme summer heat?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Thermal Insulation of Mud:
Mud is a poor conductor of heat. Thick mud walls prevent fierce daytime desert heat from penetrating inside. At night, the stored heat radiates slowly, keeping interiors comfortably warm. Thorny bush thatched roofs allow cool ventilation.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Mud as poor heat conductor explained 1.5 M
Daytime cooling and night temperature balance 1.5 M
Q8 Floating Homes • Houseboats of Kashmir
Describe a 'Houseboat' and 'Shikara' found on Dal Lake in Srinagar, Kashmir.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Water Habitats of Kashmir:
• Houseboat: A floating wooden house built of cedar (deodar) wood anchored on the lake, featuring beautiful wood carvings called Khatamband.
• Shikara: A light, flat-bottomed wooden paddle boat used as a water taxi to ferry tourists and transport fresh vegetables to floating markets.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Houseboat description and Khatamband woodwork 1.5 M
Shikara water taxi function 1.5 M
Q9 Traditional Attire • Geographic Influence
How is traditional clothing adapted to regional climate in Kashmir compared to Kerala?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Attire Adaptations:
• Kashmir: People wear long, loose, warm woolen cloaks called Phiran and carry a portable earthen fire-pot (Kangri) under it to withstand freezing winters.
• Kerala: People wear light, breathable white cotton Mundu and sarees to stay comfortable in humid, tropical coastal weather.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Kashmir Phiran/Kangri described 1.5 M
Kerala cotton Mundu described 1.5 M
Q10 Arctic Living • Igloos
What is an igloo? How does an ice house keep people warm inside in freezing polar regions?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Igloos:
An igloo is a dome-shaped winter shelter constructed from compressed blocks of packed snow. Packed snow contains trapped air pockets, which act as an excellent thermal insulator, trapping body heat inside.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Description of igloo 1 M
Trapped air insulation explained 1 M
Q11 Nomadic Living • Tents (Caravans)
Who are nomads? Why do nomadic pastoral communities live in mobile tents (like Changpa Rebo in Ladakh)?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Nomadic Tents:
Nomads are people who do not stay permanently in one place, moving seasonally with their animal herds to find fresh grazing pastures. They live in strong, portable yak-hair tents (Rebo) that can be packed and pitched easily.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Definition of nomads 1 M
Portability and yak-hair protection explained 1 M
Q12 Island Communities • Coastal Life
How does life for children living in coastal fishing villages (like Andaman and Nicobar Islands) revolve around the sea?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Coastal Livelihoods:
Children learn swimming, canoeing, and predicting sea weather from an early age. Fish and coconuts form the staple diet, and families build stilt homes with woven palm thatch.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Swimming/canoeing skills and staple diet 2 M
Q13 Mountain Farming • Step Terraces
Why do farmers in hilly mountain slopes build stepped terrace farms?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Terrace Farming:
Steep slopes cause rainwater to wash away fertile topsoil rapidly. Carving flat horizontal steps (terraces) along hills slows water runoff and retains water and soil for rice cultivation.

Soil erosion prevention and water retention 2 M

What is the famous national motto that celebrates India's incredible variety of lands, languages, and lives?

Unity in Diversity:
India's motto 'Unity in Diversity' celebrates how people of different terrains, climates, languages, religions, and customs live together harmoniously as one united nation.

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Preserving the Living Root Bridges of Meghalaya

The Khasi and Jaintia tribes of Meghalaya grow pedestrian bridges across fast mountain torrents using living rubber tree roots.

Analytical Task: Analyze why living root bridges are far superior to modern concrete or steel bridges in torrential rainforest valleys.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Unlike concrete or iron bridges that rust, rot, and get washed away by torrential monsoon floods, living root bridges made from Ficus elastica roots grow stronger and thicker over decades as the living tree continues to anchor deeper into riverbanks. They require no cement, cost zero money, cause no carbon emissions, and can endure for over 500 years in humid rainforest ecosystems.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for biological self-strengthening vs concrete deterioration; 1.5 Marks for zero ecological footprint and indigenous sustainability.
