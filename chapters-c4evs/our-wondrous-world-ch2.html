Chapter 2: Exploring Our Neighbourhood

Community Helpers, Public Utilities, Neighborhood Mapping & Civic Cleanliness | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Key public institutions in a neighborhood: Primary health clinic, post office, police station, fire station, bank, and library.
  • Community helpers who maintain civic life: Sanitation workers, electricians, plumbers, farmers, carpenters, and traffic police.
  • Reading simple neighborhood route maps with symbols and landmarks.
  • Civic responsibility: Maintaining clean public parks, segregating wet and dry waste, and preserving public property.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (16 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Institutional Inquiry • Public Utilities
Name four essential public places in a neighbourhood and explain the primary function of each.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Four Essential Public Places:
1. Primary Health Centre / Hospital: Provides first-aid, outpatient medical consultations, life-saving vaccinations, and emergency treatment for sick and injured citizens.
2. Post Office: Delivers letters, parcels, and money orders, and offers secure postal savings schemes and national insurance services.
3. Police Station: Maintains law and order, investigates local crimes, ensures neighborhood security, and assists citizens in distress.
4. Fire Station: Operates specialized fire engines and rescue personnel to extinguish fires, rescue people during building collapses, and manage flood emergencies.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Naming four valid public institutions 2.0 M
Accurate, concise explanation of each institution's function 2.0 M
Q2 Mapping & Navigation • Reading Maps
What is a 'landmark'? How do cardinal directions (North, South, East, West) and a map key (legend) help us find a place?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Landmark:
A landmark is a prominent, well-known, and easily recognizable building or natural feature (like a tall water tank, a large banyan tree, a school, or a temple) that serves as a reference point for finding locations.

2. Cardinal Directions & Map Key:
• Cardinal Directions (N, S, E, W): Give spatial orientation. On standard maps, North is at the top, South at the bottom, East to the right, and West to the left.
• Map Key (Legend): Explains the symbols, colours, and icons used on the map (e.g., a red cross for a hospital, a blue strip for a river, or a tent for a camp).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Definition of landmark with realistic examples 1.5 M
Role of cardinal directions and map legend in spatial navigation 1.5 M
Q3 Civic Responsibility • Waste Segregation
Why is it important to segregate waste at source into green and blue bins? Give two examples of items that go into each bin.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Importance of Segregating at Source:
Segregating waste at home prevents wet biodegradable garbage from mixing with dry recyclable items. Mixed waste rots in open landfills, generating toxic methane gas and groundwater-poisoning leachate. Proper segregation enables composting of wet waste and recycling of dry materials.

2. Bin Classification:
• Green Bin (Wet / Biodegradable Waste): Fruit peels, vegetable scraps, leftover food, tea leaves.
• Blue Bin (Dry / Recyclable Waste): Plastic bottles, cardboard boxes, newspapers, metal cans, glass jars.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Environmental importance of source segregation 1.5 M
Two accurate examples each for green and blue bins 1.5 M
Q4 Social Harmony • Dignity of Labour
What do you understand by 'Dignity of Labour'? How should we treat sanitation workers who clean our streets?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Dignity of Labour:
Dignity of labour means that every honest job and service—whether done by a surgeon, engineer, farmer, or sweeper—deserves equal respect, honour, and fair compensation. No honest work is 'low' or 'dirty'.

2. Treatment of Sanitation Workers:
We must treat sanitation workers with courteous language, greet them respectfully, never litter on streets, hand over properly tied and segregated waste, and support their rights to protective safety gear (gloves, masks, boots).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Concept of dignity of labour explained clearly 1.0 M
Respectful and practical civic conduct toward sanitation workers 1.0 M
Q5 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'Exploring Our Neighbourhood', describe how students can conduct an observational study in their school or residential community. What three parameters should they record in their field notebook?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Field Observation Protocol:
1. Direct Inquiry: Students observe the environment, community infrastructure, or natural ecosystem related to Exploring Our Neighbourhood during a guided campus walk.
2. Three Field Log Parameters:
• (a) Physical features, adaptations, or materials observed in nature/daily life.
• (b) Ecological dependencies (availability of clean water, sunlight, soil quality, or habitat safety).
• (c) Human actions observed (cleanliness practices, waste disposal, or conservation efforts).
3. Conclusion: Maintaining a nature journal fosters environmental sensitivity and scientific curiosity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Observation methodology clearly outlined 1.5 M
Three tangible field parameters detailed 1.5 M
Q6 Scientific Inquiry • Ecological Adaptations & Classification
Based on the environmental principles in 'Exploring Our Neighbourhood', classify living organisms or natural resources into distinct categories and state the scientific rationale behind your classification.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Scientific Rationale:
• Category 1 (Renewable / Biodegradable Resources): Elements that naturally replenish and decompose safely without releasing toxins into the soil or waterways.
• Category 2 (Non-Renewable / Non-Biodegradable Elements): Materials that persist in the environment for decades, requiring active recycling and mindful consumption.
• Scientific Rationale: Differentiating between biodegradable and non-biodegradable items empowers students to adopt zero-waste and eco-friendly lifestyles.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate distinction between the two groups 1.5 M
Clear scientific rationale highlighting sustainability 1.5 M
Q7 Community Living & Cultural Diversity • Social Harmony
How does 'Exploring Our Neighbourhood' illustrate the importance of community helpers, traditional skills, or regional diversity across India? Give two concrete examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Helpers & Regional Diversity:
1. Traditional Craftsmanship & Occupations: Across Indian states, local artisans, farmers, weavers, and sanitation workers contribute essential services that sustain healthy communities.
2. Mutual Interdependence: Every profession deserves dignity and respect. When people of diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds cooperate, villages and cities flourish in peace.
3. Examples: From weavers crafting heritage textiles to transport workers navigating rugged terrains, every occupation shapes our shared national heritage.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of community interdependence 1.5 M
Two concrete examples reflecting dignity of labor 1.5 M
Q8 Conservation Action • Water, Soil & Wildlife Protection
Suggest three practical conservation actions that a Class 4 student can implement at home and school inspired by the teachings of 'Exploring Our Neighbourhood'.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Three Student-Led Conservation Actions:
1. Water Conservation: Turn off taps while brushing teeth and reuse rinse-water from washed vegetables to water garden pots.
2. Waste Segregation: Separate organic wet kitchen peelings into compost bins while sending clean paper, cardboard, and plastics for recycling.
3. Protecting Green Cover: Plant a native sapling on birthdays and set up a bird bath on balconies during warm summer months.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Water saving technique explained 1.0 M
Waste segregation practice detailed 1.0 M
Flora/fauna protection action listed 1.0 M
Q9 Critical Thinking & Case Study • Environmental Decision Making
Analyze a real-world scenario from 'Exploring Our Neighbourhood': If a local natural waterbody or forest area is polluted by garbage, what steps should the local community take to restore it?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Restoration Action Plan:
1. Immediate Cleanup Drive: Organize student and resident volunteer drives equipped with gloves to safely collect floating plastics and litter.
2. Preventive Regulations: Install prominent awareness signboards prohibiting dumping, accompanied by secure covered dustbins.
3. Biological Rejuvenation: Plant native bank-stabilizing trees and reeds along water edges to prevent soil erosion and encourage aquatic life.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Immediate cleanup measures proposed 1.5 M
Long-term preventive community measures 1.5 M
Q10 Values & Empathy • Caring for All Living Beings
How can young citizens demonstrate empathy towards stray animals, birds, and elderly citizens in their daily lives, as highlighted in 'Exploring Our Neighbourhood'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Everyday Empathy in Practice:
1. Care for Living Creatures: Never throw stones or tease stray animals; keep a bowl of clean drinking water in shady spots.
2. Respect for Elders: Offer seats in public transport, speak politely, listen patiently to grandparents' stories, and assist them in carrying light groceries.
3. Core Principle: Compassion for all living beings builds a humane, peaceful, and responsible society.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Kindness towards animals and birds 1.5 M
Respectful support towards senior citizens 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Waste Segregation and Community Composting

Residents of an apartment society dump mixed garbage in open street corners, attracting stray dogs and foul smell.

Analytical Task: Design an action plan for the resident welfare association to solve this civic problem permanently.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
The association should implement a mandatory three-bin system (Green for wet kitchen waste, Blue for dry recyclables, Red for hazardous sanitary/electronic waste). They should set up an on-site aerated composting pit for wet waste to produce rich garden manure, partner with authorized recyclers for dry waste, and install CCTV monitoring to fine repeated open littering.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for source-segregation and composting mechanism; 1.5 Marks for community enforcement and recycling partnership.
