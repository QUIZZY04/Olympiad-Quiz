Chapter 1: Rational Numbers

NCERT Exercise Solutions — CBSE Marking Scheme

Q1
Using appropriate properties, find: (i) −2/3 × 3/5 + 5/2 − 3/5 × 1/6 (ii) 2/5 × (−3/7) − 1/6 × 3/2 + 1/14 × 2/5
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

(i) Using distributive property: −2/3 × 3/5 + 5/2 − 3/5 × 1/6

= 3/5(−2/3 − 1/6) + 5/2

= 3/5 × (−4/6 − 1/6) + 5/2 = 3/5 × (−5/6) + 5/2

= −1/2 + 5/2 = 4/2 = 2

(ii) Using distributive property: 2/5 × (−3/7) − 1/6 × 3/2 + 1/14 × 2/5

= 2/5 × (−3/7 + 1/14) − 1/4

= 2/5 × (−6/14 + 1/14) − 1/4 = 2/5 × (−5/14) − 1/4

= −1/7 − 1/4 = −4/28 − 7/28 = −11/28

CBSE Marking Scheme
Part (i): Correct application of distributive property + correct answer1 Mark
Part (ii): Correct steps + final answer −11/281 Mark
Q2
Name the property under multiplication used in each of the following: (i) −4/5 × 1 = 1 × −4/5 = −4/5 (ii) 13/17 × −2/7 = −2/7 × 13/17 (iii) −19/29 × 29/−19 = 1
1 Mark Each
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

(i) Multiplicative Identity Property — multiplying any rational number by 1 gives the number itself.

(ii) Commutativity of Multiplication — the order of multiplication does not change the product.

(iii) Multiplicative Inverse (Reciprocal) Property — a rational number multiplied by its reciprocal gives 1.

CBSE Marking Scheme
Each correct property name1 Mark each
Q3
Represent the following rational numbers on a number line: (i) −5/6 (ii) 3/4 (iii) −7/3
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

(i) −5/6: Lies between −1 and 0 on the number line. Divide the segment from −1 to 0 into 6 equal parts. Mark the 5th part from 0 towards −1. That point is −5/6.

(ii) 3/4: Lies between 0 and 1. Divide the segment from 0 to 1 into 4 equal parts. Mark the 3rd part from 0. That point is 3/4.

(iii) −7/3 = −2⅓: Lies between −3 and −2. Divide the segment from −3 to −2 into 3 equal parts. Mark the 1st part from −2 towards −3. That point is −7/3.

CBSE Marking Scheme
Correct representation of each number on number line with reasoning1 Mark each

Q1. Ravi has a ribbon of length 3/4 m. He cuts it into equal pieces, each of length 1/8 m. How many pieces does he get? If each piece costs ₹6, what is the total cost?
Number of pieces: 3/4 ÷ 1/8 = 3/4 × 8/1 = 24/4 = 6 pieces
Total cost: 6 × ₹6 = ₹36
⚖️ Assertion–Reason
Assertion (A): Every integer is a rational number.
Reason (R): A rational number is expressed in the form p/q where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0. Any integer n can be written as n/1.
(A) Both A and R true, R is correct explanation. (B) Both true, R not correct explanation. (C) A true R false. (D) A false R true.

🧠 HOTS
Q3. Is there a rational number between every pair of rational numbers? How many such numbers exist? Justify with an example.
Answer: Yes — infinitely many. Between any two rational numbers a and b (a < b), their average (a+b)/2 is always a rational number between them. Applying this repeatedly shows there are infinitely many rational numbers between any two rational numbers. Example: Between 1/3 and 1/2, the number (1/3+1/2)/2 = 5/12 lies between them, and this process never ends.

Q1
Solve: (i) 2x + 5 = 17 (ii) (3x − 4)/2 = 7 (iii) 5x − 3 = 3x + 5

(i) 2x + 5 = 17 → 2x = 12 → x = 6

(ii) (3x − 4)/2 = 7 → 3x − 4 = 14 → 3x = 18 → x = 6

(iii) 5x − 3 = 3x + 5 → 5x − 3x = 5 + 3 → 2x = 8 → x = 4

Chapter 3: Understanding Quadrilaterals

NCERT Exercise Solutions — CBSE Marking Scheme

Q1
What is the sum of the measures of angles of a convex quadrilateral? Will this property hold if the quadrilateral is not convex?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

The sum of measures of all angles of a convex quadrilateral = 360°.

Yes, this property holds for a non-convex (concave) quadrilateral too. Any quadrilateral can be divided into two triangles by drawing a diagonal. The sum of angles of each triangle = 180°, so total = 180° + 180° = 360°.

CBSE Marking Scheme
Sum = 360° stated correctly1 Mark
Yes, holds for concave quadrilateral with valid reason (two triangles)1 Mark
Q2
Three angles of a quadrilateral are 75°, 90°, and 110°. Find the fourth angle.
1 Mark
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Sum of all angles of a quadrilateral = 360°

Fourth angle = 360° − (75° + 90° + 110°) = 360° − 275° = 85°

CBSE Marking Scheme
Correct calculation showing 85°1 Mark
Q3
State the properties of a parallelogram. How is a rhombus different from a parallelogram?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Word Count: ~65 words)

Properties of a Parallelogram:

  • Opposite sides are equal and parallel.
  • Opposite angles are equal.
  • Adjacent angles are supplementary (sum = 180°).
  • Diagonals bisect each other.

Rhombus vs Parallelogram: A rhombus is a special parallelogram where all four sides are equal. Additionally, diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles (90°), which is not true for all parallelograms. Every rhombus is a parallelogram, but not vice versa.

🎯 Competency Based Questions (CBQ) — Chapter 3CBSE Pattern
⚖️ Assertion–Reason
Assertion (A): A square is a rhombus.
Reason (R): A square has all four sides equal and all angles equal to 90°.
(A) Both A and R true, R is correct explanation. (B) Both true, R not correct explanation. (C) A true R false. (D) A false R true.
Answer: (B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A. A square is a rhombus because it has all four equal sides (property of rhombus). 🧠 HOTS
Q2. A quadrilateral ABCD has AB ∥ CD and AB = CD. Is it a parallelogram? What if only AB ∥ CD but AB ≠ CD? Name the resulting quadrilateral.
Case 1 (AB ∥ CD and AB = CD): Yes, it is a parallelogram. If one pair of opposite sides is both parallel and equal, the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.
Case 2 (AB ∥ CD but AB ≠ CD): It is a Trapezium (exactly one pair of parallel sides). If the non-parallel sides are also equal, it becomes an isosceles trapezium.

Find the mean, median, and mode of: 4, 7, 3, 8, 7, 2, 9, 7, 5.
3 Marks

Arranged data: 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 7, 7, 8, 9 (n = 9)

✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Sum of 5 numbers = 5 × 18 = 90

Sum of remaining 4 numbers = 4 × 16 = 64

Excluded number = 90 − 64 = 26

Q3
What will be the unit digit of the squares of the following numbers? (i) 81 (ii) 272 (iii) 799 (iv) 3853
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

The unit digit of a square depends only on the unit digit of the original number:

  • (i) 81: Unit digit = 1 → 1² = 1 → Unit digit of 81² = 1
  • (ii) 272: Unit digit = 2 → 2² = 4 → Unit digit of 272² = 4
  • (iii) 799: Unit digit = 9 → 9² = 81 → Unit digit = 1
  • (iv) 3853: Unit digit = 3 → 3² = 9 → Unit digit = 9

2 Marks

7² is an incomplete triplet. Need one more 7 to complete the triplet 7³.

Multiply by 7 → 392 × 7 = 2744 = 2³ × 7³ = 14³ ✓

392 is not a perfect cube. Smallest multiplier = 7.

2 Marks

This is an inverse proportion (fewer hours → more workers needed).

Workers × Hours = constant

Speed = constant: 60/1.5 = x/5

x = (60 × 5)/1.5 = 300/1.5 = 200 km

Q1. Raman can type 540 words in 9 minutes. His computer breaks down after 6 minutes. How many words has he typed? How many remain if the total was 1200 words? Is this direct or inverse proportion?
This is direct proportion (more time → more words).
Rate = 540/9 = 60 words/min
Words typed in 6 min = 60 × 6 = 360 words
Remaining = 1200 − 360 = 840 words

Q1
Factorise: (i) 12x²y − 18xy² (ii) x² + 7x + 12 (iii) x² − 5x − 24
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

(i) 12x²y − 18xy² = 6xy(2x − 3y) — common factor = 6xy

= 6xy(2x − 3y)

(ii) x² + 7x + 12. Need two numbers with product 12 and sum 7: 3 and 4.

= (x + 3)(x + 4)

(iii) x² − 5x − 24. Need product −24 and sum −5: −8 and 3.

= (x − 8)(x + 3)

Q2
Using identities, factorise: (i) a² − 25 (ii) 4x² − 12x + 9 (iii) 16m² − (4m−1)²
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

(i) a² − 25 = a² − 5² = (a+5)(a−5) [using a²−b² = (a+b)(a−b)]

(ii) 4x²−12x+9 = (2x)² − 2×2x×3 + 3² = (2x−3)²

(iii) 16m²−(4m−1)² = (4m)²−(4m−1)²

= (4m+4m−1)(4m−4m+1) = (8m−1)(1) = (8m−1)

Q1. Divide 6x³ − 13x² + x + 2 by (2x−1). Find quotient and remainder. Verify using division algorithm.
Using polynomial long division of 6x³−13x²+x+2 by (2x−1):
6x³−13x²+x+2 = (2x−1)(3x²−5x−2) + 0
Quotient = 3x²−5x−2, Remainder = 0
Verification: (2x−1)(3x²−5x−2) = 6x³−10x²−4x − 3x²+5x+2 = 6x³−13x²+x+2 ✓
Further: 3x²−5x−2 = (3x+1)(x−2)

Q1
Plot the following ordered pairs on a graph sheet and name the type of figure formed by joining them: (3,5), (−1,5), (−1,−2), (3,−2).
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Plot the four points: A(3,5), B(−1,5), C(−1,−2), D(3,−2) on the coordinate plane.

AB is horizontal (y=5), CD is horizontal (y=−2), BC is vertical (x=−1), AD is vertical (x=3).

All angles are 90°. AB = CD = 4 units, BC = AD = 7 units.

The figure formed is a Rectangle.

The following table shows the side (s) and perimeter (p) of a square. Draw a graph and find: Is it a direct proportion? What is the perimeter when side = 6.5 cm?
3 Marks

Table:

  • s = 2 cm → p = 8 cm

Q3
Write the coordinates of the vertices of a rectangle OABC where O is the origin, A is at (4,0), B is at (4,3), and find the coordinates of C.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

O = (0,0), A = (4,0), B = (4,3)

In rectangle OABC, OC must be parallel to AB and OC = AB = 3 units vertical. OA is horizontal.

C lies directly above O at x = 0: C = (0, 3)

Dimensions: length OA = 4 units, width OC = 3 units. Area = 12 sq. units.

📝 Case Study
Q1. A class records daily temperature (°C) for 5 days: Mon=32, Tue=35, Wed=30, Thu=34, Fri=33. A student plots this as a line graph. Based on this:
(a) On which day was the temperature highest? (b) By how many degrees did temp change from Mon to Wed? (c) Is this a bar graph, pie chart, or line graph? Why is a line graph most suitable here?
(a) Highest temperature: Tuesday (35°C)
(b) Change Mon to Wed: 32 to 30 = decreased by 2°C
(c) This is a line graph. A line graph is most suitable for showing changes in a continuous variable (temperature) over time, as the connecting lines clearly show the trend and direction of change — rising or falling — between consecutive days.
🧠 HOTS
Q2. A point P is in quadrant III. What can you say about the signs of its x and y coordinates? If P is reflected across the x-axis, in which quadrant does the image lie?
In Quadrant III, both x and y coordinates are negative (x < 0, y < 0).
When P(x, y) is reflected across the x-axis, the image is P'(x, −y). Since x is negative and −y becomes positive, the image lies in Quadrant II (negative x, positive y).
