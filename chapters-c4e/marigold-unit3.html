Chapter 3: Run! & Nasruddin's Aim

Exuberant Poem by Mary Daunt & Humorous Folklore Story on Witty Archery Boasts | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Poem 'Run!': Escaping the crowded city to run in the rain, under trees, through meadows, and down hillsides.
  • Story 'Nasruddin's Aim': Nasruddin boasts of his unbeatable archery skills to his friends.
  • The missed shots: Attributing his first miss to 'aiming like the chief guard' and second miss to 'the general'.
  • The accidental bullseye: On the third lucky shot, hitting the center and proudly claiming: 'That was MY aim!'
  • Grammar: Compound words, rhyming words, and dialogue punctuation.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Reading is Fun • Poem 'Run!'
Where does the poet urge us to run? Why does she tell us to run away from the city into the countryside?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The poet urges us to run:
1. Away from the crowded city and into the sun.
2. Out to the country, into the raindrops, beneath trees, down hillsides, and through meadows.

She encourages this so we can escape stuffy, polluted concrete streets, breathe crisp open air, and experience pure, joyful, energetic freedom in nature.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Lists destinations: out to country, raindrops, trees, meadows 1.5 M
Explains contrast between crowded city and healthy nature 1.5 M
Q2 Reading is Fun • 'Nasruddin's Aim'
What was Nasruddin boasting about to his friends? Why did his friends burst into loud laughter after his first shot?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Nasruddin was proudly boasting that no archer could match his skill with the bow and arrow, claiming that the moment he strung the bow and took aim, the arrow never missed its mark.

His friends burst into loud laughter because on his very first attempt, the arrow fell far short of the target and landed harmlessly on the grass.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Describes boast of unmatched archery precision 1.5 M
Explains humiliating miss on first arrow shot 1.5 M
Q3 Reading is Fun • 'Nasruddin's Aim'
How did Nasruddin cleverly defend himself after his first two missed shots? What did he say when the third arrow struck the bullseye?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
First Miss: Nasruddin coolly declared: 'It wasn't my aim! It was the aim of the Chief Guard!'
Second Miss: He waved his hand and exclaimed: 'That was not my aim either; it was how the Chief of Army shoots!'

Third Shot (Bullseye): When luck struck and the third arrow hit dead center, he looked around triumphantly and exclaimed with supreme pride: 'Did you see that? That was MY aim!'
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Quotes defenses for guard and army chief misses 1.5 M
Quotes triumphant claim of ownership on bullseye 1.5 M
Q4 Word Building • Compound Words
Combine two words to create meaningful compound words: (a) Play + ground, (b) Sun + beam, (c) Water + fall, (d) Rain + drop.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
(a) Play + ground = Playground.
(b) Sun + beam = Sunbeam.
(c) Water + fall = Waterfall.
(d) Rain + drop = Raindrop.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Four correct compound words formed (0.5 M each) 2.0 M
Q5 Reading is Fun • Thematic Inquiry & Character Study
In 'Run! & Nasruddin's Aim', analyze how the central characters overcome their primary challenge. What life lesson or core value does this convey to young learners about empathy, patience, or perseverance?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Character Analysis & Thematic Understanding:
1. In Run! & Nasruddin's Aim, the characters face moments of uncertainty where teamwork, quick thinking, and kindness play pivotal roles in finding a resolution.
2. By listening to advice, respecting others, and not giving up when confronted with obstacles, they achieve success and bring happiness to those around them.
3. Core Value: The chapter reinforces that true strength lies in humility, perseverance, and mutual support during times of difficulty.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Detailed character insight and challenge identification 1.5 M
Clear deduction of core values (empathy/perseverance) 1.5 M

Q7 Grammar in Context • Parts of Speech & Tenses
Read the grammatical situation based on 'Run! & Nasruddin's Aim':
(a) Identify whether the following sentence is in past continuous or simple past tense: 'The children were walking through the green orchard when the bell rang.'
(b) Underline the prepositions of place and time in: 'The robin perched on the branch at dawn.'
(c) Change this affirmative sentence into a question: 'Neha woke up at six o'clock.'
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Grammar Analysis:
• (a) Tense Identification: 'were walking' is in the Past Continuous Tense (was/were + verb-ing), while 'rang' is in the Simple Past Tense.
• (b) Prepositions: 'on' (preposition of place) and 'at' (preposition of time).
• (c) Interrogative Transformation: 'Did Neha wake up at six o'clock?'
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of past continuous tense 1.0 M
Correct identification of prepositions 'on' and 'at' 1.0 M
Accurate question formation with helping verb 'Did' 1.0 M
Q8 Talk Time • Experiential Sharing & Perspective Taking
Imagine you experienced a situation similar to the one depicted in 'Run! & Nasruddin's Aim'. Describe how you felt, what decision you made, and what you learned from the final outcome.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Experiential Reflection:
• Situation: While participating in our school's science fair, our display model unexpectedly broke just thirty minutes before the judges arrived.
• Decision: Remembering the teamwork depicted in Run! & Nasruddin's Aim, my teammates and I stayed calm instead of panicking. We reassembled the parts using eco-friendly materials from the craft box.
• Learning Outcome: The judges appreciated our resourcefulness and teamwork. I learned that composure and collaborative problem-solving turn setbacks into triumphs.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear narrative of personal dilemma/experience 1.5 M
Maturity of reflection and actionable learning 1.5 M
Q9 Sentence Mechanics • Punctuation & Direct Speech
Punctuate and rewrite the following dialogue correctly:
mother asked rahul why are you late from school today rahul replied my cycle had a flat tire near the post office
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Corrected Dialogue with Punctuations:
Mother asked, "Rahul, why are you late from school today?"
Rahul replied, "My cycle had a flat tire near the post office."

Explanation: Added capital letters at the beginning of each sentence and proper nouns, commas after introductory reporting verbs, quotation marks for spoken words, and question mark inside quotation marks.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate quotation marks and question mark for Mother's speech 1.5 M
Accurate capitalization and punctuation for Rahul's response 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Boasting vs. True Competence

During sports trials, a student claims he can easily score 10 penalty goals in a row. When he misses the first four kicks, he blames the wet grass, the goalkeeper's gloves, and the referee's whistle.

Analytical Task: How does this student's behavior resemble Nasruddin's wit? What is the healthy difference between witty storytelling and genuine accountability in sports?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Resemblance to Nasruddin: Both deflect responsibility by attributing personal errors to external individuals or conditions to protect their pride.

Difference between Wit & Accountability: In Nasruddin's folklore, deflecting excuses is humorous theater meant to entertain friends. However, in real sports, honest accountability (analyzing technique, admitting mistakes, practicing humbly) is essential to develop true skill and character.
Rubric: 1 Mark for identifying parallel excuse-making; 1 Mark for distinguishing humorous fiction from authentic athletic accountability.
