Chapter 9: Hekko

Fascinating Indigenous Folk Game of Nagaland: Man vs. Tiger Strategy & Human Chain Defense | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Cultural heritage of Nagaland and its rich tradition of indigenous community games.
  • The linguistic root of 'Hekko': 'Hek' (to challenge) and 'Ko' (the tiger).
  • Two opposing teams: 'Aami' (The Humans / Man) and 'Aakho' (The Tigers).
  • Field setup: Eleven players in each team defending an inner circle through human chains.
  • The word 'Joko' (I surrender) and values of fair play, agility, and communal joy.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (14 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Let us Think • Comprehension
What does the word 'Hekko' literally mean, and from which Indian state does this folk game originate?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The word 'Hekko' literally translates to 'Challenging the Tiger'. It is derived from the indigenous Naga roots 'Hek' (meaning to challenge) and 'Ko' (meaning tiger).

This thrilling traditional folk game originates from the northeastern state of Nagaland.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Translates 'Challenging the Tiger' ('Hek' = challenge, 'Ko' = tiger) 1.5 M
Identifies origin from Nagaland 1.5 M
Q2 Let us Think • Comprehension
What are the two competing teams called in Hekko, and how many players participate on each side?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The two competing teams in Hekko are:
1. Aami: Representing 'Man' (Humans).
2. Aakho: Representing the 'Tiger'.

Each team consists of exactly eleven (11) players.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identifies Aami (Man) and Aakho (Tiger) 1.0 M
Specifies 11 players per team 1.0 M
Q3 Let us Think • Comprehension
How do the players of the Aami team defend their circle? What must an Aakho player say when caught?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Defense Strategy: The eleven players of the Aami team stand inside a large marked circular ring, link their hands tightly to form a defensive human chain, and coordinate their steps to block Aakho players from entering or pushing them out.

When Caught: When an attacking Aakho player steps into the circle and is tagged or encircled by the Aami defenders, he must loudly say 'Joko' (which means 'I surrender') to acknowledge capture.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Explains linked-hand human chain defense inside ring 1.5 M
Identifies surrender code 'Joko' 1.5 M
Q4 Let us Think • Think & Discuss
If you were playing Hekko, which team would you choose to join—Aami or Aakho? Give reasons based on team strategy.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
If given the choice, I would prefer to join the Aami team.

Reasons: The Aami team requires intense collective coordination, interlocking communication, and mutual trust to maintain an unbroken human chain. While the tiger (Aakho) attacks as an individual runner, the Aami defenders prove that human solidarity and organized unity can overcome raw individual strength.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear team preference stated 1.0 M
Strategic justification highlighting teamwork versus solo agility 2.0 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Inter-State Tribal Games Carnival

During the National Youth Festival, students from Punjab and Kerala were taught Hekko by Naga student delegates. The visiting students quickly observed that Hekko shared defensive chain concepts with Kabaddi.

Analytical Task: Compare the defensive human chain in Hekko with the corner-chain tackling in Kabaddi. What does this demonstrate about the cultural unity of Indian games?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Comparative Tactic: In both Hekko and Kabaddi, defenders hold hands in pairs or chains. Linking hands allows players to pivot around the attacker, generate collective pulling leverage, and prevent the raider/tiger from escaping back across the boundary line.

Cultural Unity: This structural similarity demonstrates that despite geographical distance and diverse languages, India's traditional games share universal core values of cooperative defense, strategic agility, and harmonious community play.
Rubric: 1 Mark for mechanical comparison of linked-hand chain leverage; 1 Mark for thematic unity across regional Indian games.
