NCERT In-Text & Exercise Questions — Board Examination Solutions
Q1
Answer the following in about 30 words: (i) Name one important beverage crop and specify the geographical conditions required for its growth, (ii) Name one staple crop of India and the regions where it is produced, (iii) Enlist the various institutional reform programmes introduced by the government in the interest of farmers.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

(i) Important Beverage Crop and Geographical Conditions:

  • Beverage Crop: Tea (or Coffee).
  • Geographical Conditions for Tea:
    • Climate: Tropical and sub-tropical climate with warm and moist frost-free climate throughout the year.
    • Soil: Deep, fertile, well-drained soil, rich in humus and organic matter.
    • Rainfall: Frequent showers evenly distributed throughout the year (150 to 200 cm) ensure continuous growth of tender green leaves.
    • Topography: Gently rolling hill slopes to prevent waterlogging around roots. Major producing states: Assam, West Bengal (Darjeeling hills), Tamil Nadu, and Kerala.

(ii) Staple Crop of India and Major Producing Regions:

  • Staple Crop: Rice (consumed by the majority of people in India).
  • Producing Regions: Plains of north and north-eastern India, coastal areas, and the deltaic regions (West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Odisha, and Tamil Nadu). Development of canal irrigation and tube-wells has made it possible to grow rice even in low rainfall areas of Punjab, Haryana, and western Uttar Pradesh.

(iii) Institutional Reform Programmes Introduced by the Government for Farmers:

  1. Collectivisation and Consolidation of Land Holdings: Consolidation of small fragmented holdings (chakbandi) and abolition of the exploitative zamindari system.
  2. Crop Insurance: Provision for crop insurance against drought, flood, cyclone, fire, and disease under the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana.
  3. Institutional Credit (KCC & PAIS): Establishment of Grameen banks, cooperative societies, and banks to provide loans at low interest rates; introduction of Kisan Credit Card (KCC) and Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS).
  4. Minimum Support Price (MSP): Announcement of Minimum Support Price (MSP), remunerative and procurement prices for important crops to check the exploitation of farmers by speculators and middlemen.
  5. Weather Bulletins and Agri-programmes: Special weather bulletins and agricultural programmes for farmers broadcast on radio and television.

Q2
Describe the geographical conditions required for the growth of rice.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Geographical Requirements for the Cultivation of Rice in India:

  1. Crop Type and Status: Rice is a Kharif crop and the staple food grain of the majority of the population in India. India is the second-largest producer of rice in the world after China.
  2. Temperature: Requires high temperature (above 25°C) during growing and harvesting periods.
  3. Humidity and Rainfall: High humidity with annual rainfall above 100 cm. In regions with less rainfall (like Punjab, Haryana, western UP, and parts of Rajasthan), it is cultivated successfully with the help of canal irrigation and tube-wells.
  4. Soil Type: Deep clayey and loamy alluvial soils that have high water-retention capacity are best suited for rice cultivation.
  5. Labour: Requires abundant cheap and skilled labour, as most of the operations like transplanting seedlings, weeding, and harvesting are performed manually by hand.

Q3
Describe the impact of globalisation on Indian agriculture.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Globalisation has exposed Indian agriculture to both significant opportunities and severe structural challenges, particularly since 1991:

  1. Exposure to Unfair Global Competition: Under globalisation, despite being an important producer of rice, cotton, rubber, tea, coffee, jute, and spices, Indian agricultural products are unable to compete with developed countries. This is because agriculture in developed nations (like the US and EU) is heavily subsidised by their governments.
  2. Agrarian Distress and Farmers' Suicides: Rising cost of production (expensive seeds, pesticides, and electricity) coupled with declining subsidies on fertilisers has reduced farm profitability, leading to indebtedness and agrarian distress in several states (e.g., Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Punjab).
  3. Genetic Modification and Gene Revolution: Modern biotechnology and Genetically Modified (GM) seeds (like Bt Cotton) have increased yields, but have made farmers heavily dependent on private multinational seed corporations.
  4. Rise of Organic Farming: Globalisation has created strong international demand for certified organic agricultural produce grown without chemical fertilisers or pesticides, offering lucrative export avenues for Indian organic cultivators.
  5. Need for Diversification towards High-Value Crops: Economists suggest that Indian farmers should diversify their cropping pattern from cereals to high-value crops (fruits, vegetables, medicinal herbs, flowers, and bio-diesel crops like jatropha and jojoba). This will increase incomes and reduce environmental degradation simultaneously, as fruits and herbs require much less water than sugarcane or paddy.

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'Vinoba Bhave undertook padyatra to spread Gandhiji's message across India. Once, at Pochampalli in Andhra Pradesh, some landless poor villagers demanded 80 acres of land for their economic survival. Suddenly, Shri Ram Chandra Reddy stood up and offered 80 acres of land to be distributed among 80 landless villagers. This act was known as 'Bhoodan'. Later, he travelled and introduced his ideas throughout India, and some zamindars offered to distribute some villages among the landless. This was known as 'Gramdan'.'

Question: Why is the Bhoodan-Gramdan movement initiated by Vinoba Bhave referred to as the 'Blood-less Revolution'?

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Voluntary Land Redistribution: Unlike violent communist agrarian revolutions that forcefully seized estates through bloodshed, Vinoba Bhave appealed peacefully to the conscience and moral duty of wealthy landlords.
  • Peaceful Gandhian Transformation: Landowners voluntarily gifted lakhs of acres of fertile land (Bhoodan) and entire villages (Gramdan) to landless peasants without any state coercion or violent conflict, making it a unique 'Blood-less Revolution'.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): Intensive subsistence farming is practised in areas of high population pressure on land.
Reason (R): High doses of biochemical inputs and irrigation are used for obtaining higher production from limited land due to the right of inheritance leading to fragmentation of holdings.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: Successive division of agricultural land among generations has reduced farm plot sizes to an uneconomical level. Farmers have no alternative source of livelihood, so they apply maximum possible labour, fertiliser, and irrigation to squeeze maximum output from their tiny plots.
