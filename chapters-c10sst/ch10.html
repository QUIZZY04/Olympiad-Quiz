Q1
Answer the following in about 30 words: (i) Distinguish between ferrous and non-ferrous minerals, (ii) What is a mineral? (iii) How are minerals formed in igneous and metamorphic rocks? (iv) Why do we need to conserve mineral resources?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

(i) Distinguish between Ferrous and Non-Ferrous Minerals:

Feature Ferrous Minerals Non-Ferrous Minerals
Iron Content Metallic minerals that contain iron. Metallic minerals that do not contain iron.
Economic Share Account for about three-fourths of the total value of production of metallic minerals in India. Reserves and production are comparatively less satisfactory in India, but crucial for metallurgy and electronics.
Examples Iron ore, Manganese, Chromite, Nickel, Cobalt. Copper, Bauxite (Aluminium), Lead, Zinc, Gold.

(ii) What is a Mineral?

Geologists define a mineral as a 'homogenous, naturally occurring substance with a definable internal crystalline structure.' Minerals are found in varied forms in nature, ranging from the hardest diamond to the softest talc.

(iii) How are Minerals Formed in Igneous and Metamorphic Rocks?

  • In igneous and metamorphic rocks, minerals may occur in the cracks, crevices, faults, or joints. The smaller occurrences are called veins and the larger are called lodes.
  • In most cases, they are formed when minerals in liquid/molten and gaseous forms are forced upward through cavities towards the earth’s surface. They cool and solidify as they rise. Major metallic minerals like tin, copper, zinc, and lead are obtained from veins and lodes.

(iv) Why do we need to Conserve Mineral Resources?

  1. Finite and Non-Renewable: Mineral formation requires millions of years of geological processes; the present rate of consumption vastly exceeds the infinitesimal rate of geological replenishment.
  2. Decreasing Quality and Increasing Cost: Continued extraction leads to depletion of high-grade ores, forcing mining at greater depths accompanied by lower grades and escalated extraction costs.

Q2
Describe the distribution of coal in India.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Coal is the most abundantly available fossil fuel in India, providing a substantial part of the nation's energy needs. In India, coal occurs in rock sequences of two main geological ages:

  1. Gondwana Coal Deposits (Over 200 Million Years Old):
    • Constitutes the vast bulk of India’s metallurgical and commercial coal reserves (bituminous coal).
    • Damodar Valley Basin (West Bengal–Jharkhand): The premier coalfields of India are located here: Jharia (richest coking coal), Raniganj (oldest coalfield), Bokaro, Giridih, and Karanpura.
    • Other River Valleys: Godavari valley (Singareni in Telangana), Mahanadi valley (Talcher and Ib river in Odisha), and Wardha-Son valleys (Singrauli and Chanda in MP/Maharashtra).
  2. Tertiary Coal Deposits (Only about 55 Million Years Old):
    • Younger, softer coal with higher moisture and sulfur content.
    • Found extensively in the north-eastern states, namely Meghalaya (Darangiri, Cherrapunji), Assam (Makum, Nazira), Arunachal Pradesh (Namchik-Namphuk), and Nagaland.
  3. Lignite Coal Deposits:
    • Low-grade brown coal, soft with high moisture content. The principal lignite reserves are located at Neyveli in Tamil Nadu, used predominantly for thermal power generation.

Q3
Why do you think that solar energy has a bright future in India?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Solar energy has an exceptionally bright and transformative future in India due to the following strategic, geographical, and environmental factors:

  1. Favourable Tropical Location: India is a tropical country endowed with enormous solar radiation. It receives about 300 clear sunny days a year, with an estimated solar energy potential of about 20 MW per square kilometre per annum.
  2. Vast Potential in Arid and Semi-Arid Regions: Western India, particularly Rajasthan (Thar Desert) and Gujarat, has vast barren tracts with abundant sunshine, making them ideal for massive solar photovoltaic and concentrated solar power parks (e.g., Bhadla Solar Park in Rajasthan, one of the world's largest).
  3. Cost-Effective Photovoltaic Technology: Photovoltaic technology converts sunlight directly into electricity. With rapid technological advances and economies of scale, solar tariff costs have plummeted drastically, making solar cheaper than conventional thermal power.
  4. Decentralised Power for Rural India: Solar energy can be set up in decentralized units (solar pumps, rooftop panels, solar lanterns) in remote villages where setting up high-voltage grid lines is uneconomic. This reduces rural dependence on firewood and dung cakes, conserving forests and cattle manure for agriculture.
  5. Environmental Sustainability: Solar power is 100% clean, renewable, and emits zero greenhouse gases, directly helping India fulfill its international climate commitments (Panchamrit goals / Net Zero by 2070).

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'In Meghalaya, there are large deposits of coal, iron ore, limestone and dolomite etc. Coal mining in Jowai and Cherrapunji is done by family members in the form of a long narrow tunnel, known as a 'Rat hole' mining. The National Green Tribunal (NGT) has declared such activities illegal and recommended that these should be stopped forthwith.'

Question: What is 'Rat-hole mining' and why has it been declared hazardous and illegal by the National Green Tribunal?

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Method: In Meghalaya, mineral ownership vests with tribal individuals or communities. Primitive miners dig vertical pits and crawl through dangerously narrow horizontal tunnels (like rat holes) to extract coal.
  • Hazards: It lacks basic safety equipment; during monsoons, tunnels flood instantly, drowning workers (frequently child labourers). Furthermore, it causes severe acid mine drainage, polluting surrounding rivers like the Lukha and ruining drinking water.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): Natural gas is considered an environment-friendly fuel.
Reason (R): Natural gas produces low carbon dioxide emissions on combustion and leaves behind no ash or toxic residues, making it a key transition fuel.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: Natural gas consists primarily of methane. Compared to coal and petroleum, it emits substantially less carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide, and particulate matter, making Compressed Natural Gas (CNG) and Piped Natural Gas (PNG) the fuel of choice for urban transport and households.
