Q1
What is a political party? What are its components and what functions do political parties perform in a democracy?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Meaning of a Political Party:

A political party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in the government. They agree on some policies and programmes for the society with a view to promote the collective good.

2. Three Basic Components of a Political Party:

  • The Leaders: Who frame policies, address rallies, and take strategic decisions.
  • The Active Members: Who work on committees, mobilize supporters, and conduct party campaigns.
  • The Followers: Common voters and citizens who believe in the party’s ideology and vote for its candidates.

3. Vital Functions Performed by Political Parties in a Democracy:

  1. Contest Elections: In most democracies, elections are fought mainly among the candidates put up by political parties. Parties select their candidates in different ways (by top leaders in India, or by party members in the US).
  2. Put Forward Policies and Programmes: Parties put forward different policies and programmes, and the voters choose from them. A party reduces a vast multitude of public opinions into a few basic directions which it supports.
  3. Making Laws: Parties play a decisive role in making laws for a country. Formally, laws are debated and passed in the legislature, but since most members belong to a party, they go by the direction of the party leadership irrespective of personal opinions.
  4. Form and Run Governments: Parties recruit leaders, train them, and then make them ministers to run the government in the way the party wants.
  5. Role of Opposition: Those parties that lose in the elections play the role of opposition to the parties in power, by voicing different views and criticizing government for its failures or wrong policies.
  6. Shape Public Opinion: They raise and highlight issues. Parties have lakhs of members and activists spread all over the country, launching movements for the resolution of problems faced by people.
  7. Access to Government Machinery: For an ordinary citizen, it is easy to approach a local party leader than a government officer. Parties serve as an accessible link between citizens and the state.

Q2
What are the various challenges faced by political parties in modern democracies?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Political parties across the globe face four major challenges that hinder their democratic effectiveness:

  1. Lack of Internal Democracy within Parties:
    • All over the world, there is a tendency in political parties towards the concentration of power in one or few leaders at the top.
    • Parties do not keep membership registers, do not hold regular organisational meetings, and do not conduct internal elections. Ordinary members do not get sufficient information on what happens inside the party. Disagreeing with the leadership means dismissal from the party.
  2. Dynastic Succession:
    • Since most political parties do not practice open and transparent procedures for their functioning, it is very difficult for an ordinary worker to rise to the top.
    • In many parties, the top positions are always controlled by members of one family. This is unfair to other members and bad for democracy, since people who do not have adequate experience or popular support come to occupy positions of power.
  3. Growing Role of Money and Muscle Power:
    • Since parties are focused only on winning elections, they tend to use short-cuts to win. They nominate those candidates who have or can raise lots of money.
    • Rich people and companies who give funds to the parties tend to have influence on the policies and decisions of the party. In many cases, parties support criminals who can secure votes through sheer intimidation.
  4. Failure to Offer a Meaningful Choice to Voters:
    • In recent years, there has been a significant decline in the ideological differences among parties in most parts of the world.
    • Parties agree on fundamental economic policies and differ only on minor details of implementation. Moreover, the same set of leaders keep shifting from one party to another, leaving voters with no genuine ideological alternative.

Q3
Suggest some reforms to strengthen political parties so that they perform their functions well.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

To strengthen political parties and cleanse democratic governance, several reforms have been enacted and further suggested:

  1. Measures Already Enacted:
    • Anti-Defection Law: The Constitution was amended to prevent elected MLAs and MPs from changing parties to secure ministerial berths or cash rewards. The law states that if any elected member changes parties, they lose their legislative seat.
    • Mandatory Affidavit of Assets and Criminal Cases: The Supreme Court made it mandatory for every candidate contesting elections to file an Affidavit giving details of their property and criminal cases pending against them, empowering voters with crucial information.
    • Mandatory Organisational Elections and Tax Returns: The Election Commission passed an order making it necessary for political parties to hold their organisational elections and file their income tax returns.
  2. Suggested Reforms Yet to be Enacted:
    • Regulation of Internal Affairs by Law: A law should be made to regulate the internal affairs of political parties. It should be made compulsory for political parties to maintain a register of their members, follow their own constitution, have an independent authority to act as a judge in case of party disputes, and hold open elections to the highest posts.
    • Reservation for Women: It should be made mandatory for political parties to give a minimum proportion of tickets (about one-third) to women candidates.
    • State Funding of Elections: There should be state funding of elections. The government should give parties money to support their election expenses in kind (petrol, paper, telephone) or cash on the basis of votes secured in previous elections, eliminating dependence on corrupt corporate funding.

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'A party that secures at least six per cent of the total votes in an election to the Legislative Assembly of a State and wins at least two seats is recognised as a State party. A party that secures at least six per cent of the total votes in Lok Sabha elections or Assembly elections in four States and wins at least four seats in the Lok Sabha is recognised as a National party.'

Question: State the Election Commission's criteria for recognizing a National Party and name two recognized National Parties in India.

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • National Party Criteria: Must secure at least 6% of total valid votes in Lok Sabha elections OR in assembly elections across 4 states, AND win at least 4 seats in the Lok Sabha.
  • Recognised National Parties: Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), Indian National Congress (INC), Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP), Communist Party of India (Marxist) [CPI-M], and Aam Aadmi Party (AAP).
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): Democracies that have a multi-party system often appear very messy and lead to political instability.
Reason (R): At the same time, this system allows a variety of interests and opinions to enjoy political representation.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: Multi-party systems (like India's) often require coalition arrangements that can seem volatile, but in socially and linguistically diverse countries, a multi-party system is essential to ensure that diverse regional, ethnic, and linguistic communities have an effective voice in national governance.
