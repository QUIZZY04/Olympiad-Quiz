Q1
Answer the following in about 30 words: (i) Name three states having black soil and the crop mainly grown on it, (ii) What type of soil is found in the river deltas of the eastern coast? Give three main features of this type of soil, (iii) What steps can be taken to control soil erosion in the hilly areas? (iv) What are biotic and abiotic resources? Give some examples.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

(i) Black Soil Regions and Associated Major Crop:

  • States: Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, and parts of Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and Chhattisgarh (covering the Deccan trap basalt region).
  • Crop Grown: Cotton is the premier commercial crop grown on black soil (hence widely referred to as Black Cotton Soil or Regur). It is also ideal for sugarcane, jowar, wheat, and oilseeds.

(ii) Type of Soil in the River Deltas of the Eastern Coast (Three Main Features):

  • Soil Type: Alluvial Soil (deposited by the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri river systems).
  • Feature 1 (High Fertility): Extremely fertile due to adequate proportions of potash, phosphoric acid, and lime, making it ideal for the growth of sugarcane, paddy, wheat, and cereal crops.
  • Feature 2 (Classification by Age): Classified into older alluvium (Bhangar, containing higher concentration of calcareous kankar nodules) and newer alluvium (Khadar, finer particles, renewed annually by floods, and more fertile).
  • Feature 3 (Intensive Cultivation): Because of its high fertility, regions of alluvial soils are intensively cultivated and densely populated.

(iii) Steps to Control Soil Erosion in Hilly Areas:

  1. Contour Ploughing: Ploughing along the contour lines decelerates the flow of water down the slopes, preventing gully formation.
  2. Terrace Farming: Steps or terraces are cut out on slopes making step terraces to control runoff and prevent soil washaway (practised widely in Western and Central Himalayas).
  3. Strip Cropping: Large fields are divided into strips; strips of grass are left to grow between the crops to break up the kinetic force of the wind.
  4. Shelter Belts: Planting lines of trees to create shelter breaks wind velocity and stabilizes sand dunes and slopes.

(iv) Biotic and Abiotic Resources (with Examples):

  • Biotic Resources: Resources obtained from the biosphere that possess life. Examples: Flora, fauna, human beings, fisheries, livestock, and forests.
  • Abiotic Resources: All those things which are composed of non-living matter. Examples: Rocks, metals, land, water, air, and mineral ores.

Q2
Explain land use pattern in India and why the land under forest has not increased much since 1960-61.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Land Use Pattern in India:

India possesses a total geographical area of 3.28 million square kilometres. However, land use data is available for only 93% of the total area because land-use reporting for most of the north-eastern states (except Assam) has not been done fully, and some areas of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh occupied by Pakistan and China have not been surveyed.

  • Net Sown Area (NSA): Accounts for about 45.5% of total reporting area. The pattern of net sown area varies greatly from state to state: over 80% in Punjab and Haryana, but less than 10% in Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, Manipur, and Andaman & Nicobar Islands.
  • Forest Cover: India's forest cover is roughly 24%, far lower than the essential 33% of geographical area outlined in the National Forest Policy (1952) for ecological balance.
  • Permanent Pastures: Land under permanent pasture has continually decreased, posing severe feed challenges for cattle populations.
  • Waste Land and Non-Agricultural Uses: Rocky, arid, and desert areas, along with land put to non-agricultural uses like settlements, roads, railways, and industries.

2. Reasons why Net Sown Area has not increased much:

  1. Intense Pressure from Urbanisation and Infrastructure: Rapid industrialisation, urban expansion, highways, airports, and housing projects have encroached upon prime agricultural lands.
  2. Land Degradation: Continuous use of land over long periods without adequate conservation measures has resulted in severe land degradation (over 130 million hectares of degraded land in India: water-eroded, wind-eroded, and saline/alkaline).
  3. Over-Irrigation and Waterlogging: In Punjab, Haryana, and western UP, over-irrigation has caused waterlogging leading to increased salinity and alkalinity, rendering lands uncultivable.
  4. Depletion of Soil Fertility: Excessive application of chemical fertilisers, pesticides, and lack of crop rotation have exhausted the natural organic nutrients of the soil.

Q3
How have technical and economic development led to more consumption of resources?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Technical and economic development has directly accelerated the rate of resource consumption across the world and in India through the following mechanisms:

  1. Technological Capability for Mass Exploitation: Modern technology provides sophisticated equipment, heavy earth-moving machinery, and deep-drilling tools that enable humans to extract minerals, petroleum, and groundwater at rates impossible in traditional societies.
  2. Industrialisation and Need for Raw Materials: Economic growth requires continuous expansion of manufacturing industries. Steel plants, thermal power stations, automobile units, and chemical factories devour vast quantities of coal, iron ore, limestone, water, and crude oil every day.
  3. Rising Living Standards and Consumerism: Economic development elevates per capita income and living standards. Affluent lifestyles lead to immense consumption of electricity, electronic gadgets, private motor vehicles, packaged foods, and luxury goods, multiplying resource throughput per person.
  4. Mechanisation of Agriculture: High-Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds, tractors, chemical fertilisers, and tube-wells have replaced traditional sustainable farming, depleting groundwater tables and fossil fuels.
  5. Urbanisation and Infrastructure Expansion: Concrete buildings, multi-lane expressways, bridges, and smart cities consume massive quantities of sand, gravel, water, cement, and metal ores.

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'At the international level, the Club of Rome advocated resource conservation for the first time in a more systematic way in 1968. Subsequently, in 1974, Gandhian philosophy was once again presented by Schumacher in his book Small is Beautiful. The seminal contribution with respect to resource conservation at the global level was made by the Brundtland Commission Report, 1987. This report introduced the concept of 'Sustainable Development' and advocated it as a means for resource conservation.'

Question: What is 'Sustainable Development' and how does it balance economic growth with environmental conservation?

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Definition: Sustainable economic development means 'development should take place without damaging the environment, and development in the present should not compromise with the needs of the future generations.'
  • Practical Application: It requires replacing fossil fuels with renewable energy, practicing 3Rs (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle), controlling soil and water pollution, and ensuring equitable resource distribution so present economic advancement does not exhaust the planetary life-support system.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): Arid soil develops on crystalline igneous rocks in areas of low rainfall.
Reason (R): Arid soils range from red to brown in colour, are generally sandy in texture and saline in nature, and lack humus and moisture due to dry climate and high evaporation.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Assertion (A) is false, but Reason (R) is true.

Explanation: Red and yellow soils develop on crystalline igneous rocks in areas of low rainfall (in the eastern and southern parts of the Deccan plateau). Arid soils develop in desert and dry regions like western Rajasthan due to intense weathering under desert conditions. Reason (R) accurately describes the characteristics of arid soil.
