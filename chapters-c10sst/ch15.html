Q1
Mention different aspects of life in which women are discriminated against or disadvantaged in India.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In India, women continue to face discrimination, disadvantage, and subjugation in several vital spheres of life:

  1. Disparity in Literacy Rates: The literacy rate among women is only 65.46% compared with 82.14% among men (Census 2011). Similarly, a smaller proportion of girl students go for higher studies because parents prefer to spend their resources on boys’ education rather than spending equally on sons and daughters.
  2. Unpaid and Undervalued Work: On average, an Indian woman works one hour more than an average man every day, yet much of her work (cooking, cleaning, child rearing) is domestic and not paid, and therefore often not valued.
  3. Wage Disparity in Labour Markets: Under the Equal Remuneration Act, 1976, equal wages should be paid for equal work. However, in almost all spheres of work — from sports and cinema to factories and agricultural fields — women are paid less than men, even when both do exactly the same work.
  4. Declining Child Sex Ratio (Female Foeticide): In many parts of India, parents prefer to have sons and find ways to abort female fetuses before birth. This sex-selective abortion has led to a skewed child sex ratio in the country (falling as low as 850 in some districts).
  5. Violence and Exploitation: Women face widespread harassment, exploitation, and violence both inside the domestic home (domestic abuse, dowry harassment) and in public spaces, making urban areas increasingly unsafe.
  6. Extremely Low Political Representation: Women’s proportion in the Indian Lok Sabha has historically stayed below 15 per cent, and in state assemblies less than 10 per cent, far below the world average.

Q2
State the different forms that communalism can take in politics with examples.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Communalism can take various forms in democratic and electoral politics:

  1. Everyday Religious Prejudices: The most common expression of communalism is in everyday beliefs. These routinely involve religious prejudices, stereotypes of religious communities, and belief in the inherent superiority of one’s religion over other religions.
  2. Quest for Political Dominance: A communal mind often leads to a quest for political dominance of one's own religious community. For those belonging to majority community, this takes the form of majoritarian dominance (e.g., Sinhala majoritarianism in Sri Lanka). For those belonging to the minority community, it can take the form of a desire to form a separate political unit.
  3. Political Mobilisation on Religious Lines: Political mobilization on religious lines is another frequent form of communalism. This involves the use of sacred religious symbols, religious leaders, emotional appeals, and plain fear in order to bring the followers of one religion together in the political arena to polarize votes during elections.
  4. Communal Violence, Riots and Massacres: Sometimes communalism takes its ugliest form of communal violence, riots, and massacres. India and Pakistan suffered some of the worst communal riots at the time of the Partition. Post-independence India has also seen large-scale communal violence in various states.

Q3
State two reasons to say that caste alone cannot determine election results in India.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Caste alone cannot determine election results in India due to the following structural electoral realities:

  1. No Single Caste Majority in Any Constituency: No parliamentary constituency in the country has a clear majority of one single caste. So, every candidate and party needs to win the confidence of more than one caste and community to win an election.
  2. No Party Wins All Votes of a Caste: No party wins the votes of all the voters of a caste or community. When people say that a caste is a 'vote bank' of one party, it usually means that a large proportion of the voters from that caste vote for that party, but never all.
  3. Multiple Candidates from the Same Caste: Many political parties may put up candidates from the same dominant caste (if that caste is believed to dominate the constituency). Some voters have more than one candidate from their caste, while other voters have no candidates from their caste.
  4. Voters Judge Performance of Incumbent Governments: The ruling party and the sitting MP or MLA frequently lose elections in our country. This could not have happened if all caste groups were frozen in their political loyalties. Voters actively consider inflation, employment, infrastructure, and leadership competence alongside caste identities.

Q4
Explain the constitutional provisions that make India a secular state.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The makers of the Indian Constitution chose the model of a Secular State to combat communalism and protect religious diversity:

  1. No Official State Religion: There is no official religion for the Indian state. Unlike the status of Buddhism in Sri Lanka, Islam in Pakistan, or Christianity in England, our Constitution does not give a special status to any religion.
  2. Freedom to Practice and Propagate Religion: The Constitution provides to all individuals and communities the fundamental freedom to profess, practice, and propagate any religion, or not to follow any (Articles 25–28).
  3. Prohibition of Discrimination: The Constitution strictly prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion in matters of education, employment, and public access (Article 15).
  4. State Intervention for Social Justice: At the same time, the Constitution allows the state to intervene in the matters of religion in order to ensure equality within religious communities (e.g., it bans untouchability under Article 17 and reforms family laws to ensure gender equity).

Q7
State how caste inequalities and caste prejudices are still continuing in contemporary India despite constitutional prohibitions.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Although the Constitution of India prohibited caste-based discrimination and outlawed untouchability, caste inequalities continue to persist in the following forms:

  1. Persistence of Endogamy: Even in modern times, most marriages still take place within the same caste group or community. Inter-caste marriages remain a minority and face social opposition.
  2. Economic Disparities & Land Ownership: Centuries of caste disadvantages have created deep structural economic divides. Upper castes remain disproportionately wealthy, educated, and well-represented in high-income corporate and professional jobs, while Dalits and backward communities remain over-represented among landless agricultural labourers and urban unorganized workers.
  3. Unequal Access to Higher Education: Educational attainment remains closely linked to caste hierarchy. Castes that had traditional access to learning have adapted faster to modern technical education.
  4. Caste-Based Voting and Political Polarization: Political parties appeal to caste sentiments, choose candidates based on caste calculations, and mobilize caste vote banks during elections.

Q8
Mention any four forms that communalism can take in politics.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Communalism in politics can manifest in several dangerous ways:

  1. Everyday Beliefs and Stereotypes: Religious prejudices, stereotypes of religious communities, and belief in the superiority of one's religion over other religions are routine aspects of everyday thinking.
  2. Quest for Political Dominance: A communal mind often leads to a quest for political dominance of one's own religious community. For majority communities, this takes the form of majoritarian dominance; for minority communities, it can take the form of a desire to form a separate political unit.
  3. Political Mobilisation on Religious Lines: Involves the use of sacred religious symbols, religious leaders, emotional appeals, and plain fear to bring the followers of one religion together in the political arena during elections.
  4. Communal Violence, Riots, and Massacres: In its most ugly and extreme form, communalism leads to communal violence, riots, and targeted massacres, as witnessed during the Partition of India and in recurring post-independence riots.

Q9
State two reasons why women's representation in India's national parliament has remained historically low. Describe the historic legislative remedy enacted to address this issue.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Reasons for Low Representation of Women in Parliament:

  1. Patriarchal Mindset and Social Stereotypes: A deeply entrenched patriarchal social structure expects women to prioritize domestic responsibilities and family care over public political leadership, discouraging female candidacy.
  2. Reluctance of Political Parties: Major political parties are historically reluctant to give election tickets to female candidates, citing perceived lack of 'winnability' and resistance within male-dominated party command structures. (Women constituted less than 15% of MPs in the Lok Sabha).

Historic Legislative Remedy (Women's Reservation Act):

  • The passage of the Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam (106th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023) constitutionally reserves 33% (one-third) of all seats in the Lok Sabha, State Legislative Assemblies, and the Delhi Legislative Assembly for women.
  • This landmark reform ensures substantial, guaranteed democratic representation for women in national law-making bodies.

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'Caste can take various forms in politics: when parties choose candidates, they keep in mind the caste composition of the electorate and nominate candidates from different castes so as to muster necessary support to win elections. When governments are formed, political parties usually take care that representatives of different castes and tribes find a place in it.'

Question: How does politics also influence caste system in India?

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Widening Caste Identity: Each caste group tries to become bigger by incorporating within it neighboring sub-castes or related castes which were earlier excluded.
  • Formation of Caste Coalitions: Various caste groups are required to enter into a coalition with other castes or communities and thus enter into a dialogue and negotiation.
  • New Categorizations: New kinds of caste groups have come up in the political arena like 'backward' and 'forward' caste groups. Politics thus politicizes caste, making it fluid and competitive.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): The Women's Reservation Act (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam) provides 33% reservation for women in Lok Sabha and state assemblies.
Reason (R): Feminists and women’s movements argued that unless women control political power, their problems and discrimination will not get adequate attention.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: India has had one of the lowest percentages of women lawmakers in the world (under 15%). The passage of the Women's Reservation Bill ensures statutory representation of women at policy-making levels, emulating the successful model of Panchayati Raj.
