NCERT In-Text & Exercise Questions — Board Examination Solutions
Q1
Answer the following in about 30 words: (i) State any three merits of roadways, (ii) Where and why is rail transport the most convenient means of transportation? (iii) What is the significance of the border roads? (iv) What is meant by trade? What is the difference between international and local trade?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

(i) State Any Three Merits of Roadways over Railways:

  1. Lower Construction and Maintenance Cost: Construction cost of roads is much lower than that of railway lines.
  2. Traverses Difficult Terrains: Roads can traverse comparatively more dissected and undulating topography, can negotiate higher gradients of slopes, and can easily traverse mountains like the Himalayas where laying rail tracks is prohibitive.
  3. Door-to-Door Service: Road transport provides door-to-door service, which dramatically reduces the cost of loading and unloading goods. It acts as a vital feeder to railway stations, seaports, and airports.

(ii) Where and Why is Rail Transport the Most Convenient Means of Transportation?

  • Region: The Northern Plains of India.
  • Reasons: The vast level land, high population density, rich agricultural resources, and widespread industrial activity make the Northern Plains most favourable for laying rail tracks. Railways are the principal mode of freight and passenger transport for long distances.

(iii) Significance of Border Roads:

  • Constructed and maintained by the Border Roads Organisation (BRO) established in 1960.
  • They have improved accessibility in strategic border areas of the northern and north-eastern frontier (e.g., Ladakh, Arunachal Pradesh), facilitating swift armed forces movement for national defense and stimulating rapid economic development of remote border villages.

(iv) What is Meant by Trade? Difference between International and Local Trade:

  • Trade: The exchange of goods and services among people, states, and countries through markets is referred to as trade.
  • International Trade: Trade carried out between two or more sovereign countries across international borders. It involves foreign exchange currency and is an index of a nation's economic prosperity.
  • Local Trade: Trade carried out within local jurisdictions — between cities, towns, and villages within the same country, using domestic currency.

Q2
Why are the means of transportation and communication called the lifelines of a nation and its economy?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Efficient means of transport and communication are rightly called the lifelines of a nation and its economy because of the following fundamental reasons:

  1. Integration of Spatially Dispersed Markets: Production of goods takes place at specific locations, while consumers are scattered throughout the country. Transport bridges the distance between production centres and consumer markets, enabling seamless trade.
  2. Catalyst for Agricultural and Industrial Growth: Agriculture requires quick movement of fertilizers, seeds, and farm machinery, and transport of perishable produce (milk, vegetables) to urban centers. Industries depend on railways and road networks to carry bulky raw materials (iron ore, coal) and deliver manufactured goods across India and overseas.
  3. Socio-Cultural Unification: Transport networks and mass communication (radio, television, newspapers, Internet) knit people of diverse languages, cultures, and regions into a unified national community.
  4. National Defense and Disaster Management: In times of external military threats or natural disasters (floods, earthquakes, tsunamis), transport lifelines allow rapid deployment of armed forces, relief supplies, medicines, and rescue personnel to affected regions.
  5. Global Connectivity and Foreign Exchange: Airways, major seaports, and modern digital telecommunications connect India to the global economic grid, generating billions in international trade and tourism revenue.

Q3
Write a note on the changing nature of international trade in India in the last fifteen years.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The nature of India’s international trade has undergone profound transformations over the last fifteen years:

  1. Exchange of Commodities Replaced by Knowledge and Services: While exchange of traditional bulk commodities (iron ore, agricultural goods) continues, trade in information, software, and knowledge services has surged ahead. India has emerged as a global software superpower, earning massive foreign exchange through the export of information technology, software, and Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) services.
  2. Shifting Commodity Composition:
    • Major Exports: Petroleum products (from domestic refining), gems and jewellery, engineering goods, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, and electronic goods have become dominant, replacing raw agricultural produce.
    • Major Imports: Crude petroleum and petroleum products continue to be the largest import item, followed by electronic goods, machinery, gold, and precious stones.
  3. Diversification of Trade Partners: India's trade is no longer dependent only on Britain or the US. It has expanded extensively with ASEAN countries, China, Japan, Middle East, Africa, and Latin America.
  4. Boom in Tourism as an Invisible Export: International tourism has grown by leaps and bounds. Foreign tourist arrivals contribute over Rs 1 lakh crore in foreign exchange annually. Tourism supports over 15 million people directly and promotes national integration, cultural heritage, and traditional handicrafts.
  5. Balance of Trade Dynamics: India maintains a trade deficit in merchandise due to heavy crude oil import bills, but compensates significantly through huge surpluses in service exports and foreign remittances.

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'Pipelines are a new arrival on the transportation map of India. In the past, these were used to transport water to cities and industries. Now, these are used for transporting crude oil, petroleum products and natural gas from oil and natural gas fields to refineries, fertilizer factories and big thermal power plants. Gas from the Hazira-Vijaipur-Jagdishpur (HVJ) pipeline has fueled fertilizer plants in Uttar Pradesh.'

Question: What are the initial and ongoing advantages and disadvantages of pipeline transport in India?

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Merits: Initial cost of laying pipelines is high, but subsequent running costs are minimal. It rules out trans-shipment losses or transport delays caused by road congestion or rail strikes. Can be laid across difficult terrain and underwater.
  • Demerit: Pipelines are inflexible (capacity cannot be expanded easily once laid), and underground leakages or ruptures are difficult to detect and repair immediately, posing environmental contamination risks.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): Golden Quadrilateral Super Highways connect Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai, and Mumbai by six-lane super highways.
Reason (R): The major objective of these Super Highway projects is to reduce time and distance between the mega cities of India, implemented by the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI).

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: The Golden Quadrilateral (along with North-South Corridor linking Srinagar to Kanyakumari and East-West Corridor linking Silchar to Porbandar) was constructed under the National Highways Development Project (NHDP) to speed up freight movement between economic powerhouses.
