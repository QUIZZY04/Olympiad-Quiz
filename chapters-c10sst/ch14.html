Democratic Politics – II (Civics)

Federalism

Unit 14: Civics • Federal vs Unitary Systems, Decentralisation & Centre-State Relations — CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27

NCERT In-Text & Exercise Questions — Board Examination Solutions
Q1
What is the difference between a federal form of government and a unitary one? Explain with examples.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Distinction between a Federal Form of Government and a Unitary One:

Feature Federal Government Unitary Government
Levels of Government There are two or more tiers of government (e.g., Union Government and State Governments). Either there is only one level of government or the sub-units are subordinate to the central government.
Constitutional Autonomy Different tiers govern the same citizens, but each tier has its own jurisdiction in specific matters of legislation, taxation, and administration guaranteed by the Constitution. The central government can pass on orders to the provincial or the local government; sub-units possess no independent constitutional authority.
Constitutional Amendment Fundamental provisions of the constitution cannot be unilaterally changed by one level of government; requires consent of both levels. The central legislature can amend the constitution unilaterally at will.
Role of Courts Courts have the power to interpret the constitution and the powers of different levels of government. The highest court acts as an umpire in disputes. Judiciary does not adjudicate inter-governmental federal division of powers.
Examples India, USA, Canada, Australia, Switzerland, Belgium. United Kingdom, France, China, Japan, Sri Lanka.

Levels of government comparison (Two or more tiers vs single tier)1.25 Marks
Constitutional jurisdiction and autonomy comparison1.25 Marks
Rigidity of constitutional amendments in federations1.25 Marks
Role of Supreme Court as constitutional umpire and global examples1.25 Marks
Q2
State the differences between 'coming together' federations and 'holding together' federations with examples.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Differences between 'Coming Together' Federations and 'Holding Together' Federations:

Feature 'Coming Together' Federations 'Holding Together' Federations
Formation Process Independent sovereign states come together on their own to form a bigger unit, pooling sovereignty and retaining identity to increase their security. A large country decides to divide its power between the constituent states and the national government to accommodate regional diversity.
Power Distribution All the constituent states usually have equal power and are strong vis-à-vis the federal government. The central government tends to be more powerful vis-à-vis the states. Often different constituent units have unequal powers (some granted special constitutional status).
Examples USA, Switzerland, and Australia. India, Spain, and Belgium.

Formation mechanism contrast (Independent states pooling vs large nation dividing)2 Marks
Power parity between states and center contrasted2 Marks
Standard country examples for both categories1 Mark
Q3
How is federalism practised in India? Explain the three major policies.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Federalism is successfully practiced in India not merely because of constitutional provisions, but because of the nature of democratic politics in our country:

  1. Creation of Linguistic States:
    • In 1947, the boundaries of several old states of India were changed in order to create new states. This was done to ensure that people who spoke the same language lived in the same state (e.g., Andhra Pradesh, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu).
    • Some states were created not on the basis of language but to recognize differences based on culture, ethnicity, or geography (e.g., Nagaland, Uttarakhand, and Jharkhand). Linguistic reorganisation made India more united and administration remarkably easier.
  2. Language Policy:
    • Our Constitution did not give the status of national language to any one language. Hindi was identified as the official language, but Hindi is the mother tongue of only about 44 per cent of Indians. Therefore, there are 22 languages recognized as Scheduled Languages by the Constitution.
    • States have their own official languages. The promotion of Hindi was accompanied by the continued use of English for official purposes, avoiding the linguistic conflict that plagued Sri Lanka.
  3. Restructuring Centre-State Relations and Coalition Era:
    • For a long time, the same party ruled both at the Centre and in most of the states. In those days, the Central Government would often misuse Article 356 to dismiss state governments controlled by rival parties.
    • All this changed significantly after 1990, with the rise of regional political parties and the beginning of the era of coalition governments at the Centre. Since no single party got a clear majority in the Lok Sabha, major national parties entered into alliances with regional parties, leading to a new culture of power sharing and respect for the autonomy of State Governments, supported by landmark Supreme Court judgments.

Q4
State any four differences between the local government bodies before and after the Constitutional Amendment in 1992.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

A major step towards decentralisation was taken in 1992 through the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments. The status of local government changed fundamentally:

Feature Local Government Before 1992 Local Government After 1992 (Constitutional Status)
Conduct of Elections Elections were not held regularly; controlled at the arbitrary whim of the state government. It is now constitutionally mandatory to hold regular elections to local government bodies every five years.
Election Management Managed under general state administration without an independent election authority. An independent institution called the State Election Commission was created in each state to conduct panchayat and municipal elections.
Reservation for Marginalised No statutory reservation existed for SCs, STs, and OBCs. Seats are reserved in the elected bodies and the executive heads of these institutions for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Other Backward Classes.
Women’s Representation Negligible female representation in village decision-making. At least one-third (33%) of all positions are reserved for women.
Revenue and Powers Local bodies had no powers or independent revenue resources of their own; completely dependent on state doles. State governments are required to share some powers and revenue with local government bodies, supported by State Finance Commissions.

Mandatory regular elections every 5 years vs irregular elections1.25 Marks
Creation of independent State Election Commission1.25 Marks
Statutory reservations for SC, ST, OBC and 1/3rd for women1.25 Marks
Constitutional sharing of powers and revenue with local bodies1.25 Marks
Q7
Point out one feature in the practice of federalism in India that is similar to and one feature that is different from that of Belgium.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Similar Feature (Power Sharing Between Central and Regional Governments):

  • In both India and Belgium, governmental power is divided constitutionally between a general Central (Union) Government and regional/provincial State Governments.
  • State governments are not subordinate to the central government and exercise autonomous constitutional powers in their allotted legislative jurisdictions.

2. Different Feature (Community Government vs Constitutional Linguistic Protection):

  • Belgium has a unique third tier called the 'Community Government' elected by people belonging to one language community (Dutch, French, or German), regardless of where they reside, holding exclusive power regarding cultural, educational, and language issues.
  • India does not have community governments based on language; instead, India has a third tier of democratic local self-government (Panchayats and Municipalities) based strictly on geographical territories.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Similarity: Autonomous constitutional federal division of powers1.5 Marks
Difference: Community Government in Belgium vs Territorial Local Panchayats in India1.5 Marks
Q8
Describe the three-fold distribution of legislative powers between the Union Government and the State Governments under the Constitution of India.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The Constitution of India provides a three-fold distribution of legislative subjects in the Seventh Schedule:

  1. Union List (National Importance):
    • Includes subjects of national importance where uniform policy is required nationwide.
    • The Union Government alone can make laws on these subjects.
    • Examples: Defence, Foreign Affairs, Banking, Communications, and Currency.
  2. State List (State and Local Importance):
    • Includes subjects of local and regional importance.
    • The State Governments alone have exclusive authority to pass legislation.
    • Examples: Police, Trade and Commerce, Agriculture, Irrigation, and Public Health.
  3. Concurrent List (Common Interest):
    • Includes subjects of common interest to both the Union and the States.
    • Both Parliament and State Legislatures can pass laws. In case of a conflict, the Union law prevails.
    • Examples: Education, Forests, Trade Unions, Marriage, Adoption, and Succession.
  4. Residuary Powers: Matters not present in any list (e.g., Computer software, Cyber laws) belong exclusively to the Union Parliament.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Union List: National subjects (defence, banking, currency) legislated by Centre1 Mark
State List: Regional subjects (police, agriculture, trade) legislated by States1 Mark
Concurrent List: Shared subjects (education, forests) with Union supremacy in conflicts1 Mark
Residuary powers vested in the Union Parliament (cyber laws)1 Mark
Q9
What were the major constitutional steps taken in 1992 towards decentralisation and empowering local self-governments in India?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts of 1992 made third-tier local self-government in India substantially more powerful and effective:

  1. Mandatory Periodic Elections: It was made constitutionally mandatory to hold regular elections to local government bodies (Panchayats and Municipalities) every five years.
  2. Reservation for SCs, STs, and OBCs: Seats are reserved in the elected bodies and the executive heads of these institutions for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Other Backward Classes.
  3. One-Third Reservation for Women: At least one-third (33%) of all positions and seats are constitutionally reserved for women, fostering grassroots female leadership.
  4. Creation of State Election Commission: An independent institution called the State Election Commission was created in each state to conduct panchayat and municipal elections.
  5. Mandatory Sharing of State Revenue: State governments are constitutionally required to share some powers and revenue with local government bodies through the State Finance Commission.

Mandatory regular 5-year elections to local bodies1 Mark

🎯 Competency & Case-Based Questions — Unit 14CBSE Board 2026-27
Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'The Constitution clearly provided a threefold distribution of legislative powers between the Union Government and the State Governments. Thus, it contains three lists: Union List, State List, and Concurrent List. Subjects that do not fall in any of the three lists are known as 'Residuary Subjects'.'

Question: Name two subjects from each of the three lists and identify who makes laws on 'Residuary Subjects'.

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Union List: Defense of the country, Foreign affairs, Banking, Communications, and Currency (Union Parliament alone can legislate).
  • State List: Police, Trade, Commerce, Agriculture, and Irrigation (State Legislature alone can legislate).
  • Concurrent List: Education, Forests, Trade Unions, Marriage, Adoption, and Succession (Both Centre and States can legislate; Centre's law prevails in case of conflict).
  • Residuary Subjects: Computer software, e-commerce, and space research (According to the Constitution, the Union Government has the power to legislate on residuary subjects).
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): India is a 'holding together' federation.
Reason (R): In India, the central government is constitutionally more powerful vis-à-vis the states, and some states like Jammu & Kashmir, Nagaland, and Mizoram have enjoyed special provisions under Article 371 due to historical and cultural reasons.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: Unlike 'coming together' federations (like the US) where constituent states have equal power, India decided to divide its powers between the national government and states to maintain unity amid immense linguistic and cultural diversity.
