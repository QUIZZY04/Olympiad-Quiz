Q1
Explain the difference between primary, secondary and tertiary sectors using examples.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Primary Sector:

  • Activities undertaken by directly using natural resources. It forms the base for all other products that we subsequently make. Since most of the natural products we get are from agriculture, dairy, fishing, and forestry, this sector is also called the agriculture and related sector.
  • Examples: Cultivation of cotton, dairy farming, extraction of mineral ores, fishing.

2. Secondary Sector:

  • Covers activities in which natural products are changed into other forms through ways of manufacturing that we associate with industrial activity. The product is not produced by nature but has to be made. It is also known as the industrial sector.
  • Examples: Spinning yarn and weaving cloth from cotton fiber, making sugar or gur from sugarcane, manufacturing bricks from earth, producing steel from iron ore.

3. Tertiary Sector:

  • These activities help in the development of the primary and secondary sectors. These activities, by themselves, do not produce a good, but they are an aid or support for the production process. Since these activities generate services rather than physical goods, the tertiary sector is also called the service sector.
  • Examples: Transport (trucks, trains), storage, banking, insurance, trade, telecommunications, healthcare, and education.

Q2
Why is the tertiary sector becoming so important in India? Give four reasons.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Over the last forty years, the tertiary (service) sector has emerged as the largest producing sector in India, replacing the primary sector, due to four major reasons:

  1. Demand for Basic Public Services: In any country, several services such as hospitals, educational institutions, post and telegraph services, police stations, courts, defense, transport, banks, and municipal administration are required. These are regarded as basic services. In a developing country, the government has to take responsibility for the provision of these services.
  2. Development of Agriculture and Industry: The development of agriculture and industry directly stimulates the demand for services such as transport, trade, storage, marketing, and commercial banking. Greater the agricultural and industrial production, greater the demand for such support services.
  3. Rising Income Levels and Consumer Spending: As income levels rise, sections of people start demanding many more services like dining out in restaurants, tourism, shopping, private hospitals, private schools, and professional training centers, especially in cities.
  4. Information and Communication Technology (ICT) Revolution: Over the past decade, certain new services such as those based on information and communication technology, software development, BPOs, and digital data handling have become essential and grown rapidly.

Q3
What is disguised unemployment? Explain with an example each from rural and urban areas.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Meaning of Disguised Unemployment:

Disguised unemployment (also called underemployment) is a situation where more people are engaged in an activity than are actually required. Each person appears to be working, but their potential is not fully utilised. If some people are removed, total production does not fall at all; their marginal productivity is zero.

2. Example from Rural Areas:

  • Take the case of a small farmer, Laxmi, owning a two-hectare plot of unirrigated land. All five members of her family work on the plot throughout the year. They have nowhere else to go for work.
  • Each one is doing some work, but no one is fully employed. If two members of the family move out to work on a nearby construction project or a landlord's field to earn extra wages, the output from Laxmi's farm does not decline. The three remaining members can cultivate the plot just as effectively. The two extra members were disguisedly unemployed.

3. Example from Urban Areas:

  • In urban areas, disguised unemployment is prevalent in the informal service sector. Thousands of casual workers on the streets — painters, plumbers, repair persons, and cart-pullers — spend the whole day on the road, but earn very little.
  • Similarly, in small family-run retail grocery shops, four family members sit in the shop where only two are needed to serve customers. They work because they lack alternative gainful employment opportunities.

Q7
Distinguish between Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary sectors of economic activities with two suitable examples of each.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Economic activities are grouped into three fundamental sectors:

  1. Primary Sector (Extraction of Natural Resources):
    • Involves activities undertaken by directly exploiting natural resources.
    • It forms the base for all other products that we subsequently make. (Also known as Agriculture and related sector).
    • Examples: Farming (cotton, wheat), dairy farming, fishing, forestry, and mining.
  2. Secondary Sector (Manufacturing & Processing):
    • Covers activities in which natural products are changed into other forms through ways of manufacturing that we associate with industrial activity.
    • The product is not produced by nature but has to be made. (Also known as Industrial sector).
    • Examples: Spinning cotton fibre into yarn and weaving cloth, converting sugarcane into sugar or jaggery, manufacturing steel from iron ore.
  3. Tertiary Sector (Support and Services):
    • These activities do not produce a physical good by themselves, but they are an aid or support for the production process. (Also known as Service sector).
    • Examples: Transport (trucks, trains), banking, storage, communication, healthcare, and software consultancy.

Q8
Explain the key objectives and features of the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) 2005.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) 2005 is a landmark social security legislation in India:

  1. Guaranteed 100 Days of Wage Employment: It guarantees at least 100 days of guaranteed wage employment in a financial year to every rural household whose adult members volunteer to do unskilled manual work.
  2. Right to Work as Legal Entitlement: Implements the constitutional 'Right to Work'. If the government fails to provide employment within 15 days of application, the applicant is legally entitled to a daily unemployment allowance.
  3. Priority to Sustainable Rural Works: The types of work that are given preference are aimed at addressing the causes of chronic poverty, such as drought relief, desilting ponds, soil conservation, afforestation, and rural connectivity.
  4. Gender Empowerment: One-third of all proposed jobs are constitutionally reserved for women, ensuring gender parity in wages and rural economic independence.

Q9
What is disguised unemployment? Explain with an example each from the agricultural (rural) sector and the urban service sector.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Meaning of Disguised Unemployment (Underemployment):

Disguised unemployment is a situation where more people are engaged in an activity than are actually required. Even if several people are pulled out, the total production does not fall. People appear to be working, but their productive potential is underutilized.

1. Rural Agricultural Example (Laxmi's Plot):

  • Laxmi owns a 2-hectare plot of unirrigated land. All 5 members of her family work on the plot throughout the year because they have nowhere else to go for work.
  • Each does some work, but no one is fully employed. If 2 members of the family move to work on a commercial farm or factory in town, the agricultural output from Laxmi's plot remains exactly the same. Those 2 extra workers were in a state of disguised unemployment.

2. Urban Service Sector Example:

  • Thousands of casual daily-wage workers in cities—such as painters, plumbers, repair persons, street vendors pushing handcarts, and rickshaw pullers.
  • They may spend the whole day roaming the streets, but earn very little because other workers are competing for the same limited customers. They are working because they have no alternative opportunities.

Q10
Why has the Tertiary Sector become the largest producing sector in India over the last forty years? Give four reasons.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Over the last forty years, the tertiary sector has replaced agriculture as the largest producing sector in India due to:

  1. Provision of Basic Services: In any developing country, several basic services are required—hospitals, educational institutions, post and telegraph services, police stations, courts, municipal corporations, defense, transport, and banks. The government has to take responsibility for providing these basic services.
  2. Development of Agriculture and Industry: The expansion of primary and secondary production naturally requires more transport, storage, marketing, credit, and wholesale/retail trade. The greater the development of agriculture and industry, the higher the demand for such auxiliary services.
  3. Rise in Income Levels: As income levels rise, sections of people start demanding many more luxury and discretionary services like eating out, tourism, shopping malls, private hospitals, private schools, and professional coaching centres.
  4. Boom in Information and Communication Technology (ICT): Over the past two decades, certain new services such as those based on information and communication technology (software, BPOs, telecom, call centres) have become essential and have grown rapidly.

Q11
Distinguish between the Organised Sector and the Unorganised Sector on the basis of terms of employment, job security, and working conditions.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Comparison between Organised and Unorganised Sectors:

Feature Organised Sector Unorganised Sector
1. Registration & Rules Registered by the government and has to follow its laws (Factories Act, Minimum Wages Act, Gratuity Act). Largely outside the control of the government; rules and regulations exist but are not enforced.
2. Security of Employment Workers enjoy security of employment; cannot be dismissed without formal procedures and due notice. Jobs are insecure and precarious; workers can be asked to leave at any time without reason.

4. Social Security Benefits Entitled to paid leave, payment during holidays, provident fund, medical benefits, and pension on retirement. No paid leave, no sick leave, no medical care benefits, and no retirement pension.
5. Examples Government civil servants, bank employees, registered factory workers. Daily wage construction labourers, small landless agricultural labourers, domestic maids.

Q12
How are economic activities classified on the basis of ownership into Public and Private sectors? Explain with two examples of each.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Economic activities are classified into Public and Private sectors based on who owns assets and delivers services:

  1. Public Sector:
    • Ownership & Control: The government owns most of the assets and provides all the services.
    • Primary Objective: The purpose of the public sector is not merely to earn profits, but to provide essential public goods and promote social welfare. Governments raise money through taxes to meet expenses.
    • Examples: Indian Railways, Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited (BHEL), Post Office, State Bank of India.
  2. Private Sector:
    • Ownership & Control: Ownership of assets and delivery of services is in the hands of private individuals or companies.
    • Primary Objective: Activities in the private sector are guided strictly by the motive to earn profit. To get services, consumers have to pay money to these private entities.
    • Examples: Tata Iron and Steel Company (TISCO), Reliance Industries Limited (RIL), Infosys, HDFC Bank.

Q13
Suggest any four effective ways to create more employment opportunities in rural India.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Effective strategies to generate substantial employment in rural India include:

  1. Investment in Irrigation Infrastructure: Constructing canals, dams, and digging community tubewells allows farmers to cultivate a second and third crop (e.g., wheat or vegetables after kharif rice), generating direct agricultural employment for multiple family members throughout the year.
  2. Improving Rural Transport and Storage: Investing in all-weather rural roads and cold storage facilities enables farmers to sell produce in nearby towns, creating non-farm jobs in transportation (truck drivers, mechanics), logistics, and wholesale marketing.
  3. Promoting Agro-Processing Industries in Semi-Rural Belts: Setting up small-scale processing units—such as dal mills, cold-pressed oil mills, honey collection centres, and potato chips/tomato processing plants—absorbs surplus rural labour.
  4. Provision of Cheap Formal Credit: Providing accessible low-interest loans through cooperative banks allows farmers to buy seeds, farm machinery, dairy cows, and start cottage enterprises (handlooms, pottery, poultry).

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'Under MGNREGA 2005 (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act), all those who are able to, and are in need of, work in rural areas are guaranteed 100 days of employment in a year by the government. If the government fails in its duty to provide employment, it will give unemployment allowances to the people.'

Question: How does MGNREGA 2005 help in creating durable rural assets while providing economic security?

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Statutory Right to Work: Guarantees 100 days of wage employment per rural household, preventing distress rural-to-urban migration during agricultural lean seasons.
  • Durable Asset Creation: The work projects undertaken (pond deepening, canal excavation, check-dam construction, tree planting) directly conserve soil and water, drought-proofing the village and boosting agricultural productivity in subsequent seasons.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): In India, the primary sector continues to be the largest employer, even though its share in GDP has shrunk drastically.
Reason (R): Secondary and tertiary sectors failed to create enough jobs to absorb the surplus agricultural labour force.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: While industrial output grew significantly, secondary and tertiary sector employment did not expand at the same pace. Consequently, nearly 44% of Indian workers remain stuck in agriculture producing less than 18% of GDP, resulting in severe disguised unemployment.
