Economics: Understanding Economic Development

Money and Credit

Unit 20: Economics • Medium of Exchange, Formal vs Informal Credit, Terms of Credit & SHGs — CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27

NCERT In-Text & Exercise Questions — Board Examination Solutions
Q1
How does money solve the problem of double coincidence of wants? Explain with an example of your own.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Meaning of Double Coincidence of Wants:

In a barter system where goods are directly exchanged without the use of money, double coincidence of wants is an essential condition: what a person desires to sell must be exactly what the other person wishes to buy. Both parties have to agree to sell and buy each other’s commodities.

2. How Money Solves the Problem:

  • Money eliminates the cumbersome need for double coincidence of wants by acting as an intermediate in the exchange process, known as a medium of exchange.
  • Original Example: Suppose a shoe manufacturer wants to sell shoes in the market and buy wheat. Under a barter system, the shoe manufacturer would have to find a wheat-growing farmer who not only wants to sell wheat, but also simultaneously wants to buy shoes. Finding such an exact match is extremely difficult.
  • With money, the shoe manufacturer simply sells shoes for rupees in the open market and then uses that money to purchase wheat from any farmer or grocer selling wheat. The transaction is split into two independent, effortless steps.

Q2
In situations with high risks, credit might create further problems for the borrower. Explain with the help of an example.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In high-risk situations (particularly small-scale crop cultivation), credit can push the borrower into a devastating debt trap instead of improving their earnings:

  1. Case Analysis of a Small Farmer (Swapna):
    • Swapna, a small farmer, borrows money from a local moneylender at high interest rates to meet the expenses of cultivating groundnuts on her 3 acres of land, hoping that her harvest would repay the loan.
    • Midway through the season, pests attack the crop and the crop fails despite spraying expensive pesticides. She cannot repay the moneylender, and the debt grows over the year into a large amount.
    • Next year, she takes a fresh loan for cultivation. It is a normal crop this year, but the earnings are not enough to cover the old debt plus the new loan.
    • She is caught in a debt trap. To clear the loan, she is forced to sell off a portion of her valuable ancestral farmland.
  2. Structural Vulnerability: In crop production, the risk of failure is high due to unpredictable monsoon rains, pest attacks, or price crashes in the mandi. If crops fail, credit turns from an instrument of growth into an instrument of financial ruin, leaving the borrower in a far worse situation than before.

Q3
In what ways does the Reserve Bank of India supervise the functioning of banks? Why is this necessary?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) plays a crucial supervisory role over the formal banking sector in India:

  1. Maintenance of Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR): The RBI monitors that commercial banks actually keep a minimum cash balance out of the deposits they receive. In India, banks currently maintain about 4.5% to 15% of their deposits as cash reserve to cater to daily withdrawal demands of depositors.
  2. Ensuring Credit Delivery to Priority Sectors: The RBI sees that banks give loans not just to profit-making businesses, large traders, and corporate houses, but also to small cultivators, small-scale industries, small borrowers, and SHGs. This is enforced through Priority Sector Lending (PSL) norms.
  3. Monitoring Interest Rates and Terms: Periodically, banks have to submit information to the RBI on how much they are lending, to whom, and at what interest rates, ensuring that commercial banks do not exploit vulnerable citizens with usurious terms.
  4. Why Supervision is Indispensable:
    • Without RBI supervision, commercial banks would operate solely for private profit maximization, lending exclusively to rich corporate entities in cities and neglecting poor rural farmers.
    • It prevents bank failures, safeguards public deposits, maintains confidence in the financial system, and promotes balanced regional development.

Q4
What is the basic idea behind the Self-Help Groups (SHGs) for the poor? Explain in your own words.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Self-Help Groups (SHGs) have emerged as an extraordinary institutional innovation to empower poor women and overcome the barriers of formal bank credit:

  1. Basic Structure: A typical SHG consists of 15 to 20 members, usually belonging to one neighbourhood, who meet and save money regularly. Saving per member varies from ₹25 to ₹100 or more, depending on the ability to save.
  2. Small Internal Loans without Collateral: Members can take small loans from the group itself to meet their immediate emergency needs (buying seeds, fertilizers, school fees, releasing mortgaged land). The group charges interest on these loans, but this is far lower than what informal moneylenders charge.
  3. Bank Linkage and Collective Guarantee: After a year or two, if the group is regular in savings, it becomes eligible for availing bank loans. The loan is sanctioned in the name of the group to create self-employment opportunities (buying sewing machines, cattle, handlooms). The formal bank lends to poor women without individual collateral because the entire SHG provides collective guarantee; any non-repayment is followed up rigorously by other members.
  4. Financial Independence for Women: It frees poor rural women from the clutches of predatory moneylenders, enabling them to build assets and generate steady livelihoods.
  5. Platform for Social Empowerment: The regular meetings of the SHG provide an active platform to discuss and act on a variety of social issues like health, nutrition, child marriage, and domestic violence.

Q7
Why is modern currency accepted as a medium of exchange although it is made of paper and has no intrinsic value of its own?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Modern currency (paper notes and coins) has no intrinsic value like gold, silver, or commodity cattle, but is universally accepted as a medium of exchange because:

  1. Authorized by Sovereign Government: Modern currency is officially authorized by the government of the country. In India, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) issues currency notes on behalf of the Central Government.
  2. Legal Tender: Under the law, no other individual or organisation is allowed to issue currency. The Indian law legally permits the use of the rupee as a medium of payment that cannot be refused in settling transactions anywhere in India.
  3. Universal Trust: People have absolute trust that the issuing sovereign state guarantees the value printed on the note ('I promise to pay the bearer the sum of...').
  4. Elimination of Barter Inefficiencies: It completely eliminates the burdensome requirement of double coincidence of wants associated with barter trade, providing an efficient, standardized medium of transaction.

Authorization by sovereign Central Government and Reserve Bank of India1.5 Marks
Status as Legal Tender that cannot be lawfully refused in transactions1.5 Marks
Sovereign backing and elimination of double coincidence of wants1 Mark
Q8
What are the 'terms of credit'? Describe the four essential components that constitute terms of credit.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Meaning of Terms of Credit:

Every loan agreement specifies terms and conditions that the borrower must satisfy. The interest rate, collateral and documentation requirement, and the mode of repayment together comprise what is called the terms of credit.

Four Essential Components:

  1. Interest Rate: The percentage rate specified by the lender which the borrower must pay along with the repayment of the principal amount.
  2. Collateral (Security): An asset that the borrower owns (such as land, house, livestock, bank deposits) and pledges to the lender as a guarantee until the loan is fully repaid. If the borrower defaults, the lender has the legal right to sell the collateral to recover payment.
  3. Documentation Requirement: Documents verifying the borrower's identity, employment record, residence, income proof, and title deeds of assets before sanctioning the loan.
  4. Mode of Repayment: The agreed schedule, duration, and method by which the borrower repays the loan (e.g., monthly cash instalments, automated bank deductions).

Q9
Why is cheap and affordable credit crucial for a country's overall economic development?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Cheap and affordable institutional credit is an indispensable driver of economic development because:

  1. Enables Productive Business Investments: Cheap credit enables farmers to purchase high-yielding seeds, fertilizers, and irrigation equipment, and entrepreneurs to establish factories, procure raw materials, and adopt new technologies.
  2. Prevents the Debt Trap: High-interest loans from informal moneylenders mean that a large part of the borrower's earnings is used merely to pay interest, leaving little for investment and often pushing them into an inescapable cycle of debt (the debt trap).
  3. Encourages Small-Scale Enterprises and Self-Employment: Accessible credit empowers small artisans, cottage industries, and young entrepreneurs to start small businesses, generating widespread local employment.
  4. Reduces Poverty and Promotes Inclusive Growth: When poor households obtain affordable loans, their disposable income increases, allowing them to educate their children, improve nutrition, and escape poverty.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Stimulates agricultural, industrial and infrastructural investment1 Mark
Protects borrowers from exploitative informal debt traps1 Mark
Fosters grassroots micro-enterprises and employment generation1 Mark
Promotes inclusive growth by uplifting underprivileged socioeconomic strata1 Mark
Q10
Compare formal sources of credit with informal sources of credit in India with respect to interest rates, supervision, and collateral requirements.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Comparison between Formal and Informal Sources of Credit:

Feature Formal Sources (Banks, Cooperatives) Informal Sources (Moneylenders, Traders)
1. Supervising Authority The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) strictly supervises their functioning, cash reserves, and lending priorities. There is no organisation that supervises the credit activities of lenders in the informal sector.
2. Rate of Interest Charge reasonable and standardized interest rates regulated by the RBI. Charge exorbitantly high interest rates (often 36% to 60% per annum or higher).
3. Collateral & Paperwork Strict collateral (security) and formal documentation are mandatory prerequisites. Usually lend without formal collateral since moneylenders know borrowers personally.
4. Recovery Methods Follow lawful, fair, and transparent legal recovery procedures. Often use coercive, unfair, and violent means to recover their money.
5. Objective Social welfare along with banking viability; required to lend to priority agricultural sectors. Maximising personal profit and exploiting the helplessness of vulnerable borrowers.

Q11
Why are rural poor households in India still largely dependent on informal sources of credit? Mention four reasons.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Poor rural households continue to borrow heavily from informal moneylenders despite extortionate rates due to:

  1. Absence of Bank Branches in Rural Areas: Bank branches are not present everywhere in rural India. Even where they are present, getting a loan from a bank is much more difficult than taking a loan from informal sources.
  2. Lack of Collateral (Asset Security): Bank loans require proper collateral (land titles, house deeds) and extensive documentation. Poor households lack physical assets to pledge, disqualifying them from bank credit.
  3. Cumbersome Documentation Procedures: Illiterate and semi-literate villagers find the paperwork, application forms, and bureaucratic verification process of commercial banks intimidating and difficult to navigate.
  4. Flexibility of Moneylenders: The informal moneylenders know the borrowers personally and are frequently willing to give a loan without collateral. They also accommodate delays in repayment and advance fresh loans even before earlier debts are cleared.

Q12
What is the basic idea behind Self Help Groups (SHGs) for the poor? Explain the functioning and advantages of SHGs.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Basic Idea of Self Help Groups (SHGs):

The basic idea is to organize the rural poor, especially women, into small groups, pool their savings, and build collective financial self-reliance without requiring individual collateral.

Functioning of an SHG:

  1. Membership & Regular Savings: A typical SHG consists of 15 to 20 members, usually belonging to one neighbourhood, who meet and save regularly. Saving per member varies from ₹ 25 to ₹ 100 or more, depending on ability.
  2. Internal Lending: Members can take small loans from the group itself to meet urgent needs (e.g., buying seeds, fertilizers, school fees, releasing mortgaged land) at very reasonable interest rates decided by the group.
  3. Bank Linkage: After a year or two of regular savings, the group becomes eligible for availing loans directly from commercial banks in the group's name without individual collateral.

Key Advantages:

  • Overcomes Collateral Problem: The bank lends to the group as a whole; all members are collectively responsible for timely repayment, eliminating the collateral barrier.
  • Women Empowerment: Helps rural women become financially self-reliant and provides a collective platform to discuss social issues like health, nutrition, and domestic violence.

Q13
Describe the role and functions of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) in supervising formal sources of credit.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) performs critical supervisory functions over formal commercial banks:

  1. Monitoring Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR): The RBI monitors that commercial banks actually maintain the minimum statutory cash balance (currently around 4.5% of total deposits) out of deposits to satisfy daily cash withdrawal demands of depositors.
  2. Ensuring Credit to Priority Sectors: The RBI ensures that banks do not give loans only to profit-making big businesses and wealthy traders, but also mandate lending to small cultivators, small-scale industries, small borrowers, and SHGs (Priority Sector Lending).
  3. Periodic Reporting and Scrutiny: Periodically, banks have to submit detailed information to the RBI on how much they are lending, to whom, and at what interest rates.
  4. Regulating Interest Rates and Preventing Usury: Sets benchmark policy rates (Repo Rate) to keep inflation and bank interest rates balanced, preventing unfair lending practices.

Monitoring statutory minimum Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)1 Mark
Enforcing Priority Sector Lending to small farmers and micro-enterprises1 Mark

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'Every loan agreement specifies an interest rate which the borrower must pay to the lender along with the repayment of the principal. In addition, lenders may demand collateral (security) against loans. Collateral is an asset that the borrower owns (such as land, building, vehicle, livestock, deposits with banks) and uses this as a guarantee to a lender until the loan is repaid.'

Question: Why do formal commercial banks insist on collateral before granting loans, and how does this requirement exclude poor borrowers?

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Risk Mitigation for Banks: Collateral serves as a legal guarantee; if the borrower defaults, the lender has the right to sell the asset to recover loan dues.
  • Exclusion of the Poor: Poor rural labourers and small artisans lack land deeds, permanent houses, or bank deposits to pledge as collateral. Consequently, formal banks refuse them loans, forcing them to borrow from exploitative local moneylenders at exorbitant interest rates (36%–60% per annum).
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): Cheap and affordable credit is crucial for a country's economic development.
Reason (R): High interest rates charged by informal lenders mean that a large part of the earnings of the borrowers is spent to repay the loan, leaving them with little income for reinvestment and growth.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: When farmers and small entrepreneurs have access to low-interest formal loans, they can invest in seeds, machinery, and business expansion, generating profits, higher employment, and accelerated national economic development.
