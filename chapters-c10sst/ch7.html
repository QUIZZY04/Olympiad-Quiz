Q1
Answer the following in about 30 words: (i) What is biodiversity? Why is biodiversity important for human lives? (ii) How have human activities affected the depletion of flora and fauna? Explain.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

(i) What is Biodiversity and Why is it Important for Human Lives?

  • Definition: Biodiversity (biological diversity) refers to the immensely rich variety of living organisms in an area, diverse in form and function, but closely integrated in a system through multiple networks of interdependencies.
  • Importance to Human Lives:
    • Humans depend fundamentally on ecological systems for survival: plants, animals, and micro-organisms recreate the air we breathe, the water we drink, and the soil that produces our food.
    • Forests are the primary producers on which all other living beings depend; they provide medicinal plants, timber, resin, fuel, and regulate the global hydrological and carbon cycles.

(ii) How Human Activities Have Affected the Depletion of Flora and Fauna:

  1. Agricultural Expansion: Between 1951 and 1980, according to the Forest Survey of India, over 26,200 sq. km. of forest area was converted into agricultural land all over India. Substantial parts of tribal belts, especially in the north-eastern and central India, have been deforested by shifting cultivation (jhumming).
  2. Large-scale River Valley Projects: Since 1951, over 5,000 sq. km. of forest was cleared for river valley projects (e.g., Narmada Sagar Project in Madhya Pradesh inundated 40,000 hectares of forest).
  3. Mining Activities: Mining causes severe deforestation. The Buxa Tiger Reserve in West Bengal is seriously threatened by ongoing dolomite mining.
  4. Habitat Destruction, Poaching and Hunting: Indiscriminate hunting of tigers, rhinos, and elephants for skins, horns, and tusks has pushed numerous species to the brink of extinction.
  5. Environmental Pollution and Forest Fires: Chemical poisoning, toxic agricultural runoff, and recurring forest fires destroy micro-habitats and decimate wildlife populations.

Q2
Describe how communities have conserved and protected forests and wildlife in India.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In India, traditional local communities have played an inspiring and heroic role in conserving forests and wildlife:

  1. Sariska Tiger Reserve, Rajasthan: In Sariska, villagers have fought against mining by citing the Wildlife Protection Act, successfully preventing commercial exploitation of the tiger sanctuary.
  2. Bhairodev Dakav 'Sonchuri' (Alwar, Rajasthan): The inhabitants of five villages in the Alwar district of Rajasthan have declared 1,200 hectares of forest as the Bhairodev Dakav 'Sonchuri', framing their own set of rules and regulations which do not allow hunting and protect the wildlife against any outside encroachments.
  3. The Famous Chipko Movement (Uttarakhand): The historic Chipko Movement in the Himalayas successfully resisted deforestation in several areas and demonstrated that community afforestation with indigenous species can be enormously successful.
  4. Beej Bachao Andolan and Navdanya: Farmers and citizen groups like the Beej Bachao Andolan in Tehri and Navdanya have shown that adequate levels of diversified crop production without the use of synthetic chemicals are possible and economically viable.
  5. Joint Forest Management (JFM) Programme: JFM furnishes a formal institutional framework where local village communities collaborate with state forest departments to protect degraded forests (first formal resolution passed by Odisha in 1988). In return, communities are entitled to intermediary benefits like non-timber forest produce and a share in the timber harvested.
  6. Sacred Groves (Bhairon, Deorais): Nature worship is an age-old tribal belief. Sacred groves preserved untouched by local communities are found in Meghalaya (Khasi and Jaintia Hills), Western Ghats, and Aravalli Hills of Rajasthan.

Q3
Write a detailed note on the classification of forests in India: Reserved, Protected, and Unclassed Forests.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Government Administrative Classification of Forests in India:

Forest Category Legal Status & Degree of Protection Distribution Across States
Reserved Forests Regarded as the most valuable as far as the conservation of forest and wildlife resources are concerned. More than half of the total forest land has been declared reserved. Strict prohibition on grazing, hunting, and timber felling. Jammu & Kashmir, Andhra Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal, and Maharashtra have large percentages of reserved forests.
Protected Forests Almost one-third of the total forest area is protected forest as declared by the Forest Department. This forest land is protected from any further depletion; local communities are often allowed limited grazing and firewood collection rights without causing damage. Bihar, Haryana, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Odisha, and Rajasthan have a bulk of their forests under protected category.
Unclassed Forests These are other forests and wastelands belonging to both government and private individuals and communities. Communities manage them locally with customary rights. All North-Eastern states and parts of Gujarat have a very high percentage of unclassed forests managed by local communities.

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'Project Tiger, one of the well-publicised wildlife campaigns in the world, was launched in 1973. Tiger population had dwindled to 1,827 from an estimated 55,000 at the turn of the century. The major threats to tiger population are numerous, such as poaching for trade, shrinking habitat, depletion of prey base species, and growing human population.'

Question: Why is Project Tiger viewed not just as an effort to save an endangered species, but as an exercise of great ecological magnitude?

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Apex Predator and Ecological Health: The tiger is an apex predator at the top of the food chain. Conserving the tiger necessitates preserving the entire ecosystem — protecting vast forest habitats, herbivores (deer, wild boar), flora, and water catchments.
  • Prominent Tiger Reserves: Corbett National Park (Uttarakhand), Sundarbans National Park (West Bengal), Bandhavgarh National Park (Madhya Pradesh), Sariska Wildlife Sanctuary (Rajasthan), Manas Tiger Reserve (Assam), and Periyar Tiger Reserve (Kerala) serve as crucial biodiversity repositories.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): The Himalayan Yew is a medicinal plant found in various parts of Himachal Pradesh and Arunachal Pradesh.
Reason (R): A chemical compound called 'taxol' is extracted from the bark, needles, twigs and roots of this tree, and it has been successfully used to treat cancer — the drug is now the biggest-selling anti-cancer drug in the world.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: Due to excessive commercial over-exploitation for taxol production over the last decade, thousands of yew trees have dried up in various parts of Himachal Pradesh and Arunachal Pradesh, putting the species under severe threat of extinction.
