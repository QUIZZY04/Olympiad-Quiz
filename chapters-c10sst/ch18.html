Q1
Why do different persons have different notions of development? Which of the following explanations is more important and why? Explain with examples how development for one may be destructive for another.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Different Persons can have Different Development Goals:

  • Different persons have different notions of development because people's life situations are different. People seek things that are most important for them, that which can fulfill their specific aspirations or desires.
  • Examples:
    • Landless Rural Labourer: More days of work and better wages; quality education for children in local government schools; no social discrimination.
    • Prosperous Farmer in Punjab: High family income through higher support prices for crops, hardworking and cheap migrant labour, and ability to settle children abroad.
    • An Adivasi in Narmada Valley: Protection of ancestral forest lands, no displacement by large dams, and preservation of community culture.

2. What may be Development for One may be Destructive for Another:

  • At times, two persons or groups may seek things which are conflicting. A girl expects as much freedom and opportunity as her brother, and that he also shares in the household work; her brother may not like this.
  • Similarly, industrialists may want more dams to get more electricity, but constructing large dams submerges the land and disrupts the lives of indigenous people and farmers whose lands are inundated. Hence, industrial development for one causes ecological destruction and displacement for another.

Q2
What are the non-material goals that people seek besides a higher income?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Besides income, people seek other non-material goals that are equally or more important for a good quality of life:

  1. Equal Treatment and Respect: People desire to be treated equally without discrimination based on caste, religion, or gender. Respect from others is fundamental to self-esteem and human dignity.
  2. Freedom and Liberty: Freedom to think, express oneself, practice one's faith, and make career and life choices without fear of persecution or arbitrary state harassment.
  3. Security of Life and Livelihood: A secure job that offers guaranteed income and safety in the workplace is often preferred over a higher-paying job with no job security and high physical danger.
  4. Clean and Healthy Environment: A pollution-free physical environment, access to unadulterated food, clean drinking water, and quality healthcare are vital determinants of well-being that money alone cannot buy.
  5. Social Harmony and Peace: Living in a peaceful community free from social conflict, crime, and violence allows families to thrive. Money cannot protect individuals from infectious epidemics or communal violence unless the whole community takes preventive steps.

Q3
What is the main criterion used by the World Bank in classifying different countries? What are the limitations of this criterion? How does the UNDP criterion differ from it?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Comparison of World Bank and UNDP Criteria for Measuring Development:

Feature World Bank Criteria (World Development Report) UNDP Criteria (Human Development Report)
Primary Indicator Relies exclusively on a single economic indicator: Per Capita Income (Average Income). Uses a comprehensive multi-dimensional index: Human Development Index (HDI).
Dimensions Covered Only monetary income (measured in US Dollars using Purchasing Power Parity). Combines three vital dimensions:
  1. Health: Life Expectancy at birth.
  2. Education: Mean and Expected years of schooling.
  3. Income: Per Capita Gross National Income (GNI).
Country Classification Divides countries into High Income (rich countries), Middle Income, and Low Income. Ranks all nations on a global index (0 to 1), categorizing them into Very High, High, Medium, and Low Human Development.
Limitations / Merits Limitation: It hides glaring economic inequalities. It tells us nothing about how income is distributed among citizens. Merit: It provides a holistic assessment of human capabilities and citizens' real living standards.

Q4
Why is the issue of sustainability important for development? Explain with examples of groundwater overuse and crude oil depletion.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The issue of sustainability of development is crucial for humankind because development that depletes the natural resource base today cannot be sustained for future generations:

  1. Overuse of Groundwater in India:
    • Recent evidence suggests that groundwater is under serious threat of overuse in many parts of the country. About 300 districts have reported a water level decline of over 4 metres during the past 20 years.
    • Nearly one-third of the country is over-pumping its groundwater reserves. In agricultural heartlands like Punjab, Haryana, and western UP, excessive tube-well pumping for cash crops has severely depleted aquifers. If current trends continue, 60 per cent of the country will be doing the same in the next 25 years, causing an unprecedented drinking water and agricultural crisis.
  2. Exhaustion of Global Crude Oil Reserves:
    • Crude oil is a non-renewable natural resource. According to global estimates, present reserves of crude oil will last only around 40 to 50 years if extracted at the current rate.
    • Countries like India, which depend on imports for over 85% of their crude oil, face immense economic vulnerability due to rising oil prices and geopolitical volatility.
  3. Inter-Generational Equity:
    • Sustainable development embodies the principle that 'We have not inherited the Earth from our ancestors; we have borrowed it from our children.'
    • Development must focus on renewable energy (solar, wind), eco-friendly technologies, and recycling so that present progress does not compromise the survival of future generations.

Q7
Why do different persons have different notions of development? Which of the following explanations is more important and why? (a) Because people are different. (b) Because life situations of persons are different.

✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Explanation: Explanation (b) 'Because life situations of persons are different' is far more important and accurate.

Q8
What is Per Capita Income? Mention any two limitations of using Per Capita Income as the sole indicator to measure economic development.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Meaning of Per Capita Income:

Per Capita Income (or average income) is calculated by dividing the total national income of a country by its total population: $\text{Per Capita Income} = \frac{\text{Total National Income}}{\text{Total Population}}$

Limitations of Per Capita Income:

  1. Hides Inequalities in Income Distribution: It does not tell us how income is distributed among people. A country may have very high per capita income if a few billionaires earn astronomical wealth while the vast majority live in extreme deprivation (e.g., in two countries with identical average incomes of ₹ 50,000, one may have equitable distribution while in the other, one person earns ₹ 48,000 and four earn ₹ 500 each).
  2. Ignores Non-Material Development Indicators: It completely ignores crucial human development dimensions such as infant mortality rate, life expectancy, health care access, literacy rates, clean environment, personal freedom, gender equality, and social security.

Q9
Besides average income, what other social and demographic indicators are crucial for measuring human development? Define any three of them.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Crucial non-income indicators used in measuring human development include:

  1. Infant Mortality Rate (IMR):
    • Definition: The number of children that die before completing one year of age per 1,000 live births born in that particular year.
    • Significance: It reflects the quality and availability of primary healthcare, maternal nutrition, and sanitation facilities.
  2. Literacy Rate:
    • Definition: The proportion of literate population in the 7 and above age group.
    • Significance: Measures fundamental intellectual empowerment and educational access.
  3. Net Attendance Ratio:
    • Definition: The total number of children of age group 14 and 15 years attending school as a percentage of total number of children in the same age group.
    • Significance: Highlights whether adolescents continue with secondary education or drop out.
  4. Life Expectancy at Birth: The average expected length of life of a person at the time of birth, reflecting general health and nutrition.

Q10
Why is the issue of sustainability important for development? Explain with reference to groundwater depletion in India and exhaustion of natural crude oil reserves.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Importance of Sustainability in Development:

Sustainable development means economic development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. It prevents environmental degradation that would halt or reverse human progress.

Case 1: Groundwater Depletion in India (Renewable Resource):

  • Groundwater is replenished naturally by rainfall. However, about 300 districts in India have reported a water level decline of over 4 metres during the past 20 years.
  • Nearly one-third of the country is overusing its groundwater reserves, particularly in agricultural breadbaskets (Punjab, Western UP) and hard-rock plateau areas. If this continues, 60% of the country will face catastrophic water scarcity, destroying agricultural livelihoods.

Case 2: Exhaustion of Crude Oil Reserves (Non-Renewable Resource):

  • Crude oil is a finite fossil fuel that took millions of years to form and cannot be replenished. Present global reserves are estimated to last merely around 40–50 years at current extraction rates.
  • Countries like India, which import over 80% of their crude oil, face crippling economic shocks when oil prices rise. Without transitioning to renewable solar, wind, and green hydrogen power, future generations will face systemic economic paralysis.

Q11
"The Earth has enough resources to meet the needs of all but not enough to satisfy the greed of even one person." How is this statement by Mahatma Gandhi relevant to the discussion of development?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Mahatma Gandhi's profound statement addresses the root cause of environmental unsustainability in modern development:

  1. Universal Basic Needs vs Unsustainable Consumerism: Nature provides abundant regenerative resources—arable land, clean water, forests, and solar energy—sufficient to feed, clothe, and provide decent livelihoods for all human beings on the planet if used responsibly.
  2. Destruction Caused by Corporate Greed: Over-exploitation of resources is driven by profit-maximization and reckless consumerism: excessive deforestation, open-cast mining, plastic dumping, and fossil fuel combustion that deplete finite mineral ores and pollute planetary ecosystems.
  3. Inequity in Resource Consumption: A tiny minority of affluent countries and ultra-rich individuals consume a lion's share of the planet's resources, generating disproportionate toxic waste and carbon emissions, while the impoverished suffer the brunt of climate disasters.
  4. Call for Ethical, Equitable Stewardship: The statement advocates a shift from mindless extraction to sustainable resource stewardship, renewable alternatives, and equitable distribution.

Q12
Compare the criteria used by the World Bank with that used by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) for measuring and comparing the development of different countries.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Comparison of Development Criteria: World Bank vs UNDP:

Feature World Bank (World Development Report) UNDP (Human Development Report)
1. Primary Criterion Uses exclusively Per Capita Income (PCI) / Gross National Income (GNI) per capita measured in US Dollars. Uses a multi-dimensional composite index: the Human Development Index (HDI).
2. Components Included Only material economic wealth (Total Income / Total Population). Three dimensions: Health (Life Expectancy), Education (Mean & Expected Years of Schooling), and Standard of Living (Per Capita GNI).
3. Country Classification Classifies countries strictly into: High-Income (Rich), Middle-Income, and Low-Income countries. Ranks all countries on a composite score from 0 to 1 into Very High, High, Medium, and Low Human Development tiers.
4. Scope & Vision Narrow, purely economic approach; ignores health, schooling, and income disparities. Broad, holistic human-centric approach focusing on the actual capabilities and well-being of people.

Q13
Why do you think Kerala, despite having a lower per capita income than Haryana (or Maharashtra), has a much better human development ranking? Explain.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Kerala ranks substantially higher on human development despite lower monetary per capita income due to strategic long-term investments in social infrastructure:

  1. Universal Primary Healthcare: Kerala has established an extensive, accessible network of primary health centres, resulting in an exceptionally low Infant Mortality Rate (IMR of around 10–12 per 1,000) compared to over 30 in other states.
  2. High Female and Universal Literacy: Prioritizing public education has yielded a literacy rate of over 94%, ensuring that both boys and girls complete schooling.
  3. Effective Public Distribution System (PDS): Kerala operates a well-functioning Public Distribution System that guarantees subsidized rations and essential nutritional security to all families, maintaining high baseline health and nutritional standards.
  4. Conclusion: Money in your pocket cannot buy all the essential goods and services needed to live well (such as a pollution-free environment, protection from infectious diseases, and unadulterated medicines). Collective public provision is the most effective and affordable path to human well-being.

Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): Per capita income does not reveal how income is distributed among the citizens of a country.
Reason (R): Two countries with identical per capita income can have radically different social realities — one country may have equitable distribution where most citizens are moderately prosperous, while another may have a handful of multi-billionaires and the vast majority living in extreme poverty.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: Average income is useful for broad international comparisons, but it completely obscures internal disparities. Hence, development must be assessed through multidimensional criteria like education, health, and equitable distribution, as done in the UNDP's Human Development Index.
