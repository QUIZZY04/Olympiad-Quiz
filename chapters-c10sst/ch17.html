Q1
How does democracy produce an accountable, responsive and legitimate government?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Democracy produces an accountable, responsive, and legitimate government through the following institutional processes:

  1. Accountable Government:
    • In a democracy, people have the right to choose their rulers and people have control over the rulers. Whenever possible and necessary, citizens should be able to participate in decision making that affects them all.
    • Democracy ensures that decision making is based on norms and procedures. A citizen who wants to know if a decision was taken through the correct procedures can find this out through the Right to Information (RTI). This transparency is completely missing in non-democratic regimes.
  2. Responsive Government:
    • Democracies are expected to produce a government that is attentive to the needs and demands of the people and is largely free of corruption.
    • Public debates in media, civil society protests, judicial interventions, and fear of losing upcoming elections compel democratic rulers to listen to citizens' grievances and respond promptly.
  3. Legitimate Government:
    • A democratic government may be slow, less efficient, and not always very clean, but it is people’s own government.
    • It derives its sovereign authority directly from regular, free, and fair elections where citizens vote. There is an overwhelming support for the idea of democracy all over the world because people wish to be ruled by representatives elected by them. Democracy’s ability to generate its own support is itself an outcome that cannot be ignored.

Q2
What are the conditions under which democracies accommodate social diversities?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Democracies accommodate social diversities and prevent internal civil conflicts by fulfilling two essential conditions:

  1. Condition 1: Democracy is not simply rule by majority opinion:
    • The majority always needs to work with the minority so that governments function to represent the general view. Majority and minority opinions are not permanent; they shift with each issue and debate.
    • A genuine democracy listens to dissenting voices, minority concerns, and diverse perspectives rather than steamrolling through numerical supremacy.
  2. Condition 2: Rule by majority must not become rule by majority community:
    • Rule by majority must not become rule by majority community in terms of religion, race, or linguistic group.
    • Rule by majority means that in case of every decision or in the election of every candidate, different persons and groups may and can form a majority. Democracy remains democracy only as long as every citizen has a chance of being in majority at some point in time.
    • If someone is barred from being in majority on the basis of birth, caste, or religion, then the democratic rule ceases to be accommodating for that person or group.
  3. Institutional Mechanisms for Accommodation:
    • Democracies negotiate differences through constitutional power sharing, federal division of power, minority rights guarantees, and secular laws (as successfully shown by the Belgian model).

Q3
How does democracy promote the dignity and freedom of citizens?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Democracy stands much superior to any other form of government in promoting the dignity and freedom of the individual:

  1. Rooted in Respect and Freedom: The passion for respect and freedom are the basis of democracy. Throughout history, societies across the world were male-dominated, feudal, or based on caste oppression. Democracies recognize that all individuals are equal before the law and have an inherent right to live with dignity.
  2. Dignity of Women:
    • Long struggles by women have created some sensitivity today that respect to and equal treatment of women are necessary ingredients of a democratic society.
    • Once the democratic principle is recognized constitutionally, it becomes easier for women to wage a legal and moral struggle against patriarchal practices. In a non-democratic regime, this moral and legal backing would be absent because the principle of individual freedom is not accepted.
  3. Dignity of Disadvantaged and Discriminated Castes:
    • Democracy in India has strengthened the claims of the disadvantaged and discriminated castes for equal status and equal opportunity.
    • Caste inequalities and untouchability have been legally abolished. While social discrimination still persists in practice, marginalized communities have the constitutional right to vote, organize protests, and contest elections, transforming them into active, vocal citizens.
  4. Public Scrutiny and Citizen Empowerment:
    • The most distinctive thing about democracy is that its examination never gets over. As democracy passes one test, it produces another test. As people get some benefits of democracy, they ask for more and want to make democracy even better.
    • The fact that people are complaining is itself a testimony to the success of democracy: it shows that people have transformed from subjects into active citizens who hold rulers accountable.

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'Evidence shows that in practice many democracies did not fulfill the expectation of producing economic growth and eliminating poverty. Between 1950 and 2000, dictatorships have had slightly higher rates of economic growth. But when we compare economic growth rates in poor countries under dictatorship and democracy, the difference is negligible. Economic development depends on several factors: country’s population size, global situation, cooperation from other countries, economic priorities adopted by the country, etc.'

Question: Should democracy be rejected because dictatorships have shown slightly higher economic growth rates in some decades?

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Negligible Economic Difference: When poor countries are compared, the difference in economic growth between democracy and dictatorship is virtually negligible. Economic growth depends on human capital, savings rate, and foreign trade policies, not autocratic rule.
  • Overwhelming Positive Non-Economic Outcomes: Dictatorships deny basic human rights, free speech, and dignity. Democracy guarantees civil liberties, political equality, peaceful transfer of power, and rule of law, making democracy far superior overall.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): A public expression of dissatisfaction with democracy shows the success of the democratic project.
Reason (R): It transforms people from the status of a 'subject' into that of a 'citizen' who believe that their vote makes a difference to how the government is run.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: In an autocracy, public dissatisfaction is met with imprisonment and censorship. In a democracy, citizens' complaints reflect critical political consciousness and confidence that their democratic agency can reform government policies.
