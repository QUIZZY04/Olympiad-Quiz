Q1
Answer the following in about 30 words: (i) What is manufacturing? (ii) Name any three physical and three human factors for the location of an industry, (iii) What are basic industries? Give an example, (iv) Name the important raw materials used in the manufacturing of cement.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

(i) What is Manufacturing?

Production of goods in large quantities after processing from raw materials to more valuable products is called manufacturing (e.g., paper from wood, sugar from sugarcane, iron and steel from iron ore, and aluminium from bauxite). It belongs to the secondary sector of the economy.

(ii) Factors Influencing Industrial Location:

  • Three Physical Factors:
    1. Availability of abundant raw materials near the factory site.
    2. Perennial supply of cheap water and continuous power/energy.
    3. Favourable climate and suitable topography.
  • Three Human/Socio-Economic Factors:
    1. Availability of cheap, skilled and unskilled labour.
    2. Proximity to markets and efficient transport networks.
    3. Access to banking, insurance, capital, and supportive government industrial policies.

(iii) What are Basic Industries? Give an Example:

Basic or key industries are those which supply their products or raw materials to manufacture other goods. Example: Iron and steel industry, copper smelting, and aluminium smelting. Machinery, transport equipment, and consumer tools all rely on basic steel.

(iv) Important Raw Materials Used in Manufacturing Cement:

Limestone, silica, alumina, and gypsum, along with coal and electric power.

Q2
How do industries pollute the environment? Explain the four major types of industrial pollution.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Industries are responsible for four major types of environmental pollution:

  1. Air Pollution: Caused by the presence of high proportion of undesirable gases like sulfur dioxide and carbon monoxide. Smoke is emitted by chemical and paper factories, brick kilns, refineries, and thermal power plants. Toxic gas leaks (e.g., Bhopal Gas Tragedy 1984) are hazardous to human health, flora, and fauna.
  2. Water Pollution: Caused by organic and inorganic industrial wastes and effluents discharged into freshwater bodies. The main culprits are paper, pulp, chemical, textile, dyeing, tanneries, petroleum refineries, and electroplating industries. They dump heavy metals like lead, mercury, pesticides, fertilisers, synthetic chemicals, and plastics into rivers.
  3. Thermal Pollution: Occurs when hot water from factories and thermal plants is drained into rivers and ponds before cooling. It drastically lowers dissolved oxygen, destroying aquatic life, fish eggs, and aquatic plants.
  4. Noise Pollution: Industrial and construction activities, machinery, generators, saws, and pneumatic drills create unwanted high decibel sounds, causing hearing impairment, hypertension, stress, and physiological disorders among workers.

Q3
Discuss the steps to be taken to minimise environmental degradation by industry.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

To achieve sustainable industrial growth, environmental degradation caused by industries can be effectively controlled through the following concrete measures:

  1. Minimising Water Use and Recycling: Minimising water consumption by reusing and recycling it in two or more successive stages. Harvesting rainwater to meet industrial water requirements.
  2. Three-stage Treatment of Industrial Effluents:
    • Primary Treatment: By mechanical means — screening, sedimentation, and flocculation.
    • Secondary Treatment: By biological processes (aeration and biological filters).
    • Tertiary Treatment: By biological, chemical, and physical processes including recycling of wastewater.
  3. Control of Air Pollution:
    • Fitting smoke stacks with electrostatic precipitators, fabric filters, scrubbers, and inertial separators to trap particulate matter before smoke is released.
    • Replacing coal with oil or natural gas in industrial furnaces to eliminate black soot emissions.
  4. Control of Noise Pollution: Machinery and equipment can be fitted with silencers. Redesigning machinery to make it energy-efficient and quiet; workers should wear earplugs and earphones.
  5. Ecological Zoning and Green Belts: Developing green belts of dense tree plantations around industrial complexes to absorb sound, dust, and toxic fumes.

Q4
Why is the iron and steel industry concentrated in and around the Chota Nagpur plateau region?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The Chota Nagpur Plateau region (covering Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, and West Bengal) has the maximum concentration of iron and steel industries in India due to the following relative locational advantages:

  1. Low Cost of Iron Ore: High-grade hematite iron ore is available in abundance within the immediate vicinity (e.g., Singhbhum in Jharkhand, Mayurbhanj and Kendujhar in Odisha).
  2. High-Grade Raw Materials in Close Proximity: All required heavy smelting raw materials — coking coal (Jharia, Bokaro), limestone, dolomite (Sundargarh), and manganese — are found in close geographical proximity, drastically lowering assembly transport costs.
  3. Cheap and Abundant Labour: Densely populated surrounding states of Bihar, West Bengal, Jharkhand, and Uttar Pradesh provide an abundant supply of cheap, skilled and unskilled labour.
  4. Vast Domestic and Export Market: Excellent rail and road connectivity links the region to the industrial heartlands of India and to the mega port of Kolkata, which provides export facilities and machinery import capabilities.
  5. Abundant Water and Hydroelectric Power: Perennial water supplies and cheap electricity are readily available from the Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC) and Subarnarekha river networks.

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'In recent years, there is a tendency for sugar mills to shift and concentrate in the southern and western states, especially in Maharashtra. This is because the cane produced here has a higher sucrose content. The cooler climate also ensures a longer crushing season. Moreover, the cooperatives are more successful in these states.'

Question: State three distinct geographic and organizational reasons why the sugar industry is migrating from northern India (UP/Bihar) to Maharashtra and South India.

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Higher Sucrose Content: Tropical maritime climate yields thicker sugarcane with significantly higher sugar content per tonne compared to northern cane.
  • Longer Crushing Season: The milder, cooler winter of peninsular India allows crushing operations to continue for several months longer without drying up cane juice.
  • Flourishing Cooperative Sector: In Maharashtra, sugar mills are predominantly run by well-managed farmer cooperatives, ensuring smooth supply chains, stable payments, and modern automated machinery.
