Q1
What are the factors which gave birth to the consumer movement in India? Trace its historical evolution.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The consumer movement arose in India as a 'social force' out of deep dissatisfaction of consumers against widespread exploitation by unscrupulous traders and manufacturers:

  1. Rampant Unfair Trade Practices: Traders indulged in rampant malpractices like adulteration of food and edible oil, underweight measuring, and hoarding of essential goods to create artificial scarcity and hike prices.
  2. False and Misleading Advertisements: Companies made exaggerated and deceptive claims about products (medicines, baby milk powder, cosmetics) through mass media, duping uneducated consumers without any legal liability.
  3. Absence of Legal Framework: In the 1960s, there was no legal system available to consumers to protect them from exploitation in the marketplace. The traditional market rule was caveat emptor ('let the buyer beware'), placing the entire burden of substandard goods onto the consumer.
  4. Consumer Protests and COPRA Enactment: In the 1970s, consumer organizations formed consumer groups, published articles, held exhibitions, and staged rallies against adulteration. A major concerted campaign led the Indian Parliament to enact the historic Consumer Protection Act (COPRA) in 1986, providing consumers with statutory legal rights and dedicated consumer courts.

Q2
Mention a few rights of consumers and explain the significance of each under the Consumer Protection Act.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Under the Consumer Protection Act, consumers enjoy six fundamental statutory rights:

  1. Right to Safety: Consumers have the right to be protected against the marketing of goods and delivery of services that are hazardous to life and property. For example, pressure cookers must have safety valves; substandard electrical appliances can cause fatal electric shocks or fire.
  2. Right to be Informed: Consumers have the right to be informed about the particulars of goods and services that they purchase — including ingredients, manufacturing date, expiry date, batch number, MRP, and directions for safe use. Consumers can also use Right to Information (RTI Act 2005) to know about government public services.
  3. Right to Choose: Any consumer who receives a service in whatever capacity, regardless of age, gender, and nature of service, has the right to choose whether to continue to receive the service. A gas distributor cannot force a consumer to buy a gas stove as a condition to grant a new cylinder connection.
  4. Right to Seek Redressal: Consumers have the right to seek redressal against unfair trade practices or exploitation. If any damage is done to a consumer, they have the right to get compensation depending on the degree of damage (refund, replacement, or financial damages).
  5. Right to Represent (Right to be Heard): Consumers have the right to represent themselves and be heard in consumer forums and judicial bodies.
  6. Right to Consumer Education: The right to acquire knowledge and skill to be an informed consumer throughout life (propagated via government awareness campaigns like 'Jago Grahak Jago').

Q3
Explain the three-tier quasi-judicial machinery set up under the Consumer Protection Act for the redressal of consumer disputes.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Under COPRA, a three-tier quasi-judicial machinery at the district, state, and national levels was established for redressal of consumer disputes:

Judicial Tier Pecuniary Jurisdiction (Claim Value) Appellate Authority
District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (District Commission) Deals with consumer claims and compensation claims up to ₹1 Crore (updated under CPA 2019). Located in every district headquarters. If a consumer is not satisfied with the District Commission's order, they can appeal to the State Commission within 45 days.
State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (State Commission) Entertains consumer claims exceeding ₹1 Crore and up to ₹10 Crore. Located at the state capital. Appeals against State Commission orders can be filed before the National Commission within 30 days.
National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (NCDRC) Deals with claims exceeding ₹10 Crore. Located in New Delhi. Appeals against the National Commission can be taken directly to the Supreme Court of India.

Q4
Explain the significance of quality certification logos: ISI, AGMARK, and HALLMARK. Why should consumers look for these marks?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Standardisation logos and marks help consumers ascertain that products meet prescribed safety, health, and quality standards established by government certification bodies:

  1. ISI Mark: Issued by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS). It is mandatory for industrial and consumer safety products such as electrical appliances (heaters, irons), LPG cylinders, cement, packaged drinking water, and infant milk food. It guarantees that the product has undergone strict laboratory testing.
  2. AGMARK: Certification mark for agricultural products in India, issued by the Directorate of Marketing and Inspection. Found on pulses, cereals, spices, edible oils, ghee, and honey, certifying purity and absence of adulteration.
  3. HALLMARK (BIS Hallmark): Official certification mark guaranteeing the purity of gold and silver jewellery. It displays the BIS logo, purity grade in carats/fineness (e.g., 916 for 22-carat gold), assaying centre's identification mark, and a unique 6-digit HUID code.
  4. FSSAI Logo: Issued by the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India. Mandatory on all packaged food items, certifying that food products meet food safety and hygienic processing standards.

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'Reji Mathew, a healthy student, joined a private coaching institute for a two-year professional course and paid the entire fee of ₹61,000 for two years at the time of admission. However, after one year, he decided to leave the course because he found the teaching standard inadequate. When he asked for a refund of ₹30,500 for the second year, the institute refused. He filed a case in the District Consumer Forum.'

Question: Which consumer right did Reji Mathew exercise, and what is the consumer court's legal verdict in such educational fee disputes?

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Right Exercised: Right to Choose and Right to Seek Redressal.
  • Consumer Court Verdict: The State Commission and National Commission have repeatedly ruled that coaching institutes and colleges cannot collect fees for the entire course in advance. If a student leaves after one year, charging fees for services not rendered is an unfair trade practice; the institute must refund the proportionate fee for the remaining period with interest and litigation costs.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): The Consumer Protection Act 2019 replaced the older COPRA 1986 to address modern challenges.
Reason (R): The 2019 Act covers e-commerce platforms, provides for product liability, established the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA), and introduced mediation as an alternate dispute resolution mechanism.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: With the massive boom in online shopping, digital fraud, and deceptive teleshopping ads, the 1986 law was inadequate. The 2019 Act modernized consumer law by introducing strict penalties for misleading advertisements, holding e-commerce platforms liable, and enabling online filing of complaints (e-daakhil).
