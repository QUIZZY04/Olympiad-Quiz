Q1
What are the factors which gave birth to the consumer movement in India? Trace its historical evolution.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The consumer movement arose in India as a 'social force' out of deep dissatisfaction of consumers against widespread exploitation by unscrupulous traders and manufacturers:

  1. Rampant Unfair Trade Practices: Traders indulged in rampant malpractices like adulteration of food and edible oil, underweight measuring, and hoarding of essential goods to create artificial scarcity and hike prices.
  2. False and Misleading Advertisements: Companies made exaggerated and deceptive claims about products (medicines, baby milk powder, cosmetics) through mass media, duping uneducated consumers without any legal liability.
  3. Absence of Legal Framework: In the 1960s, there was no legal system available to consumers to protect them from exploitation in the marketplace. The traditional market rule was caveat emptor ('let the buyer beware'), placing the entire burden of substandard goods onto the consumer.
  4. Consumer Protests and COPRA Enactment: In the 1970s, consumer organizations formed consumer groups, published articles, held exhibitions, and staged rallies against adulteration. A major concerted campaign led the Indian Parliament to enact the historic Consumer Protection Act (COPRA) in 1986, providing consumers with statutory legal rights and dedicated consumer courts.

Q2
Mention a few rights of consumers and explain the significance of each under the Consumer Protection Act.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Under the Consumer Protection Act, consumers enjoy six fundamental statutory rights:

  1. Right to Safety: Consumers have the right to be protected against the marketing of goods and delivery of services that are hazardous to life and property. For example, pressure cookers must have safety valves; substandard electrical appliances can cause fatal electric shocks or fire.
  2. Right to be Informed: Consumers have the right to be informed about the particulars of goods and services that they purchase — including ingredients, manufacturing date, expiry date, batch number, MRP, and directions for safe use. Consumers can also use Right to Information (RTI Act 2005) to know about government public services.
  3. Right to Choose: Any consumer who receives a service in whatever capacity, regardless of age, gender, and nature of service, has the right to choose whether to continue to receive the service. A gas distributor cannot force a consumer to buy a gas stove as a condition to grant a new cylinder connection.
  4. Right to Seek Redressal: Consumers have the right to seek redressal against unfair trade practices or exploitation. If any damage is done to a consumer, they have the right to get compensation depending on the degree of damage (refund, replacement, or financial damages).
  5. Right to Represent (Right to be Heard): Consumers have the right to represent themselves and be heard in consumer forums and judicial bodies.
  6. Right to Consumer Education: The right to acquire knowledge and skill to be an informed consumer throughout life (propagated via government awareness campaigns like 'Jago Grahak Jago').

Q3
Explain the three-tier quasi-judicial machinery set up under the Consumer Protection Act for the redressal of consumer disputes.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Under COPRA, a three-tier quasi-judicial machinery at the district, state, and national levels was established for redressal of consumer disputes:

Judicial Tier Pecuniary Jurisdiction (Claim Value) Appellate Authority
District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (District Commission) Deals with consumer claims and compensation claims up to ₹1 Crore (updated under CPA 2019). Located in every district headquarters. If a consumer is not satisfied with the District Commission's order, they can appeal to the State Commission within 45 days.
State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (State Commission) Entertains consumer claims exceeding ₹1 Crore and up to ₹10 Crore. Located at the state capital. Appeals against State Commission orders can be filed before the National Commission within 30 days.

Q4
Explain the significance of quality certification logos: ISI, AGMARK, and HALLMARK. Why should consumers look for these marks?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Standardisation logos and marks help consumers ascertain that products meet prescribed safety, health, and quality standards established by government certification bodies:

  1. ISI Mark: Issued by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS). It is mandatory for industrial and consumer safety products such as electrical appliances (heaters, irons), LPG cylinders, cement, packaged drinking water, and infant milk food. It guarantees that the product has undergone strict laboratory testing.
  2. AGMARK: Certification mark for agricultural products in India, issued by the Directorate of Marketing and Inspection. Found on pulses, cereals, spices, edible oils, ghee, and honey, certifying purity and absence of adulteration.
  3. HALLMARK (BIS Hallmark): Official certification mark guaranteeing the purity of gold and silver jewellery. It displays the BIS logo, purity grade in carats/fineness (e.g., 916 for 22-carat gold), assaying centre's identification mark, and a unique 6-digit HUID code.
  4. FSSAI Logo: Issued by the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India. Mandatory on all packaged food items, certifying that food products meet food safety and hygienic processing standards.

Q7
What are the various ways in which consumers are exploited by unscrupulous producers and traders in the marketplace?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Consumers are exploited in the marketplace through numerous unfair trade practices:

  1. Underweighing and Undermeasuring: Traders often use tampered scales and weighing stones, giving consumers less than what they paid for.
  2. Adulteration and Substandard Quality: Mixing harmful foreign substances in food products (e.g., adulterated milk, edible oils, spices), or selling defective electrical goods that pose serious fire hazards.
  3. Overcharging Beyond MRP: Charging prices higher than the printed Maximum Retail Price (MRP) or adding unauthorized hidden charges and taxes.
  4. Misleading Advertisements: Companies make false and exaggerated claims through television and print advertisements regarding the efficacy, health benefits, or durability of products (e.g., claiming a hair oil prevents baldness or a baby food boosts IQ).
  5. Defective Post-Sale Service: Refusal to provide warranty repairs, non-replacement of damaged goods, and refusing to attend to customer complaints after receiving payment.

Q8
What factors gave birth to the consumer movement in India as a 'social force'? When was National Consumer Day designated?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Factors that Gave Birth to the Consumer Movement:

  1. Rampant Exploitation of Consumers: Dissatisfaction of consumers arose because of rampant unfair trade practices by sellers, including food shortages, hoarding, black marketing, and adulteration of edible oils and food grains in the 1960s.
  2. Absence of Legal Protection: Till the 1970s, consumer organisations largely wrote articles and held exhibitions. There was no legal framework protecting consumers from defective goods; the prevailing rule was caveat emptor ('let the buyer beware').
  3. Consumer Agitations in the 1970s: The movement succeeded in bringing pressure on business firms and government to correct business conduct which had become anti-consumer.
  4. Enactment of COPRA (1986): A major step taken in 1986 by the Indian Government was the enactment of the Consumer Protection Act 1986, popularly known as COPRA.

National Consumer Day: 24 December is observed as National Consumer Rights Day in India every year, commemorating the date the Consumer Protection Act was signed by the President in 1986.

Q9
Describe the three-tier quasi-judicial machinery set up under the Consumer Protection Act (COPRA) for redressal of consumer disputes in India.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

COPRA instituted a unique, inexpensive, three-tier quasi-judicial mechanism for speedy justice:

  1. District Consumer Disputes Redressal Forum (District Commission):
    • Set up at the district level across all districts of India.
    • Deals with consumer claims where the value of goods or services paid does not exceed ₹ 1 Crore (updated as per Consumer Protection Act 2019; historically ₹ 20 Lakhs).
  2. State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (State Commission):
    • Set up at the state capital.
    • Deals with claims where the value of goods or services is between ₹ 1 Crore and ₹ 10 Crores (historically between ₹ 20 Lakhs and ₹ 1 Crore). Also hears appeals against District Commission orders.
  3. National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (National Commission):
    • Located at New Delhi at the apex level.
    • Deals with claims where the value of goods or services exceeds ₹ 10 Crores (historically exceeding ₹ 1 Crore). Also acts as the appellate court for appeals from State Commissions.

Q10
Explain any three rights of consumers guaranteed under the Consumer Protection Act in India.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The Consumer Protection Act guarantees essential consumer rights:

  1. Right to Safety: Consumers have the right to be protected against the marketing of goods and services which are hazardous to life and property. For example, pressure cookers, electrical appliances, and medicines must comply with strict safety standards.
  2. Right to be Informed: Consumers have the right to be informed about the particulars of quality, quantity, potency, purity, standard, date of manufacture, expiry date, ingredients, directions for use, and Maximum Retail Price (MRP).
  3. Right to Choose: Any consumer who receives a service in whatever capacity, regardless of age, gender, and nature of service, has the right to choose whether to continue to receive the service. A seller cannot force a consumer to buy unwanted accessories (e.g., buying a gas connection cannot be conditioned on buying a stove from the same dealer).
  4. Right to Seek Redressal: Consumers have the right to seek redressal against unfair trade practices or exploitation, including replacement of defective goods, refund, and financial compensation for injury.

Q11
What are the essential duties and responsibilities that a consumer should observe while purchasing goods and services in the market?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Consumers must exercise vigilance and responsibility to protect themselves:

  1. Insist on Cash Memo and Bill: A consumer must always insist on a valid cash memo, tax invoice, and warranty card. The cash memo is the indispensable legal proof of purchase required while filing a complaint in a consumer court.
  2. Check Standard Certification Marks: Look for quality assurance marks—such as ISI on electrical goods, Agmark on agricultural foods, FSSAI on packaged food, and Hallmark on gold jewellery.
  3. Carefully Read Labels: Check prices, Maximum Retail Price (MRP), manufacturing date, expiry date, weight, and batch number before purchasing, especially for medicines and food items.
  4. Exercise Independent Choice: Do not fall prey to deceptive advertising and misleading promises made by celebrity endorsements.
  5. File Complaints against Genuine Grievances: Form consumer groups and file formal complaints even for small financial losses to discourage corrupt market conduct.

Why are standard quality certification logos like ISI, Agmark, and Hallmark important for consumers? Explain each certification mark with an example.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Importance of Certification Marks:

These logos ensure that the manufacturer has followed prescribed quality standards, safety audits, and hygiene rules monitored by government certification agencies.

Three Major Quality Certification Marks in India:

  1. ISI Mark (Bureau of Indian Standards - BIS):
    • Guarantees strict safety and technical specifications for industrial, electrical, and consumer durable goods.
    • Examples: LPG cylinders, electric irons, switches, circuit breakers, helmets, cement, and packaged drinking water.
  2. Agmark (Directorate of Marketing and Inspection):
    • Quality certification mark for agricultural products and food produce, ensuring absence of adulterants.
    • Examples: Edible mustard oil, ghee, honey, pulses, spices, and flour.
  3. Hallmark (BIS Precious Metals Certification):
    • Certifies the purity and fineness of precious gold and silver jewellery through government-approved assaying centres.
    • Examples: 22K (916) and 18K gold ornaments.

Q13
What are the major challenges and practical limitations faced by the consumer movement and redressal process in India today?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Despite legal provisions, the consumer redressal process in India faces practical hurdles:

  1. Cumbersome and Time-Consuming Process: The consumer redressal process is becoming cumbersome, expensive, and time-consuming. Consumers often have to engage lawyers, attend multiple court hearings, and spend long hours waiting for judgments.
  2. Absence of Cash Memos: In a vast majority of daily retail transactions in India, purchases are made from local unorganized shops without cash memos. In the absence of written proof of purchase, establishing evidence in consumer courts is very difficult.
  3. Existing Laws Weak on Compensation: The existing laws are not very clear on the issue of compensation to consumers injured by defective goods or medical negligence.
  4. Enforcement Delays: Even after favorable consumer court orders, unscrupulous sellers often file endless appeals in State or National Commissions, delaying actual financial compensation to poor victims.
  5. Low Consumer Awareness: Over 40 years after COPRA, consumer awareness in rural and semi-urban India remains low; few consumers know how to draft and file a complaint.

Competency Case-Based Analysis
Read the following extract and answer the question:
'Reji Mathew, a healthy student, joined a private coaching institute for a two-year professional course and paid the entire fee of ₹61,000 for two years at the time of admission. However, after one year, he decided to leave the course because he found the teaching standard inadequate. When he asked for a refund of ₹30,500 for the second year, the institute refused. He filed a case in the District Consumer Forum.'

Question: Which consumer right did Reji Mathew exercise, and what is the consumer court's legal verdict in such educational fee disputes?

CBSE Competency Answer:

  • Right Exercised: Right to Choose and Right to Seek Redressal.
  • Consumer Court Verdict: The State Commission and National Commission have repeatedly ruled that coaching institutes and colleges cannot collect fees for the entire course in advance. If a student leaves after one year, charging fees for services not rendered is an unfair trade practice; the institute must refund the proportionate fee for the remaining period with interest and litigation costs.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): The Consumer Protection Act 2019 replaced the older COPRA 1986 to address modern challenges.
Reason (R): The 2019 Act covers e-commerce platforms, provides for product liability, established the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA), and introduced mediation as an alternate dispute resolution mechanism.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Competency Answer:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: With the massive boom in online shopping, digital fraud, and deceptive teleshopping ads, the 1986 law was inadequate. The 2019 Act modernized consumer law by introducing strict penalties for misleading advertisements, holding e-commerce platforms liable, and enabling online filing of complaints (e-daakhil).
