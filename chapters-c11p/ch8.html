Question 8.1
NCERT Exercise
A steel wire of length $4.7\text{ m}$ and cross-sectional area $3.0 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^2$ stretches by the same amount as a copper wire of length $3.5\text{ m}$ and cross-sectional area of $4.0 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^2$ under a given load. What is the ratio of the Young’s modulus of steel to that of copper?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Steel wire: Length $L_s = 4.7\text{ m}$, Area $A_s = 3.0 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^2$
• Copper wire: Length $L_c = 3.5\text{ m}$, Area $A_c = 4.0 \times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^2$
• Same load applied: $F_s = F_c = F$
• Equal elongation: $\Delta L_s = \Delta L_c = \Delta L$

Young’s Modulus Formula:
$$Y = \frac{\text{Stress}}{\text{Strain}} = \frac{F / A}{\Delta L / L} = \frac{F L}{A \Delta L}$$

Ratio of Young’s Moduli:
$$\frac{Y_s}{Y_c} = \frac{\frac{F L_s}{A_s \Delta L}}{\frac{F L_c}{A_c \Delta L}} = \frac{L_s}{L_c} \times \frac{A_c}{A_s}$$
$$\frac{Y_s}{Y_c} = \frac{4.7}{3.5} \times \frac{4.0 \times 10^{-5}}{3.0 \times 10^{-5}} = \frac{4.7 \times 4.0}{3.5 \times 3.0} = \frac{18.8}{10.5} \approx \mathbf{1.79}$$

$$\therefore \text{The ratio of Young’s modulus of steel to that of copper is } \mathbf{1.79} \text{ (or } 1.8:1\text{)}.$$

Question 8.2
NCERT Exercise
Figure 8.11 shows the strain-stress curve for a given material. What are (a) Young’s modulus and (b) approximate yield strength for this material?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Refer to the standard NCERT stress-strain curve:

• (a) Young’s Modulus ($Y$):
In the linear proportional region (Hooke’s law region):
For a strain $\epsilon = 0.002$, corresponding stress $\sigma = 150 \times 10^6\text{ N m}^{-2} = 150\text{ MPa}$.
$$Y = \frac{\text{Stress}}{\text{Strain}} = \frac{150 \times 10^6\text{ N m}^{-2}}{0.002} = \frac{150 \times 10^6}{2 \times 10^{-3}} = \mathbf{7.5 \times 10^{10}\text{ N m}^{-2} = 75\text{ GPa}}$$

• (b) Approximate Yield Strength (Elastic Limit):
Yield strength is the maximum stress the material can endure before entering the plastic deformation regime.
From the graph, the yield point occurs at the end of the elastic limit:
$$\mathbf{\text{Yield Strength} \approx 300 \times 10^6\text{ N m}^{-2} = 300\text{ MPa}}.$$

Question 8.3
NCERT Exercise
The stress-strain graphs for materials A and B are shown in Fig. 8.12. The graphs are drawn to the same scale.
(a) Which of the materials has the greater Young’s modulus?
(b) Which of the two is the stronger material?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• (a) Greater Young’s Modulus:
Young's modulus is represented by the slope of the linear proportional region of the stress-strain curve ($Y = \frac{\text{Stress}}{\text{Strain}} = \text{Slope}$).
Comparing the two graphs drawn to identical scales, the linear slope of material A is visibly steeper than that of material B ($\text{Slope}_A > \text{Slope}_B$).
$$\mathbf{\text{Therefore, Material A has the greater Young’s modulus.}}$$

• (b) Stronger Material:
The mechanical strength of a material is determined by its ultimate tensile strength (the peak point on the stress-strain curve before fracture).
Material A can withstand a significantly larger maximum stress before failure compared to material B.
$$\mathbf{\text{Therefore, Material A is the stronger material.}}$$

Question 8.4
NCERT Exercise
Read the following two statements below carefully and state, with reasons, if it is true or false.
(a) The Young’s modulus of rubber is greater than that of steel;
(b) The stretching of a coil is determined by its shear modulus.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• (a) False:
Young's modulus is the ratio of stress to strain ($Y = \frac{F/A}{\Delta L/L}$).
For a given stretching force and identical cross-sectional area, steel wire produces an extremely minute elongation $\Delta L$, whereas rubber stretches easily with a very large elongation.
Therefore, $\text{Strain}_{rubber} \gg \text{Strain}_{steel}$, which means:
$$Y_{steel} \gg Y_{rubber}$$
In fact, $Y_{steel} \approx 200\text{ GPa}$, while $Y_{rubber} \approx 10^{-3}\text{ GPa}$. Steel is vastly more elastic in the physical sense.

• (b) True:
When a helical coil spring is stretched, the length of the spring increases, but the wire itself does not undergo significant tensile elongation. Instead, every segment of the wire undergoes twisting (torsional shear).
Since twisting is a pure shear deformation, the stretching of a coil spring is governed by the shear modulus (modulus of rigidity, $G$) of the wire material.

(a) False: Explaining that steel requires vastly more stress for same strain ($Y_{steel} > Y_{rubber}$)1.5 Marks

Question 8.5
NCERT Exercise
Two wires of diameter $0.25\text{ cm}$, one made of steel and the other made of brass are loaded as shown in Fig. 8.13. The unloaded length of steel wire is $1.5\text{ m}$ and that of brass wire is $1.0\text{ m}$. Compute the elongations of the steel and the brass wires. ($Y_{steel} = 2.0 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$, $Y_{brass} = 0.91 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$, $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$).
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Diameter $d = 0.25\text{ cm} = 2.5 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m} \implies r = 1.25 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}$.
• Cross-sectional area $A = \pi r^2 = 3.1416 \times (1.25 \times 10^{-3})^2 \approx 4.909 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2$.
• Steel wire ($L_s = 1.5\text{ m}$): Supports both the $4.0\text{ kg}$ mass and the $6.0\text{ kg}$ mass below it.
Total mass on steel wire: $M_s = 4.0 + 6.0 = 10.0\text{ kg}$.
Tension $F_s = 10.0 \times 9.8 = 98\text{ N}$.
• Brass wire ($L_b = 1.0\text{ m}$): Supports only the bottom $6.0\text{ kg}$ mass.
Tension $F_b = 6.0 \times 9.8 = 58.8\text{ N}$.

1. Elongation of Steel Wire ($\Delta L_s$):
$$\Delta L_s = \frac{F_s L_s}{A Y_s} = \frac{98 \times 1.5}{(4.909 \times 10^{-6}) \times (2.0 \times 10^{11})} = \frac{147}{9.818 \times 10^5} \approx \mathbf{1.497 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m} \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}}$$

2. Elongation of Brass Wire ($\Delta L_b$):
$$\Delta L_b = \frac{F_b L_b}{A Y_b} = \frac{58.8 \times 1.0}{(4.909 \times 10^{-6}) \times (0.91 \times 10^{11})} = \frac{58.8}{4.467 \times 10^5} \approx \mathbf{1.316 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m} \approx 1.3 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}}$$

$$\therefore \text{Elongation of steel wire } = \mathbf{1.5 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}}, \quad \text{Elongation of brass wire } = \mathbf{1.3 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}}.$$

Question 8.6
NCERT Exercise
The edge of an aluminium cube is $10\text{ cm}$ long. One face of the cube is firmly fixed to a vertical wall. A mass of $100\text{ kg}$ is then attached to the opposite face of the cube. What is the vertical deflection of this face? (Shear modulus of aluminium $= 25\text{ GPa}$, $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$).
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Side edge of cube $L = 10\text{ cm} = 0.1\text{ m}$
• Area of face $A = L^2 = (0.1)^2 = 0.01\text{ m}^2 = 10^{-2}\text{ m}^2$
• Tangential shearing force: $F = M g = 100\text{ kg} \times 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2} = 980\text{ N}$
• Shear modulus (modulus of rigidity) $G = 25\text{ GPa} = 25 \times 10^9\text{ N m}^{-2}$

Shear Modulus Formula:
$$G = \frac{\text{Shear Stress}}{\text{Shear Strain}} = \frac{F / A}{\Delta x / L}$$
$$\Delta x = \frac{F L}{A G}$$

Calculation of Vertical Deflection ($\Delta x$):
$$\Delta x = \frac{980 \times 0.1}{(10^{-2}) \times (25 \times 10^9)} = \frac{98}{2.5 \times 10^8} = 39.2 \times 10^{-8}\text{ m} = \mathbf{3.92 \times 10^{-7}\text{ m}}$$

$$\therefore \text{The vertical deflection of the opposite face is } \mathbf{3.92 \times 10^{-7}\text{ m}}.$$

Question 8.7
NCERT Exercise
Four identical hollow cylindrical columns of mild steel support a big structure of mass $50,000\text{ kg}$. The inner and outer radii of each column are $30\text{ cm}$ and $60\text{ cm}$ respectively. Assuming the load distribution to be uniform, calculate the compressional strain of each column. ($Y_{steel} = 2.0 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$, $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$).
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Total mass $M = 50,000\text{ kg}$
• Total load $W = M g = 50000 \times 9.8 = 4.9 \times 10^5\text{ N}$
• Load on each of the four columns: $F = \frac{W}{4} = \frac{4.9 \times 10^5}{4} = 1.225 \times 10^5\text{ N}$
• Radii: Inner $r_1 = 30\text{ cm} = 0.3\text{ m}$, Outer $r_2 = 60\text{ cm} = 0.6\text{ m}$

1. Cross-sectional Area of each hollow column ($A$):
$$A = \pi (r_2^2 - r_1^2) = \pi (0.6^2 - 0.3^2) = \pi (0.36 - 0.09) = 0.27 \pi\text{ m}^2 \approx 0.8482\text{ m}^2$$

2. Compressional Stress on each column:
$$\sigma = \frac{F}{A} = \frac{1.225 \times 10^5}{0.27 \pi} = \frac{1.225 \times 10^5}{0.8482} \approx 1.444 \times 10^5\text{ N m}^{-2}$$

3. Compressional Strain:
$$\text{Strain} = \frac{\sigma}{Y} = \frac{1.444 \times 10^5\text{ N m}^{-2}}{2.0 \times 10^{11}\text{ N m}^{-2}} = \mathbf{7.22 \times 10^{-7}}$$

$$\therefore \text{Compressional strain of each column is } \mathbf{7.22 \times 10^{-7}}.$$

A piece of copper having a rectangular cross-section of $15.2\text{ mm} \times 19.1\text{ mm}$ is pulled in tension with $44,500\text{ N}$ force, producing only elastic deformation. Calculate the resulting strain? ($Y_{copper} = 1.1 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$).
[3 Marks]

$$\text{Strain} = \frac{\text{Stress}}{Y} = \frac{F / A}{Y} = \frac{F}{A Y}$$

Calculation:
$$\text{Strain} = \frac{44500}{(2.9032 \times 10^{-4}) \times (1.1 \times 10^{11})} = \frac{44500}{3.1935 \times 10^7} \approx \mathbf{1.39 \times 10^{-3}}$$

$$\therefore \text{The resulting tensile strain is } \mathbf{1.39 \times 10^{-3}} \text{ (or } 0.139\%\text{)}.$$

Question 8.9
NCERT Exercise
A steel cable with a radius of $1.5\text{ cm}$ supports a chairlift at a ski area. If the maximum stress is not to exceed $10^8\text{ N m}^{-2}$, what is the maximum load the cable can support?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Radius of cable $r = 1.5\text{ cm} = 1.5 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m}$
• Maximum permissible stress: $\sigma_{max} = 10^8\text{ N m}^{-2}$

1. Cross-sectional Area ($A$):
$$A = \pi r^2 = 3.1416 \times (1.5 \times 10^{-2})^2 = 3.1416 \times 2.25 \times 10^{-4} = 7.0686 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2$$

$$\sigma_{max} = \frac{F_{max}}{A} \implies F_{max} = \sigma_{max} \times A$$
$$F_{max} = 10^8\text{ N m}^{-2} \times (7.0686 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2) = \mathbf{7.07 \times 10^4\text{ N}}$$

$$\therefore \text{The maximum load the cable can support is } \mathbf{7.07 \times 10^4\text{ N}} \text{ (or a mass of } \approx 7214\text{ kg}\text{)}.$$

Question 8.10
NCERT Exercise
A rigid bar of mass $15\text{ kg}$ is supported symmetrically by three wires each $2.0\text{ m}$ long. Those at each end are of copper and the middle one is of iron. Determine the ratio of their diameters if each is to have the same tension. ($Y_{copper} = 1.1 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$, $Y_{iron} = 1.9 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$).
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Symmetrical arrangement with rigid horizontal bar.
• Since the bar is rigid and remains horizontal, all three wires undergo the exact same elongation: $\Delta L_{copper} = \Delta L_{iron} = \Delta L$.
• Initial lengths are identical: $L_{copper} = L_{iron} = 2.0\text{ m}$.
• Given condition: Each wire has the same tension: $F_{copper} = F_{iron} = F$.

Elongation and Young’s Modulus Relation:
$$\Delta L = \frac{F L}{A Y} = \frac{F L}{\left(\frac{\pi d^2}{4}\right) Y} = \frac{4 F L}{\pi d^2 Y}$$

Since $\Delta L, F, L$ are identical for both copper and iron wires:
$$d^2 Y = \text{constant} \implies d_c^2 Y_c = d_i^2 Y_i$$
$$\left(\frac{d_c}{d_i}\right)^2 = \frac{Y_i}{Y_c}$$
$$\frac{d_c}{d_i} = \sqrt{\frac{Y_i}{Y_c}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.9 \times 10^{11}}{1.1 \times 10^{11}}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.9}{1.1}} = \sqrt{1.727} \approx \mathbf{1.314 \approx 1.3}$$

$$\therefore \text{The ratio of the diameter of the copper wire to that of iron wire is } \mathbf{1.31:1}.$$

Question 8.11
NCERT Exercise
A $14.5\text{ kg}$ mass, fastened to the end of a steel wire of unstretched length $1.0\text{ m}$, is whirled in a vertical circle with an angular velocity of $2\text{ rev s}^{-1}$ at the bottom of the circle. The cross-sectional area of the wire is $0.065\text{ cm}^2$. Calculate the elongation of the wire when the mass is at the lowest point of its path. ($Y_{steel} = 2.0 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$, $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$).
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Mass $m = 14.5\text{ kg}$
• Radius / length of wire $L = 1.0\text{ m}$
• Frequency at bottom: $\nu = 2\text{ rev s}^{-1} \implies \omega = 2\pi \nu = 2\pi(2) = 4\pi\text{ rad s}^{-1} \approx 12.566\text{ rad s}^{-1}$
• Area $A = 0.065\text{ cm}^2 = 0.065 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2 = 6.5 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2$
• $Y = 2.0 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$

1. Net Tension at the Lowest Point ($T$):
At the lowest point of the vertical circle, tension must support both gravity and provide centripetal acceleration:
$$T = m g + m \omega^2 L = m(g + \omega^2 L)$$
$$\omega^2 = (4\pi)^2 = 16\pi^2 \approx 16 \times 9.8696 \approx 157.91\text{ s}^{-2}$$
$$T = 14.5 \times (9.8 + 157.91 \times 1.0) = 14.5 \times 167.71 = \mathbf{2431.8\text{ N}}$$

2. Elongation of the Wire ($\Delta L$):
$$\Delta L = \frac{T L}{A Y} = \frac{2431.8 \times 1.0}{(6.5 \times 10^{-6}) \times (2.0 \times 10^{11})} = \frac{2431.8}{1.3 \times 10^6} \approx \mathbf{1.87 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m} = 1.87\text{ mm}}$$

$$\therefore \text{The elongation of the wire at the lowest point is } \mathbf{1.87\text{ mm}}.$$

Question 8.12
NCERT Exercise
Compute the bulk modulus of water from the following data: Initial volume $= 100.0\text{ litre}$, pressure increase $= 100.0\text{ atm}$ ($1\text{ atm} = 1.013 \times 10^5\text{ Pa}$), final volume $= 100.5\text{ litre}$ (volume decrease $= 0.5\text{ litre}$). Compare the bulk modulus of water with that of air (at constant temperature $\approx 1.0 \times 10^5\text{ Pa}$). Explain in simple terms why the ratio is so large.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Initial volume $V = 100.0\text{ L}$
• Change in volume $\Delta V = -0.5\text{ L}$
• Pressure increase $\Delta P = 100.0\text{ atm} = 100.0 \times 1.013 \times 10^5\text{ Pa} = 1.013 \times 10^7\text{ Pa}$

1. Bulk Modulus of Water ($B$):
$$B = -\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V / V} = \frac{\Delta P \times V}{|\Delta V|} = \frac{1.013 \times 10^7 \times 100.0}{0.5} = 2.026 \times 10^9\text{ Pa} \approx \mathbf{2.03 \times 10^9\text{ Pa}}$$

2. Comparison with Bulk Modulus of Air:
At constant temperature (isothermal conditions), bulk modulus of air equals atmospheric pressure: $B_{air} = P = 1.013 \times 10^5\text{ Pa}$.
$$\frac{B_{water}}{B_{air}} = \frac{2.026 \times 10^9}{1.013 \times 10^5} \approx \mathbf{2 \times 10^4 = 20,000}$$

3. Physical Explanation:
Gases have vast intermolecular spaces between molecules, so applying pressure easily compresses them. In liquids, molecules are closely packed together almost in contact, with powerful repulsive intermolecular forces resisting compression.
$$\mathbf{\text{Hence, water is 20,000 times less compressible (much more rigid) than air.}}$$

Question 8.13
NCERT Exercise
What is the density of water at a depth where pressure is $80.0\text{ atm}$, given that its density at the surface is $1.03 \times 10^3\text{ kg m}^{-3}$? (Bulk modulus of water $= 2.2 \times 10^9\text{ N m}^{-2}$).
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Surface density $\rho_0 = 1.03 \times 10^3\text{ kg m}^{-3}$
• Pressure difference $\Delta P = 80.0\text{ atm} = 80.0 \times 1.013 \times 10^5\text{ Pa} = 8.104 \times 10^6\text{ Pa}$
• Bulk modulus $B = 2.2 \times 10^9\text{ Pa}$

Fractional Change in Density:
Since mass is constant ($m = \rho V = \text{const}$), taking differentials:
$$\frac{d\rho}{\rho} = -\frac{dV}{V}$$
From bulk modulus definition: $-\frac{dV}{V} = \frac{\Delta P}{B}$.
$$\frac{\Delta \rho}{\rho_0} = \frac{\Delta P}{B} = \frac{8.104 \times 10^6}{2.2 \times 10^9} \approx 3.684 \times 10^{-3}$$

New Density ($\rho$):
$$\rho = \rho_0 \left(1 + \frac{\Delta P}{B}\right) = 1.03 \times 10^3 \times (1 + 0.003684) = 1.03 \times 10^3 \times 1.003684$$
$$\rho = \mathbf{1.0338 \times 10^3\text{ kg m}^{-3} \approx 1.034 \times 10^3\text{ kg m}^{-3}}$$

$$\therefore \text{The density of water at that depth is } \mathbf{1.034 \times 10^3\text{ kg m}^{-3}}.$$

Question 8.14
NCERT Exercise
Compute the fractional change in volume of a glass slab, when subjected to a hydraulic pressure of $10\text{ atm}$. (Bulk modulus of glass $= 37\text{ GPa}$).
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Hydraulic pressure increase $\Delta P = 10\text{ atm} = 10 \times 1.013 \times 10^5\text{ Pa} = 1.013 \times 10^6\text{ Pa}$
• Bulk modulus of glass $B = 37\text{ GPa} = 37 \times 10^9\text{ Pa}$

Fractional Change in Volume:
$$B = \frac{\Delta P}{|\Delta V| / V} \implies \frac{|\Delta V|}{V} = \frac{\Delta P}{B}$$

$$\frac{|\Delta V|}{V} = \frac{1.013 \times 10^6\text{ Pa}}{37 \times 10^9\text{ Pa}} \approx \mathbf{2.74 \times 10^{-5}}$$

In percentage: $2.74 \times 10^{-3}\% = 0.00274\%$.

$$\therefore \text{The fractional change in volume of the glass slab is } \mathbf{2.74 \times 10^{-5}}.$$

Question 8.15
NCERT Exercise
Determine the volume contraction of a solid copper cube, $10\text{ cm}$ on an edge, when subjected to a hydraulic pressure of $7.0 \times 10^6\text{ Pa}$. (Bulk modulus of copper $= 140\text{ GPa}$).
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Edge of copper cube $L = 10\text{ cm} = 0.1\text{ m}$
• Initial volume $V = L^3 = (0.1\text{ m})^3 = 10^{-3}\text{ m}^3$
• Hydraulic pressure $\Delta P = 7.0 \times 10^6\text{ Pa}$
• Bulk modulus of copper $B = 140\text{ GPa} = 140 \times 10^9\text{ Pa} = 1.4 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$

Volume Contraction Formula:
$$B = \frac{\Delta P}{|\Delta V| / V} \implies |\Delta V| = \frac{\Delta P \times V}{B}$$

Calculation:
$$|\Delta V| = \frac{(7.0 \times 10^6\text{ Pa}) \times (10^{-3}\text{ m}^3)}{1.4 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}} = \frac{7.0 \times 10^3}{1.4 \times 10^{11}} = \mathbf{5.0 \times 10^{-8}\text{ m}^3 = 0.05\text{ cm}^3}$$

$$\therefore \text{The volume contraction of the copper cube is } \mathbf{5.0 \times 10^{-8}\text{ m}^3} \text{ (or } 0.05\text{ cm}^3\text{)}.$$

Question 8.18
NCERT Exercise
A rod of length $1.05\text{ m}$ having negligible mass is supported at its ends by two wires of steel (wire A) and aluminium (wire B) of equal lengths as shown in Fig. 8.15. The cross-sectional areas of wires A and B are $1.0\text{ mm}^2$ and $2.0\text{ mm}^2$, respectively. At what point along the rod should a mass $m$ be suspended in order to produce (a) equal stresses and (b) equal strains in both steel and aluminium wires? ($Y_{steel} = 2.0 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$, $Y_{Al} = 7.0 \times 10^{10}\text{ Pa}$).
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Length of rod $L = 1.05\text{ m}$.
• Let mass $m$ be suspended at distance $x$ from the steel wire A.
• Distance from aluminium wire B is $(1.05 - x)$.
• Areas: $A_s = 1.0\text{ mm}^2$, $A_{al} = 2.0\text{ mm}^2$.
• Young’s moduli: $Y_s = 2.0 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$, $Y_{al} = 7.0 \times 10^{10}\text{ Pa} = 0.7 \times 10^{11}\text{ Pa}$.

Torque Equilibrium about point of suspension:
$$F_s \times x = F_{al} \times (1.05 - x) \implies \frac{F_s}{F_{al}} = \frac{1.05 - x}{x} \quad \text{--- (Eq 1)}$$

• (a) Condition for Equal Stresses:
$$\sigma_s = \sigma_{al} \implies \frac{F_s}{A_s} = \frac{F_{al}}{A_{al}} \implies \frac{F_s}{F_{al}} = \frac{A_s}{A_{al}} = \frac{1.0}{2.0} = 0.5$$
Substituting into Eq 1:
$$\frac{1.05 - x}{x} = 0.5 \implies 1.05 - x = 0.5 x \implies 1.5 x = 1.05$$
$$x = \frac{1.05}{1.5} = \mathbf{0.70\text{ m} = 70\text{ cm}}$$
$$\mathbf{\text{For equal stresses, mass should be suspended } 0.70\text{ m from the steel wire.}}$$

• (b) Condition for Equal Strains:
$$\epsilon_s = \epsilon_{al} \implies \frac{F_s}{A_s Y_s} = \frac{F_{al}}{A_{al} Y_{al}} \implies \frac{F_s}{F_{al}} = \frac{A_s Y_s}{A_{al} Y_{al}}$$
$$\frac{F_s}{F_{al}} = \frac{1.0 \times (2.0 \times 10^{11})}{2.0 \times (7.0 \times 10^{10})} = \frac{2.0}{1.4} = \frac{10}{7}$$
Substituting into Eq 1:
$$\frac{1.05 - x}{x} = \frac{10}{7} \implies 7(1.05 - x) = 10 x$$
$$7.35 - 7 x = 10 x \implies 17 x = 7.35$$
$$x = \frac{7.35}{17} \approx \mathbf{0.432\text{ m} = 43.2\text{ cm}}$$
$$\mathbf{\text{For equal strains, mass should be suspended } 0.43\text{ m from the steel wire.}}$$
