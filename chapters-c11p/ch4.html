Question 4.1
NCERT Exercise
Give the magnitude and direction of the net force acting on
(a) a drop of rain falling vertically with a constant speed,
(b) a cork of mass $10\text{ g}$ floating on water,
(c) a kite skillfully held stationary in the sky,
(d) a car moving with a constant velocity of $30\text{ km h}^{-1}$ on a rough road,
(e) a high-speed electron in space far from all gravitational objects, and free of electric and magnetic fields.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Newton’s First Law of Motion: If a body is at rest or moving with uniform velocity in a straight line, its acceleration is zero ($a = 0$), so the net external force acting on it is identically zero ($\vec{F}_{net} = m\vec{a} = \mathbf{0}$).

• (a) Raindrop falling at constant speed: Acceleration $a = 0$. Downward gravity is balanced by upward viscous air drag and buoyant force.
$$\mathbf{\text{Net force} = 0}$$
• (b) Cork of mass $10\text{ g}$ floating on water: Cork is at rest on the water surface ($a = 0$). Downward gravitational weight is exactly balanced by the upward buoyant upthrust.
$$\mathbf{\text{Net force} = 0}$$
• (c) Kite skillfully held stationary: The kite is at rest ($a = 0$). The upward aerodynamic lift is balanced by tension in the string and gravity.
$$\mathbf{\text{Net force} = 0}$$
• (d) Car moving with constant velocity $30\text{ km h}^{-1}$: Since velocity is constant (both in magnitude and direction), $a = 0$. The forward driving force of the engine exactly balances backward friction and air resistance.
$$\mathbf{\text{Net force} = 0}$$
• (e) High-speed electron far in field-free space: The electron experiences no gravitational, electric, or magnetic fields. There is no external agent exerting force.
$$\mathbf{\text{Net force} = 0}$$

Justification and zero net force for parts (a) and (b)1.5 Marks
Justification and zero net force for parts (c), (d), and (e)2.5 Marks
Question 4.2
NCERT Exercise
A pebble of mass $0.05\text{ kg}$ is thrown vertically upwards. Give the direction and magnitude of the net force on the pebble,
(a) during its upward motion,
(b) during its downward motion,
(c) at the highest point where it is momentarily at rest. Do your answers change if the pebble was thrown at an angle of $45^\circ$ with the horizontal direction? Ignore air resistance.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given mass of pebble $m = 0.05\text{ kg}$, $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$.
Once released, the only force acting on the pebble (neglecting air resistance) is the gravitational force of Earth:
$$F = mg = 0.05\text{ kg} \times 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2} = \mathbf{0.49\text{ N}}$$

• (a) During upward motion: Magnitude = $\mathbf{0.49\text{ N}}$, directed vertically downwards.
• (b) During downward motion: Magnitude = $\mathbf{0.49\text{ N}}$, directed vertically downwards.
• (c) At the highest point: Even though instantaneous velocity is zero, acceleration is still $g$. Magnitude = $\mathbf{0.49\text{ N}}$, directed vertically downwards.

• If thrown at $45^\circ$ angle: The horizontal velocity component ($u \cos 45^\circ$) remains constant, but the horizontal acceleration is zero ($a_x = 0$). The only acceleration is still $g$ vertically downwards. Therefore, the answer does not change; in all cases, the net force remains $\mathbf{0.49\text{ N}}$ vertically downwards.

Question 4.3
NCERT Exercise
Give the magnitude and direction of the net force acting on a stone of mass $0.1\text{ kg}$,
(a) just after it is dropped from the window of a stationary train,
(b) just after it is dropped from the window of a train running at a constant velocity of $36\text{ km h}^{-1}$,
(c) just after it is dropped from the window of a train accelerating with $1\text{ m s}^{-2}$,
(d) lying on the floor of a train which is accelerating with $1\text{ m s}^{-2}$, the stone being at rest relative to the train. Neglect air resistance throughout.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Mass $m = 0.1\text{ kg}$, $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$.

• (a) Dropped from stationary train: The moment the stone leaves the train, contact is broken. The only force acting is gravity:
$$F = mg = 0.1 \times 9.8 = \mathbf{0.98\text{ N, vertically downwards}}$$

• (b) Dropped from train with constant velocity $36\text{ km h}^{-1}$: The stone retains the horizontal velocity by inertia, but no horizontal force acts on it. The only force is gravity:
$$F = mg = \mathbf{0.98\text{ N, vertically downwards}}$$

• (c) Dropped from train accelerating with $1\text{ m s}^{-2}$: The moment it is dropped, no contact force or pseudo force exists from the train. The only acting force is gravity:
$$F = mg = \mathbf{0.98\text{ N, vertically downwards}}$$

• (d) Lying on floor of accelerating train ($a = 1\text{ m s}^{-2}$): The vertical normal reaction balances gravity ($N = mg$). Since the stone is at rest relative to the accelerating train, it shares the train’s acceleration ($a = 1\text{ m s}^{-2}$ in the horizontal forward direction), provided by static friction:
$$F_{net} = ma = 0.1\text{ kg} \times 1\text{ m s}^{-2} = \mathbf{0.1\text{ N, horizontally in the direction of motion of the train}}$$

Question 4.4
NCERT Exercise
One end of a string of length $l$ is connected to a particle of mass $m$ and the other to a small peg on a smooth horizontal table. If the particle moves in a circle with speed $v$ the net force on the particle (directed towards the centre) is:
(i) $T$,
(ii) $T - \frac{m v^2}{l}$,
(iii) $T + \frac{m v^2}{l}$,
(iv) $0$
[$T$ is the tension in the string]. [Choose the correct alternative].
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Correct Option: (i) $T$

Justification:
The particle is moving on a smooth horizontal table in a circular path. The normal reaction from the table balances the downward weight ($N = mg$).
The only physical horizontal force acting on the particle is the tension $T$ exerted by the string directed towards the centre peg.
According to Newton’s second law, this net inward force provides the required centripetal acceleration:
$$F_{net} = T = \frac{m v^2}{l}$$
Therefore, the net force acting on the particle directed towards the centre is simply $\mathbf{T}$.
(Note: $\frac{mv^2}{l}$ is the value of $m\vec{a}$, not an additional physical force).

Question 4.5
NCERT Exercise
A constant retarding force of $50\text{ N}$ is applied to a body of mass $20\text{ kg}$ moving initially with a speed of $15\text{ m s}^{-1}$. How long does the body take to stop?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Mass $m = 20\text{ kg}$
• Initial speed $u = 15\text{ m s}^{-1}$
• Final speed $v = 0$
• Retarding force $F = -50\text{ N}$

1. Retardation (acceleration):
$$a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{-50\text{ N}}{20\text{ kg}} = -2.5\text{ m s}^{-2}$$

2. Time taken to stop ($t$):
Using the first equation of motion $v = u + at$:
$$0 = 15 + (-2.5)t \implies 2.5 t = 15$$
$$t = \frac{15}{2.5} = \mathbf{6.0\text{ seconds}}$$

$$\therefore \text{The body takes } \mathbf{6.0\text{ seconds}} \text{ to come to rest.}$$

Question 4.6
NCERT Exercise
A constant force acting on a body of mass $3.0\text{ kg}$ changes its speed from $2.0\text{ m s}^{-1}$ to $3.5\text{ m s}^{-1}$ in $25\text{ s}$. The direction of the motion of the body remains unchanged. What is the magnitude and direction of the force?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Mass $m = 3.0\text{ kg}$
• Initial velocity $u = 2.0\text{ m s}^{-1}$
• Final velocity $v = 3.5\text{ m s}^{-1}$
• Time interval $t = 25\text{ s}$

1. Acceleration ($a$):
$$a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{3.5 - 2.0}{25} = \frac{1.5}{25} = 0.06\text{ m s}^{-2}$$

2. Magnitude of Force ($F$):
$$F = ma = 3.0\text{ kg} \times 0.06\text{ m s}^{-2} = \mathbf{0.18\text{ N}}$$

3. Direction of Force:
Since velocity increases in the original direction, the acceleration is in the direction of motion.
$$\mathbf{\text{The force is } 0.18\text{ N in the direction of motion of the body.}}$$

Question 4.7
NCERT Exercise
A body of mass $5\text{ kg}$ is acted upon by two perpendicular forces $8\text{ N}$ and $6\text{ N}$. Give the magnitude and direction of the acceleration of the body.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Mass $m = 5\text{ kg}$
• Perpendicular forces: $F_1 = 8\text{ N}$, $F_2 = 6\text{ N}$ (angle $\theta = 90^\circ$).

1. Magnitude of Resultant Force ($F$):
$$F = \sqrt{F_1^2 + F_2^2} = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{64 + 36} = \sqrt{100} = \mathbf{10\text{ N}}$$

2. Magnitude of Acceleration ($a$):
$$a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{10\text{ N}}{5\text{ kg}} = \mathbf{2.0\text{ m s}^{-2}}$$

3. Direction of Acceleration:
The acceleration is in the direction of the resultant force. Let $\theta$ be the angle made with the $8\text{ N}$ force:
$$\tan \theta = \frac{F_2}{F_1} = \frac{6}{8} = 0.75$$
$$\theta = \arctan(0.75) \approx \mathbf{36.87^\circ} \approx \mathbf{37^\circ}$$

$$\therefore \text{Acceleration has magnitude } \mathbf{2\text{ m s}^{-2}} \text{ directed at } \mathbf{37^\circ} \text{ to the } 8\text{ N force.}$$

Question 4.8
NCERT Exercise
The driver of a three-wheeler moving with a speed of $36\text{ km h}^{-1}$ sees a child standing in the middle of the road and brings his vehicle to rest in $4.0\text{ s}$ just in time to save the child. What is the average retarding force on the vehicle? The mass of the three-wheeler is $400\text{ kg}$ and the mass of the driver is $65\text{ kg}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Initial speed $u = 36\text{ km h}^{-1} = 36 \times \frac{5}{18} = 10\text{ m s}^{-1}$
• Final speed $v = 0$
• Time taken $t = 4.0\text{ s}$
• Total mass $M = \text{mass of vehicle} + \text{mass of driver} = 400 + 65 = 465\text{ kg}$

1. Retardation ($a$):
$$a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{0 - 10}{4.0} = -2.5\text{ m s}^{-2}$$

2. Average Retarding Force ($F$):
$$F = M |a| = 465\text{ kg} \times 2.5\text{ m s}^{-2} = \mathbf{1162.5\text{ N}} \approx \mathbf{1.16 \times 10^3\text{ N}}$$

$$\therefore \text{The average retarding force on the vehicle is } \mathbf{1162.5\text{ N}}.$$

Question 4.9
NCERT Exercise
A rocket with a lift-off mass $20,000\text{ kg}$ is blasted upwards with an initial acceleration of $5.0\text{ m s}^{-2}$. Calculate the initial thrust (force) of the blast.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Mass of rocket $m = 20,000\text{ kg} = 2 \times 10^4\text{ kg}$
• Upward acceleration $a = 5.0\text{ m s}^{-2}$
• Acceleration due to gravity $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$ (or $10\text{ m s}^{-2}$)

Equation of Motion:
Thrust force $T$ acts vertically upwards, while gravity $mg$ acts vertically downwards:
$$T - mg = ma \implies T = m(g + a)$$

Using $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$:
$$T = 20000 \times (9.8 + 5.0) = 20000 \times 14.8 = \mathbf{296,000\text{ N}} = \mathbf{2.96 \times 10^5\text{ N}}$$

(If $g = 10\text{ m s}^{-2}$ is used: $T = 20000 \times 15.0 = 3.0 \times 10^5\text{ N}$).
$$\therefore \text{Initial thrust of the blast is } \mathbf{2.96 \times 10^5\text{ N}} \text{ vertically upwards.}$$

Question 4.10
NCERT Exercise
A body of mass $0.40\text{ kg}$ moving initially with a constant speed of $10\text{ m s}^{-1}$ to the north is subject to a constant force of $8.0\text{ N}$ directed towards the south for $30\text{ s}$. Take the instant the force is applied to be $t = 0$, the position of the body at that time to be $x = 0$, and predict its position at $t = -5\text{ s}, 25\text{ s}, 100\text{ s}$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Choose North as positive ($+x$) and South as negative ($-x$).
Given: $m = 0.40\text{ kg}$, initial velocity $u = +10\text{ m s}^{-1}$ at $t = 0$, $x(0) = 0$.
Force for $0 \le t \le 30\text{ s}$: $F = -8.0\text{ N}$.
Acceleration during $0 \le t \le 30\text{ s}$:
$$a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{-8.0}{0.40} = -20\text{ m s}^{-2}$$

• 1. At $t = -5\text{ s}$ (before force is applied):
The body moved with uniform velocity $u = 10\text{ m s}^{-1}$ with $a = 0$.
$$x(-5) = u t = 10 \times (-5) = \mathbf{-50\text{ m}}$$

• 2. At $t = 25\text{ s}$ (during action of force):
$$x(25) = u t + \frac{1}{2} a t^2 = 10(25) + \frac{1}{2}(-20)(25)^2 = 250 - 10(625) = 250 - 6250 = \mathbf{-6000\text{ m} = -6.0\text{ km}}$$

• 3. At $t = 100\text{ s}$ (force ceases after $30\text{ s}$):
At $t = 30\text{ s}$, position is:
$$x(30) = 10(30) + \frac{1}{2}(-20)(30)^2 = 300 - 9000 = -8700\text{ m}$$
Velocity at $t = 30\text{ s}$:
$$v(30) = u + a t = 10 + (-20)(30) = 10 - 600 = -590\text{ m s}^{-1}$$
For the remaining interval of $70\text{ s}$ (from $t = 30$ to $100\text{ s}$), force is zero, so velocity remains constant at $-590\text{ m s}^{-1}$:
$$x(100) = x(30) + v(30) \times (100 - 30) = -8700 + (-590)(70) = -8700 - 41300 = \mathbf{-50000\text{ m} = -50\text{ km}}$$

$$\therefore \text{Positions are: } x(-5) = \mathbf{-50\text{ m}}, \quad x(25) = \mathbf{-6\text{ km}}, \quad x(100) = \mathbf{-50\text{ km}}.$$

Question 4.11
NCERT Exercise
A truck starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at $2.0\text{ m s}^{-2}$. At $t = 10\text{ s}$, a stone is dropped by a person standing on the top of the truck ($6\text{ m}$ high from the ground). What are the (a) velocity, and (b) acceleration of the stone at $t = 11\text{ s}$? (Neglect air resistance.)
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given: $u = 0$, $a_{truck} = 2.0\text{ m s}^{-2}$, stone dropped at $t = 10\text{ s}$.

• Horizontal velocity at the instant of drop ($t = 10\text{ s}$):
$$v_x = u + a_{truck} t = 0 + 2.0(10) = 20\text{ m s}^{-1}$$
After being dropped, no horizontal force acts on the stone (neglecting air resistance), so $v_x = 20\text{ m s}^{-1}$ remains constant.

• (a) Velocity at $t = 11\text{ s}$:
Time elapsed since drop: $\Delta t = 11 - 10 = 1\text{ s}$.
Vertical velocity acquired under gravity ($g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$):
$$v_y = 0 + g \Delta t = 9.8 \times 1 = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-1} \text{ (downwards)}$$
Net velocity magnitude:
$$v = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2} = \sqrt{20^2 + (9.8)^2} = \sqrt{400 + 96.04} = \sqrt{496.04} \approx \mathbf{22.27\text{ m s}^{-1} \approx 22.3\text{ m s}^{-1}}$$
Direction with horizontal:
$$\tan \theta = \frac{v_y}{v_x} = \frac{9.8}{20} = 0.49 \implies \theta = \arctan(0.49) \approx \mathbf{26.1^\circ} \text{ below the horizontal.}$$

• (b) Acceleration at $t = 11\text{ s}$:
Once released, the stone is a free projectile under gravity.
$$\mathbf{a = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2} \text{ vertically downwards.}}$$

Question 4.12
NCERT Exercise
A bob of mass $0.1\text{ kg}$ hung from the ceiling of a room by a string $2\text{ m}$ long is set into oscillation. The speed of the bob at its mean position is $1\text{ m s}^{-1}$. What is the trajectory of the bob if the string is cut when the bob is (a) at one of its extreme positions, (b) at its mean position?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• (a) Cut at one of the extreme positions:
At the extreme position of an oscillation, the instantaneous velocity of the bob is zero ($v = 0$).
When the string is cut, there is no initial horizontal velocity. The only force acting on the bob is gravity vertically downwards.
Therefore, the bob falls vertically downwards in a straight line under gravity.

• (b) Cut at its mean position:
At the mean position, the bob possesses a horizontal velocity of $v = 1\text{ m s}^{-1}$ directed tangential to its circular arc.
When the string is cut, it behaves like a projectile launched horizontally with initial velocity $u_x = 1\text{ m s}^{-1}$ and acceleration $g$ vertically downwards.
Therefore, the trajectory of the bob is a parabolic path until it hits the ground.

(a) Zero velocity at extreme leading to straight vertical free fall1.5 Marks
(b) Horizontal velocity at mean position resulting in parabolic trajectory1.5 Marks
Question 4.13
NCERT Exercise
A man of mass $70\text{ kg}$ stands on a weighing scale in a lift which is moving
(a) upwards with a uniform speed of $10\text{ m s}^{-1}$,
(b) downwards with a uniform acceleration of $5\text{ m s}^{-2}$,
(c) upwards with a uniform acceleration of $5\text{ m s}^{-2}$.
What would be the readings on the scale in each case?
(d) What would be the reading if the lift mechanism failed and it hurtled down freely under gravity? (Take $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$).
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given mass of man $m = 70\text{ kg}$, $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$.
The reading of the weighing scale is the normal reaction $R$ (apparent weight). Scale reading in $\text{kg-wt} = R/g$.

• (a) Uniform speed upwards ($a = 0$):
$$R = mg = 70 \times 9.8 = \mathbf{686\text{ N}}$$
$$\text{Reading} = \frac{686}{9.8} = \mathbf{70\text{ kg}}$$

• (b) Downward acceleration $a = 5\text{ m s}^{-2}$ ($a < g$):
$$R = m(g - a) = 70(9.8 - 5.0) = 70 \times 4.8 = \mathbf{336\text{ N}}$$
$$\text{Reading} = \frac{336}{9.8} \approx \mathbf{34.29\text{ kg}}$$

• (c) Upward acceleration $a = 5\text{ m s}^{-2}$:
$$R = m(g + a) = 70(9.8 + 5.0) = 70 \times 14.8 = \mathbf{1036\text{ N}}$$
$$\text{Reading} = \frac{1036}{9.8} \approx \mathbf{105.71\text{ kg}}$$

• (d) Free fall ($a = g$):
$$R = m(g - g) = \mathbf{0\text{ N}}$$
$$\text{Reading} = \mathbf{0\text{ kg}} \text{ (State of Weightlessness)}$$

Question 4.15
NCERT Exercise
Two bodies of masses $10\text{ kg}$ and $20\text{ kg}$ respectively, kept on a smooth, horizontal surface are tied to the ends of a light string. A horizontal force $F = 600\text{ N}$ is applied to (i) A, (ii) B along the direction of string. What is the tension in the string in each case?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given: $m_A = 10\text{ kg}$, $m_B = 20\text{ kg}$, $F = 600\text{ N}$.
Common acceleration of the combined system:
$$a = \frac{F}{m_A + m_B} = \frac{600}{10 + 20} = \frac{600}{30} = 20\text{ m s}^{-2}$$

• Case (i): Force applied to body A ($10\text{ kg}$):
The tension $T$ in the string is responsible for accelerating body B behind it:
$$T = m_B a = 20\text{ kg} \times 20\text{ m s}^{-2} = \mathbf{400\text{ N}}$$

• Case (ii): Force applied to body B ($20\text{ kg}$):
The tension $T$ in the string is responsible for accelerating body A behind it:
$$T = m_A a = 10\text{ kg} \times 20\text{ m s}^{-2} = \mathbf{200\text{ N}}$$

$$\therefore \text{Tension is } \mathbf{400\text{ N}} \text{ in Case (i), and } \mathbf{200\text{ N}} \text{ in Case (ii)}.$$

Question 4.16
NCERT Exercise
Two masses $8\text{ kg}$ and $12\text{ kg}$ are connected at the two ends of a light inextensible string that goes over a frictionless pulley. Find the acceleration of the masses, and the tension in the string when the masses are released.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given: $m_1 = 8\text{ kg}$, $m_2 = 12\text{ kg}$, $g = 9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}$.
This is Atwood's machine. Mass $m_2$ accelerates downwards while $m_1$ accelerates upwards.

1. Acceleration ($a$):
$$a = \frac{m_2 - m_1}{m_1 + m_2} g = \frac{12 - 8}{12 + 8} \times 9.8 = \frac{4}{20} \times 9.8 = \frac{9.8}{5} = \mathbf{1.96\text{ m s}^{-2}}$$

2. Tension in the string ($T$):
$$T = \frac{2 m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2} g = \frac{2 \times 8 \times 12}{8 + 12} \times 9.8 = \frac{192}{20} \times 9.8 = 9.6 \times 9.8 = \mathbf{94.08\text{ N}} \approx \mathbf{94.1\text{ N}}$$

$$\therefore \text{Acceleration } a = \mathbf{1.96\text{ m s}^{-2}}, \quad \text{Tension } T = \mathbf{94.1\text{ N}}.$$

Question 4.17
NCERT Exercise
A nucleus is at rest in the laboratory frame of reference. Show that if it disintegrates into two smaller nuclei the products must be emitted in opposite directions.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Conservation of Linear Momentum:
Let $M$ be the mass of the parent nucleus at rest.
Initial linear momentum of the nucleus:
$$\vec{P}_i = \mathbf{0}$$

Let the nucleus disintegrate into two fragments of masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ with final velocities $\vec{v}_1$ and $\vec{v}_2$ respectively.
Final linear momentum of the system:
$$\vec{P}_f = m_1\vec{v}_1 + m_2\vec{v}_2$$

Since no external force acts during the internal nuclear decay, the total linear momentum is conserved:
$$\vec{P}_f = \vec{P}_i \implies m_1\vec{v}_1 + m_2\vec{v}_2 = \mathbf{0}$$
$$m_1\vec{v}_1 = -m_2\vec{v}_2 \implies \vec{v}_1 = -\left(\frac{m_2}{m_1}\right)\vec{v}_2$$

Since masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ are positive scalars, the ratio $\frac{m_2}{m_1}$ is a positive real number.
The negative sign demonstrates that the velocity vector $\vec{v}_1$ is strictly antiparallel to $\vec{v}_2$.
$$\mathbf{\text{Hence, the two daughter fragments must move in diametrically opposite directions.}}$$

Question 4.18

Two billiard balls each of mass $0.05\text{ kg}$ moving in opposite directions with speed $6\text{ m s}^{-1}$ collide and rebound with the same speed. What is the impulse imparted to each ball due to the other?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given mass of each ball $m = 0.05\text{ kg}$, speed $u = 6\text{ m s}^{-1}$.
Let the initial direction of motion of the first ball be positive.

• For the first ball:
Initial momentum: $p_i = m u = 0.05 \times (+6) = +0.3\text{ kg m s}^{-1}$.
Final momentum after rebound: $p_f = m (-u) = 0.05 \times (-6) = -0.3\text{ kg m s}^{-1}$.
$$\text{Impulse imparted to first ball} = p_f - p_i = -0.3 - (+0.3) = \mathbf{-0.6\text{ kg m s}^{-1} = -0.6\text{ N s}}$$

• For the second ball:
Initial momentum: $p_i = m (-u) = -0.3\text{ kg m s}^{-1}$.
Final momentum after rebound: $p_f = m (+u) = +0.3\text{ kg m s}^{-1}$.
$$\text{Impulse imparted to second ball} = p_f - p_i = +0.3 - (-0.3) = \mathbf{+0.6\text{ kg m s}^{-1} = +0.6\text{ N s}}$$

$$\therefore \text{The magnitude of impulse imparted to each ball is } \mathbf{0.6\text{ N s}} \text{ (opposite in directions).}$$

A batsman deflects a ball by an angle of $45^\circ$ without changing its initial speed which is equal to $54\text{ km h}^{-1}$. What is the impulse imparted to the ball? (Mass of the ball is $0.15\text{ kg}$.)
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Mass of ball $m = 0.15\text{ kg}$
• Speed $u = 54\text{ km h}^{-1} = 54 \times \frac{5}{18} = 15\text{ m s}^{-1}$
• Total deflection angle $= 45^\circ$

Vector Resolution:
Let the line of symmetry bisecting the angle of deflection be the normal (perpendicular to the bat face).
Angle of incidence with normal: $\alpha = \frac{45^\circ}{2} = 22.5^\circ$.
Angle of reflection with normal: $\alpha = 22.5^\circ$.

• The component of velocity parallel to the bat surface remains unchanged: $v_{\parallel} = u \sin 22.5^\circ$.
• The component of velocity perpendicular to the bat surface is reversed from $+u \cos 22.5^\circ$ to $-u \cos 22.5^\circ$.

Impulse Imparted:
$$J = |\Delta p| = m |\Delta v_{\perp}| = m \left(u \cos 22.5^\circ - (-u \cos 22.5^\circ)\right) = 2 m u \cos 22.5^\circ$$
$$\cos 22.5^\circ = \sqrt{\frac{1 + \cos 45^\circ}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{1 + 0.7071}{2}} = \sqrt{0.85355} \approx 0.9239$$
$$J = 2 \times 0.15\text{ kg} \times 15\text{ m s}^{-1} \times 0.9239 = 4.5 \times 0.9239 = \mathbf{4.157\text{ N s} \approx 4.16\text{ N s}}$$

$$\therefore \text{The impulse imparted to the ball is } \mathbf{4.16\text{ N s}} \text{ directed normal to the bat away from it.}$$

A stone of mass $0.25\text{ kg}$ tied to the end of a string is whirled round in a circle of radius $1.5\text{ m}$ with a speed of $40\text{ rev./min}$ in a horizontal plane. What is the tension in the string? What is the maximum speed with which the stone can be whirled around if the string can withstand a maximum tension of $200\text{ N}$?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Given:
• Mass $m = 0.25\text{ kg}$, Radius $r = 1.5\text{ m}$
• Frequency $\nu = 40\text{ rev/min} = \frac{40}{60} = \frac{2}{3}\text{ rev s}^{-1}$
• Angular velocity $\omega = 2\pi \nu = 2\pi \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{4\pi}{3}\text{ rad s}^{-1} \approx 4.189\text{ rad s}^{-1}$

1. Tension in the string ($T$):
$$T = m \omega^2 r = 0.25 \times \left(\frac{4\pi}{3}\right)^2 \times 1.5 = 0.25 \times \frac{16\pi^2}{9} \times 1.5 = \frac{6\pi^2}{9} \approx \frac{6 \times 9.8696}{9} = \mathbf{6.58\text{ N} \approx 6.6\text{ N}}$$

2. Maximum speed ($v_{max}$) for $T_{max} = 200\text{ N}$:
$$T_{max} = \frac{m v_{max}^2}{r} \implies v_{max}^2 = \frac{T_{max} r}{m}$$
$$v_{max}^2 = \frac{200 \times 1.5}{0.25} = \frac{300}{0.25} = 1200$$
$$v_{max} = \sqrt{1200} = 20\sqrt{3} \approx \mathbf{34.64\text{ m s}^{-1}}$$

$$\therefore \text{Current tension } = \mathbf{6.6\text{ N}}, \quad \text{Maximum permissible speed } = \mathbf{34.6\text{ m s}^{-1}}.$$

Question 4.22
NCERT Exercise
If, in Exercise 4.21, the speed of the stone is increased beyond the maximum permissible value, and the string breaks suddenly, which of the following correctly describes the trajectory of the stone after the string breaks:
(a) the stone moves radially outwards,
(b) the stone flies off tangentially from the instant the string breaks,
(c) the stone moves off at an angle with the tangent whose magnitude depends on the speed of the particle?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
Correct Option: (b)

Physical Reason:
At any instant during circular motion, the velocity vector of the stone is oriented strictly tangentially to the circular path.
The tension in the string provides the centripetal force that continuously changes the direction of this tangential velocity.
The moment the string breaks, tension becomes zero ($T = 0$), so no centripetal force acts on the stone.
By Newton’s First Law of Motion (law of inertia), an object in motion continues in its state of uniform rectilinear motion along the direction it possessed at that instant.
$$\mathbf{\text{Therefore, the stone flies off tangentially from the point of break.}}$$

Question 4.23
NCERT Exercise
Explain why
(a) a horse cannot pull a cart and run in empty space,
(b) passengers are thrown forward from their seats when a speeding bus stops suddenly,
(c) it is easier to pull a lawn mower than to push it,
(d) a cricketer moves his hands backwards while holding a catch.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Solution
• (a) Horse in empty space: To move forward, the horse must push backward against the ground with its hooves. By Newton’s Third Law, the ground exerts an equal and opposite forward reaction force that propels the horse and cart forward. In empty space, there is no ground or external medium to provide this reaction force; hence, the horse cannot move.

• (b) Passengers thrown forward when bus stops: When the bus is moving, the passengers’ bodies share the forward velocity. When brakes are applied suddenly, the lower part of the body in contact with the seat comes to rest immediately, but the upper body tends to remain in motion due to inertia of motion (Newton’s First Law).

• (c) Pulling vs Pushing a lawn mower:
- When pushing, the applied force $F$ has a downward vertical component ($F \sin \theta$), increasing the effective normal reaction: $N = mg + F \sin \theta$. The friction force increases ($f_k = \mu N$).
- When pulling, the applied force has an upward vertical component ($F \sin \theta$), decreasing the normal reaction: $N = mg - F \sin \theta$. The friction force decreases ($f_k = \mu N$).
Hence, pulling encounters less friction and is much easier.

• (d) Cricketer moving hands backward: Impulse $J = F_{avg} \times \Delta t = \Delta p$. The momentum change $\Delta p$ is fixed. By pulling his hands backward, the cricketer increases the impact time interval $\Delta t$. Since $F_{avg} = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$, increasing $\Delta t$ significantly reduces the impact force exerted on his palms, preventing pain and injury.
