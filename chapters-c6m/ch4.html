📌 Key Statistical Concepts & Representations
  • Data: A collection of numbers gathered to give information. Raw data is unorganized data.
  • Tally Marks: Marks used for counting in groups of five: $\text{I, II, III, IIII, } \cancel{\text{IIII}}$ (fifth mark crosses the four vertical strokes).
  • Pictograph: Represents data through pictures or symbols of objects. A scale or key must indicate what each symbol represents (e.g., $\bigstar = 10\text{ books}$).
  • Bar Graph: A visual representation of numbers using bars of uniform width drawn horizontally or vertically with equal spacing between them. The length of each bar represents the given quantity according to a chosen scale on the axis.

Q6
Explain the essential differences between a pictograph and a bar graph. What are the advantages of using a bar graph over a pictograph for large quantities?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Differences & Advantages:
1. Representation: A pictograph uses visual symbols or drawings, whereas a bar graph uses rectangular bars of uniform width and varying heights.
2. Precision for Large Quantities: In pictographs, fractional or intermediate values (e.g. 73 when 1 symbol = 10) require difficult partial drawings. In bar graphs, any precise value can be accurately marked on a calibrated continuous axis.
3. Speed of Construction: Drawing numerous detailed icons is time-consuming; bars can be drawn swiftly using a ruler.

Q7
The scores of 7 students in a science quiz out of 25 are: 18, 22, 14, 25, 19, 22, 20.
(a) Find the highest and lowest scores.
(b) Calculate the range of the scores.
(c) Find the average (mean) score of the group.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Highest and Lowest:
- Highest score = 25
- Lowest score = 14
(b) Range:
$\text{Range} = \text{Highest Score} - \text{Lowest Score} = 25 - 14 = \mathbf{11}$.
(c) Mean Score:
$\text{Mean} = \frac{\text{Sum of scores}}{\text{Number of students}} = \frac{18 + 22 + 14 + 25 + 19 + 22 + 20}{7} = \frac{140}{7} = \mathbf{20}$.

Q8
What is meant by "scale" on a bar graph? Why is choosing an appropriate scale critical when representing large statistical datasets?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Definition of Scale:
The scale of a bar graph is the ratio that compares the unit length on the axis to the actual quantity represented (e.g., 1 cm = 100 people).
Importance:
If the scale chosen is too small (e.g. 1 unit = 1 person for 50,000 people), the graph will not fit on paper. If the scale is too large (e.g. 1 unit = 20,000 for values differing by 50), the bars will look identical and differences cannot be distinguished.

Q10
State whether each of the following statements is True or False. Justify your answer:
(a) In a bar graph, the widths of the bars can vary according to preference.
(b) The spaces between adjacent bars must always be uniform.
(c) A pictograph can only be drawn horizontally.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) False: The widths of all bars must be strictly uniform. Width carries no numerical meaning, only the bar length or height represents data.
(b) True: Equal spacing between bars is mandatory to maintain visual clarity and fair comparison.
(c) False: Pictographs can be drawn either horizontally (rows) or vertically (columns).

CBSE Competency-Based Question (School Survey Case Study)
Case Study: School Canteen Healthy Fruit Preference Survey:
The school wellness committee surveyed 400 middle-school students regarding their preferred fruit choice for the subsidized canteen menu. The recorded preferences were:
- Apple: 120 students
- Banana: 100 students
- Orange: 80 students
- Guava: 60 students
- Papaya: 40 students
(a) If you choose a scale of 1 unit length = 20 students, what will be the heights of the bars for Apple and Orange?
(b) What percentage of the surveyed students selected either Apple or Banana?
(c) Formulate a recommendation for the canteen manager based on this data.
Detailed Analytical Solution & Marking Scheme

(a) Bar Heights (Scale: 1 unit = 20 students):
- Height for Apple = $\frac{120}{20} = \mathbf{6\text{ units}}$.
- Height for Orange = $\frac{80}{20} = \mathbf{4\text{ units}}$.

(b) Percentage for Apple or Banana:
Total choosing Apple or Banana = $120 + 100 = 220$ students.
Percentage = $\frac{220}{400} \times 100 = \mathbf{55\%}$.
More than half of the student population prefers Apple or Banana.

(c) Canteen Recommendation:
The canteen manager should allocate at least 55% of the fruit procurement budget to Apples and Bananas to avoid food wastage, while stocking moderate quantities of Guava and Papaya.
