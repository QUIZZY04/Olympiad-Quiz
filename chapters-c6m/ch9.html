📌 Key Symmetry Concepts & Properties
  • Line of Symmetry (Axis of Symmetry): An imaginary line that divides a figure into two identical halves such that folding along the line makes both halves coincide exactly.
  • Lines of Symmetry in Regular Polygons: A regular polygon with $n$ sides has exactly $n$ lines of symmetry:
    • Equilateral Triangle: 3 lines of symmetry.
    • Square: 4 lines of symmetry (2 along medians, 2 along diagonals).
    • Regular Pentagon: 5 lines of symmetry.
    • Regular Hexagon: 6 lines of symmetry.
    • Circle: Infinitely many lines of symmetry (every diameter is an axis of symmetry).
  • Reflection and Symmetry: The object and its mirror reflection are symmetrical about the mirror line. The distance of an object point from the mirror line equals the distance of its image point.

NCERT Exercise 9.1: Identifying Lines of Symmetry
Q1
Find the number of lines of symmetry for each of the following shapes:
(a) An equilateral triangle
(b) An isosceles triangle
(c) A scalene triangle
(d) A square
(e) A rectangle
(f) A rhombus
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Equilateral Triangle: 3 lines of symmetry (along the altitudes / medians from each vertex).
(b) Isosceles Triangle: 1 line of symmetry (from the vertex angle bisecting the non-equal base).
(c) Scalene Triangle: 0 lines of symmetry (no two sides or angles are equal).
(d) Square: 4 lines of symmetry (2 along the midpoints of opposite sides, 2 along diagonals).
(e) Rectangle: 2 lines of symmetry (along the lines joining midpoints of opposite sides). Diagonals are NOT lines of symmetry.
(f) Rhombus: 2 lines of symmetry (along its two diagonals).

Q3
Why is a diagonal of a rectangle not a line of symmetry? Explain using a paper-folding argument.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Explanation:
When a rectangular sheet of paper is folded along one of its diagonals, the two opposite halves do not coincide exactly.
The corner of one half overlaps outside the boundary of the other half. For a line to be an axis of symmetry, the two folded parts must superimpose exactly and cover each other completely without any overhang. Hence, a diagonal of a non-square rectangle is not a line of symmetry.

Q4
State the number of lines of symmetry for:
(a) A regular pentagon    (b) A regular hexagon    (c) A regular octagon    (d) A circle
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
General Rule: A regular polygon of $n$ sides has exactly $n$ lines of symmetry.
(a) Regular Pentagon (5 equal sides): 5 lines of symmetry.
(b) Regular Hexagon (6 equal sides): 6 lines of symmetry.
(c) Regular Octagon (8 equal sides): 8 lines of symmetry.
(d) Circle: Infinitely many lines of symmetry (any straight line passing through its centre is an axis of symmetry).

Q5
What is reflection symmetry? How is the distance of an object point from the mirror line related to the distance of its image point?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Reflection Symmetry:
Reflection symmetry occurs when an object and its reflected image are symmetrical with respect to a mirror line (the axis of reflection).
Distance Property:
The distance of any object point from the mirror line is strictly equal to the distance of its corresponding image point from the mirror line ($d_1 = d_2$). Furthermore, the line segment joining the object and its image is perpendicular to the mirror line.

CBSE Competency-Based Question (Ceramic Tile Mosaic Design)
Case Study: Architectural Ceramic Tile Mosaic:
An interior designer creates a square floor tile of side 40 cm featuring a decorative star pattern.
(a) If the star pattern inside the square has 8-fold symmetry (4 lines of symmetry), what are the orientations of these 4 axes?
(b) If a single motif is painted at coordinates (10 cm, 5 cm) from the center, at what other three coordinates must identical motifs be placed to maintain reflection symmetry across both horizontal and vertical axes?
(c) Explain why symmetrical tiles simplify tiling patterns in buildings.
Detailed Analytical Solution & Marking Scheme

(a) Orientations of 4 Axes:
The 4 axes of symmetry of a square are: the vertical axis (y-axis), the horizontal axis (x-axis), and the two diagonal axes (sloping at 45° and 135°).

(b) Coordinates of Symmetrical Motifs:
Taking the center as origin $(0, 0)$:
- Original motif: $(10, 5)$
- Horizontal reflection (across y-axis): $(-10, 5)$
- Vertical reflection (across x-axis): $(10, -5)$
- Double reflection (across origin / both axes): $(-10, -5)$.

(c) Advantage in Architecture:
Symmetrical tiles can be rotated in any 90° orientation during laying without breaking the pattern continuity, significantly reducing installation errors and labor costs.
