Chapter 6: Perimeter and Area

NCERT Ganita Prakash (Class 6) — Perimeter of Polygons, Area of Rectangles & Squares, Grid Estimation & Mensuration Applications | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Mensuration Formulas & Units
  • Perimeter: The distance covered along the boundary forming a closed figure when you go around the figure once. Units: cm, m, km.
  • Perimeter Formulas:
    • Rectangle: $P = 2 \times (\text{Length} + \text{Breadth}) = 2(l + b)$.
    • Square: $P = 4 \times \text{Side} = 4s$.
    • Equilateral Triangle: $P = 3 \times \text{Side} = 3s$.
    • Regular Polygon of $n$ sides: $P = n \times \text{Side}$.
  • Area: The amount of surface enclosed by a closed figure. Units: sq cm ($\text{cm}^2$), sq m ($\text{m}^2$).
  • Area Formulas:
    • Rectangle: $\text{Area} = \text{Length} \times \text{Breadth} = l \times b$.
    • Square: $\text{Area} = \text{Side} \times \text{Side} = s^2$.
  • Estimating Area on Squared Paper: Count fully covered squares as 1, more than half as 1, exactly half as $\frac{1}{2}$, and ignore less than half.
NCERT Exercise 6.1: Perimeter of Polygons
Q1
Find the perimeter of each of the following figures:
(a) A rectangle of sides 15 cm and 9 cm.
(b) A square of side 8.5 cm.
(c) An equilateral triangle of side 12 cm.
(d) A regular hexagon of side 6 cm.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Rectangle: $P = 2(l + b) = 2(15 + 9) = 2(24) = \mathbf{48\text{ cm}}$.
(b) Square: $P = 4s = 4 \times 8.5 = \mathbf{34\text{ cm}}$.
(c) Equilateral Triangle: $P = 3s = 3 \times 12 = \mathbf{36\text{ cm}}$.
(d) Regular Hexagon: $P = 6s = 6 \times 6 = \mathbf{36\text{ cm}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Each correct perimeter calculation with units0.75 Mark each (Total 3 Marks)
Q2
A piece of string is 30 cm long. What will be the length of each side if the string is used to form:
(a) A square?
(b) An equilateral triangle?
(c) A regular hexagon?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
The perimeter of each figure equals the total string length = 30 cm.
(a) Square (4 equal sides): Side = $\frac{30}{4} = \mathbf{7.5\text{ cm}}$.
(b) Equilateral Triangle (3 equal sides): Side = $\frac{30}{3} = \mathbf{10\text{ cm}}$.
(c) Regular Hexagon (6 equal sides): Side = $\frac{30}{6} = \mathbf{5\text{ cm}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
1 Mark per part with division and units3 Marks
Q3
Two sides of a triangle are 12 cm and 14 cm. The perimeter of the triangle is 36 cm. What is the length of its third side?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Perimeter of triangle = Sum of all three sides.
$12 + 14 + \text{Third Side} = 36$
$26 + \text{Third Side} = 36$
$\text{Third Side} = 36 - 26 = \mathbf{10\text{ cm}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Setting up equation 26 + Third Side = 361 Mark
Answer 10 cm1 Mark
Q4
Find the cost of fencing a rectangular park of length 175 m and breadth 125 m at the rate of ₹ 12 per metre.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Step 1: Find perimeter of rectangular park:
$P = 2(l + b) = 2(175 + 125) = 2(300) = 600\text{ m}$.
Step 2: Cost of fencing:
$\text{Cost} = 600 \times \text{₹ } 12 = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 7,200}$.

NCERT Exercise 6.2: Area of Rectangles & Squares
Q5
Find the area of a rectangle whose length and breadth are 12 cm and 4 cm respectively. Also find the area of a square of side 7 cm.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Rectangle: $\text{Area} = l \times b = 12\text{ cm} \times 4\text{ cm} = \mathbf{48\text{ cm}^2}$.
Square: $\text{Area} = s^2 = 7\text{ cm} \times 7\text{ cm} = \mathbf{49\text{ cm}^2}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Rectangle area: 48 cm²1 Mark
Square area: 49 cm²1 Mark
Q6
The area of a rectangular garden 50 m long is 300 sq m. Find the width of the garden.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
$\text{Area} = \text{Length} \times \text{Width}$
$300 = 50 \times \text{Width}$
$\text{Width} = \frac{300}{50} = \mathbf{6\text{ metres}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Formula substitution: 300 = 50 × Width1 Mark
Answer: 6 metres1 Mark
Q7
What is the cost of tiling a rectangular plot of land 500 m long and 200 m wide at the rate of ₹ 8 per hundred sq m?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Step 1: Calculate area of plot:
$\text{Area} = 500\text{ m} \times 200\text{ m} = 1,00,000\text{ m}^2$.
Step 2: Cost rate is ₹ 8 per 100 sq m:
Number of hundreds = $\frac{1,00,000}{100} = 1,000$.
Total Cost = $1,000 \times \text{₹ } 8 = \mathbf{\text{₹ } 8,000}$.

Area calculation: 1,00,000 m²1.5 Marks

Q8
A room is 4 m long and 3 m 50 cm wide. How many square metres of carpet is needed to cover the floor of the room?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Length $l = 4\text{ m}$.
Breadth $b = 3\text{ m } 50\text{ cm} = 3.5\text{ m}$.
$\text{Area of floor} = l \times b = 4 \times 3.5 = \mathbf{14\text{ sq metres}}$.
Therefore, 14 sq m of carpet is needed.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Converting breadth to 3.5 m1 Mark
Calculating area 14 m²1 Mark
Q9
How many tiles whose length and breadth are 12 cm and 5 cm respectively will be needed to fit in a rectangular region whose length and breadth are 100 cm and 144 cm?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Step 1: Area of the rectangular region = $100\text{ cm} \times 144\text{ cm} = 14,400\text{ cm}^2$.
Step 2: Area of one tile = $12\text{ cm} \times 5\text{ cm} = 60\text{ cm}^2$.
Step 3: Number of tiles needed = $\frac{\text{Area of region}}{\text{Area of one tile}} = \frac{14,400}{60} = \mathbf{240\text{ tiles}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Area of region (14,400) and tile (60)1.5 Marks
Division yielding 240 tiles1.5 Marks
Q10
Can two different rectangles have the same perimeter but different areas? Justify your answer with two concrete numerical examples.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Yes.
Consider two rectangles with perimeter = 20 cm ($l + b = 10$):
- Rectangle 1: $l = 6\text{ cm}, b = 4\text{ cm}$.
Perimeter = $2(6+4) = 20\text{ cm}$. Area = $6 \times 4 = \mathbf{24\text{ cm}^2}$.
- Rectangle 2: $l = 8\text{ cm}, b = 2\text{ cm}$.
Perimeter = $2(8+2) = 20\text{ cm}$. Area = $8 \times 2 = \mathbf{16\text{ cm}^2}$.
Both have perimeter 20 cm, but their areas are 24 cm² and 16 cm².
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Affirmative statement "Yes"0.5 Mark
Two numerical examples with equal perimeter and distinct areas1.5 Marks
Q11
A wire of length 36 cm is bent in the form of a square. If the same wire is straightened and rebent into a rectangle of breadth 6 cm, find the length of the rectangle and compare the areas of both shapes.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Square: $4s = 36 \implies s = 9\text{ cm}$.
Area of square = $9 \times 9 = \mathbf{81\text{ cm}^2}$.
Rectangle: $2(l + 6) = 36 \implies l + 6 = 18 \implies l = \mathbf{12\text{ cm}}$.
Area of rectangle = $12 \times 6 = \mathbf{72\text{ cm}^2}$.
Comparison: The square encloses more area than the rectangle ($81\text{ cm}^2 > 72\text{ cm}^2$).

Square side 9 cm and area 81 cm²1 Mark
Rectangle length 12 cm and area 72 cm²1 Mark
Comparison conclusion1 Mark
CBSE Competency-Based Question (Sports Field Budgeting)
Case Study: School Football Ground & Running Track:
A standard school football pitch is 100 m long and 64 m wide. Surrounding the pitch is an athletics running track path of uniform width 3 m.
(a) Find the playing area of the football pitch.
(b) Find the outer perimeter of the running track.
(c) Calculate the total area of the running track path.
Detailed Analytical Solution & Marking Scheme

(a) Playing Area of Football Pitch:
$\text{Area} = 100\text{ m} \times 64\text{ m} = \mathbf{6,400\text{ m}^2}$.

(b) Outer Dimensions & Perimeter:
- Outer length = $100 + 2(3) = 106\text{ m}$.
- Outer breadth = $64 + 2(3) = 70\text{ m}$.
Outer Perimeter = $2(106 + 70) = 2(176) = \mathbf{352\text{ metres}}$.

(c) Area of the Running Track Path:
Outer Area = $106 \times 70 = 7,420\text{ m}^2$.
Inner Area = $6,400\text{ m}^2$.
$\text{Path Area} = 7,420 - 6,400 = \mathbf{1,020\text{ m}^2}$.
