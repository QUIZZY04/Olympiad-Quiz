Chapter 3: Number Play

NCERT Ganita Prakash (Class 6) — Place Value Systems, Large Numbers, Estimation, Brackets, Roman Numerals & Magic Squares | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Number Concepts & Place Value Systems
  • Indian System of Numeration: Commas after Hundreds (3 digits), then after every 2 digits: Crores, Ten Lakhs, Lakhs, Ten Thousands, Thousands, Hundreds, Tens, Ones. (e.g., $7,34,56,819$).
  • International System of Numeration: Commas after every 3 digits from right: Billions, Millions (Hundred, Ten, One), Thousands (Hundred, Ten, One), Hundreds, Tens, Ones. (e.g., $73,456,819$).
  • Estimation (Rounding Off): To round off to nearest 10, look at ones digit (< 5 round down, $\ge$ 5 round up). To nearest 100, look at tens digit; to nearest 1000, look at hundreds digit.
  • Use of Brackets & Order of Operations: Brackets indicate precedence. Rule: $\text{Bar} \to \text{Round}() \to \text{Curly}\{\} \to \text{Square}[]$. Within brackets, follow DMAS (Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction).
  • Roman Numerals: $I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100, D = 500, M = 1000$. Symbol cannot be repeated more than 3 times; $V, L, D$ are never repeated or subtracted.
  • Magic Squares: A square grid where numbers in each row, column, and both diagonals sum to the exact same constant (Magic Constant). For a $3 \times 3$ grid using numbers $1$ to $9$, Magic Sum $= 15$.
NCERT Exercise 3.1: Large Numbers & Numeration Systems
Q1
Write the number 8,75,95,762 according to:
(i) Indian System of Numeration with commas and in words.
(ii) International System of Numeration with commas and in words.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(i) Indian System of Numeration:
Placement of commas: 8,75,95,762
In words: Eight crore, seventy-five lakh, ninety-five thousand, seven hundred sixty-two.
(ii) International System of Numeration:
Re-grouping in sets of 3 digits from right: 87,595,762
In words: Eighty-seven million, five hundred ninety-five thousand, seven hundred sixty-two.

Q2
Find the difference between the place value and the face value of the digit 7 in the number 5,78,49,236.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Given number: 5,78,49,236.
The digit 7 is at the Ten Lakhs position.
- Place Value of 7 = $7 \times 10,00,000 = \mathbf{70,00,000}$.
- Face Value of 7 = $\mathbf{7}$.
Difference = $70,00,000 - 7 = \mathbf{69,99,993}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Identifying place value (70,00,000) and face value (7)1 Mark
Subtracting to get 69,99,9931 Mark
Q3
Use the digits 4, 0, 7, 1, 9, 5 to form:
(i) The greatest 6-digit number using each digit only once.
(ii) The smallest 6-digit number using each digit only once.
(iii) Find the difference between the greatest and smallest numbers formed.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(i) Greatest 6-digit number:
Arrange digits in descending order: 9, 7, 5, 4, 1, 0 $\implies \mathbf{9,75,410}$.
(ii) Smallest 6-digit number:
Since 0 cannot be the leading digit, start with the smallest non-zero digit 1, followed by 0, then remaining digits in ascending order: 1, 0, 4, 5, 7, 9 $\implies \mathbf{1,04,579}$.
(iii) Difference:
$9,75,410 - 1,04,579 = \mathbf{8,70,831}$.

Part (i): Forming 9,75,4101 Mark
Part (ii): Forming 1,04,579 (noting 0 cannot lead)1 Mark
Part (iii): Accurate difference 8,70,8311 Mark
NCERT Exercise 3.2: Estimation & Rounding Off
Q4
Round off the number 73,485 to the nearest:
(a) Tens
(b) Hundreds
(c) Thousands
(d) Ten thousands
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Nearest 10: Ones digit is 5 $\implies$ round up $\implies \mathbf{73,490}$.
(b) Nearest 100: Tens digit is 8 (> 5) $\implies$ round up $\implies \mathbf{73,500}$.
(c) Nearest 1000: Hundreds digit is 4 (< 5) $\implies$ round down $\implies \mathbf{73,000}$.
(d) Nearest 10,000: Thousands digit is 3 (< 5) $\implies$ round down $\implies \mathbf{70,000}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Each correct rounding with stated reason0.5 Mark each (Total 2 Marks)
Q5
Estimate the following using the general rule:
(a) 730 + 998
(b) 7,961 - 428
(c) 578 × 161
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
General Rule: Round off each number to its greatest place value.
(a) 730 + 998:
- 730 rounds off to nearest 100 $\implies$ 700.
- 998 rounds off to nearest 100 $\implies$ 1,000.
Estimated Sum = $700 + 1,000 = \mathbf{1,700}$.
(b) 7,961 - 428:
- 7,961 rounds to nearest 1,000 $\implies$ 8,000.
- 428 rounds to nearest 100 $\implies$ 400.
Estimated Difference = $8,000 - 400 = \mathbf{7,600}$.
(c) 578 × 161:
- 578 rounds to nearest 100 $\implies$ 600.
- 161 rounds to nearest 100 $\implies$ 200.
Estimated Product = $600 \times 200 = \mathbf{1,20,000}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Part (a): 1,7001 Mark
Part (b): 7,6001 Mark
Part (c): 1,20,0001 Mark
NCERT Exercise 3.3: Roman Numerals & Brackets
Q6
Write the following in Roman numerals:
(a) 69    (b) 98    (c) 144    (d) 490
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) 69: $60 + 9 = (50 + 10) + (10 - 1) = \text{LX} + \text{IX} = \mathbf{LXIX}$.
(b) 98: $90 + 8 = (100 - 10) + 8 = \text{XC} + \text{VIII} = \mathbf{XCVIII}$.
(c) 144: $100 + 40 + 4 = \text{C} + (50 - 10) + (5 - 1) = \text{C} + \text{XL} + \text{IV} = \mathbf{CXLIV}$.
(d) 490: $400 + 90 = (500 - 100) + (100 - 10) = \text{CD} + \text{XC} = \mathbf{CDXC}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Each correct Roman numeral with expansion0.5 Mark each (Total 2 Marks)
Q7
Simplify the following expressions using brackets and order of operations:
(i) 15 + (12 - 3) × 4
(ii) 45 - [38 - {60 ÷ 3 - (6 - 9 ÷ 3)}]
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(i) 15 + (12 - 3) × 4:
Step 1 (Inside bracket): $12 - 3 = 9$.
Step 2 (Multiplication): $9 \times 4 = 36$.
Step 3 (Addition): $15 + 36 = \mathbf{51}$.
(ii) 45 - [38 - {60 ÷ 3 - (6 - 9 ÷ 3)}]:
Innermost round bracket $(6 - 9 \div 3)$: $9 \div 3 = 3$, so $6 - 3 = 3$.
Next curly bracket: $\{60 \div 3 - 3\} = \{20 - 3\} = 17$.
Next square bracket: $[38 - 17] = 21$.
Final subtraction: $45 - 21 = \mathbf{24}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Part (i): Step-by-step simplification to 511 Mark
Part (ii): Nested bracket hierarchy correctly executed to 242 Marks
NCERT Exercise 3.4: Magic Squares & Word Problems
Q8
A 3 × 3 magic square contains numbers 1 to 9 without repetition. The sum of numbers in every row, column, and diagonal is 15.
(i) Explain why the central number must be 5.
(ii) Construct the complete 3 × 3 magic square.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(i) Proof that Central Number is 5:
The sum of numbers 1 to 9 is: $\frac{9 \times 10}{2} = 45$.
Since there are 3 rows, Magic Sum = $\frac{45}{3} = 15$.
The center cell belongs to 4 lines (row 2, col 2, and both diagonals). Adding these 4 lines:
$4 \times 15 = 60$.
In this sum, all 9 cells are covered, plus the center cell is counted 3 extra times:
$45 + 3 \times (\text{Center}) = 60 \implies 3 \times (\text{Center}) = 15 \implies \mathbf{\text{Center} = 5}$.
(ii) Completed 3 × 3 Magic Square:
 8  |  1  |  6
---+---+---
 3  |  5  |  7
---+---+---
 4  |  9  |  2

Row sums: $8+1+6=15$; $3+5+7=15$; $4+9+2=15$. Diagonals: $8+5+2=15$; $6+5+4=15$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Part (i): Mathematical proof that Center = 51.5 Marks
Part (ii): Correct 3 × 3 grid verification1.5 Marks
Q9
A medicine manufacturing company packs tablets in boxes. Each box contains 2,00,000 tablets, and each tablet weighs 20 mg. Find the total weight of all tablets in the box in:
(i) Milligrams    (ii) Grams    (iii) Kilograms
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(i) Total weight in milligrams:
Weight = $2,00,000 \times 20\text{ mg} = \mathbf{40,00,000\text{ mg}}$.
(ii) Total weight in grams:
Since $1\text{ g} = 1,000\text{ mg}$:
Weight = $\frac{40,00,000}{1,000} = \mathbf{4,000\text{ grams}}$.
(iii) Total weight in kilograms:
Since $1\text{ kg} = 1,000\text{ g}$:
Weight = $\frac{4,000}{1,000} = \mathbf{4\text{ kilograms}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Part (i): 40,00,000 mg1 Mark
Part (ii): 4,000 g1 Mark
Part (iii): 4 kg1 Mark
Q10
A merchant had ₹ 78,592 with her. She placed an order for purchasing 40 radio sets at ₹ 1,200 each. How much money will remain with her after the purchase?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Total money with merchant = ₹ 78,592.
Cost of 1 radio set = ₹ 1,200.
Cost of 40 radio sets = $40 \times 1,200 = \text{₹ } 48,000$.
Money remaining = ₹ 78,592 - ₹ 48,000 = ₹ 30,592.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Calculating total expenditure ₹ 48,0001 Mark
Subtracting to get ₹ 30,5921 Mark
Q11
A vessel has 4 litres and 500 mL of curd. In how many glasses, each of 25 mL capacity, can it be filled?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Total capacity of vessel = 4 L 500 mL.
Convert to millilitres: $4 \times 1,000\text{ mL} + 500\text{ mL} = 4,500\text{ mL}$.
Capacity of each glass = 25 mL.
Number of glasses = $\frac{4,500}{25} = \mathbf{180\text{ glasses}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Conversion: 4 L 500 mL = 4,500 mL1 Mark

Q12
To stitch a shirt, 2 m 15 cm cloth is needed. Out of 40 m cloth, how many shirts can be stitched and how much cloth will remain?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Convert measurements into centimetres:
- Cloth for 1 shirt = $2\text{ m } 15\text{ cm} = 200 + 15 = 215\text{ cm}$.
- Total available cloth = $40\text{ m} = 40 \times 100 = 4,000\text{ cm}$.
Dividing 4,000 by 215:
$4,000 = 215 \times 18 + 130$.
Quotient = 18; Remainder = 130 cm = 1 m 30 cm.
Answer: 18 shirts can be stitched and 1 m 30 cm cloth will remain.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Conversion to centimetres (215 cm and 4,000 cm)1 Mark
Concluding 18 shirts and 1 m 30 cm cloth left1 Mark
CBSE Competency-Based Question (Electoral Data Case Study)
Case Study: Assembly Election Voter Turnout Analysis:
In an assembly election, the winning candidate registered 5,77,500 votes and his nearest rival secured 3,48,700 votes. A third independent candidate secured 42,350 votes, while 7,450 votes were declared invalid.
(a) By what margin of votes did the successful candidate win the election?
(b) Find the total number of votes polled in the constituency.
(c) Express the total votes polled in the International System of Numeration.
Detailed Analytical Solution & Marking Scheme

(a) Victory Margin:
Margin = Winning votes - Nearest rival votes
Margin = $5,77,500 - 3,48,700 = \mathbf{2,28,800\text{ votes}}$.

(b) Total Votes Polled:
Total votes = $5,77,500 + 3,48,700 + 42,350 + 7,450 = \mathbf{9,76,000\text{ votes}}$.

(c) International System Notation:
Grouping by sets of three digits: 976,000.
In words: Nine hundred seventy-six thousand.
