Chapter 7: Fractions

NCERT Ganita Prakash (Class 6) — Proper, Improper & Mixed Fractions, Number Line Models, Equivalent Fractions, Simplest Form & Arithmetic | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Fraction Concepts & Operation Rules
  • Fraction: A number representing a part of a whole. In $\frac{a}{b}$, $a$ is numerator and $b$ is denominator ($b \neq 0$).
  • Types of Fractions:
    • Proper Fraction: Numerator < Denominator (value < 1), e.g., $\frac{3}{7}$.
    • Improper Fraction: Numerator $\ge$ Denominator (value $\ge$ 1), e.g., $\frac{11}{4}$.
    • Mixed Fraction: Combination of a whole number and a proper fraction, e.g., $2\frac{3}{4} = \frac{2 \times 4 + 3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$.
  • Equivalent Fractions: Fractions having identical values, obtained by multiplying or dividing numerator and denominator by the same non-zero integer.
  • Simplest Form: A fraction $\frac{a}{b}$ is in simplest form if $\text{HCF}(a, b) = 1$.
  • Addition & Subtraction: Convert unlike fractions to like fractions using the LCM of their denominators before adding or subtracting.
NCERT Exercise 7.1: Visual Fractions & Number Line
Q1
Write the fraction representing the shaded portion in the following figures:
(a) A circle divided into 8 equal parts with 3 parts shaded.
(b) A rectangle divided into 6 equal parts with 5 parts shaded.
(c) What fraction of a day is 8 hours?
(d) What fraction of an hour is 40 minutes?
Visual Representations: Shaded Fractions
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Circle: Shaded parts = 3, Total parts = 8 $\implies \mathbf{\frac{3}{8}}$.
(b) Rectangle: Shaded parts = 5, Total parts = 6 $\implies \mathbf{\frac{5}{6}}$.
(c) 8 hours of a day (24 hours): $\frac{8}{24} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{3}}$.
(d) 40 minutes of an hour (60 minutes): $\frac{40}{60} = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Each correct fraction in lowest form0.5 Mark each (Total 2 Marks)
Q2
Represent the following fractions on a number line:
(a) 2/5    (b) 3/5    (c) 7/5
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Construction on Number Line:
1. Draw a straight line and mark 0, 1, and 2.
2. Divide the unit interval between 0 and 1 into 5 equal parts (each step is $\frac{1}{5}$).
3. Point at 2 steps from 0 marks 2/5.
4. Point at 3 steps from 0 marks 3/5.
5. Divide the interval between 1 and 2 into 5 equal parts. Point at 2 steps beyond 1 ($1 + \frac{2}{5} = \frac{7}{5}$) marks 7/5.

NCERT Exercise 7.2: Types of Fractions & Conversions
Q3
(a) Express the following as mixed fractions: (i) 20/3    (ii) 27/5
(b) Express the following as improper fractions: (i) 7 3/4    (ii) 5 6/7
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Mixed Fractions:
(i) $20 \div 3 = 6$ (quotient) with remainder 2 $\implies \mathbf{6\frac{2}{3}}$.
(ii) $27 \div 5 = 5$ (quotient) with remainder 2 $\implies \mathbf{5\frac{2}{5}}$.
(b) Improper Fractions:
(i) $7\frac{3}{4} = \frac{7 \times 4 + 3}{4} = \frac{28 + 3}{4} = \mathbf{\frac{31}{4}}$.
(ii) $5\frac{6}{7} = \frac{5 \times 7 + 6}{7} = \frac{35 + 6}{7} = \mathbf{\frac{41}{7}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Each conversion correct0.5 Mark each (Total 2 Marks)
NCERT Exercise 7.3: Equivalent Fractions & Simplest Form
Q4
Find the equivalent fraction of 3/5 having:
(a) Denominator 20
(b) Numerator 9
(c) Denominator 30
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Denominator 20: Multiply numerator and denominator by 4:
$\frac{3 \times 4}{5 \times 4} = \mathbf{\frac{12}{20}}$.
(b) Numerator 9: Multiply numerator and denominator by 3:
$\frac{3 \times 3}{5 \times 3} = \mathbf{\frac{9}{15}}$.
(c) Denominator 30: Multiply numerator and denominator by 6:
$\frac{3 \times 6}{5 \times 6} = \mathbf{\frac{18}{30}}$.

1 Mark per correct equivalent fraction3 Marks
Q5
Reduce the following fractions to their simplest (lowest) form by dividing by HCF:
(a) 48/60    (b) 84/98    (c) 12/52
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) 48/60: $\text{HCF}(48, 60) = 12$.
$\frac{48 \div 12}{60 \div 12} = \mathbf{\frac{4}{5}}$.
(b) 84/98: $\text{HCF}(84, 98) = 14$.
$\frac{84 \div 14}{98 \div 14} = \mathbf{\frac{6}{7}}$.
(c) 12/52: $\text{HCF}(12, 52) = 4$.
$\frac{12 \div 4}{52 \div 4} = \mathbf{\frac{3}{13}}$.

1 Mark per reduction with HCF step3 Marks
NCERT Exercise 7.4: Fraction Comparison & Ordering
Q6
Compare the following pairs of fractions and place appropriate sign (<, =, >):
(a) 3/6 and 5/6
(b) 1/4 and 1/7
(c) 4/5 and 5/6
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) 3/6 and 5/6: Like fractions; since $3 < 5$, $\mathbf{\frac{3}{6} < \frac{5}{6}}$.
(b) 1/4 and 1/7: Same numerator; the fraction with the smaller denominator is larger: $\mathbf{\frac{1}{4} > \frac{1}{7}}$.
(c) 4/5 and 5/6: Unlike fractions. $\text{LCM}(5, 6) = 30$.
$\frac{4}{5} = \frac{24}{30}$ and $\frac{5}{6} = \frac{25}{30}$.
Since $24 < 25$, $\mathbf{\frac{4}{5} < \frac{5}{6}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
1 Mark per comparison with clear reasoning3 Marks
NCERT Exercise 7.5: Addition & Subtraction of Fractions
Q7
Solve the following:
(a) 1/18 + 1/18
(b) 8/15 + 3/15
(c) 7/7 - 5/7
(d) 1 - 2/3
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) $\frac{1}{18} + \frac{1}{18} = \frac{2}{18} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{9}}$.
(b) $\frac{8}{15} + \frac{3}{15} = \mathbf{\frac{11}{15}}$.
(c) $\frac{7}{7} - \frac{5}{7} = \mathbf{\frac{2}{7}}$.
(d) $1 - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{3} - \frac{2}{3} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{3}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Each correct evaluation in simplest form0.5 Mark each (Total 2 Marks)
Q8
Find the sum: 2/3 + 3/4 + 1/2.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Step 1: Find LCM of denominators 3, 4, and 2:
$\text{LCM}(3, 4, 2) = 12$.
Step 2: Convert to like fractions:
$\frac{2}{3} = \frac{8}{12}$; $\frac{3}{4} = \frac{9}{12}$; $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{6}{12}$.
Step 3: Add numerators:
$\text{Sum} = \frac{8 + 9 + 6}{12} = \frac{23}{12} = \mathbf{1\frac{11}{12}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
LCM = 12 and conversion to like fractions1 Mark
Sum = 23/12 or 1 11/121 Mark
Q9
Subtract 2 1/5 from 4 2/3.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions:
$4\frac{2}{3} = \frac{14}{3}$; $2\frac{1}{5} = \frac{11}{5}$.
$\text{LCM}(3, 5) = 15$.
$\frac{14}{3} = \frac{70}{15}$; $\frac{11}{5} = \frac{33}{15}$.
Difference = $\frac{70 - 33}{15} = \frac{37}{15} = \mathbf{2\frac{7}{15}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Improper conversion and LCM = 151 Mark
Answer 37/15 or 2 7/151 Mark
Q10
Sarita bought 2/5 metre of ribbon and Lalita 3/4 metre of ribbon. What is the total length of the ribbon they bought?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Total length = $\frac{2}{5} + \frac{3}{4}\text{ metres}$.
$\text{LCM}(5, 4) = 20$.
$\frac{2}{5} = \frac{8}{20}$; $\frac{3}{4} = \frac{15}{20}$.
Total = $\frac{8 + 15}{20} = \frac{23}{20} = \mathbf{1\frac{3}{20}\text{ metres}}$ (or 1.15 m).
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Formulating sum with LCM = 201 Mark
Answer 23/20 m or 1 3/20 m1 Mark
Q11
A piece of wire 7/8 metre long broke into two pieces. One piece was 1/4 metre long. How long is the other piece?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Length of other piece = Total length - First piece
$= \frac{7}{8} - \frac{1}{4}$.
$\text{LCM}(8, 4) = 8$.
$\frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{8}$.
Length = $\frac{7 - 2}{8} = \mathbf{\frac{5}{8}\text{ metre}}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27
Subtraction setup with LCM1 Mark
Answer: 5/8 metre1 Mark
CBSE Competency-Based Question (Culinary Recipe Fractions)
Case Study: Culinary Recipe Scaling & Nutritional Fractions:
A chef is baking a traditional carrot cake. The original recipe requires $2\frac{1}{2}$ cups of flour, $\frac{3}{4}$ cup of brown sugar, and $1\frac{1}{4}$ cups of grated carrots.
(a) If the chef wants to bake double the batch (2 times), how much flour and brown sugar will be required?
(b) If the chef has only 3 cups of flour available, how much more flour is needed for the double batch?
(c) Express the total liquid sweetener and vegetable ratio as a combined fraction.
Detailed Analytical Solution & Marking Scheme

(a) Doubling the Ingredients:
- Flour = $2 \times 2\frac{1}{2} = 2 \times \frac{5}{2} = \mathbf{5\text{ cups}}$.
- Sugar = $2 \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2} = \mathbf{1\frac{1}{2}\text{ cups}}$.

(b) Extra Flour Needed:
$5\text{ cups} - 3\text{ cups} = \mathbf{2\text{ cups of flour}}$.

(c) Combined Sugar and Carrots:
$\frac{3}{4} + 1\frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4} + \frac{5}{4} = \frac{8}{4} = \mathbf{2\text{ cups}}$.
