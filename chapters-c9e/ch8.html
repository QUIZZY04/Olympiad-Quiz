I. Thinking about the Text — Short Questions (30–40 Words / 2–3 Marks)
Q1
Contrast the atmosphere at Pashupatinath temple with that at the Baudhnath stupa.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Pashupatinath Temple (Feverish Chaos):
The Hindu shrine of Pashupatinath exists in an atmosphere of 'febrile confusion'. Priests, hawkers, devotees, tourists, cows, monkeys, and pigeons roam the grounds. Devotees jostle and elbow each other for entrance; a royal princess arrives causing bowing; monkeys fight and leap over shivalingas; and cremations take place along the sacred Bagmati river amidst washerwomen and trash.

2. Baudhnath Stupa (Immaculate Serenity):
In stark contrast, the Buddhist stupa of Baudhnath offers a haven of profound silence, peace, and stillness. Its immense white dome is ringed by a quiet road lined with small Tibetan shops selling felt bags and silver jewelry. There are no crowds, noise, or pushiness; it radiates an oasis of calm detachment.

Q4
How does Vikram Seth describe the flute seller in Kathmandu? (Max Word Count)
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Appearance and Setup:
Near his hotel, Vikram Seth observes a flute seller standing in the corner of a square. In his hand, he holds a pole from which protrudes an attachment carrying fifty or sixty bamboo flutes in all directions, resembling the quills of a porcupine. The instruments are classical bansuris, reed flutes, and recorders.

2. Unique Demeanor:
Unlike aggressive street hawkers who yell loudly to attract customers, the flute seller does not shout or pester people. He places the pole on the ground, selects a flute, and plays meditatively, slowly, and without excessive display. His music rises clearly above the din of traffic and hawkers' cries.

3. Casual Business:
Occasionally he makes a sale, but in an offhanded, casual manner, as if selling flutes were merely incidental to his artistic pleasure. Then he returns to playing his flute with quiet absorption.

II. Long Answer Questions — Philosophical Reflection on the Flute (100–120 Words / 6 Marks)
Q5
Why does Vikram Seth find the music of the flute so universally compelling and deeply moving? (Max Word Count)
6 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Universal Presence of the Flute:
Vikram Seth observes that the flute is the most universal and common musical instrument across all human cultures. Every society has its own variant: the reed neh of the Middle East, the cross-flutes of Japan, the high-pitched folk flutes of South America, the classical bansuri of India, and the European recorder. While each differs in finger-holes, compass, and fingering, all flutes share an identical essence.

2. The Flute as Metaphor for Human Life:
Seth reflects that the flute operates through the most fundamental human function: the breath of life. To produce music, a human must breathe into the instrument, pausing periodically to inhale. The music pauses and resumes just as human existence pulses with each living breath.

3. Emotional Resonance:
Listening to the Kathmandu bansuri, Seth finds himself transported. His awareness shifts from superficial travel impressions to the profound oneness of all human beings, united by the universal language of breath and melody.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 8CBSE 2026-27
Sensory Imagery & Style
Literary Craft: How does Vikram Seth use vivid sensory imagery to bring the streets of Kathmandu alive?

Seth engages all five senses to create a vibrant mosaic of Kathmandu. Visually, he juxtaposes the chaotic crowds and flower-strewn deities of Pashupatinath with the immaculate white dome of Baudhnath. Auditory details abound—blaring car horns, bicycle bells, Hindi film songs from radios, and the serene notes of the bansuri. Smells of incense smoke mingle with exhaust fumes and cremations by the river, while tastes of roasted corn and marzipan sweets evoke street life. Through precise verbs and lively adjectives, Seth immerses the reader into the living texture of the city.

Value-Based Reflection
Spiritual Contrasts: What philosophical insight emerges from comparing Pashupatinath with Baudhnath?

Pashupatinath and Baudhnath represent two complementary facets of Eastern spirituality. Pashupatinath reflects the passionate, colorful, and noisy engagement with worldly life—devotion, trade, animals, and death existing side by side. Baudhnath embodies Buddhist tranquility, detachment, and peaceful contemplation. Together, they show that spirituality embraces both the turbulent storm of everyday existence and the still, silent harbor of inner peace.
