Chapter 1: The Lost Child

Mulk Raj Anand — Innocence, Fair Temptations & Filial Bond — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I. Thinking about the Text — Short Questions (30–40 Words / 2–3 Marks)
Q1
What are the things the child sees on his way to the fair? Why does he lag behind?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

On his way to the spring fair, the child is captivated by a kaleidoscope of sights: toy shops lined along the road, bright dragonflies fluttering their purple wings over a mustard field, little insects and worms crawling along the path, and showers of fragrant young flower petals falling from groves.

He lags behind his parents repeatedly because he stops in fascination to gaze at each novelty, lost in wonder until his parents call out: 'Come, child, come!'

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Sights seen: Toys, dragonflies, mustard field, insects, falling flower petals1½ Marks
Reason for lagging: Fascination and childlike absorption in nature and toys1½ Marks
Q2
In the fair, what things does the child want? Why does he not wait for an answer?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In the bustling fair, the child is enticed by several pleasures:

  • Mouth-watering sweets (especially his favorite gulab-jamun and burfi).
  • A garland of fragrant gulmohur flowers.
  • A rainbow-colored cluster of flying balloons.
  • The mesmerizing tune played by a snake charmer.

He does not wait for an answer because he knows from past experience that his parents will dismiss his requests with stern looks or excuses—calling him greedy, calling the garland cheap, or calling the snake music coarse.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Enumerating desires: Burfi, gulmohur garland, balloons, snake charmer1½ Marks
Reason for not waiting: Pre-empting parents' routine refusal and excuses1½ Marks
Q3
When does the child realize that he has lost his parents? How have his anxiety and insecurity been described?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. The Moment of Realization:
The child watches people enjoying the spinning roundabout. Gathering courage, he makes a bold request: 'I want to go on the roundabout, please, Father, Mother.' When no reply comes, he turns around. His parents are nowhere to be seen. He looks all around—front, back, sides—they have vanished.

2. Visceral Description of Panic:
A deep, full cry rises from his dry throat, and with a sudden jerk of his body, he runs frantically in blind terror. Tears roll down from his eyes, hot and fierce. His face is flushed with panic; his turban comes untied and his clothes become soaked and muddy as he runs hysterically through the jostling crowd, shrieking: 'Mother, Father!'

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Roundabout incident: Bold request met with silence; realization of separation2 Marks
Physical/emotional panic: Tears, screams, untied yellow turban, frantic running2 Marks
Q4
Why does the lost child lose interest in the things he had wanted earlier? What does the kind stranger offer him?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. The Stranger's Generous Offers:
A compassionate stranger rescues the child from being trampled beneath the crowd at the temple shrine. To soothe his hysterical weeping, the man carries him to all the attractions the child had previously coveted—the roundabout swing, the snake charmer, a bright balloon, a fragrant gulmohur garland, and the sweet shop offering sweets.

2. Refusal and Underlying Truth:
The child turns his eyes and face away from every single offering, sobbing inconsolably: 'I want my mother, I want my father!' All the glittering material temptations of the fair lose their magic in an instant. The child realizes that worldly pleasures are utterly worthless without the safety, love, and presence of his parents. Parental security is the supreme foundation of a child's universe.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Stranger's attempts: Offering roundabout, snake music, balloons, flowers, sweets2 Marks
Child's complete rejection: Sobbing only for mother and father1½ Marks
Theme: Primacy of parental love over material consumerism1½ Marks
Q5
How does Mulk Raj Anand convey the dramatic psychological transformation in the child when he realizes his parents are missing at the roundabout?
3 Marks (50–60 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The author portrays a poignant shift from joyful consumer greed to pure existential panic:

  • Prior to Separation: The child was intoxicated by the spring fair, eagerly gazing at burfi, gulmohur garlands, rainbow balloons, snake charmer music, and toy stalls, lingering behind his parents with pleading eyes.
  • The Moment of Realization: Watching the swirling roundabout, he boldly asked for a ride. Getting no reply, he turned around and discovered his parents were nowhere in sight.
  • Desperate Panic: A full, deep cry rose within his dry throat; with tears rolling down his flushed cheeks and clothes disheveled, he ran frantically in all directions crying: 'Mother, Father!'
  • Total Rejection of Material Luxuries: When a kindly stranger rescued him and offered him sweets, balloons, and garland to soothe him, the child pushed them aside, sobbing only: 'I want my mother, I want my father!' Parental love instantly outweighed all worldly desires.
CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Shift from festive fascination to pure existential terror1.5 Marks
Rejection of sweets, balloons, and toys in favor of parents' safety1.5 Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 18CBSE 2026-27
Child Psychology
Psychological Insight: How does Mulk Raj Anand explore child psychology through the shifting desires of the protagonist?

Anand masterfully captures the dual nature of childhood desire. In the security of his parents' presence, the child is intensely curious and attracted to worldly pleasures—sweets, bright colors, toys, and music. However, this desire is entirely dependent on emotional safety. The moment parental security is severed, material desires instantly evaporate, replaced by primal panic. The child's refusal of the very burfi and balloons he cried for minutes earlier demonstrates that for a child, emotional attachment infinitely outweighs physical possessions.

Value-Based Reflection
Compassion in Society: What does the role of the unknown stranger signify in 'The Lost Child'?

The unnamed stranger represents the universal spirit of human kindness and empathy. Amidst an indifferent, jostling crowd where the child could have easily been trampled to death, the stranger risks himself, lifts the sobbing boy into his arms, and patiently offers comfort and gifts without asking for any reward. His presence highlights that while the world contains danger and loss, it is also sustained by spontaneous human compassion.
