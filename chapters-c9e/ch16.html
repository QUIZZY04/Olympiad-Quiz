Poem 7: On Killing a Tree

Gieve Patel — Nature's Resilience vs Brutal Destruction — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

It takes much time to kill a tree, Not a simple jab of the knife Will do it. It has grown Slowly consuming the earth, Rising out of it, feeding Upon its crust, absorbing Years of sunlight, air, water, And out of its leprous hide Sprouting leaves... So hack and chop But this alone won't do it... The bleeding bark will heal And from close to the ground Will rise curled green twigs... No, The root is to be pulled out— Out of the anchoring earth; It is to be roped, tied, And pulled out—snapped out... And then it is done.
— Gieve Patel
I. Thinking about the Poem — Short Questions (30–40 Words / 2–3 Marks)
Q1
Can a 'simple jab of the knife' kill a tree? Why not? What does 'consuming the earth' mean?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

No, a simple jab of a knife cannot kill a tree. A tree has grown slowly over decades by absorbing nutrients from the soil, years of sunlight, air, and water. It possesses immense vitality and deep subterranean roots. Superficial wounds cannot extinguish its deeply anchored life force.

Q2
What is the meaning of 'bleeding bark'? What makes it bleed? How does it heal?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

'Bleeding bark' refers to the wound inflicted when branches are chopped or hacked by an axe, causing the tree to exude sap that resembles blood oozing from a human wound. The tree has miraculous recuperative powers; its bleeding bark gradually heals, and from near the ground, miniature curled green boughs sprout, eventually expanding back to former size.

Q3
What finally kills the tree? What are the successive steps of destruction?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The tree is finally killed only when its life-source—the root—is completely uprooted from the anchoring earth. The destructive process involves:

  1. Roping, tying, and pulling the root entirely out of the earth cave.
  2. Exposing the white, wet, sensitive root to harsh sunlight and air.
  3. Browning, hardening, twisting, withering, and complete drying out until it is utterly dead.

Q4
Analyze Gieve Patel's tone in 'On Killing a Tree'. Why does he describe the killing like a surgical procedure? (Max Word Count)
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Sarcastic and Clinical Tone:
Gieve Patel, who was himself a practicing medical doctor and poet, employs a dry, clinical, matter-of-fact tone laced with bitter irony. Rather than indulging in sentimental tears, he writes an explicit, cold 'instruction manual' on how to thoroughly murder a living tree, deliberately exposing human cruelty.

2. Tree as a Sentient Human Entity:
He employs somatic, biological vocabulary: the bark is a 'leprous hide', it 'bleeds' sap, the root is its 'sensitive white heart', and the earth is an 'anchoring womb'. By presenting the tree's execution as a premeditated murder that requires meticulous pulling, snapping, scorching, and choking, Patel forces the reader to confront the sheer violence and horror of deforestation.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 16CBSE 2026-27
Environmental Ethics
Deep Ecology: What does the tree's resilience teach us about nature's enduring power and human destructive capacity?

The poem illustrates that nature possesses astonishing powers of resilience and renewal; a hacked tree sprouts green twigs and regrows. However, man's destructive ingenuity is equally relentless. Humans do not stop at superficial cuts; they dig deep to rip out the roots, destroying the very foundation of life. The poem warns that while nature can heal from casual injury, industrial-scale ecocide destroys roots permanently, reminding society that killing nature is an act of self-extinction.

Poetic Devices
Literary Analysis: Explain the significance of the metaphors 'anchoring earth' and 'earth cave'.

1. 'Anchoring Earth': Compares the earth to a firm seabed where a ship's anchor holds it safe against raging storms, showing how the soil securely cradles the tree's roots.
2. 'Earth Cave': Refers to the deep subterranean hollow where the root system has rested sheltered and hidden for decades. Pulling the root out of this womb-like cave exposes its vulnerable, naked life-source to fatal sunlight.
