Part I: Evelyn Glennie Listens to Sound without Hearing It — Exercise Q&A
Q1
How old was Evelyn when she went to the Royal Academy of Music? When was her deafness first noticed and confirmed?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Age at Royal Academy: Evelyn was seventeen years old when she auditioned for and gained admission to the prestigious Royal Academy of Music in London.

2. When Deafness was Noticed: Her hearing impairment was first noticed when she was eight years old. Her mother, Isabel Glennie, observed that Evelyn did not respond when her name was called to play the piano.

3. When Deafness was Confirmed: Her deafness was medically confirmed when she was eleven years old. Her deteriorating school grades prompted the headmistress to urge her parents to consult a specialist, who discovered that her hearing was severely damaged as a result of gradual nerve damage.

Q2
Who helped Evelyn to continue with music? What did he do and say?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Master percussionist Ron Forbes spotted Evelyn's extraordinary potential when others discouraged her. Instead of telling her to listen with her ears, he tuned two large drums to different notes and told her: 'Don't listen through your ears, try to sense it some other way.'

Forbes began playing rhythms and encouraged Evelyn to feel the vibrations. Evelyn suddenly discovered that she could sense the higher drum from the waist up and the lower drum from the waist down. Forbes repeated the exercise, and Evelyn unlocked the secret of opening her mind and body to musical vibrations.

Q3
How does Evelyn hear music? (Max Word Count / 100–120 Words)
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Evelyn Glennie does not hear music through her auditory canals; she feels and senses it throughout her entire body.

  • Percussion Vibrations: When playing drums, she feels higher notes vibrating in the upper half of her torso above the waist and lower resonant notes vibrating in the lower half of her body below the waist.
  • Xylophone Resonance: When playing the xylophone, she feels the melody and sound waves traveling up the wooden mallets directly into her fingertips.
  • Resonating Drums: By leaning against large bass drums, she absorbs the echoes and resonances flowing into her chest cavity and torso.
  • Barefoot Performances: On wooden stages, she always removes her shoes and performs barefoot so that vibrations travel directly through her feet and move upward into her legs.
  • Skin and Hair: Evelyn explains that sound literally tingles in her skin, her cheekbones, and even her hair roots, transforming her entire physical frame into an exquisitely attuned acoustic resonator.

Part II: The Shehnai of Bismillah Khan — Exercise Q&A
Q4
Why did Emperor Aurangzeb ban the playing of the pungi? How is a shehnai different from a pungi?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Why Pungi was Banned:
Emperor Aurangzeb banned the playing of the musical instrument pungi in the royal residence because it possessed a shrill, piercing, unpleasant, and unmelodious sound. It became generic name for reeded noisemakers.

2. How Shehnai is Different from Pungi:
A barber (nai) belonging to a family of professional musicians decided to improve the tonal quality of the pungi. He chose a natural hollow stem pipe that was longer and broader than the pungi and drilled seven holes into its body. When played by opening and closing some holes, it produced soft, melodious, and sweet notes. Since it was first played in the Shah's chambers by a nai (barber), it was named the shehnai.

Q5
Where was the shehnai played traditionally? How did Bismillah Khan change this?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Traditional Use:
Traditionally, the shehnai was played only as part of the naubat (traditional ensemble of nine instruments found at royal courts). It was strictly confined to temples and North Indian wedding rituals as an auspicious accompaniment.

2. Bismillah Khan's Revolution:
Ustad Bismillah Khan revolutionized the instrument by bringing the shehnai onto the classical concert stage. He invented new ragas and improvised melodies that were previously believed to be impossible on a reeded wind instrument, establishing the shehnai as an esteemed sovereign instrument of Indian classical music worldwide.

Q6
When and how did Bismillah Khan get his big break? What happened on 15 August 1947?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Big Break (1938):
Bismillah Khan received his historic national breakthrough with the opening of All India Radio in Lucknow in 1938. He soon became an often-heard and immensely popular shehnai broadcaster across the country.

2. 15 August 1947 — Historic Day of Independence:
On 15 August 1947, when India achieved independence, Bismillah Khan became the first Indian to greet the newly born nation with his shehnai from the historic ramparts of the Red Fort in Delhi. He poured his heart out into Raag Kafi before an audience that included Mahatma Gandhi and Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru (who subsequently delivered his famous 'Tryst with Destiny' speech).

Q7
Why did Bismillah Khan refuse to start a shehnai school in the USA? Find instances in the text showing his love for Benaras and Dumraon.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Refusal to Settle in the USA:
One of Bismillah Khan's devoted students requested him to head a shehnai school in the United States and promised to recreate the entire atmosphere of Benaras by replicating the temples there. Bismillah Khan declined with polite humor, asking if the student would also be able to transport the Holy River Ganga to America. He emphasized that he could never live away from his motherland.

2. Deep Love for Benaras and Dumraon:
The text abounds with vivid instances showcasing his passionate attachment to his native roots:

  • Dumraon: He fondly remembered his childhood in Dumraon (Bihar), where he sang the Bhojpuri 'Chaita' at the Bihariji temple and received a big 1.25 kg laddu as an award from the local Maharaja.
  • River Ganga at Benaras: As a young apprentice, the flowing waters of the Ganga inspired him to improvise and invent new ragas on the quiet ghats of Varanasi and the temple of Mangala Maiya.
  • Universal Patriotism: Bismillah Khan famously declared: 'Whenever I am in a foreign country, I keep yearning to see Hindustan. While in Mumbai, I think only of Benaras and the holy Ganga; and while in Benaras, I miss the unique mattha of Dumraon.'

HOTS / Inspiring Lives
Comparative Analysis: Both Evelyn Glennie and Ustad Bismillah Khan achieved global excellence through unwavering dedication. What common character virtues enabled them to triumph?

Both Evelyn Glennie and Bismillah Khan embody passionate devotion (riyaaz), resilience, and absolute humility. Evelyn faced the catastrophic setback of profound deafness at age eleven, yet refused to abandon her dream, converting her whole body into an acoustic sensor. Bismillah Khan, despite attaining India's highest civilian honor—the Bharat Ratna—lived an austere, simple life dedicated entirely to music, rejecting commercial materialism and foreign citizenship. Both artists demonstrate that true mastery transcends physical limitations and material allure when propelled by pure love for one's craft.
