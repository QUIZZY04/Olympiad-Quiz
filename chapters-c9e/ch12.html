Poem 3: Rain on the Roof

Coates Kinney — Memory, Melancholy & Maternal Love — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

When the humid shadows hover Over all the starry spheres And the melancholy darkness Gently weeps in rainy tears, What a bliss to press the pillow Of a cottage-chamber bed And lie listening to the patter Of the soft rain overhead! Now in memory comes my mother, As she used in years agone, To regard the darling dreamers Ere she left them till the dawn: O! I feel her fond look on me As I list to this refrain Which is played upon the shingles By the patter of the rain.
— Coates Kinney
I. Thinking about the Poem — Short Questions (30–40 Words / 2–3 Marks)
Q1
What does the poet like to do when it rains?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

When it rains, the poet considers it pure bliss to lie comfortably in bed inside a cozy cottage-chamber, press his head into a soft pillow, and peacefully listen to the rhythmic patter of gentle raindrops on the shingled roof overhead.

Q2
What is the 'single major memory' that comes to the poet? Who are the 'darling dreamers'?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Single Major Memory: The single major memory that enters the poet's consciousness is the tender, affectionate image of his late mother looking down upon him in his childhood.

2. The 'Darling Dreamers': The darling dreamers are the poet and his siblings who slept and experienced sweet dreams as children under the vigilant, loving gaze of their mother before she left them until dawn.

Q3
Is the poet now a child? Is his mother still alive?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

No, the poet is not a child now; he is a grown adult reminiscing about his bygone youth. His mother is no longer alive, as indicated by the nostalgic phrase 'years agone'; she survives only in his tender, sacred memories.

Q4
Explain: 'Every tinkle on the shingles has an echo in the heart; and a thousand dreamy fancies into busy being start.' (Max Word Count)
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Acoustic and Emotional Echo:
The poet captures the profound psychological connection between sound and memory. Every sharp metallic 'tinkle' of rain striking the roof tiles (shingles) awakens a corresponding emotional resonance and rhythmic echo in the poet's sensitive heart.

2. Awakening of Imagination and Nostalgia:
The sound unleashes a multitude ('a thousand') of imaginative daydreams and buried childhood memories into active life ('busy being start'). Memory weaves these forgotten threads like air-woven fabric ('thousand recollections weave their air-threads into woof'), resurrecting his mother's loving gaze as he listens to the raindrops' repetitive melody.

Poetic Devices
Literary Craft: Identify the onomatopoeia, imagery, and alliteration in 'Rain on the Roof'.

1. Onomatopoeia: Use of sound-imitating words: 'patter' and 'tinkle' evoking raindrops on tiles.
2. Alliteration: 'humid shadows hover' (/h/), 'starry spheres' (/s/), 'press the pillow' (/p/), 'cottage-chamber' (/k/), 'busy being' (/b/).
3. Transferred Epithet: 'melancholy darkness' transfers human sadness to night sky.
4. Metaphor: 'rainy tears' comparing rain to weeping tears of sky.

Therapeutic Nature
Theme of Healing: How does nature's music heal human grief in the poem?

The poem depicts nature's rainfall not as an inconvenience, but as emotional catharsis. The rhythmic patter of rain acts as acoustic therapy for the adult poet burdened by modern anxieties. It transports him backward through time, re-immersing his weary soul in the comforting, unconditional warmth of his mother's maternal love, proving that nature's rhythms can soothe sorrow and revitalize the human spirit.
