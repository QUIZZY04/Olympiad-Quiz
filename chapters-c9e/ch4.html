Chapter 4: A Truly Beautiful Mind

Albert Einstein — Genius Scientist and World Citizen — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I. Thinking about the Text — Short Questions (30–40 Words / 2–3 Marks)
Q1
Why did Einstein's mother think he was a freak? What was unusual about his childhood speech?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Einstein's mother considered him a 'freak' because his head seemed abnormally and unusually large. Furthermore, Albert did not speak at all until the age of two-and-a-half. Even when he finally began talking, he uttered everything twice in a hesitant, repetitive manner. Because he did not know what to do with other children, his playmates mocked him as 'Brother Boring'.

Mother's perception: Abnormally large head1 Mark
Speech delay: Did not speak until 2½ years1 Mark
Peculiarity: Uttered everything twice; dubbed 'Brother Boring'1 Mark
Q2
What did a school headmaster once say about Einstein? What instrument did Einstein learn to play?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

A headmaster once told Einstein's father that whatever Albert chose as a profession would never matter because 'he'll never make a success at anything.' Despite this discouraging prediction, Einstein learned to play the violin at his mother's insistence at age six, becoming a gifted amateur violinist throughout his life.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Headmaster's verdict: Never succeed at anything1 Mark
Violin mastery: Began at age 6, gifted amateur for life1 Mark
Q3
Why did Einstein leave the school in Munich for good?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Einstein left the high school in Munich at age fifteen because he detested the school's suffocating regimentation, strict discipline, and authoritarian teachers. The mechanical learning environment stifled his creative thinking, and he felt so stifled that he left the institution permanently to study in liberal Switzerland.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Reasons: Suffocating regimentation, strict authoritarian discipline1 Mark
Felt stifled and moved to liberal, open-minded Switzerland1 Mark
Q4
Why did Einstein want to study in Switzerland rather than in Munich? Who was Mileva Maric?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Preference for Switzerland: Einstein preferred to continue his education in German-speaking Switzerland (University of Zurich) because the academic atmosphere was significantly more liberal, tolerant, and open to individual reasoning than rigid Munich.

2. Mileva Maric: Mileva Maric was a young Serbian student whom Einstein met at Zurich University. He found her to be a 'clever creature' who shared his passion for science and literature and despised the philistines (unintellectual people). They fell in love and later married.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Switzerland's liberal, open-minded academic culture1½ Marks
Mileva Maric: Serbian fellow student, intellectual companion, later wife1½ Marks
Q5
What did Einstein call his desk drawer at the patent office in Bern? Why?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

While working as a technical expert at the patent office in Bern, Einstein jokingly referred to his desk drawer as the 'Bureau of Theoretical Physics'. This was because while officially assessing other inventors' patent applications, he secretly worked on his own pathbreaking theoretical ideas in physics and kept his secret research notes locked inside that drawer.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Name: 'Bureau of Theoretical Physics'1 Mark
Reason: Developing his own groundbreaking physics theories in secret1 Mark
Q6
Why did Einstein write a letter to Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1939? What was its impact?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Reason for Letter: When the Nazis came to power in Germany and discovered nuclear fission in Berlin, American scientists were terrified that Hitler would construct an atomic bomb. Urged by colleagues, Einstein wrote to US President Franklin D. Roosevelt on 2 August 1939, warning him that a single bomb of this type could destroy an entire port along with surrounding territory.

2. Impact: The letter prompted the American government to launch the secret 'Manhattan Project', which culminated in the development and dropping of atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Warning about Nazi Germany building atomic bomb via nuclear fission1½ Marks
Impact: US launched Manhattan Project, leading to atomic bomb creation1½ Marks
II. Long Answer Questions — Global Legacy and Scientific Humanism (100–120 Words / 6 Marks)
Q7
Why is the chapter titled 'A Truly Beautiful Mind'? How did Einstein's scientific genius merge with his advocacy for world peace? (Max Word Count)
6 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Significance of the Title:
The title 'A Truly Beautiful Mind' highlights that Albert Einstein was not merely a brilliant intellectual giant in mathematics and physics, but also a profoundly compassionate human being possessing a noble, philanthropic soul dedicated to global harmony.

2. Agony over Hiroshima and Nagasaki:
When the United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945, Einstein was deeply devastated by the catastrophic loss of human life. In response, he wrote a passionate public missive to the United Nations proposing the formation of a unified World Government to prevent international military destruction.

3. Devotion to World Peace and Democracy:
Unlike scientists who divorced morality from science, Einstein spent his later decades actively campaigning against atomic weapons, advocating nuclear disarmament, promoting social justice, and championing individual freedom. When he passed away in 1955 at age 76, he was celebrated not merely as the discoverer of Relativity, but as a beloved world citizen and champion of humanity. His mind was truly 'beautiful' because his genius served life, peace, and universal brotherhood.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Title justification: Synthesis of intellectual genius and humanitarian empathy1½ Marks
Response to atomic bombings: Devastation and UN proposal for World Government1½ Marks
Later activism: Disarmament campaign, anti-war crusade, world peace1½ Marks
Legacy: Celebrated as a 'world citizen' who harmonized science with ethics1½ Marks
Q8
Summarise Albert Einstein's major scientific milestones in 1905 and 1915, and describe how he was honoured with the Nobel Prize in Physics.
4 Marks (60–80 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Albert Einstein transformed our fundamental understanding of space, time, matter, and energy:

  • The Miraculous Year (1905): Working as a modest technical assistant in the Bern patent office, Einstein published four revolutionary papers covering the Photoelectric Effect, Brownian Motion, and the Special Theory of Relativity (from which emerged the iconic mass-energy equation E = mc²).
  • General Theory of Relativity (1915): He formulated the General Theory of Relativity, providing a revolutionary new interpretation of gravity. During the solar eclipse of 1919, British astronomers proved his prediction of starlight deflection by gravitational deflection was accurate, leading newspapers to proclaim a 'Scientific Revolution'.
  • The Nobel Prize (1921): In 1921, Einstein was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for his discovery of the law of the photoelectric effect. He was showered with international honours and invitations worldwide.

1905 Special Relativity and E = mc^2 formula1.5 Marks
1915 General Theory of Relativity and 1919 eclipse proof1.5 Marks
1921 Nobel Prize in Physics for Photoelectric Effect1.0 Mark
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 4CBSE 2026-27
Competency-Based / Scientific Ethics
Science and Morality: 'Einstein was deeply shaken by the extent of the destruction.' How does Einstein's reaction to the atomic bomb reflect the moral responsibility of scientists?

Einstein's profound grief demonstrates that scientific discovery must never be detached from ethical responsibility. Although his famous equation E = mc² laid the theoretical foundation for nuclear energy, he was horrified when politicians weaponized his insight into an instrument of mass slaughter. Einstein actively utilized his global fame to campaign for disarmament and peaceful co-existence. His life reminds contemporary researchers that scientific advancement without moral consciousness leads to annihilation, and scientists bear an ongoing duty to protect humanity from the misuse of their inventions.

HOTS / Milestone Analysis
Analytical Thinking: Explain the significance of the year 1905 and the 1919 solar eclipse in Einstein's life.

The year 1905 is known as Einstein's 'Annus Mirabilis' (Miracle Year), during which the 26-year-old patent clerk published four revolutionary papers on light quanta, Brownian motion, electrodynamics, and the Special Theory of Relativity containing E = mc². In 1915, he published his General Theory of Relativity. The solar eclipse of 1919 provided empirical proof that gravity bends light rays from distant stars, precisely as Einstein had mathematically calculated. Newspapers across the world hailed his work as a 'Scientific Revolution', transforming him overnight into an international legend.
