Chapter 3: Iswaran the Storyteller

R.K. Laxman — Suspense, Oral Storytelling & Ghostly Delusions — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I. Thinking about the Text — Short Questions (30–40 Words / 2–3 Marks)
Q1
In what way is Iswaran an asset to Mahendra?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Iswaran was an indispensable, loyal asset to Mahendra. He accompanied Mahendra uncomplainingly to all remote construction sites, cooked delicious meals from scratch with ingredients conjured miraculously from nowhere, washed clothes, tidied the makeshift sheds, and entertained Mahendra at night with an endless repertoire of dramatic stories, folklore, and suspenseful tales.

Q2
How does Iswaran narrate the story of the uprooted tree? What does it reveal about his storytelling art?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Instead of simply saying a tree had fallen across the highway, Iswaran dramatized the scene with arched eyebrows, spread hands, and dramatic pauses. He described the road as deserted and himself alone, suddenly spotting an 'enormous bushy beast lying sprawled across the road'. As he stepped closer, he revealed it was only a fallen tree with dry branches spread out. This reveals his flair for suspense, sensationalism, and vivid theatrical delivery.

Q3
How does Iswaran recount his encounter with the rogue elephant? How did he supposedly paralyze it?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. The Rogue Elephant Rampage:
Iswaran narrated that a tusker escaped from a timber yard, went mad, trampled bushes, smashed fruit stalls in town, broke down school brick walls, and entered the school playground where children hid in classrooms.

2. Iswaran's Heroic Feat:
Claiming he was a brave junior student, Iswaran grabbed a cane from a teacher, marched boldly toward the trumpet-blowing beast, and struck its third toenail with lightning speed. The elephant looked stunned, shivered from head to foot, collapsed, and was knocked unconscious—allegedly paralyzed by the martial art of karate or ju-jitsu!

Q4
What story did Iswaran tell about the factory site on a full-moon night? What did Mahendra see that terrified him?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Iswaran's Ghost Tale:
One full-moon night after dinner, Iswaran claimed that their timber construction site had once been a burial ground. He claimed he frequently saw skulls lying on path, and described seeing an ugly, female ghost on full-moon nights with matted hair, a shriveled dried-up face, holding a fetus in its arms like a skeleton. Mahendra shivered and angrily rebuked Iswaran, calling it a figment of his fevered imagination.

2. Mahendra's Terrifying Experience:
However, on the very next full-moon night, Mahendra was awakened by a low, mournful moaning sound outside his bedroom window. Peering out into the moonlight, he beheld a dark, cloudy shape clutching a bundle! Paralyzed with terror, he broke into a cold sweat and collapsed onto his pillow, panting heavily, unable to shake off the horrifying vision.

Q5
How does Iswaran blend suspense, dramatic gestures, and supernatural thrills into his daily cooking and storytelling for Mahendra?
3 Marks (50–60 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

R.K. Laxman presents Iswaran as an unvarnished master of dramatic oral folklore:

  1. Theatrical Storytelling: Iswaran never simply stated an event. He would raise his hands in suspenseful arcs, contort his eyebrows, drop his voice to a whisper, and pantomime actions—whether describing a fallen tree as an enormous bushy beast or wrestling a mad tusker with karate.
  2. Culinary and Domestic Dedication: He cooked delicious meals, washed Mahendra's clothes, and chatted amiably at night in remote timber campsites lacking television.
  3. The Ghost Story Climax: On a full-moon night, he wove an eerie tale of a female ghost clutching a fetus that haunted their factory site. He built up the atmosphere so convincingly that rational Mahendra hallucinated tapping sounds outside his window and fled the campsite in terror.
CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Theatrical body language, suspenseful cliffhangers, and expressions1.5 Marks
Ghost story on full moon night driving rational Mahendra to flee1.5 Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 20CBSE 2026-27
Power of Suggestion
Psychological Impact: Did Mahendra genuinely encounter a supernatural ghost, or was he a victim of psychological autosuggestion?

Mahendra was a rational, educated supervisor with a scientific mindset, yet he fell victim to psychological autosuggestion. Iswaran's hypnotic, vivid descriptions had planted a seed of terror in Mahendra's subconscious. On the full-moon night, when he heard a stray cat yowling in the shadows, his terrified mind projected Iswaran's story onto reality, hallucinating a spectral woman. When Iswaran casually remarked the next morning that he had heard Mahendra's moaning, Mahendra's psychological composure shattered completely, prompting him to resign and flee the site.

Literary Craft
Humor & Suspense: How does R.K. Laxman weave humor and gothic suspense together in this story?

R.K. Laxman blends comedy with gothic eerie suspense. Iswaran's larger-than-life culinary descriptions and absurd claims of felling a rogue elephant with a cane create delightful humor. However, the atmosphere shifts sharply into spine-chilling gothic horror during the ghost story, replete with full moons, rattling skulls, and mournful wailing. By leaving the ending ambiguous—was it a real specter or psychological fear?—Laxman keeps readers both amused and haunted.
