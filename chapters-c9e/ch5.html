II. Long Answer Questions — Humor, Irony, and Vanity (100–120 Words / 6 Marks)
Q6
How does the story 'The Snake and the Mirror' skillfully contrast human vanity with mortal terror? Discuss with examples. (Max Word Count)
6 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Human Vanity and Narcissism:
At the opening of the story, the young homeopathic doctor is deeply consumed by self-admiration. Living in poverty in a shabby rat-infested room, he nevertheless sits before the mirror, obsessively adjusting his hair parting, smiling broadly, and resolving to grow a mustache to amplify his bachelor charm. He feels proud of being a handsome doctor and indulges in laughable fantasies about marrying a rich fat woman.

2. Sudden Confrontation with Death:
His bloated vanity is abruptly punctured when a venomous cobra drops onto his shoulder and coils around his arm. In an instant, his arrogant pride evaporates. He feels completely helpless, realizing that death is hovering barely four inches away. Stripped of his medical arrogance, he acknowledges his weakness, calling himself a 'poor, foolish and stupid doctor' who did not even keep anti-venom medicine in his room.

3. Ironic Resolution:
The humor peaks when the snake, too, exhibits vanity—abandoning the doctor to admire its own reflection in the mirror, allowing the terrified doctor to sprint for his life into the night.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 5CBSE 2026-27
Humor & Irony
Literary Analysis: What role does the mirror play as a central motif in the story?

The mirror serves as the crucial literary pivot of the narrative. In the first half, it acts as a catalyst for human vanity, encouraging the young doctor to indulge in narcissistic daydreams and superficial plans. In the second half, the mirror becomes an instrument of salvation. When the deadly cobra looks into the mirror, it is lured away by its own image, saving the doctor's life without a single drop of blood spilled. The mirror thus reflects both human vanity and animal curiosity, binding tension and comedy together.

Character Sketch
Narrative Voice: How does Vaikom Muhammad Basheer employ self-deprecating humor through the doctor's narrative?

The doctor recounts his terrifying ordeal with delightful self-mockery. Instead of portraying himself as a heroic survivor, he openly confesses his vanity, his poverty, and his cowardice. He chuckles at his sixty rupees, his lone black coat, and his ridiculous thoughts about marrying a fat woman. When facing the snake, he candidly admits that he felt like a foolish simpleton who had no medicines in his room. Even in the aftermath, when thieves ransack his room and leave behind his dirty vest as a final insult, the doctor laughs along with his listeners, highlighting his warm, cheerful acceptance of life's absurdities.
