Poem 2: Wind

Subramania Bharati — Inner Strength, Adversity & Friendship with Nature — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

Wind, come softly. Don't break the shutters of the windows. Don't scatter the papers. Don't throw down the books on the shelf... The wind god winnows and crushes them all. He won't do what you tell him. So, come, let's build strong homes, Let's joint the doors firmly. Practise to firm the body. Make the heart steadfast. Do this, and the wind will be friends with us.
— Subramania Bharati (Translated by A.K. Ramanujan)
I. Thinking about the Poem — Short Questions (30–40 Words / 2–3 Marks)
Q1
What are the things the wind does in the first stanza of the poem?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In the first stanza, the destructive wind violently breaks the window shutters, scatters papers across the room, throws down books from shelves, tears their pages, and brings torrential rain, creating widespread disorder and wreckage.

Q2
What does the poet say the wind god winnows? What does winnowing mean?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Winnowing Process: Winnowing is the agricultural process of separating grain from chaff using wind power.

2. What the Wind God Winnows: Metaphorically, the wind god winnows and mercilessly crushes all weak structures—crumbling houses, crumbling doors, crumbling rafters, crumbling wood, crumbling bodies, crumbling lives, and crumbling hearts, sparing only what is sturdy and strong.

Q3
What should we do to make friends with the wind? (Max Word Count)
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Physical Preparedness:
Since the wind god refuses to heed human pleading, the poet advises that we must construct sturdy, strong houses and fasten our doors firmly to withstand furious gales.

2. Mental and Physical Resilience:
Beyond physical structures, the poet urges us to train our bodies to be firm and fortify our hearts with unwavering resolve and courage.

3. Transformation of Adversity:
The poet observes that the wind blows out weak fires but makes strong fires roar and flourish. If we cultivate inner strength and resilience, the ferocious wind ceases to be an enemy and transforms into a loyal friend that strengthens our growth. The friendship of the wind is noble for those who are strong.

Q4
What does Subramania Bharati say the wind god does to weak fires and strong fires? What is the deeper moral message?
3 Marks (50–60 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In the poem 'Wind', Bharati observes that the wind behaves differently depending on the strength of the flame:

  • Weak Fires: The wind blows out and destroys weak fires mercilessly, just as adversity crushes people who possess weak wills and fragile bodies.
  • Strong Fires: The wind fans and invigorates strong fires, making them roar and flourish with increased intensity.
  • Moral Message: Obstacles and hardships defeat the cowardly and faint-hearted, but empower and elevate resilient, courageous individuals who embrace struggle as a forging fire.

Q5
How does the poet advise us to make friends with the destructive force of the wind?
3 Marks (50–60 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Since the wind god does not listen to human pleadings, the poet counsels us to build inner and outer resilience:

  1. Physical Strengthening: Construct strong homes with sturdy roofs, joint the doors firmly, and practice to firm the physical body.
  2. Mental and Spiritual Fortitude: Make the heart steadfast and unwavering against emotional storms.
  3. Positive Alliance: Once we become strong, the wind ceases to be an enemy and becomes a supportive friend, just as harsh life challenges bring out our highest potential.

Symbolism & Life Skills
Metaphorical Depth: How is 'Wind' a symbolic representation of life's struggles and adversities?

In the poem, 'wind' symbolizes trials, misfortunes, hardships, and economic or emotional crises. Weak personalities collapse under adversity like crumbling houses in a storm. However, individuals with mental fortitude, steadfast hearts, and discipline view challenges as opportunities to grow stronger. Just as fierce wind fan flames into a roaring fire, life's adversities fortify the resilient, proving that inner strength is the ultimate shield against life's tempests.

Poetic Devices
Literary Analysis: Identify the figures of speech employed in 'Wind'.

1. Personification: The wind is addressed as a living, willful being ('You tore the pages', 'He won't do what you tell him').
2. Anaphora: Repetition of 'Don't' in lines 2, 3, 4 and 'You' in lines 6, 7.
3. Repetition: The word 'crumbling' is repeated seven times to emphasize destruction.
4. Symbolism: Wind symbolizes relentless adversity; fires symbolize human spirits.
