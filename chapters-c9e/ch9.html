Q5
How did Gerrard trap the Intruder? (Max Word Count)
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Gerrard told the Intruder that his accomplice was stationed at the road corner to signal if the police arrived. When the telephone rang, Gerrard claimed it was the warning signal. He urged the Intruder to escape with him in his car through the garage. Gerrard opened a door, claiming it led straight to the garage. When the Intruder leaned forward to inspect it with his revolver ready, Gerrard gave him a sudden, forceful push into the dark cupboard, knocked the revolver out of his hand, slammed the door shut, and locked him securely inside. He then picked up the phone to call the police.

II. Long Answer Questions — Wit vs Brute Force (100–120 Words / 6 Marks)
Q6
How does the play 'If I Were You' establish that presence of mind and intellect triumph over brute force and violence? (Max Word Count)
6 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. The Power Asymmetry:
The play pits a dangerous, gun-toting criminal against an unarmed playwright. The Intruder represents brute force, ruthlessness, and arrogant malice, believing his revolver gives him absolute dominance over Gerrard's life.

2. Gerrard's Intellectual Strategy:
Instead of groveling or crying, Gerrard weaponizes his dramatic intellect and psychological acumen. He recognizes the Intruder's foolish vanity and manipulates his gullibility. By maintaining an air of witty nonchalance, Gerrard disarms the criminal mentally. He invents an airtight backstory claiming he is also a murderer on the run, backing his claim with genuine theatrical props (disguise clothes and false mustaches).

3. Decisive Climax:
Gerrard orchestrates a brilliant trap, tricking the Intruder into inspecting a cupboard disguised as a garage exit. With a swift push and slammed door, intellect cleanly neutralizes violence without firing a single bullet. The play delivers a timeless lesson: cool presence of mind, wit, and composure in a crisis will always triumph over blind aggression.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 9CBSE 2026-27
Dramatic Irony
Literary Analysis: How does Douglas James utilize dramatic irony and theatrical elements in 'If I Were You'?

The play is soaked in rich theatrical irony. Gerrard is an actual playwright whose daily life revolves around stagecraft, disguises, and melodrama. When confronted by a real-life villain, Gerrard simply casts the criminal into an impromptu drama. The false moustaches and disguise kit—meant for a theatrical production—serve as authenticating 'proof' of his criminal persona. The Intruder, thinking he is the director of the crime, becomes a hapless puppet in Gerrard's play, culminating in the ironic twist where Gerrard promises to put the entire episode into his next stage play.

Character Sketch
Character Analysis: Write a character sketch of Gerrard.

Vincent Charles Gerrard is a refined, quick-witted, and extraordinarily courageous playwright. He possesses sharp observational powers, dry British humor, and iron nerves. Even when staring down the barrel of a loaded revolver, he banters playfully, refusing to show terror. Gerrard is a master of psychology; he quickly detects the Intruder's intellectual limitations and crafts a spontaneous fiction to deceive him. Cool, resourceful, and physically agile when opportunity strikes, Gerrard embodies supreme presence of mind under mortal peril.
