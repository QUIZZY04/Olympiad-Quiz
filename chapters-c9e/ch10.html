BEEHIVE POETRY

Poem 1: The Road Not Taken

Robert Frost — Choices, Indecision & Destiny — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

Two roads diverged in a yellow wood, And sorry I could not travel both And be one traveler, long I stood And looked down one as far as I could To where it bent in the undergrowth; I shall be telling this with a sigh Somewhere ages and ages hence: Two roads diverged in a wood, and I— I took the one less traveled by, And that has made all the difference.
— Robert Frost
I. Thinking about the Poem — Short Questions (30–40 Words / 2–3 Marks)
Q1
Where does the traveler find himself? What problem does he face?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The traveler finds himself in a forest with yellow autumn leaves where the road diverges into two separate paths. His dilemma is that being a single traveler, he cannot travel both paths simultaneously and must make an irreversible choice between them.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Location: Yellow autumn wood at a road fork1 Mark
Dilemma: Cannot travel both roads; must choose one1 Mark
Q2
What do the following phrases mean in the poem: (i) 'a yellow wood' (ii) 'it was grassy and wanted wear' (iii) 'leaves no step had trodden black'?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

(i) 'A yellow wood': Represents an autumn forest where leaves of deciduous trees have turned yellow before shedding, symbolizing aging, transition, and decay of the year.

(ii) 'It was grassy and wanted wear': The path was covered with fresh green grass and needed to be walked upon; it had not been worn down by many travelers.

(iii) 'Leaves no step had trodden black': The fallen leaves that morning were fresh and untouched, not yet crushed, bruised, or blackened by human footsteps.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
(i) Autumn forest with discolored leaves / transition1 Mark
(ii) Grassy path that had not been frequently walked upon1 Mark
(iii) Fresh fallen leaves uncrushed by human feet1 Mark
Q3
Is there any difference between the two roads as described by the poet in stanzas two and three?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In stanza two, the poet claims that the second road was 'just as fair' and perhaps had 'the better claim' because it was grassy and less worn. However, he immediately concedes that as far as passing there was concerned, both had been worn 'really about the same'. In stanza three, he confirms that both paths lay equally covered with fresh morning leaves untouched by steps, indicating that their physical difference was practically nonexistent.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Stanza 2 claim: Second path seemed less worn initially1½ Marks
Poet's honest admission: Both were worn 'really about the same'1½ Marks
Q4
What does the traveler mean by 'sigh' in the last stanza? What is the deeper metaphorical meaning of the poem?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Meaning of the 'Sigh':
The 'sigh' may signify regret, nostalgia, relief, or contemplation. The poet realizes that choosing one path means abandoning another forever ('way leads on to way'). Years later, he looks back wondering how his life might have unfolded had he chosen the other road.

2. Metaphorical Meaning:
The diverging roads symbolize crucial life choices—career, moral decisions, or personal paths. Every individual must make independent choices. Taking the unconventional, challenging road ('the one less traveled by') shapes one's character and individuality, ultimately determining the destiny that 'makes all the difference'.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Sigh interpreted: Ambiguity of regret, nostalgia, and acceptance2 Marks
Metaphor of roads: Diverging pathways and decisions of human life1½ Marks
Theme: Irreversibility of choices shaping personal destiny1½ Marks
Q5
What is the metaphorical significance of the 'two roads diverged in a yellow wood' in Robert Frost's poem? How does choice shape human destiny?
3 Marks (50–60 Words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In Frost's masterpiece, the fork in the yellow woods is an extended metaphor for the inevitable moral and career choices every individual must confront in life:

  • The Yellow Wood: Symbolizes a transitional autumn phase of maturity when one must make independent decisions.
  • The Choice of Roads: One road was beaten and popular; the other was grassy, less traveled, and fraught with uncertainty.
  • Irreversibility of Choice: Frost observes that one decision leads inexorably to another ('way leads on to way'), making it virtually impossible to return and try the other option. Choosing the less conventional path made all the difference to his life.
CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Two roads as life choices in career and ethics1.5 Marks
Irreversible nature of human decisions shaping destiny ('made all the difference')1.5 Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 10CBSE 2026-27
Poetic Devices & Theme
Literary Analysis: Identify the rhyme scheme and primary poetic devices in Robert Frost's 'The Road Not Taken'.

1. Rhyme Scheme: The poem consists of four stanzas of five lines each (quintets) with a strict rhyme scheme of ABAAB (wood/both/stood/could/undergrowth).
2. Poetic Devices:
• Metaphor: The diverging roads are an extended metaphor for life's choices.
• Anaphora: Repetition of 'And' at the beginning of lines 2, 3, and 4.
• Alliteration: 'grassy and wanted wear' (/w/ sound), 'first for another' (/f/ sound).
• Symbolism: The 'yellow wood' symbolizes autumn and maturity.

Value-Based / Life Choices
Reflective Insight: Why is it impossible to retrace one's steps in life? How does Frost convey this truth?

Frost conveys the irreversibility of human choices through the line: 'Yet knowing how way leads on to way, I doubted if I should ever come back.' In life, one decision inevitably opens a series of subsequent paths, circumstances, and consequences that carry an individual further away from the original crossroads. Even if one physically returns to the same spot, one is no longer the same person, nor are the circumstances identical. Hence, decisions must be made with thoughtful deliberation and courageous ownership.
