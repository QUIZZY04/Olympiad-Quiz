Poem 6: No Men Are Foreign

James Kirkup — Universal Brotherhood & Anti-War Philosophy — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

Remember, no men are strange, no countries foreign Beneath all uniforms, a single body breathes Like ours: the land our brothers walk upon Is earth like this, in which we all shall lie. They, too, aware of sun and air and water, Are fed by peaceful harvests, by war's long winter starv'd. Their hands are ours, and in their lines we read A labour not different from our own. It is the human earth that we defile. Our hells of fire and dust outrage the innocence Of air that is everywhere our own, Remember, no men are foreign, and no countries strange.
— James Kirkup
I. Thinking about the Poem — Short Questions (30–40 Words / 2–3 Marks)
Q1
What does the poet mean by 'uniforms' and 'a single body breathes'? What commonalities unite all human beings?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

'Uniforms' refers to the military uniforms worn by soldiers of different hostile nations, as well as distinct cultural, linguistic, and national garbs. Beneath these superficial outward coverings, the same biological human body breathes. All humans share identical vital needs: the same warming sun, air to breathe, water to drink, nourishment from peaceful harvests, and a shared mother earth where all walk and ultimately lie buried.

Q2
How are human beings across the world starved by 'war's long winter'? How do their hands resemble ours?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

During peaceful times, all nations thrive on abundant agricultural harvests. However, war acts like a long, harsh winter that freezes productivity, leading to starvation, devastation, and famine for everyone regardless of borders. Furthermore, their hands carry the same calluses and lines of hard daily labor, proving that all people work equally hard to earn their livelihood.

Q3
Whenever we are told to hate our brothers, who tells us? What happens when we take up arms against each other?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Corrupt politicians, warmongers, and self-serving leaders manipulate public sentiments and instruct citizens to hate other nationalities. When we take up arms against our fellow human beings, we do not defeat an alien enemy; we betray, dispossess, and condemn ourselves. War pollutes the innocent air, defiles our shared mother earth with weapons and ashes, and murders our common humanity.

Q4
Discuss the central theme of 'No Men Are Foreign'. How does James Kirkup establish universal brotherhood? (Max Word Count)
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Demolition of Artificial Boundaries:
James Kirkup delivers a profound anti-war manifesto asserting the indivisible unity of humankind. Geopolitical borders, flags, and military uniforms are artificial human constructs. In essence, all people belong to a single global fraternity.

2. Physiological and Psychological Equivalence:
The poet demonstrates that all human beings share identical biological organs, eyes that sleep and wake, hearts that respond to love, and hands that toil. We all enjoy the same sun, air, and water, and endure the same misery when war strikes.

3. Anti-War Philosophy:
War is an act of collective suicide. By taking up arms, we poison the common atmosphere and desecrate the holy earth that sustains us. Kirkup ends with an inverted reiteration of his opening line: 'Remember, no men are foreign, and no countries strange.'

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 15CBSE 2026-27
Global Citizenship
Global Peace: How does Kirkup's poem resonate with modern international conflicts and peace initiatives?

In an era marred by war, geopolitical polarization, and xenophobia, Kirkup's poem stands as an urgent humanitarian appeal. It challenges state-sponsored propaganda that dehumanizes enemies. By reminding us that the 'enemy' soldier has eyes that wake and sleep and children who need bread, the poem compels us to recognize our own reflection in the other. True defense lies not in armaments, but in international empathy, trade, and shared stewardship of the planet.

Poetic Devices
Literary Craft: Discuss the refrain and metaphors in 'No Men Are Foreign'.

1. Refrain: The opening line 'Remember, no men are strange, no countries foreign' is repeated at the conclusion with subtle inversion ('no men are foreign, and no countries strange'), reinforcing the poem's moral thesis.
2. Metaphor: 'War's long winter' compares war to a barren, freezing winter that starves humanity; 'hells of fire and dust' compares battlefield violence to infernal damnation.
3. Imagery: Sensory details of eyes waking, hands laboring, and breathing bodies emphasize visceral human equality.
