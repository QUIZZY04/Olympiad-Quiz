Chapter 4: In the Kingdom of Fools

Kannada Folk Tale (A.K. Ramanujan) — Absurdity, Folly & Wisdom — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I. Thinking about the Text — Short Questions (30–40 Words / 2–3 Marks)
Q1
What are the two strange rules followed in the Kingdom of Fools?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The King and Minister of the Kingdom of Fools instituted two absurd rules:

  1. Day and Night Reversal: People were ordered to sleep during the day and work only after sunset (plowing fields and running shops at night). Anyone disobeying was sentenced to death.
  2. Equal Pricing: Everything in the market cost exactly the same price—one single duddu (measure of currency)—whether one bought a bunch of bananas or a sack of pure ghee.

Q2
Why does the guru decide to leave the kingdom, while the disciple decides to stay?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The wise guru realized that a kingdom ruled by fools was unpredictable and hazardous, warning: 'This is a city of fools; you can't tell what they'll do to you next.' However, the greedy disciple refused to leave because food was incredibly cheap; he could buy mountains of sweets, rice, wheat, and ghee for pennies, wanting to feast and grow fat like a sacred street bull.

Q3
Trace the comic chain of blame in the trial after the burglar died.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

When an ancient wall collapsed and crushed a burglar, the king initiated a laughable trial where each person shifted responsibility in an absurd chain of blame:

  1. The Merchant: Blamed the bricklayer for building a weak wall.
  2. The Bricklayer: Blamed the dancing girl whose jingling anklets distracted his attention while laying bricks.
  3. The Dancing Girl: Blamed the goldsmith who repeatedly delayed making her jewelry, forcing her to walk up and down the street.
  4. The Goldsmith: Blamed a rich merchant whose urgent wedding order forced him to delay the dancing girl's ornaments.
  5. The Full Circle: The rich merchant was none other than the original merchant's deceased father, meaning the current merchant inherited his father's guilt and was sentenced to death!

Q4
Why was the fat disciple arrested for the execution? How did the Guru save his life? (Max Word Count)
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Why the Disciple was Arrested:
When the execution stake was built, the merchant turned out to be too thin to fit the stake properly. Instead of abandoning the execution, the foolish king decreed that a man fat enough to fit the stake must be found immediately. Royal guards rounded up the disciple, who had fattened himself on bananas, rice, and ghee.

2. The Guru's Ingenious Trick:
The guru used his magical foresight, arrived swiftly, whispered a secret plan to his disciple, and staged an enthusiastic quarrel with him over who would be executed first. The guru explained to the king that this stake was the divine stake of the God of Justice; whoever died on it first would be reborn as the King in his next life, and whoever died second would become Minister!

3. The Foolish King's Choice:
Terrified of losing their kingdom in the next life, the King and Minister secretly entered the prison at night, freed the guru and disciple, disguised themselves in their place, and willingly stepped onto the execution stake, getting executed by their own executioners!

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 21CBSE 2026-27
Folklore & Wisdom
Moral Philosophy: What timeless moral lesson does 'In the Kingdom of Fools' teach about greed and foolish rulers?

The folktale demonstrates that living in an irrational society governed by foolish leaders is inherently perilous. The disciple's gluttony blinded him to danger; temporary material benefits (cheap food) led him to the executioner's noose. The story warns that when leadership abandons reason, justice becomes a farce. It also celebrates the supremacy of wisdom; the guru's sharp wit and understanding of human psychology outmaneuvered tyranny without violence, proving that wisdom is the only antidote to folly.

Satirical Humour
Satire on Justice: How does the trial scene satirize bureaucratic judicial systems?

The trial scene is a biting satire on legal systems that prioritize ritualistic procedural completion over common sense. A burglar dying while committing a crime is absurdly treated as a state murder, and responsibility is tossed from citizen to citizen through ridiculous causality chains. The king's insistence on executing someone—anyone—simply because a stake had been built satirizes bureaucratic obstinacy where procedures must be fulfilled even if innocent lives are sacrificed.
