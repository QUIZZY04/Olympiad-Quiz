Chapter 2: The Adventures of Toto

Ruskin Bond — Mischief, Chaos & The Untameable Monkey — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I. Thinking about the Text — Short Questions (30–40 Words / 2–3 Marks)
Q1
How does Toto come to grandfather's private zoo? Why was his presence kept a secret from grandmother?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Purchase: Grandfather bought Toto, a pretty little red monkey, from a tonga-driver for the sum of five rupees. The driver used to keep the monkey tied to a feeding-trough, where Toto looked so out of place that Grandfather decided to add him to his private zoo.

2. Secret from Grandmother: Grandmother always fussed and created an uproar whenever Grandfather brought home any new bird or animal. Therefore, Toto's presence was kept a secret until Grandmother was in an exceptionally good mood.

Q2
Describe Toto's physical appearance. Why does the author call him a 'pretty monkey'?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Toto was an exceptionally attractive baby monkey. He had bright, sparkling eyes deep-set beneath mischievous eyebrows, and pearly white teeth which he bared frequently in a smile that frightened elderly Anglo-Indian ladies. His hands were dried and wrinkled as if pickled in the sun, but his fingers were swift and wicked. His long tail served as a third hand to hang from branches and scoop up treats.

Sparkling mischievous eyes and pearly white teeth1 Mark

Q3
How did Toto travel to Saharanpur? What happened at the railway turnstile?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Journey in Canvas Bag:
Grandfather had to collect his pension in Saharanpur, so he carried Toto in a large black canvas kit-bag with straw at the bottom. Toto could not get his paws through the strong zipper, and when he jumped or rolled inside, the bag rolled about on the Dehradun railway platform, attracting a curious crowd.

2. Incident at Turnstile:
While Grandfather was producing his ticket at the Saharanpur railway turnstile, Toto suddenly poked his head out of the bag and grinned widely at the ticket collector. The startled collector recovered his composure and insisted on charging three rupees extra, classifying Toto as a dog!

Q4
How did Toto take his warm bath in winter? How did he once nearly boil himself alive?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Bath Ritual: Toto cunningly copied the author's bathing routine. He would test the temperature of warm water in a bowl with his hand, step in foot by foot up to his neck, and rub himself all over with soap. When the water cooled, he would leap out and sprint to the kitchen fire to dry himself.

2. Boiling Kettle Incident: One day, a large kitchen kettle had been left on the fire to boil for tea. Finding the water comfortably warm, Toto climbed in with his head sticking out. When the water began to boil, Toto hopped up and down, unable to escape because it was cold outside. Fortunately, Grandmother arrived just in time, shrieked, and hauled him half-boiled out of the bubbling kettle!

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Bath routine: Testing temp with hand, stepping in, using soap, drying at kitchen fire2 Marks
Kettle incident: Climbed into tea kettle on fire; saved by grandmother when boiling2 Marks
Q5
Why does the author say, 'Toto was not the sort of pet we could keep for long'?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Toto was incorrigibly destructive and untameable. He tore wall-paper to shreds, shredded the author's school blazer into ribbons, constantly harassed Nana the family donkey, smashed dinner plates, broke teacups, and threw dishes of pulao down from a jackfruit tree. Grandfather's family was not well-to-do and could not afford the frequent loss of clothes, curtains, dishes, and wallpaper. In despair, Grandfather found the tonga-driver and sold Toto back to him for only three rupees, accepting a loss of two rupees.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 19CBSE 2026-27
Humor & Animal Behavior
Theme of Pet Ownership: How does Ruskin Bond highlight the responsibilities and boundaries of keeping exotic wild animals as domestic pets?

Ruskin Bond's affectionate humor contains an underlying lesson regarding animal welfare. While Grandfather loves animals passionately, he fails to realize that wild creatures like monkeys are biologically unsuited for domestic confinement. Toto's destructive antics are natural expressions of his wild instincts and boundless energy being trapped inside a human household. By concluding with Toto's return to the tonga-driver, the story gently reminds readers that wild animals belong in their natural habitat, not in living rooms.

Character Sketch
Character Analysis: Write a character sketch of Grandfather in 'The Adventures of Toto'.

Grandfather is an eccentric, kind-hearted, and passionate animal lover with a private zoo comprising a tortoise, rabbits, a tame squirrel, goats, and a donkey. He possesses infinite patience and amusement toward animal antics. Even when Toto destroys wallpaper or shreds a blazer, Grandfather admires the monkey's cleverness rather than losing his temper. However, he is also pragmatic; when Toto's mischief threatens family finances, he realistically recognizes that wild nature cannot be domesticated, making the wise decision to part with the pet.
