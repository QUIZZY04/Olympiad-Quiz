Chapter 6/9: The Beggar

Anton Chekhov — Redemption, Labor & Olga's Selfless Compassion — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I. Thinking about the Text — Short Questions (30–40 Words / 2–3 Marks)
Q1
What lies did the beggar Lushkoff tell Advocate Sergei to extract money from him?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Lushkoff told pathetic, conflicting lies. On one day, he begged for charity claiming he was an innocent rural schoolteacher who had lost his post through village intrigues. On another day, he told Sergei he was an expelled student who had an offer of work in the province of Kaluga but had no money for the journey.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Claimed to be an impoverished rural schoolteacher1½ Marks
Claimed to be an expelled university student needing fare for Kaluga1½ Marks
Q2
How did Advocate Sergei catch the beggar lying? What work did Sergei offer him?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Sergei recognized Lushkoff because two days earlier he had seen the same man in Sadovaya Street begging under the guise of an expelled student, whereas now he claimed to be a schoolteacher. Furiously threatening to call the police for swindling, Sergei offered him honest manual labor: chopping wood in his yard for half a ruble.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Sergei recalled seeing him in Sadovaya Street with contradictory story1½ Marks
Threatened with police, then offered wood-chopping work in his yard1½ Marks
Q3
Was Lushkoff willing to chop wood? Why did he agree to do it?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

No, Lushkoff was not at all willing to chop wood. His physical strength was thoroughly undermined by vodka, and he was weak, sluggish, and had no appetite for hard manual labor. He agreed to go along solely out of shame, trapped pride, and because his lies had been publicly exposed by Sergei.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Unwilling due to alcohol-induced physical weakness and laziness1½ Marks
Agreed out of wounded pride, embarrassment, and trapped situation1½ Marks
Q4
Where did Sergei meet Lushkoff two years later? What had Lushkoff become?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Two years later, while standing at the ticket window of a theatre paying for his seat, Sergei noticed Lushkoff standing beside him. Lushkoff had transformed into a clean, respectable, and well-dressed notary earning thirty-five rubles a month.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Meeting: Theatre ticket counter two years later1½ Marks
Transformation: Clean, respectable notary earning 35 rubles a month1½ Marks
Q5
Who was truly responsible for reforming Lushkoff—Advocate Sergei or Olga the cook? Discuss. (Max Word Count)
6 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Sergei's Superficial Role:
Advocate Sergei believed that his stern rebuke and strict insistence on honest physical labor had transformed the beggar. He took self-righteous pride in having set Lushkoff on the right path, reminding him that he had provided the opportunity and tough love.

2. The Shocking Truth (Olga's Selfless Sacrifice):
Lushkoff confessed to Sergei that it was not Sergei, but his cook, Olga, who saved his soul. Whenever Lushkoff arrived to chop wood, Olga scolded him fiercely, calling him a drunkard and a wretch. But then she would weep bitterly, looking into his face, crying: 'You miserable creature! There is no happiness for you in this world, and in the next you will burn in hell!'

3. True Transformation Through Love:
Moved by deep, selfless pity, Olga chopped every single log of wood herself for Lushkoff, allowing the drunkard to collect the wages without lifting a finger. Her tearful sacrifices, unconditional compassion, and selfless labor pierced Lushkoff's hardened heart, inspiring him to stop drinking, reform his character, and become an honorable man.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Sergei's role: Provided tough opportunity, but remained aloof and prideful2 Marks
Olga's secret: Chopped all the wood herself while weeping for his soul2 Marks
Moral core: Stern lectures fail; unconditional empathy and sacrifice redeem human beings2 Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 26CBSE 2026-27
Compassion vs Discipline
Theme of Human Redemption: How does Chekhov show that love and empathy are far more powerful than judgment and discipline?

Chekhov explores the psychology of moral redemption. Sergei represents institutional justice and bourgeois morality—he shames the beggar and demands work, feeling self-congratulatory pride. However, shaming someone only drives them further into defensive deceit. Olga, on the other hand, embodies Christ-like love. Beneath her harsh, scolding tongue lies profound sorrow for Lushkoff's wasted life. By secretly taking his punishment upon herself and doing his labor, she awakens his buried conscience, proving that genuine transformation occurs through grace and sacrifice, not through harsh judgment.

Character Sketch
Character Analysis: Contrast the characters of Advocate Sergei and Olga the cook.

Advocate Sergei is respectable, wealthy, righteous, and somewhat pompous. He is eager to take credit for Lushkoff's salvation, congratulating himself on his moral discipline. In contrast, Olga is a coarse, humble domestic cook whose exterior is gruff and foul-tempered, but whose soul is pure gold. She seeks no praise, no payment, and never boasts of her charity. Her tears and secret labor are acts of pure, anonymous grace, making her the true spiritual hero of the story.
