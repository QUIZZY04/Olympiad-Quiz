Chapter 7: The Last Leaf

Q1
What was Johnsy's illness? What was the doctor's diagnosis regarding her chances of recovery?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In cold November, Johnsy caught pneumonia. She lay helplessly on her bed, gazing out the window. The doctor diagnosed that medicines were failing because Johnsy had lost her will to live. She had superstitiously convinced herself that she would die, and the doctor remarked that unless she regained hope, medicines had only a one-in-ten chance of curing her.

Q4
How did Behrman save Johnsy's life? What happened to Behrman? (Max Word Count)
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. The Secret Midnight Mission:
When Sue told Behrman about Johnsy's morbid fantasy, the old painter was deeply moved. That night, a savage storm raged with driving rain and freezing sleet; the last natural leaf fell. Braving the howling tempest, Behrman climbed a ladder with a lantern and green and yellow paints, painting a realistic, vibrant ivy leaf on the brick wall.

2. Johnsy's Transformation:
The next morning, Johnsy ordered the blind drawn and was stunned to see the lone leaf clinging tenaciously to the vine. When it survived another day of storm, Johnsy realized her despair was wicked and rekindled her will to live.

3. Behrman's Sacrifice:
Meanwhile, Behrman was found in his bed wet, frozen, and helpless with acute pneumonia, his shoes and clothes dripping ice. He died in the hospital two days later, having sacrificed his own life to create his masterpiece—the painted leaf that saved Johnsy.

Q5
Why does Sue call the painted ivy leaf Behrman's 'masterpiece'?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Sue calls the painted leaf Behrman's masterpiece for two profound reasons:

  1. Artistic Perfection: The leaf was painted with such exquisite botanical accuracy—dark green near the stem with yellow serrated edges—that both artists (Sue and Johnsy) mistook it for a living leaf.
  2. Spiritual Triumph: A masterpiece is defined by its power to transform human souls. Behrman's leaf saved a human life and rekindled Johnsy's dying spirit, created at the ultimate cost of the artist's own life.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 24CBSE 2026-27
Power of the Human Mind
Psychological Healing: How does O. Henry highlight the profound connection between mental state and physical recovery?

O. Henry demonstrates that the mind holds sovereign sway over bodily health. Johnsy's pneumonia was treatable, but her negative autosuggestion neutralized the efficacy of medicines. When her mental framework shifted from despair to hope upon seeing the unyielding leaf, her immune system rebounded rapidly. The doctor himself confirmed that her chances rose to fifty-fifty the moment she declared a desire to live and paint the Bay of Naples, proving that optimism and hope are the true foundations of physical survival.

Literary Irony
Classic O. Henry Twist: Analyze the signature twist ending in 'The Last Leaf'.

O. Henry is world-renowned for his surprise endings with emotional resonance. Throughout the narrative, readers believe the miracle is a natural phenomenon—a hardy leaf stubbornly clinging to a storm-tossed wall. The revelation in the final paragraph that the leaf was an illusion painted by old Behrman completely recontextualizes the story. The cantankerous, boozy failure is revealed as a noble martyr who traded his life for Johnsy's, transforming a simple story of illness into a sublime testament of love.
