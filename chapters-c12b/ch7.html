Q1
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
What are the various public health measures, which you would suggest as safeguard against infectious diseases?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Public health measures are preventative interventions aimed at breaking transmission vectors, eradicating reservoirs, and providing population-wide immunity:

  1. Maintenance of Personal & Public Hygiene:
    • Keeping the physical body clean, bathing daily, and consuming clean drinking water, uncontaminated food, fruits, and vegetables.
    • Proper sanitary disposal of human excreta, domestic sewage, and chemical wastes; regular cleaning and periodic disinfection of municipal water storage reservoirs, overhead tanks, and swimming pools.
  2. Control and Elimination of Disease Vectors:
    • Preventing stagnancy of water in residential drains, coolers, tires, and pots to eliminate breeding of mosquito vectors (Anopheles, Culex, Aedes).
    • Introducing larvivorous fish like Gambusia in ornamental ponds and water bodies to devour mosquito larvae biologically.
    • Spraying insecticides like DDT/malathion in drainage swamps, spraying oil film over ditches, and using wire mesh/screens on doors and windows.
  3. Universal Immunisation & Vaccination Programmes: Mass inoculation campaigns using childhood vaccines (e.g., BCG, Polio drops, DPT, MMR, Hepatitis-B) to build herd immunity.
  4. Quarantine and Isolation: Prompt clinical isolation of infected individuals suffering from highly contagious airborne or contact diseases (e.g., Pneumonia, Tuberculosis, Chickenpox) to prevent community outbreaks.

Q2
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
In which way has the study of biology helped us to control infectious diseases?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

The advancements in modern biological disciplines (microbiology, immunology, pathology, biochemistry, and recombinant DNA technology) have revolutionized our capability to manage, control, and eradicate infectious diseases:

  1. Discovery of Microbial Etiology & Life Cycles: Identifying causative pathogens (bacteria, viruses, protozoans, fungi, helminths) and elucidating their vectors and life cycles allowed the formulation of targeted prophylactic measures (e.g., breaking the vector cycle of the malaria parasite).
  2. Immunology & Vaccine Development: Understanding the principles of humoral and cell-mediated immunological memory enabled the development of life-saving vaccines. This resulted in the total global eradication of Smallpox and near-complete elimination of Polio, Tetanus, and Diphtheria.
  3. Discovery of Antibiotics & Antimicrobial Drugs: Fleming's discovery of Penicillin and subsequent discovery of wide-spectrum antibiotics, antifungals, and synthetic antimalarials (chloroquine, artemisinin) transformed formerly fatal bacterial infections into easily treatable conditions.
  4. Recombinant DNA Technology: Genetic engineering enabled the scalable, ultra-pure production of safe vaccines (e.g., Recombinant Hepatitis-B vaccine produced from transgenic yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae).
  5. Advanced Molecular Diagnostics: Techniques like ELISA and PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction) allow early, ultra-sensitive detection of pathogens (e.g., HIV, Hepatitis) even before overt clinical symptoms appear.

Q3
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
How does the transmission of each of the following diseases take place?
(a) Amoebiasis
(b) Malaria
(c) Ascariasis
(d) Pneumonia
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution
(a) Amoebiasis (Amoebic Dysentery):
Caused by the protozoan parasite Entamoeba histolytica. Transmitted via the faecal-oral route. Houseflies (Musca domestica) act as mechanical carriers, transferring mature tetranucleate cysts from the faeces of infected individuals to drinking water and food items, which are subsequently ingested.
(b) Malaria:
Caused by protozoans of the genus Plasmodium (P. vivax, P. malariae, P. falciparum). Transmitted biologically through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito, which injects infectious motile sporozoites along with its salivary secretions into the human bloodstream during a blood meal.
(c) Ascariasis:
Caused by the common intestinal roundworm endoparasite Ascaris lumbricoides. The microscopic embryonated eggs of the parasite are excreted in the faeces of infected persons, contaminating soil, groundwater, and growing vegetables/fruits. A healthy person acquires infection through ingestion of contaminated water, unwashed vegetables, or unhygienic dirty fingers.
(d) Pneumonia:
Caused by bacteria such as Streptococcus pneumoniae and Haemophilus influenzae. Transmitted via airborne droplet / aerosol inhalation released by an infected person during coughing or sneezing, or through indirect contact by sharing contaminated utensils, glasses, or handkerchiefs.

Q4
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
What measure would you take to prevent water-borne diseases?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Water-borne diseases (e.g., Cholera, Typhoid, Amoebiasis, Hepatitis-A, and Ascariasis) can be comprehensively prevented through the following sanitary interventions:

  1. Safe and Potable Drinking Water Supply: Ensuring that drinking water is sourced from clean, protected aquifers, subjected to municipal coagulation, sand filtration, and chemical chlorination / ozonation to destroy enteric pathogens.
  2. Household Water Treatment: Boiling drinking water vigorously for 10–15 minutes (which kills heat-sensitive vegetative bacteria, amoebic cysts, and viruses) or using certified domestic ultraviolet (UV) and Reverse Osmosis (RO) water purifiers.
  3. Proper Sewage & Faecal Disposal: Eliminating open defecation through the construction of hygienic sanitary latrines, septic tanks, and modern underground municipal sewage treatment plants (STPs) to prevent subterranean sewage leakage into drinking water mains.
  4. Periodic Disinfection of Reservoirs: Regular de-silting, cleaning, and chemical disinfection of overhead municipal tanks, wells, and domestic storage cisterns using potassium permanganate or bleaching powder.
  5. Food Hygiene and Fly Control: Keeping cooked food and drinking water covered at all times to prevent mechanical contamination by common filth flies.

Q5
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Discuss with your teacher what does 'a suitable gene' means, in the context of DNA vaccines.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

In the context of modern third-generation DNA vaccines (genetic vaccines), the term "a suitable gene" refers to a specific, carefully characterized, and engineered segment of DNA (cloned into an expression plasmid) that satisfies the following criteria:

  1. Codes for a Dominant Immunogenic Antigen: It contains the exact coding nucleotide sequence that directs host cells to synthesize a major outer surface protein or antigenic capsid peptide of the target pathogen (e.g., the gene encoding the Spike glycoprotein of SARS-CoV-2 or the envelope surface antigen HBsAg of Hepatitis-B virus).
  2. Elicits Protective Immune Response: When expressed inside the host cell, the translated antigenic protein is processed and presented on the cell surface via MHC Class I and MHC Class II molecules, stimulating both:
    • Humoral Immunity: Triggering B-lymphocytes to produce neutralising antibodies.
    • Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI): Activating Cytotoxic CD8+ T cells and Memory T cells.
  3. Completely Non-Pathogenic & Safe: The gene chosen must strictly code for an immunogenic structural subunit and must NOT code for any virulent toxin, pathogenic replication enzyme, or oncogenic factor. Thus, the vaccine cannot cause actual disease or revert to virulence.
  4. Optimal Expression Architecture: The gene is paired with a strong eukaryotic promoter (e.g., CMV promoter) and polyadenylation signal ensuring robust transcription and translation within human cells.

Q6
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Name the primary and secondary lymphoid organs.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Lymphoid organs are the specialised anatomical organs where the origin, maturation, and proliferation of lymphocytes occur. They are divided into two categories:

1. Primary Lymphoid Organs

Sites where immature lymphocytes differentiate and mature into antigen-sensitive lymphocytes:

  • Bone Marrow (Site of origin of all blood cells and maturation site of B-lymphocytes)
  • Thymus (Lobular organ located near heart, site of maturation of T-lymphocytes)
2. Secondary Lymphoid Organs

Sites where mature lymphocytes interact with antigens and proliferate into effector cells:

  • Spleen (Large bean-shaped organ, filter of blood)
  • Lymph Nodes (Trap tissue fluid antigens)
  • Tonsils
  • Peyer's Patches of the Small Intestine
  • Appendix
  • MALT (Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue - ~50% of lymphoid tissue)

Differentiate the following and give examples of each:
(a) Innate and acquired immunity
(b) Active and passive immunity
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution
(a) Innate Immunity vs Acquired Immunity
Innate Immunity Acquired (Adaptive) Immunity
Inborn, non-specific defence present from the time of birth. Pathogen-specific defence acquired during an individual's lifetime after exposure to foreign antigens.
Lacks immunological memory; responds with equal intensity upon repeated exposures. Characterised by long-lasting immunological memory; mounts rapid, heightened anamnestic response upon re-exposure.
Examples: Physical barrier (Skin, mucus), Physiological (HCl in stomach, Lysozyme in tears), Cellular (PMNL neutrophils, Macrophages), Cytokine (Interferons). Examples: Antibodies produced against measles infection; immunity developed after vaccination with Polio or BCG vaccine.
(b) Active Immunity vs Passive Immunity
Active Immunity Passive Immunity
Developed when the host's own immune system is challenged by living or dead microbes/antigens to synthesize antibodies. Conferred by introducing ready-made preformed antibodies directly into the recipient's body from an external donor.
Slow in onset, takes time to develop full response, but provides long-lasting immunological protection. Provides immediate, fast-acting emergency protection, but is temporary and does not generate immunological memory.
Examples: Recovery from natural chickenpox infection; active immunisation with MMR or Polio vaccines. Examples: Secretory IgA received by neonate through mother's colostrum; maternal IgG crossing placenta; Anti-tetanus serum (ATS); Anti-venom for snakebite.

Q9
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Draw a well-labelled diagram of an antibody molecule.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Structural Description of an Antibody Molecule:

  • An antibody (immunoglobulin, Ig) is a Y-shaped glycoprotein represented as H2L2.
  • It consists of four polypeptide chains:
    • Two identical long Heavy (H) chains (~440 amino acids each, ~50 kDa).
    • Two identical short Light (L) chains (~220 amino acids each, ~25 kDa).
  • The four chains are held together in a Y-configuration by covalent inter-chain disulphide (-S-S-) bonds and non-covalent interactions.
  • Variable Region (Fab fragment): The N-terminal tips of both heavy and light chains form the Antigen-Binding Site (Paratope), highly specific to a particular antigen epitope (lock-and-key fit). Each antibody molecule has two antigen-binding sites (bivalent).
  • Constant Region (Fc fragment): The C-terminal stem region mediates effector biological functions (complement activation, binding to macrophage Fc receptors).

Labels required in standard CBSE diagram:

Antigen-binding site • Light Chain • Heavy Chain • Disulphide Bonds (-S-S-) • Variable Region (VH, VL) • Constant Region (CH, CL) • C-terminus • N-terminus

Q10
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
What are the various routes by which transmission of human immunodeficiency virus takes place?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), the causative lentivirus of AIDS, is transmitted exclusively through specific parenteral body fluid exchanges:

  1. Unprotected Sexual Intercourse: Sexual contact (vaginal, anal, or oral) with an HIV-infected partner without consistent barrier contraception (condom), allowing mucosal fluid transmission.
  2. Transfusion of Contaminated Blood & Blood Products: Transfusion of unscreened blood or infusing clotting factor concentrates from infected donors.
  3. Sharing of Contaminated Needles & Syringes: Inoculation via unsterilized hypodermic needles, particularly among intravenous (IV) drug abusers sharing injection paraphernalia.
  4. Perinatal / Vertical Transmission from Mother to Child: Transmission from an infected mother to her fetus across the placenta during pregnancy, during parturition (childbirth), or post-natally through breastfeeding.
  5. Accidental Occupational Exposure: Needlestick injuries or mucosal splashes encountered by healthcare personnel handling infected blood specimens.

Important Note: HIV is NOT transmitted through casual non-sexual contact, shaking hands, hugging, sharing meals, sharing toilets, coughing, or mosquito bites.

Q11
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
What is the mechanism by which the AIDS virus causes deficiency of immune system of the infected person?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

HIV progressively dismantles the host's immune system through targeted replication inside helper T-lymphocytes:

  1. Viral Entry: HIV enters the host body and binds specifically via its envelope glycoprotein gp120 to the CD4 receptors and chemokine co-receptors (CCR5/CXCR4) present on the surface of macrophages and Helper T-lymphocytes (TH cells).
  2. Macrophage as HIV Factory:
    • Upon entering the macrophage, the viral single-stranded genomic RNA is reverse-transcribed into viral double-stranded DNA by the viral enzyme Reverse Transcriptase.
    • The viral DNA enters the host nucleus and incorporates into the host chromosome via the viral enzyme Integrase.
    • The infected macrophage continuously transcribes and sheds new viral progeny, acting as an HIV factory without immediately undergoing lysis.
  3. Attack on Helper T-Lymphocytes (TH Cells): Simultaneously, HIV enters Helper T-cells (CD4+ cells), replicates vigorously, and causes massive cellular lysis, releasing thousands of daughter virions that attack uninfected helper T-cells in the bloodstream and lymphoid organs.
  4. Progressive Depletion of CD4+ T Cells: The number of helper T-cells steadily drops from normal levels (900–1200 cells/mm3) to below 200 cells/mm3.
  5. Immune Collapse & Opportunistic Infections: Because Helper T-cells are essential for orchestrating both humoral (B-cell activation) and cell-mediated immunity, their depletion renders the patient severely immunocompromised. The individual becomes vulnerable to fatal opportunistic infections by pathogens that are normally benign (e.g., Mycobacterium tuberculosis, Cytomegalovirus, fungi like Candida, and the protozoan Toxoplasma gondii).

Q12
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
How is a cancerous cell different from a normal cell?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Cancer cells exhibit profound morphological, physiological, and genetic deviations from normal somatic cells:

Feature Normal Cell Cancerous Cell
1. Contact Inhibition Shows contact inhibition: physical contact with neighbouring cells halts cell division when a monolayer is formed. Lacks contact inhibition: cells continue dividing uncontrollably, piling on top of each other to form neoplastic masses (tumours).
2. Cell Proliferation & Lifespan Division is strictly regulated by cell cycle checkpoints; undergoes programmed cell death (apoptosis) after a finite number of divisions (Hayflick limit). Undergoes unconstrained, autonomous, indefinite division (immortalization due to persistent telomerase expression); evades apoptosis.
3. Differentiation Highly specialized and structurally differentiated to perform specific tissue functions. Undifferentiated or poorly differentiated (anaplasia); loses tissue-specific structural architecture.
4. Metastasis & Invasiveness Adheres securely to extracellular matrix; does not invade neighbouring tissues or migrate. Exhibits metastasis: breaks loose from primary tumour, enters bloodstream/lymph, and seeds secondary tumours at distant body sites.
5. Angiogenesis Normal, physiological microvasculature. Secretes tumour angiogenic factors (e.g., VEGF) inducing rapid neo-vascularisation to hijack host nutrients.

Q13
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Explain what is meant by metastasis.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Definition:

Metastasis is the pathological process by which malignant cancer cells slough off from the primary tumour, intravasate into circulatory vessels (blood or lymphatic channels), travel to distant anatomical sites in the body, extravasate into normal tissues, and establish secondary tumours.

Significance & Mechanism:

  1. Metastasis is the most dreaded and dangerous characteristic property of malignant neoplasms, distinguishing malignant tumours from benign tumours (which remain localized encapsulated masses).
  2. Stepwise Metastatic Cascade:
    • Local Infiltration: Cancer cells secrete matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) to digest the extracellular basement membrane.
    • Intravasation: Cells penetrate into capillary blood vessels or lymphatics.
    • Transport & Evasion: Cells survive hemodynamic shear stress and evade immune surveillance in circulation.
    • Extravasation & Colonisation: Cells arrest in microvessels of distant vital organs (lungs, liver, bones, brain), extravasate into parenchyma, and proliferate rapidly into aggressive secondary tumours.

Q14
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
List the harmful effects of smoking.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Tobacco smoke contains over 4,000 toxic chemical compounds, including at least 60 known human carcinogens, nicotine, and carbon monoxide. The major deleterious health consequences include:

  1. Cardiovascular Hazards:
    • Nicotine: An alkaloid that stimulates the adrenal medulla to release adrenaline and noradrenaline into circulation, causing tachycardia (rapid heart rate) and acute elevation of blood pressure (hypertension).
    • Carbon Monoxide (CO): Binds to haemoglobin with 200-fold higher affinity than oxygen, forming carboxyhaemoglobin (CO-Hb). This significantly reduces the oxygen-carrying capacity of blood, leading to tissue hypoxia and arteriosclerosis.
  2. Cancers: Strongly linked to malignant cancers of the lungs, urinary bladder, throat, oral cavity, larynx, and esophagus.
  3. Respiratory Pathologies:
    • Emphysema: Chronic inhalation destroys alveolar septa, severely reducing the respiratory surface area available for gas exchange.
    • Chronic Bronchitis: Paralyses respiratory ciliated epithelium and causes chronic hypersecretion of mucus, persistent coughing, and airway obstruction.
  4. Gastric Ulcers: Tobacco increases gastric hydrochloric acid secretion, increasing incidence of peptic and duodenal ulcers.
  5. Pregnancy Complications: Smoking during pregnancy restricts fetal oxygenation, causing intrauterine growth retardation (IUGR), low birth weight, and spontaneous miscarriage.

Do you think that friends can influence one to take alcohol/drugs? If yes, how may one protect himself/herself from such an influence?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Yes, peer influence is one of the most decisive initiating triggers for drug and alcohol experimentation among adolescents. During adolescence, youngsters crave peer acceptance, fear social exclusion, and are particularly vulnerable to peer pressure.

Measures to Protect Oneself from Negative Peer Influence:

  1. Learn to Say "NO" Assertively: Cultivate clear personal boundaries and assertively refuse offers of alcohol, cigarettes, or drugs without guilt or hesitation (e.g., firmly stating "No, I don't consume alcohol/drugs").
  2. Choose Healthy Friendships: Distance oneself from peer groups that glorify substance abuse or indulge in illicit activities; actively surround oneself with peers who share constructive academic, athletic, and creative interests.
  3. Avoid Undue Peer Pressure: Do not compromise personal moral values, physical health, or future ambitions simply to "fit in" or appear mature and rebellious.
  4. Seek Counsel from Parents and Mentors: Share personal conflicts, peer dilemmas, or anxieties openly with parents, teachers, or elder siblings, seeking their guidance.
  5. Channel Energy into Positive Pursuits: Direct physical energy and intellectual curiosity into sports, yoga, music, reading, martial arts, or community service.
  6. Seek Professional Psychological Support: If unable to cope with peer pressure or experiencing chronic anxiety/depression, seek help from school counsellors or clinical psychologists.

Q16
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Why is that once a person starts taking alcohol or drugs, it is difficult to get rid of this habit? Discuss it with your teacher.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

The biological and psychological difficulty in breaking free from substance abuse stems from two interrelated neurological phenomena: Addiction (Tolerance) and Dependence (Withdrawal Syndrome):

  1. Neurobiological Addiction & Reward Circuitry:
    • Drugs and alcohol stimulate the mesolimbic dopamine reward pathway in the brain, inducing intense feelings of euphoria and a temporary sense of well-being.
    • With repeated use, the brain's neuroreceptors undergo down-regulation; their tolerance level increases. Consequently, the user requires progressively higher and more frequent doses to experience the same initial high.
  2. Physiological and Psychological Dependence:
    • The body adapts homeostatically to the constant presence of the chemical. The individual becomes completely dependent on the drug to function normally.
  3. Severe Withdrawal Syndrome:
    • If regular intake of the drug or alcohol is abruptly discontinued, the individual experiences agonizing, clinically manifest withdrawal symptoms.
    • These include severe anxiety, trembling/tremors, profuse sweating, nausea, vomiting, insomnia, depression, and hallucinations (e.g., delirium tremens in alcohol withdrawal), which can be life-threatening.
    • To escape this unbearable psychological and physical distress, the individual relapses and consumes the drug again, perpetuating a vicious trap.
  4. Compulsive Craving & Loss of Self-Control: The drug alters prefrontal cortical circuits responsible for decision making, judgment, and impulse control.

Q17
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
In your view what motivates youngsters to take to alcohol or drugs and how can this be avoided?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

1. Factors that Motivate Youngsters to Experiment with Alcohol or Drugs:

  • Curiosity and Experimentation: Inherent adolescent desire to explore the unknown or experience a novel "high".
  • Peer Pressure & Need to Belong: The pressure to fit into popular social cliques or project an image of "coolness" and adulthood.
  • Escape from Stress and Academic Pressure: Inability to manage severe competition, high parental expectations, and academic stress.
  • Unstable Family Environment: Broken homes, domestic abuse, lack of parental warmth, or parental substance abuse.
  • Media Glamorisation: Depiction of smoking, drinking, and drug usage in movies, television series, and social media as symbols of glamour, freedom, and rebellion.

2. Preventative & Remedial Interventions:

  1. Avoid Undue Peer Pressure: Parents and teachers should not thrust unfair comparisons or unachievable expectations on adolescents; each child's individual pace, strengths, and personality must be respected.
  2. Education and Open Counselling: Imparting comprehensive awareness about the biological, physiological, and social hazards of drug abuse, teaching youngsters to face problems constructively.
  3. Seeking Help from Parents and Peers: Youngsters facing anxiety or distress should be encouraged to vent emotions freely with parents, teachers, and close friends rather than seeking solitary chemical solace.
  4. Looking for Danger Signs: Vigilant parents and teachers should look out for early behavioural warnings (sudden drop in grades, absenteeism, aggressive defiance, isolation, bloodshot eyes) and initiate early interventions.
  5. Seeking Professional Medical Help: Enrolling affected youth in specialised medical de-addiction and rehabilitation clinics for clinical detoxification and cognitive behavioural therapy.
