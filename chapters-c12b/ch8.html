NCERT Exercises (15 Verbatim Questions)
Q1
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Bacteria cannot be seen with the naked eyes, but these can be seen with the help of a microscope. If you have to carry a sample from your home to your biology laboratory to demonstrate the presence of microbes under a microscope, which sample would you carry and why?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Sample to Carry:

The best and most accessible sample to carry is a fresh drop of Curd (Yoghurt) or buttermilk.

Scientific Reasons:

  1. Vast Bacterial Density: A single drop of curd contains millions of living bacteria, primarily Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB) such as Lactobacillus acidophilus.
  2. Ease of Demonstration: Preparing a temporary wet-mount slide or stained smear is straightforward:
    • Take a tiny loop of curd diluted in a drop of water on a clean glass slide.
    • Heat-fix gently and stain with methylene blue or crystal violet.
    • Under the high-power objective (40X or 100X oil immersion), distinct rod-shaped (bacilli) Lactobacillus cells can be clearly visualised individually and in chains.
  3. Safety & Non-Pathogenicity: LAB are harmless, probiotic, food-grade microorganisms safe for student handling.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Identification of curd/yoghurt as the ideal domestic sample1 Mark
Scientific rationale: Rich in Lactobacillus (LAB) and safe/easy to mount and stain under microscope1 Mark
Q2
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Give examples to prove that microbes release gases during metabolism.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Microbes release gases (primarily CO2, CH4, and H2) during anaerobic fermentation and cellular respiration, proven by the following examples:

  1. Puffed-up Appearance of Dough (Dosa and Idli Batter):
    • The dough used for making idli and dosa puffs up and becomes aerated due to the production of carbon dioxide gas (CO2) through anaerobic fermentation by natural bacterial microflora (e.g., Leuconostoc mesenteroides and Streptococcus faecalis).
  2. Leavening of Bread by Baker's Yeast:
    • In bread-making, Baker's Yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) ferments dough sugars into ethanol and CO2. The trapped bubbles of escaping CO2 gas cause the dough to rise and give baked bread its characteristic spongy, porous texture.
  3. Large Holes in Swiss Cheese:
    • The characteristic large holes ("eyes") in Swiss cheese are created by the release of copious amounts of carbon dioxide (CO2) gas by the bacterium Propionibacterium freudenreichii / P. shermanii during propionic acid fermentation.
  4. Biogas (Gobar Gas) Production:
    • Methanogenic bacteria (e.g., Methanobacterium) growing anaerobically on cellulosic livestock dung produce large volumes of inflammable biogas consisting of methane (CH4, 50–70%), CO2 (30–40%), and H2S.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Puffed-up dough for idli/dosa due to CO2 released by fermenting bacteria1 Mark
Bread making: Sponginess due to CO2 released by Baker's yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae)1 Mark
Swiss cheese holes (Propionibacterium shermanii) and Biogas production (Methanobacterium releasing CH4/CO2)1 Mark
Q3
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
In which food would you find lactic acid bacteria? Mention some of their useful applications.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Occurrence:

Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB), such as Lactobacillus and related species, are prominently found in Curd (Yoghurt), Buttermilk, and Fermented Milk Cheeses.

Useful Applications of Lactic Acid Bacteria:

  1. Conversion of Milk into Curd:
    • A small amount of curd added to fresh lukewarm milk acts as an inoculum or starter containing millions of LAB.
    • At optimal incubation temperature (~37–40°C), LAB multiply and produce lactic acid, which coagulates and partially digests milk proteins (casein), converting liquid milk into solid curd.
  2. Enhancement of Nutritional Quality:
    • LAB fermentation dramatically enriches the nutritional value of curd by substantially increasing the concentration of Vitamin B12.
  3. Inhibition of Gut Pathogens:
    • In the human gastrointestinal tract, LAB act as protective probiotics by producing bacteriocins and maintaining an acidic luminal pH that checks and suppresses the growth of harmful disease-causing enteric pathogens.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Naming food source: Curd, buttermilk, and fermented dairy products1 Mark
Conversion of milk to curd via lactic acid coagulation of casein1 Mark
Nutritional enrichment with Vitamin B12 and probiotic checking of gut pathogens1 Mark
Q4
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Name some traditional Indian foods made of wheat, rice and Bengal gram (or their products) which involve use of microbes.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Traditional fermented Indian culinary items prepared through microbial action include:

  1. Foods Made from Rice and Bengal Gram (Black Gram / Urad Dal):
    • Idli: Steamed soft savory cakes prepared from a fermented mixture of soaked rice and black gram batter, fermented by bacteria like Leuconostoc mesenteroides and Streptococcus faecalis.
    • Dosa: Crisp crepes prepared from the same fermented rice and black gram batter.
    • Dhokla: A Gujarati delicacy made from a fermented batter of coarsely ground Bengal gram (chana dal) or chickpeas and rice, fermented by lactic bacteria.
    • Vada: Savoury fried fritters made from soaked and lightly fermented Bengal gram / black gram paste.
  2. Foods Made from Wheat:
    • Bhatura & Naan: Traditional leavened flatbreads made from refined wheat flour (maida) mixed with curd, warm water, and yeast / bacterial starter, leavened by anaerobic CO2 production.
    • Bread: Leavened wheat loaf baked using Baker's yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae).
    • Jalebi: Sweet spirals made from a fermented batter of refined wheat flour (maida) allowed to ferment overnight.
  3. Traditional Drink (Bonus Mention):
    • Toddy: A traditional refreshing South Indian beverage prepared by fermenting the sugary sap of palm trees with wild yeasts.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Rice and Bengal gram foods: Idli, Dosa, Dhokla, Vada with microbial role1.5 Marks
Wheat products: Bhatura, Naan, leavened bread, Jalebi batter1 Mark
Reference to traditional fermented drink (Toddy) or specific fermenting microbes0.5 Mark
Q5
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
In which way have microbes played a major role in controlling diseases caused by harmful bacteria?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Microorganisms have played a revolutionary role in controlling and curing bacterial diseases primarily through the discovery and industrial manufacturing of Antibiotics:

  1. Production of Antibiotics:
    • Antibiotics are chemical substances produced by certain microorganisms that can kill or inhibit the growth of other disease-causing pathogenic microorganisms even at very low concentrations.
    • Alexander Fleming's Discovery of Penicillin (1928): Discovered from the mold Penicillium notatum; its full therapeutic potential as an antibiotic was established by Ernest Chain and Howard Florey during World War II.
    • Other life-saving antibiotics discovered from actinomycetes/bacteria include:
      • Streptomycin (from Streptomyces griseus) for tuberculosis.
      • Tetracycline, Chloramphenicol, and Erythromycin for typhoid, pneumonia, and broad-spectrum bacterial infections.
  2. Production of Vaccines: Attenuated or killed bacterial strains (e.g., BCG for tuberculosis, cholera vaccine, and recombinant bacterial antigens) stimulate host antibody production and immunological memory.
  3. Competitive Exclusion via Probiotics: Beneficial bacteria (e.g., Lactobacillus) in the gut synthesize bacteriocins and maintain an acidic pH that competitively prevents colonisation by pathogens like Salmonella and Shigella.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Role of antibiotics as chemical substances produced by microbes killing pathogenic bacteria1 Mark
Historic examples: Penicillin (Penicillium notatum) and Streptomycin (Streptomyces griseus)1 Mark
Impact on controlling fatal plagues, diphtheria, leprosy, and role of vaccines/probiotics1 Mark

Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Name any two species of fungus, which are used in the production of the antibiotics.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Two fungal species industrially utilized for the commercial production of antibiotics are:

  1. Penicillium notatum / Penicillium chrysogenum:
    • Used for the industrial production of Penicillin (a β-lactam bactericidal antibiotic that inhibits bacterial peptidoglycan cell-wall synthesis).
  2. Cephalosporium acremonium (now known as Acremonium chrysogenum):
    • Used for the industrial production of Cephalosporins (a broad-spectrum class of β-lactam antibiotics effective against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria).

Q7
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
What is sewage? In which way can sewage be harmful to us?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Definition of Sewage:

Sewage is the municipal wastewater generated in large quantities daily in human settlements, towns, and cities. It contains human excreta, domestic kitchen and bathroom discharge, wash waters, organic debris, and toxic street runoff.

Harmful Effects of Untreated Sewage:

  1. Spread of Fatal Water-Borne Epidemics: Sewage contains dense populations of dangerous pathogenic bacteria, viruses, and parasites. Discharging raw sewage into rivers contaminates potable drinking supplies, triggering devastating epidemics of cholera, typhoid, hepatitis-A, dysentery, and polio.
  2. Eutrophication & Severe Oxygen Depletion (Fish Mortality): Raw sewage has an extremely high Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD). Aerobic decomposition by saprophytic bacteria rapidly consumes all dissolved oxygen (DO) in receiving water bodies, suffocating fish and collapsing aquatic ecosystems.
  3. Bioaccumulation of Toxic Chemical Contaminants: Modern municipal sewage contains non-biodegradable detergents, heavy metals (lead, mercury, cadmium), microplastics, and pharmaceutical residues that bioaccumulate in aquatic food chains.
  4. Foul Odour and Environmental Degradation: Anaerobic putrefaction produces foul-smelling toxic gases like hydrogen sulphide (H2S) and methane, turning water bodies into toxic open cesspools.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Definition of municipal sewage (wastewater containing human excreta, domestic waste, organic matter)1 Mark

Q8
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
What is the key difference between primary and secondary sewage treatment?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Sewage treatment is carried out in two distinct successive stages:

Primary Treatment (Physical) Secondary Treatment (Biological)
A strictly physical process involving the mechanical removal of large and small particulate debris. A biological process involving the metabolic action of aerobic and anaerobic microorganisms.
Accomplished by sequential filtration (removes floating trash) and sedimentation (removes grit, soil, and pebbles). Accomplished in huge aeration tanks where vigorous air pumping stimulates growth of flocs (bacteria associated with fungal filaments).
Produces primary sludge (solids that settle) and primary effluent (supernatant). Produces activated sludge (settled microbial flocs) and biogas via anaerobic sludge digesters.
Does not alter the dissolved organic load; BOD remains extremely high. Microbes digest dissolved organic matter, drastically reducing the Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) by 90–95%.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Primary treatment: Physical removal of debris via sequential filtration and sedimentation1 Mark
Secondary treatment: Biological digestion by aerobic microbial flocs in aeration tanks1 Mark
Outcome contrast: Primary separates coarse sludge vs Secondary drastically reduces BOD of effluent1 Mark
Q9
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Do you think microbes can also be used as source of energy? If yes, how?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Yes, microbes are widely used as a renewable, decentralized source of clean energy.

Scientific Mechanism: Biogas (Gobar Gas) Generation:

  1. Role of Methanogens:
    • Certain anaerobic bacteria, collectively called methanogens (e.g., Methanobacterium), grow anaerobically on cellulosic materials such as cow dung (gobar), agricultural crop residues, and human excreta.
    • Methanogens are naturally present in the rumen (first stomach chamber) of cattle, aiding in cellulose breakdown; hence, cattle dung is exceptionally rich in these bacteria.
  2. Operation of a Biogas Plant:
    • Cattle dung and water are mixed in a 1:1 ratio to form a slurry, fed into a concrete digester pit (10–15 feet deep).
    • Under strict anaerobic conditions, methanogens digest the slurry in three metabolic phases: hydrolysis, acidogenesis, and methanogenesis.
    • This produces Biogas, a gaseous mixture consisting of:
      • Methane (CH4): 50–70% (primary combustible fuel)
      • Carbon dioxide (CO2): 30–40%
      • Traces of H2, N2, and H2S
  3. Utility: Biogas is piped directly into rural households for smokeless cooking, domestic lighting, and running agricultural water pumps. The residual spent slurry serves as rich organic manure (biofertiliser).
  4. Microbial Biofuels (Bonus): Yeast (Saccharomyces) ferments molasses/corn starch into bioethanol, used as green biofuel blended with petrol.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Clear affirmation (Yes) and identification of Methanobacterium / methanogens1 Mark
Process of anaerobic digestion of cellulosic dung producing biogas (50-70% methane)1 Mark
Practical utilisation for smokeless rural cooking/lighting and bioethanol production1 Mark
Q10
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Microbes can be used to decrease the use of chemical fertilisers and pesticides. Explain how this can be accomplished.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Indiscriminate use of chemical fertilisers and synthetic pesticides degrades soil health, causes groundwater pollution, and leads to biomagnification. Microbes offer eco-friendly, biological alternatives:

1. Microbes as Biofertilisers (Replacing Chemical NPK Fertilisers):

  • Symbiotic Nitrogen Fixers: Rhizobium forms root nodules in leguminous crops, fixing atmospheric N2 into organic forms readily absorbed by plants.
  • Free-Living & Associative Nitrogen Fixers: Azotobacter and Azospirillum enrich soil nitrogen content in non-leguminous crops.
  • Mycorrhizal Fungi: Fungi of the genus Glomus establish symbiotic mycorrhizal associations with plant roots. The fungal hyphae absorb phosphorus from soil and pass it to the plant, while conferring resistance to root pathogens and tolerance to drought.
  • Cyanobacteria: Free-living or symbiotic autotrophs (e.g., Anabaena, Nostoc, Oscillatoria) fix nitrogen and add organic matter, acting as critical biofertilisers in paddy (rice) fields.

2. Microbes as Biocontrol Agents (Replacing Chemical Pesticides):

  • Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt): Spores of Bt produce insecticidal crystal (Cry) proteins. When sprayed on crops (e.g., brassicas and fruit trees), caterpillars ingest the spores. The protoxin dissolves in the alkaline insect gut, creating pores that kill the pest without harming birds, beneficial insects, or mammals.
  • Trichoderma Species: Free-living soil-dwelling fungi present in root ecosystems that act as potent biological control agents against diverse plant root fungal pathogens.
  • Baculoviruses (Genus Nucleopolyhedrovirus - NPV): Pathogens that attack insects and other arthropods. They are excellent biological control agents because of their narrow-spectrum, species-specific insecticidal action, having zero negative impact on non-target insects, mammals, birds, or beneficial pollinators (ideal for Integrated Pest Management - IPM).
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Biofertilisers: Nitrogen fixers (Rhizobium, Azotobacter, Cyanobacteria) and phosphorus-absorbing Glomus mycorrhiza2 Marks
Biocontrol: Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) for caterpillars and Trichoderma for soil root pathogens1 Mark
Baculoviruses (NPV) providing narrow-spectrum, species-specific IPM control1 Mark
Q11
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Three water samples namely river water, untreated sewage water and secondary effluent discharged from a sewage treatment plant were subjected to BOD test. The samples were labelled A, B and C; but the laboratory attendant forgot to label which was which. The BOD values of the three samples A, B and C were recorded as 20 mg/L, 8 mg/L and 400 mg/L, respectively.
Which sample of the water is most polluted? Can you assign the correct label to each assuming the river water is relatively clean?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Scientific Principle of the BOD Test:

  • Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) is the amount of oxygen required by aerobic microbes to oxidize all the biodegradable organic matter present in one liter of water.
  • There is a direct linear relationship between BOD and organic pollution: higher the organic waste load, greater is microbial respiration, higher is the BOD, and lower is the dissolved oxygen (DO).

1. Identification of the Most Polluted Water Sample:

Sample C is the most polluted water sample because it has the highest BOD value (400 mg/L).

2. Assigning Correct Labels to Samples A, B, and C:

  • Sample C (BOD = 400 mg/L): Untreated Sewage Water
    Contains raw human excreta and highest dissolved/suspended organic matter; hence consumes maximum dissolved oxygen during microbial decomposition.
  • Sample A (BOD = 20 mg/L): Secondary Effluent from STP
    Has undergone primary sedimentation and secondary biological digestion in aeration tanks; its BOD has been reduced by 90–95%, but still retains minor residual organic load.
  • Sample B (BOD = 8 mg/L): Clean River Water
    Relatively clean, natural flowing surface water containing minimal organic contaminants and supporting healthy aquatic life; hence has the lowest BOD.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Identification of Sample C (400 mg/L) as the most polluted based on BOD principle1 Mark
Correct assignment of Sample C = Untreated sewage water0.5 Mark
Correct assignment of Sample A = Secondary effluent from STP (20 mg/L)0.5 Mark
Correct assignment of Sample B = Relatively clean river water (8 mg/L)1 Mark
Q12
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Find out the name of the microbes from which Cyclosporin A (an immunosuppressive drug) and Statins (blood cholesterol lowering agents) are obtained.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

The producing microorganisms for these two clinically indispensable bioactive molecules are:

1. Cyclosporin A (Immunosuppressive Drug)

Producing Microbe: The filamentous fungus Trichoderma polysporum.

Clinical Utility: Used as an essential immunosuppressive agent in organ-transplant recipients to prevent graft rejection by suppressing T-lymphocyte activation.

2. Statins (Blood Cholesterol-Lowering Agents)

Producing Microbe: The yeast Monascus purpureus.

Biochemical Mechanism: Statins act by competitively inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme responsible for de novo endogenous cholesterol biosynthesis in the liver.

CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Cyclosporin A obtained from the fungus Trichoderma polysporum1 Mark
Statins obtained from the yeast Monascus purpureus (with competitive inhibition of cholesterol synthesis)1 Mark
Q13
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Find out the role of microbes in the following and discuss it with your teacher:
(a) Single cell protein (SCP)
(b) Soil
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution
(a) Single Cell Protein (SCP)

Microorganisms represent an alternate, protein-rich biomass for human food and animal feed to combat global malnutrition and food insecurity:

  • Spirulina (Cyanobacterium): Cultivated easily on potato processing wastewater, straw, molasses, or sewage. Rich in proteins, minerals, vitamins, essential fatty acids, and carbohydrates.
  • Methylophilus methylotrophus (Bacterium): Because of its rapid growth rate and high biomass productivity, 250 g of this micro-organism can produce an astounding 25 tonnes of protein in 24 hours (compared to a 250 kg cow producing only ~200 g protein/day).
  • Mushrooms (e.g., Agaricus bisporus): Edible basidiomycete fungi grown on agricultural compost.
(b) Soil

Microbes form the foundation of pedogenesis, soil texture, fertility, and biochemical biogeochemical cycling:

  • Decomposition & Humification: Saprophytic bacteria and fungi decompose leaf litter, dead animal carcasses, and cellulose into rich, dark amorphous humus, releasing immobilized N, P, K, and micro-nutrients back into the soil solution.
  • Nitrogen Cycling: Biological nitrogen fixation by Rhizobium, nitrification by Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter, and denitrification by Pseudomonas balance terrestrial nitrogen.
  • Soil Aggregation: Fungal hyphae and bacterial exopolysaccharides bind fine mineral particles together, improving soil porosity, crumb structure, and water retention capacity.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(a) Role in SCP: Microbes (Spirulina, Methylophilus methylotrophus) providing protein-rich biomass with rapid yield2 Marks
(b) Role in Soil: Decomposition, humus formation, biogeochemical nutrient recycling, and improving soil aeration2 Marks
Q14
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
Arrange the following in the decreasing order (most important first) of their importance, for the welfare of human society. Give reasons for your answer:
Biogas, Citric acid, Penicillin and Curd.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Arrangement in Decreasing Order of Importance for Human Welfare:

1. Penicillin > 2. Biogas > 3. Curd > 4. Citric Acid

Detailed Rationale for Hierarchy:

  1. 1. Penicillin (Most Critical - Life Saving):
    Antibiotics like penicillin are indispensable for human survival. Before its discovery, minor bacterial wounds, pneumonia, and childbirth sepsis caused millions of deaths. It revolutionized medicine by treating life-threatening infectious diseases worldwide.
  2. 2. Biogas (Ecological & Energy Security):
    Biogas provides a renewable, smokeless, clean fuel for rural households, addressing rural deforestation, energy poverty, and global warming while transforming foul cattle excreta into pathogen-free organic manure.
  3. 3. Curd (Nutritional & Probiotic Health):
    A staple domestic dairy food item consumed daily across millions of households. It supplies high biological-value milk protein, synthesizes Vitamin B12, and acts as a gut probiotic checking harmful pathogen growth.
  4. 4. Citric Acid (Industrial Food Additive):
    Produced by the mold Aspergillus niger. While widely used as an industrial acidifier, flavouring agent, and preservative in beverages and confectioneries, its societal importance is commercial rather than vital to life preservation.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Correct hierarchy: Penicillin > Biogas > Curd > Citric acid1 Mark
Justification for Penicillin (life-saving antibiotic) and Biogas (renewable energy/sanitation)1 Mark
Justification for Curd (daily nutritional/probiotic food) and Citric acid (commercial food additive)1 Mark
Q15
Exercises (NCERT Verbatim)
How do biofertilisers enrich the fertility of the soil?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step Biological Solution

Biofertilisers are living cultures of beneficial microorganisms that enrich the nutrient quality and physical fertility of soil through biological pathways:

  1. Biological Nitrogen Fixation (BNF):
    • Atmospheric nitrogen (N2) cannot be assimilated directly by plants. Biofertilisers possess the enzyme nitrogenase that reduces atmospheric N2 into ammonia (NH3):
      • Symbiotic bacteria: Rhizobium forms nodules in legumes, fixing up to 50–100 kg N/ha/year.
      • Free-living bacteria: Azotobacter (aerobic) and Clostridium (anaerobic) enrich arable soil.
      • Associative symbionts: Azospirillum colonises the rhizosphere of cereals (sorghum, maize).
  2. Phosphate Solubilisation & Uptake (Mycorrhiza):
    • Fungi of the genus Glomus form symbiotic endo-mycorrhizal associations with plant roots. Their extensive fungal mycelial network explores soil volumes far beyond root hairs to absorb insoluble phosphorus and deliver it to host plants.
    • Mycorrhizae also confer systemic resistance to root-borne pathogens, tolerance to salinity and drought, and increase overall plant vigor.
  3. Enrichment via Cyanobacteria:
    • In wetland paddy (rice) agroecosystems, autotrophic cyanobacteria (e.g., Anabaena, Nostoc, Aulosira, Oscillatoria) fix nitrogen in specialised thick-walled cells called heterocysts and contribute substantial organic biomass, preventing soil erosion.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Nitrogen fixation: Nitrogenase converting atmospheric N2 to ammonia (Rhizobium, Azotobacter, Azospirillum)1 Mark
Phosphate mobilization: Mycorrhizal fungi (Glomus) absorbing phosphorus and conferring drought/disease resistance1 Mark
Cyanobacteria (Anabaena, Nostoc) in paddy fields adding nitrogen and organic matter1 Mark
