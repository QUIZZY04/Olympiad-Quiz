Chapter 6: Trains & The Story of the Road

James S. Tippett's Poem on Trains & The Morning Awakening of the Village Road | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Poem 'Trains': Over mountains, plains, and rivers carry passenger cars, mail, and precious freight day and night.
  • Story 'The Story of the Road': Early morning silence on the road, awakened by chirping sparrows, caw of crows, newspaper boy's cycle bell, and vegetable seller.
  • Anthropomorphic storytelling: The road wishing the noisy crow would realize it is already wide awake.
  • Transportation vocabulary: freight, passengers, mail, level crossing, bicycle bell.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (17 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Reading is Fun • Poem & Story
What precious loads do the trains carry across the country in 'Trains'? Who are the first creatures and people to awaken the road in the morning?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
1. Precious Cargo: Trains carry thousands of passengers, precious mail, letters, and essential freight across mountains, plains, and rivers without failing.
2. First to Awaken the Road: The birds (chirping sparrows and cawing crows), the newspaper boy ringing his bicycle bell ('Tring-a-ling!'), and the vegetable seller calling out fresh peas, cauliflowers, and potatoes.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct freight, mail, and passenger details 1.5 M
Correct list of early morning road characters 1.5 M
Q2 Story Humour • The Annoyed Road
Why was the road annoyed with the crow in 'The Story of the Road'?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
The crow kept cawing foolishly: 'Caw! Caw! Wake up! Wake up!'. The road was annoyed because it was already wide awake, seeing the newspaper boy, smelling the vegetables, and hearing school children chattering. The road thought: 'Can't you see, you silly bird? I am wide awake!'
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Explanation of crow's repetitive wake up call 1.0 M
Road's annoyance at already being wide awake 1.0 M

[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Sound Matching:
• 1. Bicycle bell → Tring-tring
• 2. Shoes of children → Tramp-tramp

CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme

Q4 Poem Comprehension • 'Trains'
In the poem 'Trains':
(a) Name three geographical places over which the trains travel.
(b) What valuable things do the freight trains carry?
(c) Do trains stop traveling at night or during dark hours?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Poem Analysis:
(a) Geographical Places:
• Over the mountains, over the plains, and over the rivers.

(b) Cargo:
• They carry thousands of passengers and heavy freight / precious mail.

(c) Night Travel:
• No, trains rush on continuously through day and dark, through dawn and dusk without stopping.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Mountains, plains, rivers named 1.0 M
Passengers and precious mail noted 1.0 M
Continuous day-night travel described 1.0 M
Q5 Reading is Fun • The Story of the Road
In 'The Story of the Road':
(a) What sounds awaken the road early in the morning?
(b) Who is the first person to travel down the road in the morning?
(c) Why is the road annoyed with the crow?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Story Details:
(a) Morning Sounds:
• Birds chirping ('chirp-chirp'), crows cawing ('caw-caw'), newspaper boy's bicycle bell ringing ('trin-trin'), and vegetable man's cart.

(b) First Person:
• The newspaper boy on his bicycle, followed closely by the vegetable man.

(c) Road's Annoyance with Crow:
• The crow keeps shouting 'Caw, caw, wake up!' but the road is already wide awake and says, 'Foolish bird! Can't you see I am already wide awake!'
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Morning sounds listed 1.0 M
Newspaper boy / vegetable man identified 1.0 M
Humorous dialogue with crow explained 1.0 M
Q6 Road Safety • Traffic Signs
What do the following road traffic signs mean?
(a) Red light on traffic signal.
(b) Zebra crossing markings on road.
(c) Green light on traffic signal.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Road Safety Rules:
• (a) Red Light: Vehicles must Stop completely.
• (b) Zebra Crossing: Marked zone where pedestrians can safely cross the road on foot.
• (c) Green Light: Vehicles can Go forward.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct meaning of red light (Stop) 1.0 M
Correct meaning of zebra crossing (pedestrian safe crossing) 1.0 M
Correct meaning of green light (Go) 1.0 M

Q13 Vocabulary • Word Families
Add '-ing' to the following action words correctly: (a) Sing (b) Dance (c) Run (d) Play.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Adding -ing:
• (a) Sing + ing = Singing
• (b) Dance + ing = Dancing (drop the 'e')
• (c) Run + ing = Running (double the 'n')
• (d) Play + ing = Playing.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Singing, Dancing, Running, Playing 2 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Road Safety Rules for Children

Every morning, hundreds of students walk, cycle, or ride buses along busy city roads to reach school safely.

Analytical Task: State three golden road safety rules every primary school student must follow when walking to school.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Use the Footpath: Always walk on the designated pedestrian pavement or footpath.
2. Zebra Crossing: Cross the road only at marked zebra crossings or pedestrian footbridges.
3. Look Both Ways: Stop, look right, look left, and look right again before crossing, never running blindly across roads.
Rubric: 1M footpath rule + 1M zebra crossing + 1M look both ways rule.
