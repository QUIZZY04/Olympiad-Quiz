Chapter 8: What's in the Mailbox? & My Silly Sister

Poem on Receiving Letters & Rabindranath Tagore's Humorous Story on an Innocent Baby Sister | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Poem 'What's in the Mailbox?': Child watching postman bring letters for parents and deciding to write letters to friends so they write back.
  • Story 'My Silly Sister': An elder brother complaining affectionately to his mother about his baby sister's innocent antics.
  • Baby antics: Eating pebbles thinking they are real food, tearing book pages, laughing when scolded.
  • Affectionate sibling bonds: The loving warmth underlying playful sibling teasing.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (17 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Reading is Fun • Poem & Story
What does the child plan to do in 'What's in the Mailbox?' so that letters arrive for him? What funny things does the baby sister do in Tagore's story?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
1. Child's Plan: The child decides to write letters to his own friends so that they will write back and the postman will bring letters with his name on them.
2. Baby Sister's Antics:
• She thinks pebbles are real food and tries to put them in her mouth.
• When her brother opens a book to study, she tears the pages with joy.
• When her brother scolds her by shaking his head, she giggles thinking it is a fun game.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct child's strategy to write letters first 1.5 M
Two humorous baby antics described accurately 1.5 M
Q2 Vocabulary • Letter Writing Terms
What four things must be written on an envelope to ensure a letter reaches the correct destination?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Essential Envelope Details:
1. Recipient's Full Name: Name of the person receiving the letter.
2. House Number & Street: Exact residence location.
3. City / Village & State: Geographic town and province.
4. PIN Code (Postal Index Number): 6-digit postal code for accurate delivery.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
All 4 postal address elements identified 2.0 M
Q3 Letter Writing Activity • Personal Letter
Write a short 3-line invitation letter to your friend inviting them to your birthday party on Sunday.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Sample Letter:
Dear Rohan,
Please come to my 8th birthday party this Sunday, 15th October, at 5:00 PM at my home. We will play fun games, burst balloons, and eat delicious cake together. I look forward to seeing you!
Your friend,
Aarav.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Salutation and clear invitation details (date, time, place) 1.5 M
Correct closing and friendly sign-off 1.5 M
Q4 Reading is Fun • My Silly Sister
In Rabindranath Tagore's story 'My Silly Sister':
(a) What silly things does the baby sister do with pebbles and books?
(b) What does she do when the elder brother gets cross and shakes his head at her?
(c) When father is away, what does the baby sister do when the brother says 'Father'?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Story Details:
(a) Silly Antics:
• She thinks pebbles are real food and tries to put them in her mouth. When a book is opened, she tears the pages with her hands and roars with joy.

(b) Brother Getting Cross:
• Instead of being scared, she laughs joyfully and thinks it is great fun.

(c) Mentioning Father:
• She looks around quickly in excitement, thinking her father is near.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Pebbles and book page tearing described 1.0 M
Laughing at brother's scolding 1.0 M
Excitement at hearing father's name 1.0 M
Q5 Poem Comprehension • 'What's in the Mailbox?'
In the poem 'What's in the Mailbox?':
(a) Who gets letters when the postman visits the house?
(b) Does the child in the poem ever receive a letter?
(c) What does the child decide to do so that she will also receive letters?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Poem Analysis:
(a) Who Gets Letters:
• Letters always come for Mother or Father.

(b) Child's Situation:
• No, the child never gets any letter.

(c) The Child's Plan:
• The child decides to write letters to some of her friends, so that they will write back and send letters addressed to her.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Parents receiving letters 1.0 M
Child never receives letters 1.0 M
Clever plan of writing letters first to get replies 1.0 M

2. House Number and Street Name.
3. City, District, and State Name.

Q7 Community Helpers • The Postman
Write two sentences describing how the postman helps our society.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The Postman's Service:
1. The postman delivers letters, parcels, money orders, and urgent official documents safely to our doorstep in all weather conditions.
2. He connects distant family members and friends by carrying their heartfelt written messages across villages and cities.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Two well-formed sentences highlighting community postal service 2.0 M

Q13 Vocabulary • Word Families
Add '-ing' to the following action words correctly: (a) Sing (b) Dance (c) Run (d) Play.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Adding -ing:
• (a) Sing + ing = Singing
• (b) Dance + ing = Dancing (drop the 'e')
• (c) Run + ing = Running (double the 'n')
• (d) Play + ing = Playing.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Singing, Dancing, Running, Playing 2 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Digital Communication vs Traditional Post

Today, people send quick WhatsApp messages and emails, but receiving a handwritten postcard in the mailbox still feels magical.

Analytical Task: Why does a handwritten postcard from a grandparent create a deeper emotional connection than a short text message?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. A handwritten postcard shows personal time, care, and effort: the sender chose the card, wrote carefully in their own handwriting, bought a stamp, and posted it.
2. It is a tangible keepsake that a child can touch, re-read, and cherish in an album for years.
Rubric: 1.5M personal effort and tangible keepsake + 1.5M emotional permanence.
