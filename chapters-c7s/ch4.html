📌 Key Scientific Concepts & Neutralisation Reactions
  • Acids: Substances with a sour taste, turn blue litmus red, pH < 7. Contain hydrogen ions (H⁺).
    • Natural/Organic Acids: Acetic acid (Vinegar), Formic acid (Ant sting), Citric acid (Lemons, Oranges), Lactic acid (Curd), Oxalic acid (Spinach), Ascorbic acid/Vitamin C (Amla, Citrus), Tartaric acid (Tamarind, Grapes).
    • Mineral Acids: Hydrochloric acid (HCl), Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄), Nitric acid (HNO₃).
  • Bases: Substances bitter in taste and soapy/slippery to touch, turn red litmus blue, pH > 7. Contain hydroxide ions (OH⁻).
    • Calcium hydroxide (Lime water), Ammonium hydroxide (Window cleaner), Sodium hydroxide / Potassium hydroxide (Soap), Magnesium hydroxide (Milk of Magnesia).
  • Indicators: Special chemical substances that exhibit distinct colour changes in acidic and basic solutions:
    • Litmus (from Lichens): Mauve/purple in neutral; Red in acid; Blue in base.
    • Turmeric: Yellow in acid/neutral; turns Reddish-brown in base.
    • China Rose Petal Solution: Turns dark pink (magenta) in acid; green in base.
    • Phenolphthalein: Colourless in acid; turns pink in base.
  • Neutralisation Reaction: The reaction between an acid and a base producing a salt and water with the evolution of heat:
    Acid + Base ⎯⎯→ Salt + Water + Heat
    HCl (Hydrochloric acid) + NaOH (Sodium hydroxide) ⎯⎯→ NaCl (Sodium chloride) + H₂O + Heat

Page 49 — Intext Q1 (Boojho's Caution)
Boojho asks: 'Can I taste all unknown substances in the laboratory to determine whether they are acidic or basic?' What warning does Paheli give him?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Answer: No, we must NEVER taste unknown chemicals or substances.

Paheli's Warning & Scientific Justification:
1. Severe Toxicity & Corrosiveness: Many laboratory acids (like concentrated sulfuric acid, nitric acid, hydrochloric acid) and strong bases (like sodium hydroxide, potassium hydroxide) are extremely corrosive and toxic. Even a microscopic droplet can burn mouth tissues, destroy mucous membranes, or cause fatal chemical poisoning.
2. Safe Scientific Testing: Scientists use safe, specialized chemical and natural indicators (such as litmus paper, turmeric paper, phenolphthalein, and China rose petals) to test whether an unknown substance is acidic or basic without tasting or touching.

Page 50 — Intext Q2 (Paheli's Query)
Paheli noticed that a yellow curry (turmeric) stain on her white shirt turns reddish-brown when washed with bathing soap. Why does this happen, and why does it turn yellow again on rinsing with plenty of water?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Scientific Explanation:
1. Turmeric is a Natural Indicator: Turmeric contains the natural pigment curcumin, which remains yellow in acidic and neutral environments but turns distinctive reddish-brown in basic (alkaline) solutions.
2. Soap is Basic: Laundry and bathing soaps contain sodium or potassium salts of fatty acids, which are alkaline (pH > 7). When soap is rubbed onto the curry stain, the base reacts with turmeric, shifting its color to reddish-brown.
3. Restoration to Yellow: When the shirt is thoroughly rinsed with copious clean water (or treated with lemon juice), the alkaline soap is washed away, restoring the neutral/acidic condition, and the stain reverts to its original yellow color.

Q4
Is the distilled water acidic/basic/neutral? How would you verify it?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Nature of Distilled Water: Distilled water is completely Neutral (pH = 7).

Experimental Verification:
  1. Take two strips of litmus paper: one red litmus paper and one blue litmus paper.
  2. Place a few drops of pure distilled water onto both strips using a clean dropper.
  3. Observation:
    • Red litmus paper does not change colour (remains red).
    • Blue litmus paper does not change colour (remains blue).
  4. Conclusion: Since the water affects neither blue nor red litmus, it is neither acidic nor basic; it is confirmed to be strictly neutral.

Q5
Describe the process of neutralisation with the help of an example.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Definition: Neutralisation is the chemical reaction between an acid and a base. The acidic nature of the acid and the alkaline nature of the base destroy each other, resulting in the formation of a neutral salt and water along with the release of thermal energy:
Acid + Base ⎯⎯→ Salt + Water + Heat
Experimental Example:
  1. Take 5 mL of dilute Hydrochloric acid (HCl) in a conical flask and add 2–3 drops of phenolphthalein indicator. The solution remains colourless.
  2. Using a burette, add dilute Sodium hydroxide solution (NaOH) drop by drop with continuous swirling.
  3. At the exact neutralisation point, the solution turns a faint persistent pink.
  4. Now add one more drop of dilute HCl; the pink colour vanishes. Add a drop of NaOH; pink colour returns.
  5. Touching the flask reveals that the mixture has become warm, showing that heat is evolved.
Chemical Equation:
HCl (Hydrochloric acid) + NaOH (Sodium hydroxide) ⎯⎯→ NaCl (Sodium chloride) + H₂O (Water) + Heat

Q8
Explain why:
(a) An antacid tablet is taken when you suffer from acidity.
(b) Calamine solution is applied on the skin when an ant bites.
(c) Factory waste is neutralised before disposing it into the water bodies.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) Antacid for Acidity: The stomach naturally secretes hydrochloric acid (HCl) to digest food. During indigestion or hyperacidity, excess HCl causes irritation, pain, and burning sensations. Antacid tablets contain a mild, non-toxic base such as Milk of Magnesia (Magnesium hydroxide) or aluminium hydroxide. The base neutralises the excess stomach acid, forming salt and water, providing rapid relief:
2HCl + Mg(OH)₂ ⎯⎯→ MgCl₂ + 2H₂O
(b) Calamine on Ant Bite: When an ant stings, it injects acidic Formic acid (methanoic acid) into the dermal skin layers, causing burning pain, inflammation, and itching. Calamine solution contains zinc carbonate (ZnCO₃), which is a mild base. Applying calamine neutralises the acidic formic acid, relieving swelling and pain immediately.
(c) Neutralisation of Factory Waste: Industrial effluents from chemical and manufacturing plants often contain high concentrations of toxic acids or strong alkalis. If discharged directly into rivers, lakes, or oceans, the sharp change in aquatic pH will kill fish, aquatic plants, and beneficial microorganisms. Hence, factories neutralise acidic wastes by adding basic substances (like slaked lime) before safe discharge.

Q9
Three liquids are given to you. One is hydrochloric acid, another is sodium hydroxide and third is a sugar solution. How will you identify them using only turmeric indicator?
3 Marks

Turmeric paper is naturally bright yellow. It remains yellow in acidic and neutral solutions, but turns reddish-brown in basic solutions. We can identify all three liquids systematically:

Step 1: Identifying Sodium Hydroxide (Base):
Place a drop of each of the three liquids on three separate strips of yellow turmeric paper.
The liquid that turns the yellow turmeric paper REDDISH-BROWN is Sodium hydroxide (NaOH).
Step 2: Differentiating Acid (HCl) and Neutral (Sugar Solution):
Take two separate portions of the reddish-brown turmeric paper (or red-turned paste) created in Step 1:
• Add a few drops of the second liquid to one red strip, and the third liquid to the other.
• The liquid that neutralises the base and turns the reddish-brown paper back to YELLOW is Hydrochloric acid (HCl).
• The remaining liquid that causes no colour change on the red paper is the Sugar solution (Neutral).

Q10
Blue litmus paper is dipped in a solution. It remains blue. What is the nature of the solution? Explain.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

The solution can be either Basic or Neutral.

Scientific Explanation:
  • Possibility 1 (Basic Solution): Bases turn red litmus blue and have no effect on blue litmus paper. Therefore, a basic solution will leave blue litmus paper blue.
  • Possibility 2 (Neutral Solution): Neutral substances (such as pure water, salt solution, sugar solution) cause no colour change on either red or blue litmus paper. Hence, blue litmus paper remains blue in a neutral solution as well.
Confirmation: To definitively distinguish between the two, dip a strip of red litmus paper into the solution. If red litmus turns blue, it is basic; if it stays red, it is neutral.

Case Study: Soil Treatment in Agriculture
Agricultural Case: A soil testing lab analyzed samples from two different agricultural fields:
• Field A: Overused with excessive chemical nitrogenous fertilisers; tested highly acidic (pH 4.8). Crop roots are decaying.
• Field B: Located near limestone quarries; tested excessively alkaline (pH 8.9). Nutrients are insoluble.

(i) What should the farmer add to Field A to treat the soil? Explain the chemical principle.
(ii) What should be added to Field B to remediate the alkaline soil?
(iii) Why does extreme soil pH prevent healthy crop growth?

(i) Treatment for Field A (Acidic Soil): The farmer should treat the soil with bases such as quicklime (calcium oxide, CaO) or slaked lime (calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂). The alkaline lime chemically neutralises the excessive soil acidity, raising the pH to an optimal neutral level (~6.5–7).

(ii) Treatment for Field B (Basic Soil): The farmer should add organic matter (manure or compost). As organic manure decays, soil microorganisms release organic acids that neutralise the excessive alkaline bases in the soil.

(iii) Biological Reason: Plants cannot absorb essential minerals (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium) efficiently when soil is either too acidic or too basic, leading to stunted growth and wilting.

⚡ Assertion & Reason (HOTS)
Assertion (A): When soap is rubbed onto a yellow curry stain on a white shirt, the stain turns reddish-brown.
Reason (R): Soap solution contains bases (sodium/potassium salts of fatty acids), which react with the natural turmeric indicator present in the curry stain to produce a reddish-brown colour.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Explanation: Curry contains turmeric, a natural pH indicator. Turmeric contains curcumin, which remains yellow in acidic or neutral mediums but turns reddish-brown at alkaline pH (above 7.5). Washing soap is basic, causing the instantaneous colour change. When rinsed with plenty of acidic lemon water, the yellow color returns.
