Chapter 9: Motion and Time

NCERT Exercise Solutions — Complete Step-by-Step Answers with CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

📌 Key Scientific Concepts & Kinematic Equations
  • Motion & Speed:
    • Speed: The total distance covered by an object divided by the total time taken to cover that distance.
      Speed = Total Distance Covered / Total Time Taken
    • SI Units: Basic unit of distance is metre (m); basic unit of time is second (s). Hence, the SI basic unit of speed is metre per second (m/s). Larger commercial unit is kilometre per hour (km/h).
      Conversion:
      1 km/h = (1000 m) / (3600 s) = 5/18 m/s
    • Uniform vs Non-uniform Motion: An object moving along a straight line with a constant speed is in uniform motion. If the speed keeps changing with time, the motion is non-uniform.
  • Measurement of Time & Periodic Motion:
    • Clocks and watches rely on regular, repeating periodic events.
    • Simple Pendulum: Consists of a small metallic sphere (bob) suspended from a rigid stand by a light thread.
    • Oscillation: One complete to-and-fro motion of the bob from one extreme position to another and back.
    • Time Period (T): The time taken by the pendulum bob to complete one full oscillation.
      Time Period (T) = Total Time Taken / Number of Oscillations
      Discovery: Galileo Galilei observed that the time period of a simple pendulum depends only on the length of the suspension string, and remains constant regardless of the amplitude of swing.
  • Distance-Time Graphs:
    • A graphical representation plotting time on the horizontal X-axis and distance on the vertical Y-axis.
    • A straight line sloping upwards indicates uniform speed (constant speed).
    • A horizontal line parallel to the time axis indicates an object at rest (speed = 0).
    • A curved line indicates non-uniform speed (accelerating or decelerating motion).
    • The steeper the slope (gradient) of the distance-time graph, the greater is the speed of the object.

Q2
Which of the following are not correct?
(i) The basic unit of time is second.
(ii) Every object moves with a constant speed.
(iii) Distances between two cities are measured in kilometres.
(iv) The time period of a given pendulum is not constant.
(v) The speed of a train is expressed in m/h.
2.5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

The statements that are not correct (incorrect) are:

(ii) Incorrect: "Every object moves with a constant speed."
Correction: Most natural objects move with variable (non-uniform) speeds because of traffic, friction, turns, starting, and stopping.
(iv) Incorrect: "The time period of a given pendulum is not constant."
Correction: The time period of a given pendulum of fixed length is constant (isochronism of pendulums discovered by Galileo).
(v) Incorrect: "The speed of a train is expressed in m/h."
Correction: Because trains travel long distances at high speeds, their speed is practically expressed in km/h (kilometres per hour) or in SI units as m/s.
Statements (i) and (iii) are completely correct: The SI basic unit of time is the second (s), and geographical distances between cities are measured in kilometres (km).

Q3
A simple pendulum takes 32 s to complete 20 oscillations. What is the time period of the pendulum?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given Data:
Total number of complete oscillations (n) = 20
Total time taken (t) = 32 seconds
Formula:
The time period (T) of a simple pendulum is defined as the time required to complete one single oscillation:
Time Period (T) = Total Time Taken / Total Number of Oscillations
Calculation:
T = 32 / 20 = 1.6 seconds
Conclusion:
The time period of the simple pendulum is 1.6 seconds.

Q4
The distance between two stations is 240 km. A train takes 4 hours to cover this distance. Calculate the speed of the train.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given Data:
Distance covered by the train (D) = 240 km
Time taken to travel the distance (t) = 4 hours (h)
Formula:
Speed = Distance / Time
Calculation:
Speed = 240 km / 4 h = 60 km/h
In SI units (m/s):
Speed = 60 × (5/18) = 300 / 18 ≈ 16.67 m/s
Conclusion:
The speed of the train is 60 km/h (or 16.67 m/s).

Q5
The odometer of a car reads 57321.0 km when the clock shows the time 08:30 AM. What is the distance moved by the car, if at 08:50 AM, the odometer reading has changed to 57336.0 km? Calculate the speed of the car in km/min during this time. Express the speed in km/h also.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Step 1: Calculate Distance Covered:
Initial odometer reading at 08:30 AM = 57321.0 km
Final odometer reading at 08:50 AM = 57336.0 km
Distance = Final Reading - Initial Reading = 57336.0 - 57321.0 = 15.0 km
Step 2: Calculate Elapsed Time:
Time elapsed = 08:50 AM - 08:30 AM = 20 minutes
Step 3: Calculate Speed in km/min:
Speed = Distance / Time = 15 km / 20 min = 0.75 km/min
Step 4: Express Speed in km/h:
Since 1 hour = 60 minutes, time in hours = 20/60 h = 1/3 h.
Speed = 15 km / (1/3 h) = 15 × 3 = 45 km/h
(Alternatively: Speed in km/h = 0.75 km/min × 60 min/h = 45 km/h)
Final Summary:
• Distance moved = 15.0 km
• Speed in km/min = 0.75 km/min
• Speed in km/h = 45 km/h

Q6
Salma takes 15 minutes from her house to reach her school on a bicycle. If the bicycle has a speed of 2 m/s, calculate the distance between her house and the school.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Given Data:
Speed of bicycle (v) = 2 m/s
Time taken (t) = 15 minutes
Unit Conversion:
To use the speed in m/s, time must be converted from minutes into seconds:
Time (t) = 15 × 60 s = 900 seconds
Formula:
Distance = Speed × Time
Calculation:
Distance = 2 m/s × 900 s = 1800 metres
Converting to kilometres:
Distance = 1800 / 1000 = 1.8 km
Conclusion:
The distance between Salma's house and her school is 1800 m (or 1.8 km).

Q7
Show the shape of the distance-time graph for the motion in the following cases:
(i) A car moving with a constant speed.
(ii) A car parked on a side road.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Case (i): A car moving with a constant speed:
• Shape of Graph: A straight line inclined at an angle to the time axis (sloping upwards linearly from the origin).
• Scientific Explanation: When an object covers equal distances in equal intervals of time, the ratio Distance / Time remains constant. Hence, the graph is a linear upward diagonal line representing uniform motion.
Case (ii): A car parked on a side road:
• Shape of Graph: A horizontal straight line parallel to the time axis (X-axis) at a fixed distance value.
• Scientific Explanation: As time progresses continuously, the car remains stationary at the exact same location, so distance does not change (Slope = 0, Speed = 0).
Exam Diagram Note:
Always label Time on the horizontal X-axis (with units, e.g., seconds or minutes) and Distance on the vertical Y-axis (with units, e.g., metres or kilometres).

Q10
A car moves with a speed of 40 km/h for 15 minutes and then with a speed of 60 km/h for the next 15 minutes. The total distance covered by the car is:
(i) 100 km
(ii) 25 km
(iii) 15 km
(iv) 10 km
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Correct Option: (ii) 25 km

Detailed Step-by-Step Calculation:
• Time duration for each part = 15 minutes = 15 / 60 hours = 1/4 hour = 0.25 h.

• Part 1: Distance covered in first 15 minutes (d₁):
d₁ = Speed₁ × Time₁ = 40 km/h × (1/4) h = 10 km

• Part 2: Distance covered in next 15 minutes (d₂):
d₂ = Speed₂ × Time₂ = 60 km/h × (1/4) h = 15 km

• Total Distance:
Total Distance = d₁ + d₂ = 10 km + 15 km = 25 km

Answer: Vehicle A is moving faster than Vehicle B.

Q13
Which of the following distance-time graphs shows a truck moving with speed which is not constant? Explain how graph geometry identifies variable speed.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Answer: The graph with a curved line (non-linear graph) shows a truck moving with a non-constant (variable) speed.

Geometric and Physical Reasons:
  • Constant Speed (Uniform Motion): Represented by a straight line inclined to the time axis because equal distances are covered in equal time intervals.
  • Zero Speed (Rest): Represented by a straight horizontal line parallel to the time axis.
  • Non-Constant Speed (Non-uniform Motion): Represented by a curved line where the slope continuously changes from moment to moment. A changing slope indicates that the distance covered per unit time is either increasing (acceleration) or decreasing (deceleration).

Case Study: High-Speed Train Vande Bharat Telemetry
Railway Engineering Case: During trial runs of the semi-high-speed Vande Bharat Express between New Delhi and Varanasi (distance = 760 km), the train covered the first 160 km in 1 hour, the next 300 km in 2 hours, and the remaining 300 km in 3 hours.

(i) Calculate the average speed of the train over the entire journey in km/h.
(ii) Did the train maintain uniform motion throughout the journey? Justify.
(iii) Convert the overall average speed into SI units (m/s).

(i) Average Speed:
Total Distance = 160 + 300 + 300 = 760 km
Total Time = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 hours
Average Speed = 760 / 6 ≈ 126.67 km/h

(ii) Motion Type: Non-uniform motion. The speeds in the three legs were 160 km/h (leg 1), 150 km/h (leg 2), and 100 km/h (leg 3). Because the speed varied across sections, the motion was non-uniform.

(iii) SI Units Conversion:
126.67 × (5/18) = 633.35 / 18 ≈ 35.19 m/s

⚡ Assertion & Reason (HOTS)
Assertion (A): If the distance-time graph of a moving scooter is a straight line parallel to the time axis, the scooter is at complete rest.
Reason (R): The slope of a distance-time graph gives the speed of an object, and a horizontal line parallel to the time axis has a slope equal to zero.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Explanation: When distance remains fixed as time elapses, Δd = 0. Therefore, Speed = Δd / Δt = 0. This translates graphically to zero slope (a horizontal line), proving that the vehicle is stationary.
