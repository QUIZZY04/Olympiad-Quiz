📌 Key Scientific Concepts & Optics Fundamentals
  • Rectilinear Propagation & Reflection of Light:
    • Light travels in straight lines (rectilinear propagation). When light falls on a polished, shiny surface, its direction changes — a phenomenon known as reflection of light.
    • Plane Mirror Image Characteristics:
      1. The image is erect (upright) and virtual (cannot be caught on a screen).
      2. The size of the image is identical to the object.
      3. The distance of the image behind the mirror is equal to the distance of the object in front of the mirror.
      4. The image exhibits lateral inversion: the left side of the object appears as the right side of the image, and vice versa.
  • Spherical Mirrors:
    • Concave Mirror (Converging): Inner curved surface is reflecting. Can form real, inverted images (diminished, same size, or magnified) when the object is beyond the focus; forms an erect, enlarged, and virtual image when placed very close (between pole and focus). Used by dentists, in solar furnaces, torch reflectors, and shaving mirrors.
    • Convex Mirror (Diverging): Outer bulging surface is reflecting. Always forms an erect, virtual, and diminished (smaller) image over a very wide field of view. Used as rear-view mirrors in automobiles.
  • Spherical Lenses:
    • Convex Lens (Converging): Thicker at the middle than at edges. Converges incident parallel light rays. Can form real and inverted images, or a virtual, erect, and magnified image when held close to an object (used as a magnifying glass and in spectacles for hypermetropia).
    • Concave Lens (Diverging): Thinner at the middle than at edges. Diverges incident parallel light rays. Always forms an erect, virtual, and diminished image regardless of object distance.
  • Dispersion of Light & Sunlight:
    • White sunlight consists of seven distinct constituent colours: Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red (VIBGYOR).
    • A glass prism splits white light into its seven constituent colours (dispersion of light), forming a rainbow spectrum.
    • Newton's Disc: A circular disc painted with the seven rainbow segments rotated rapidly blends into a uniform white appearance, demonstrating that white light is a composite of the seven colours.
NCERT Intext Questions & Scientific Enquiries
Page 137 — Intext Q1 (Boojho's Query)
Boojho observed an emergency ambulance and wondered why the word AMBULANCE is written in reverse mirror-image lettering on the vehicle's front bonnet.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Optical Lateral Inversion Principle:
1. Plane and convex rear-view mirrors exhibit lateral inversion, meaning that an object's left side appears on the right side of the mirror image, and vice versa.
2. When the driver of a vehicle ahead glances into their rear-view mirror, the pre-inverted lettering on the ambulance bonnet undergoes lateral inversion in the mirror.
3. As a result, the reversed lettering appears right-side up as perfectly readable standard AMBULANCE. This allows drivers to instantly identify the approaching emergency vehicle and yield immediate right-of-way.

Paheli asks: Can we see the seven rainbow colours on a sunny day even without natural rainfall? How?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Artificial Dispersion of Sunlight:
Yes, rainbow colors can easily be produced and viewed without rainfall through any of the following optical methods:
  • Water Fountain / Garden Hose: Stand with your back towards the sun and spray a fine mist of water droplets with a garden hose into the air. Tiny suspended water droplets act as micro-prisms, refracting, reflecting, and dispersing sunlight into a vibrant miniature rainbow.
  • Glass Prism: Allow a narrow beam of sunlight through a window slit to pass through a triangular glass prism onto a white screen to see the 7 VIBGYOR colors.
  • Soap Bubbles & Compact Discs: Thin films of soap bubbles or micro-grooves on the back of a CD reflect and disperse white light into shimmering rainbow spectral bands.

Q4
State the characteristics of the image formed by a plane mirror.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

The image formed by a flat plane mirror possesses the following distinct physical characteristics:

  1. Virtual Image: It is formed behind the mirror where reflected rays appear to diverge from, and cannot be captured or projected on a screen.
  2. Erect (Upright): The image stands right side up in the exact orientation as the object.
  3. Same Size: The height and width of the image are exactly identical to the dimensions of the object (Magnification m = +1).
  4. Equal Distance: The distance of the image behind the mirror surface is exactly equal to the distance of the object in front of the mirror (Image Distance = Object Distance).
  5. Laterally Inverted: The image exhibits lateral inversion, meaning the left side of the real object appears as the right side in the mirror image, and vice versa.

Q5
Find out the letters of English alphabet or any other language known to you in which the image formed in a plane mirror appears exactly like the letter itself. Discuss your findings.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Symmetrical Capital Letters of English Alphabet:
The letters whose plane mirror image appears identical to the original letter itself are:
A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y
Discussion & Scientific Reason:
  • Although every image in a plane mirror undergoes lateral inversion (reversal of left and right), these specific 11 letters possess a vertical line of reflective symmetry through their middle.
  • Because their left half is the exact mirror image of their right half, swapping the left and right sides during mirror reflection does not produce any visible change in their overall appearance.
  • Conversely, asymmetric letters like B, C, D, E, F, G, J, K, L, N, P, Q, R, S, Z do not have a vertical axis of symmetry, so their lateral inversion is immediately noticeable as reversed characters.

Q6
What is a virtual image? Give one situation where a virtual image is formed.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Definition of Virtual Image:
A virtual image is an optical image formed when light rays originating from an object do not actually intersect at a physical point, but only appear to diverge from a location behind the reflecting or refracting surface. Consequently, a virtual image cannot be captured or obtained on a screen.
Situations where a virtual image is formed:
  • The everyday image of our face seen when looking into a plane mirror inside a dressing room.
  • The smaller, upright rear view of following highway traffic observed in the convex side-view mirror of an automobile.
  • The magnified upright letters viewed through a convex lens (magnifying glass) held close to a page.

Q10
Which type of lens forms always a virtual image?
1 Mark
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Answer: A Concave Lens.

Scientific Explanation: A concave lens is a diverging lens that bends refracted light rays outwards away from the principal axis. These rays never converge in real space; when traced backwards, they appear to intersect, forming an image that is always virtual, erect, and diminished, irrespective of the object's distance.

Q12
David is observing his image in a plane mirror. The distance between the mirror and his image is 4 m. If he moves 1 m towards the mirror, then the distance between David and his image will be:
(i) 3 m
(ii) 5 m
(iii) 6 m
(iv) 8 m
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Correct Option: (iii) 6 m

Detailed Step-by-Step Calculation:
  1. In a plane mirror, Object Distance = Image Distance from the mirror surface.
  2. Initially, the distance between the mirror and the image is 4 m. Hence, the initial distance of David (object) from the mirror was also 4 m.
  3. When David walks 1 m towards the mirror, his new distance from the mirror becomes:
    New Object Distance = 4 m - 1 m = 3 m
  4. Consequently, the new image will form 3 m behind the mirror:
    New Image Distance = 3 m
  5. The total distance between David and his image is the sum of object distance and image distance:
    Total Distance = 3 m + 3 m = 6 m

Q13
The rear view mirror of a car is a plane mirror. A driver is reversing his car at a speed of 2 m/s. The driver sees in his rear view mirror the image of a truck parked behind his car. The speed at which the image of the truck appears to approach the driver will be:
(i) 1 m/s
(ii) 2 m/s
(iii) 4 m/s
(iv) 8 m/s
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Correct Option: (iii) 4 m/s

Physical & Relative Velocity Proof:
  • The truck is stationary (parked). As the car reverses towards the truck at a speed of 2 m/s, the physical distance between the car's mirror and the parked truck decreases at a rate of 2 m/s.
  • In a plane mirror, the distance of the image behind the mirror is always equal to the distance of the object from the mirror.
  • Therefore, when the object distance decreases by 2 metres in 1 second, the image distance behind the mirror also decreases by 2 metres in that same 1 second.
  • Hence, relative to the driver sitting in the moving car:
    • The mirror moves 2 m closer to the truck.
    • The image advances 2 m closer towards the mirror.
    • The rate at which the image approaches the driver = 2 m/s + 2 m/s = 4 m/s.

Case Study: Automobile Safety & "AMBULANCE" Inscription
Optical Application: On emergency response ambulances, the word AMBULANCE is painted in reverse lettering on the front bonnet.

(i) Which optical phenomenon of plane mirrors is this design based on?
(ii) How does this reverse lettering assist the driver in a car moving in front of the ambulance?
(iii) Why are convex mirrors, rather than plane mirrors, used for the wing/side-view mirrors in vehicles?

(i) Optical Phenomenon: Lateral Inversion — the property of plane mirrors where left and right sides are interchanged.

(ii) Practical Benefit: When the driver of a vehicle ahead looks into their rear-view mirror, the laterally reversed word undergoes a second lateral inversion in the mirror, reading perfectly correctly as standard readable AMBULANCE. This allows the driver to immediately recognize the emergency and yield right-of-way without hesitation.

(iii) Reason for Convex Mirrors: Convex mirrors are curved outwards (diverging), which allows them to capture rays from a much wider angle. They produce upright, diminished images that give the driver a substantially broader panoramic field of view of trailing highway traffic compared to flat plane mirrors.

⚡ Assertion & Reason (HOTS)
Assertion (A): When white sunlight passes through a triangular glass prism, it emerges as a rainbow spectrum band of seven colours.
Reason (R): White light is a composite mixture of seven colours (VIBGYOR), each having a different speed and angle of bending (refraction) inside glass.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Explanation: Dispersion occurs because violet light slows down the most and bends through the greatest angle, while red light slows down the least and bends the least upon entering glass. This differential refraction spreads the composite beam into the visible spectrum band (VIBGYOR).
