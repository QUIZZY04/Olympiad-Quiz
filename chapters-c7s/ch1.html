Chapter 1: Nutrition in Plants

NCERT Exercise Solutions — Complete Step-by-Step Answers with CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

📌 Key Scientific Concepts & Chemical Equations
  • Modes of Nutrition:
    • Autotrophic Nutrition: Organisms synthesise their own food from simple inorganic raw materials (CO₂, H₂O, minerals) using sunlight and chlorophyll. E.g., all green plants and blue-green algae.
    • Heterotrophic Nutrition: Organisms depend directly or indirectly on autotrophs for food. E.g., animals, non-green plants, fungi.
  • Photosynthesis: The biochemical process by which green leaves prepare food using chlorophyll, sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water:
    Carbon dioxide (6CO₂) + Water (6H₂O) ⎯⎯⎯[Sunlight / Chlorophyll]⎯⎯→ Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) + Oxygen (6O₂)
  • Specialised Modes of Nutrition in Plants:
    • Parasitic Plants: Non-green plants that obtain readymade food from a host plant using specialised absorbing roots (haustoria). E.g., Cuscuta (Amarbel).
    • Insectivorous Plants: Partially autotrophic plants growing in nitrogen-deficient bogs that trap and digest insects to obtain nitrogenous compounds. E.g., Pitcher plant (Nepenthes), Venus flytrap.
    • Saprotrophic Nutrition: Organisms secrete digestive juices onto dead and decaying matter and absorb dissolved nutrients. E.g., Fungi, Mushrooms, bread mould (Rhizopus).
    • Symbiosis: Mutually beneficial association between two organisms sharing shelter and nutrients. E.g., Lichens (Alga + Fungus), Rhizobium bacteria in root nodules of leguminous plants.
  • Stomata: Tiny microscopic pores present predominantly on the underside of leaves, flanked by kidney-shaped guard cells, responsible for gaseous exchange (CO₂ and O₂) and transpiration.
NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions
Q1
Why do organisms take food?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

All living organisms require food for the following essential life functions:

1. Energy Provision: Food acts as fuel, releasing energy through cellular respiration to perform all vital life activities (locomotion, digestion, circulation, reproduction).
2. Growth and Development: Nutrients in food (proteins, minerals) provide building materials for cellular division, tissue formation, and body growth.
3. Repair and Maintenance: Damaged body tissues, worn-out cells, and injured organs require nutrients for cellular repair and regeneration.
4. Protection Against Diseases: Vitamins and minerals strengthen the immune system and protect the body from infections.

Q2
Distinguish between a parasite and a saprotroph.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Feature Parasite Saprotroph
Source of Food Derives nutrients directly from the living body of another organism called the host. Absorbs nutrients in solution form from dead and decaying organic matter.
Host Impact Harms the host by depriving it of valuable nutrients and sometimes causes diseases. Does not harm living organisms; acts as a natural cleaner/decomposer.
Digestive Mechanism Absorbs already digested or synthesised food via specialised sucking roots (haustoria). Secretes extracellular digestive juices onto dead matter and absorbs the liquified solution.
Examples Cuscuta (Amarbel), tapeworm, mistletoe. Fungi, mushrooms, yeast, bread mould (Rhizopus).

Q3
How would you test the presence of starch in leaves?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

The presence of starch in green leaves is tested using the Iodine Test following a standard laboratory procedure:

Step 1 (Killing the cells): Pluck a green leaf from a healthy plant exposed to sunlight for a few hours. Boil the leaf in a beaker of water for 3 to 5 minutes to break down cell membranes and make it permeable.
Step 2 (Decolourisation): Place the boiled leaf in a test tube containing alcohol (ethanol). Place this test tube inside a water bath (beaker filled with boiling water) and heat gently until all the green pigment (chlorophyll) dissolves out into the alcohol and the leaf turns pale white/colourless.
Caution: Alcohol is highly flammable; never heat it directly over a flame!
Step 3 (Washing & Softening): Remove the brittle bleached leaf and rinse it gently in warm water to soften it and remove trace alcohol.
Step 4 (Iodine Addition): Spread the decolourised leaf flat on a white ceramic tile and add a few drops of dilute iodine solution onto its surface.
Observation & Conclusion: The leaf turns blue-black in colour. This confirms the presence of starch, proving that photosynthesis has taken place.

Q4
Give a brief description of the process of synthesis of food in green plants.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Green plants synthesise their food through the physiological process of Photosynthesis. Leaves act as the "food factories" of the plant.

1. Absorption of Water & Minerals: Roots absorb water and dissolved minerals from the soil. These are transported upwards through specialised vascular tissues called xylem vessels to the leaves.
2. Intake of Carbon Dioxide: Carbon dioxide from atmospheric air enters the leaves through microscopic pores called stomata, controlled by guard cells.
3. Trapping Solar Energy: Green leaves contain a special pigment called chlorophyll, which captures radiant solar energy.
4. Chemical Conversion: Solar energy is converted into chemical energy to combine CO₂ and H₂O into carbohydrate (glucose). Oxygen is released as a vital byproduct into the air:
6CO₂ + 6H₂O ⎯⎯⎯[Sunlight / Chlorophyll]⎯⎯→ C₆H₁₂O₆ (Glucose) + 6O₂ (Oxygen)

The synthesised glucose is converted and stored in plant organs as starch.

Q5
Show with the help of a sketch that plants are the ultimate source of food.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

All living organisms on Earth depend directly or indirectly on green plants for nourishment, as plants alone convert solar energy into chemical energy:

☀️ Sun (Ultimate Source of Energy)
↓ (trapped by chlorophyll)
🌱 Green Plants / Producers (Autotrophs)
↓ (consumed by herbivores)
🦗 Herbivores / Primary Consumers (e.g., Deer, Grasshopper, Goat)
↓ (consumed by carnivores)
🦁 Carnivores / Secondary Consumers (e.g., Lion, Frog, Eagle)
↓ (on death of organisms)
🍄 Decomposers / Saprotrophs (Bacteria, Fungi) → Soil Nutrients → Plants

Explanation: Herbivores directly eat plants. Carnivores eat herbivores that fed on plants. If plants did not exist, herbivores would starve, leading to the extinction of carnivores. Thus, plants are the ultimate source of all food on Earth.

Q13
Why do farmers grow many fruits and vegetable crops inside large green houses? What are the advantages to the farmers?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

A greenhouse (glasshouse or polyhouse) provides a controlled microenvironment for plant cultivation. Farmers grow crops inside greenhouses due to the following specific advantages:

1. Optimal Temperature & Light Control: Glass walls trap solar heat (greenhouse effect), maintaining a warmer, optimum temperature for photosynthesis even during cold winter seasons.
2. Protection from Harsh Weather & Pests: Enclosed structures shield delicate crops from high winds, hailstorms, heavy rainfall, frost, birds, insects, and air-borne pests.
3. Controlled Carbon Dioxide & Humidity: Farmers can enrich the internal air with higher concentrations of CO₂ and maintain ideal humidity, leading to faster growth rates, higher yields, and better-quality produce.
4. Year-Round Farming: It enables farmers to grow off-season vegetables and fruits, fetching premium market prices.

Case Study: Nitrogen Fixation in Agriculture
Scenario: Farmer Ramesh noticed that after harvesting wheat, the soil becomes depleted of nitrogen. However, his agricultural advisor recommended rotating the crop by planting gram (chickpea) or peas before sowing wheat again, without applying synthetic nitrogen fertilisers.

(i) Name the bacterium that lives in the roots of gram and pea plants.
(ii) What type of relationship exists between this bacterium and the leguminous plant?
(iii) Why is crop rotation with legumes economically and ecologically beneficial?

(i) Bacterium Name: Rhizobium.

(ii) Biological Relationship: Symbiotic relationship (Symbiosis). The bacterium cannot synthesise its own food; it receives shelter, carbohydrates, and organic nutrients from the host plant roots. In exchange, Rhizobium converts atmospheric gaseous nitrogen (N₂) into water-soluble nitrogenous compounds (nitrates) that the plant easily absorbs.

(iii) Benefits: (a) Economic: Saves farmers significant money on expensive chemical fertilisers. (b) Ecological: Prevents soil degradation, preserves soil texture, and stops chemical runoff from polluting nearby water bodies (eutrophication).

⚡ Assertion & Reason (HOTS)
Assertion (A): Pitcher plant performs photosynthesis but still traps and digests insects.
Reason (R): Pitcher plants grow in nitrogen-deficient boggy soils and feed on insects to fulfil their nitrogen requirement.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Explanation: Pitcher plants possess green chlorophyll and synthesise their own carbohydrates through photosynthesis. However, the waterlogged, marshy soils they inhabit are severely deficient in available nitrogen minerals. To synthesise essential proteins, the leaf lamina modifies into a pitcher trap with digestive enzymes to digest trapped insects and absorb amino acids.
