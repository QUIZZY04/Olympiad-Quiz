📌 Key Scientific Concepts & Chemical Reactions
  • Physical Changes: Changes in which only the physical properties (shape, size, state, appearance) of a substance undergo transformation, with no new chemical substance formed. Generally temporary and easily reversible. E.g., melting of ice, boiling of water, tearing of paper, dissolution of sugar in water.
  • Chemical Changes (Chemical Reactions): Changes in which one or more entirely new substances with distinct chemical compositions and properties are formed. Irreversible and accompanied by energy changes (heat, light, sound), evolution of gas, change in colour, or precipitate formation. E.g., burning of fuels, rusting of iron, curdling of milk, digestion of food.
  • Classic Laboratory Chemical Reactions:
    • Burning of Magnesium Ribbon: Burns with a dazzling white flame:
      2Mg (Magnesium) + O₂ (Oxygen) ⎯⎯→ 2MgO (Magnesium oxide - basic ash)
      MgO + H₂O ⎯⎯→ Mg(OH)₂ (Magnesium hydroxide — turns red litmus blue)
    • Copper Sulphate and Iron Nail Reaction (Displacement):
      CuSO₄ (Blue solution) + Fe (Iron nail) ⎯⎯→ FeSO₄ (Light green solution) + Cu (Brown deposit on nail)
    • Baking Soda and Vinegar Reaction:
      CH₃COOH (Vinegar / Acetic acid) + NaHCO₃ (Baking soda) ⎯⎯→ CO₂ (Carbon dioxide) + H₂O + Sodium acetate
      CO₂ + Ca(OH)₂ (Lime water) ⎯⎯→ CaCO₃ (Insoluble Calcium carbonate, milky) + H₂O
  • Rusting of Iron: Corrosion of iron in the simultaneous presence of both oxygen (air) and moisture (water):
    4Fe + 3O₂ + 2xH₂O ⎯⎯→ 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (Hydrated iron(III) oxide / Rust)
    Prevention: Barrier protection (painting, greasing, oiling), alloying (stainless steel), and galvanisation (coating iron with a sacrificial protective layer of molten zinc metal).
  • Crystallisation: A physical purification technique in which pure, well-defined solid crystals of a substance are separated from its hot, saturated solution upon cooling.
NCERT Intext Questions & Scientific Enquiries
Page 58 — Intext Q1
Paheli asks: 'When we tear a large sheet of paper into small pieces, we cannot join the pieces back to get the original sheet. Then why is tearing paper classified as a physical change?'
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Definition of Physical Change:
1. A change is classified as physical if only the physical properties (size, shape, texture, state) of a substance are altered, with no new chemical substance being formed.
2. When paper is torn, each small bit of paper is still made of the exact same chemical cellulose molecules as the original sheet.
3. Even though the mechanical process is physically irreversible (we cannot un-tear paper), no chemical bonds were broken or formed, and no new chemical species emerged. Therefore, tearing paper is purely a physical change.

Page 62 — Intext Q2 (Boojho's Query)
Boojho asks: 'Why does passing carbon dioxide gas through freshly prepared lime water turn it milky? Give the chemical equation.'
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Chemical Reaction & Explanation:
1. Fresh lime water is a clear, aqueous solution of calcium hydroxide: Ca(OH)₂.
2. When carbon dioxide gas (CO₂) is bubbled through lime water, it reacts chemically to form insoluble white precipitate particles of calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) suspended in water:
Carbon dioxide (CO₂) + Lime water [Ca(OH)₂] → Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) [Milky ppt] + Water (H₂O)

3. The formation of these insoluble white calcium carbonate particles scatters light, making the clear solution appear distinctly milky. This serves as the standard confirmatory test for carbon dioxide gas.

Q4
When baking soda is mixed with lemon juice, bubbles are formed with the evolution of a gas. What type of change is it? Explain.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Type of Change: It is a Chemical change.

Scientific Explanation:
  • When lemon juice (which contains citric acid) reacts with baking soda (sodium hydrogen carbonate), a brisk effervescence occurs, releasing a new gas: carbon dioxide (CO₂) along with water and sodium citrate salt.
  • Since entirely new chemical substances with distinct properties are produced that cannot be converted back by simple physical means, the reaction is chemical:
    Lemon juice (Citric acid) + Baking soda (Sodium hydrogen carbonate) ⎯⎯→ Carbon dioxide (bubbles) + Sodium citrate + Water

Q5
When a candle burns, both physical and chemical changes take place. Identify these changes. Give another example of a familiar process in which both physical and chemical changes take place.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. In Burning of a Candle:
  • Physical Change: The heat of the flame melts the solid wax near the wick into liquid wax. When it drips down and cools, it resolidifies into solid wax without changing its chemical composition. This change of state is a physical change.
  • Chemical Change: Molten wax rises through the wick and vaporises; the wax vapour combusts in atmospheric oxygen producing heat, light, carbon dioxide (CO₂), water vapour (H₂O), and soot. These new substances cannot be converted back into wax; hence, it is a chemical change.
2. Another Example Exhibiting Both Changes:
  • Digestion of Food in the Human Body:
    • Physical Change: Mechanical mastication and grinding of food by teeth into smaller physical pieces in the mouth.
    • Chemical Change: Enzymatic breakdown of complex starch into maltose by saliva and digestion of proteins into amino acids by gastric juices.
  • (Alternative: LPG cylinder usage — physical conversion of liquid LPG into gas as pressure drops, followed by chemical combustion of gas into CO₂ and H₂O).

Q6
How would you show that setting of curd is a chemical change?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Setting of curd is proven to be a chemical change based on the following scientific criteria:

1. Formation of a New Substance: When a small amount of curd (inoculum) is added to warm milk, the bacterium Lactobacillus ferments lactose sugar present in milk into lactic acid. The acid coagulates the milk protein (casein), forming an entirely new chemical substance: curd.
2. Change in Properties: Curd has a distinctly sour taste, acidic pH, and a thick semi-solid texture completely different from sweet liquid milk.
3. Irreversibility: Once curd is formed, it cannot be reversed back into milk by any physical separation method.

Q7
Explain why burning of wood and cutting it into small pieces are considered as two different types of changes.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Cutting of Wood (Physical Change):
  • When a wooden log is sawn or chopped into smaller pieces, only its physical dimensions (size and shape) are altered.
  • The chemical composition of each wood chip remains identical to the original log (cellulose and lignin).
  • No new chemical substance is formed; therefore, cutting wood is purely a physical change.
2. Burning of Wood (Chemical Change):
  • When wood is ignited, the carbon and organic compounds present in the wood chemically react with atmospheric oxygen at high temperatures (combustion).
  • Entirely new chemical substances are formed: ash, carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, water vapour, and soot, alongside the liberation of heat and light.
  • The ash and smoke cannot be recombined to reconstruct the original wood; hence, it is a permanent and irreversible chemical change.

Cutting wood: change in size only, no new substance (physical)1.5 Marks

Q8
Describe how crystals of copper sulphate are prepared.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Pure, large crystals of copper sulphate (CuSO₄·5H₂O) are prepared through the physical purification process of Crystallisation:

Apparatus & Materials: Beaker, tripod stand, wire gauze, bunsen burner, water, dilute sulphuric acid, copper sulphate powder, filter paper, china dish.
Procedure:
  1. Take approximately 100 mL of water in a clean beaker and add a few drops of dilute sulphuric acid (to prevent hydrolysis).
  2. Heat the water over a burner on a tripod stand until it starts boiling.
  3. While boiling, add impure copper sulphate powder gradually while stirring continuously with a glass rod.
  4. Continue adding copper sulphate powder until no more powder can dissolve; the solution becomes a saturated solution.
  5. Filter the hot saturated solution using filter paper to remove insoluble solid impurities into a clean china dish.
  6. Allow the hot filtrate to cool slowly at room temperature undisturbed. Do not shake, stir, or move the solution while cooling.
Observation: After a few hours, beautiful, well-defined, transparent blue triclinic crystals of copper sulphate appear at the bottom of the dish.

Q9
Explain how painting of an iron gate prevents it from rusting.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Conditions for Rusting: Rusting of iron is a chemical oxidation reaction that strictly requires the concurrent presence of both oxygen (air) and water / moisture:
4Fe + 3O₂ + 2xH₂O ⎯⎯→ 2Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (Rust)
2. Barrier Mechanism of Paint: Applying a coat of paint over the iron gate creates a continuous, impervious physical barrier. This coating completely cuts off direct contact between the underlying metallic iron atoms and atmospheric moisture and oxygen. In the absence of moisture and air, the oxidation reaction cannot initiate, effectively protecting the iron gate from rusting.

Q10
Explain why rusting of iron objects is faster in coastal areas than in deserts.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. High Humidity in Coastal Areas: Coastal regions (such as Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata) are surrounded by sea water, creating an atmosphere with very high relative humidity (moisture content). Water vapour readily condenses on iron surfaces, providing the necessary aqueous medium for oxidation.
2. Catalytic Effect of Sea Salts: Coastal air carries tiny droplets of sea spray containing dissolved salts (primarily sodium chloride, NaCl). Saline water acts as an excellent electrolyte, accelerating the electrochemical corrosion process and causing rapid, severe rusting.
3. Arid Conditions in Deserts: In contrast, desert areas (such as Thar) have extremely dry, hot air with virtually negligible atmospheric moisture. Without sufficient water vapour, iron cannot readily react with oxygen, making rusting exceptionally slow.
CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
High humidity + presence of salt in coastal air accelerating corrosion1 Mark

🎯 Competency-Based Questions (CBQ) & HOTS CBSE Board Exam Pattern
Case Study: Displacement Reaction in Iron Nails & Copper Sulphate
Laboratory Experiment: Priyanka dissolved a spoonful of blue vitriol (copper sulphate) in 100 mL of water and added a few drops of dilute sulphuric acid. She dropped a clean iron nail into the blue solution and left it undisturbed for 45 minutes.

(i) What colour change does the solution undergo, and why?
(ii) What observation is recorded on the surface of the iron nail?
(iii) Write the complete word and chemical equation for this reaction.

(i) Colour Shift of Solution: The solution changes from its initial deep blue to a light green colour. This occurs because iron is chemically more reactive than copper; it displaces copper from copper sulphate solution to form iron(II) sulphate (ferrous sulphate, FeSO₄), which is light green.

(ii) Surface of Iron Nail: A distinct reddish-brown powdery coating is deposited on the submerged portion of the iron nail. This reddish-brown coating is pure metallic copper (Cu) displaced from the solution.

(iii) Equation:
Copper sulphate solution (Blue) + Iron (Nail) ⎯⎯→ Iron sulphate solution (Light green) + Copper (Brown deposit)
CuSO₄ (aq) + Fe (s) ⎯⎯→ FeSO₄ (aq) + Cu (s)

⚡ Assertion & Reason (HOTS)
Assertion (A): Ships operating in sea water require frequent replacement of sacrificial zinc blocks attached to their steel hulls.
Reason (R): Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it corrodes preferentially (sacrificial protection), preventing the steel ship hull from rusting in saline ocean water.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Explanation: Saline ocean water acts as a strong electrolyte, dramatically accelerating iron corrosion. Large zinc blocks bolted to the submerged steel hull undergo galvanic sacrificial oxidation (zinc oxidises to Zn²⁺ instead of Fe to Fe³⁺). Once the zinc blocks are completely consumed, they must be renewed to safeguard the ship structure.
