📌 Key Scientific Concepts & Wastewater Treatment Plant (WWTP)
  • Sewage & Contaminants:
    • Sewage: Liquid waste containing dissolved and suspended impurities called contaminants.
    • Organic Impurities: Human excreta, animal waste, oil, urea, pesticides, herbicides, fruit/vegetable waste.
    • Inorganic Impurities: Nitrates, phosphates, heavy metals.
    • Nutrients: Phosphorus and Nitrogen.
    • Bacteria & Microbes: Pathogens causing cholera (Vibrio cholerae), typhoid (Salmonella typhi), dysentery, and hepatitis.
  • Wastewater Treatment Plant (WWTP) Stages:
    • Physical Stage:
      1. Bar Screens: Screen out large floating debris like plastic bottles, rags, cans, sticks, and napkins.
      2. Grit & Sand Removal Tank: Wastewater flows slowly, allowing heavy sand, grit, and pebbles to settle down by gravity.
      3. Clarifier / Sedimentation Tank: Suspended solid faeces settle at the bottom as sludge (scraped off); lighter grease and oils float to the top and are removed by a skimmer. The output is clarified water.
    • Biological Stage: Aeration Tank: Air is continuously pumped into clarified water to encourage the rapid growth of aerobic bacteria. These microbes consume remaining human waste, food particles, soaps, and organic impurities.
    • Disinfection Stage: Treated water is disinfected using chemical agents like Chlorine or Ozone (O₃) or ultraviolet light before being safely discharged into natural rivers or the ocean.
  • Sludge & Biogas Utilization: Sludge from the clarifier is transferred to an anaerobic digester where anaerobic bacteria digest it, generating combustible biogas (used for electricity/cooking fuel). The remaining dried sludge forms nutrient-rich organic manure.
NCERT Intext Questions & Scientific Enquiries
Page 163 — Intext Q1 (Paheli's Query)
Paheli asks: Why should we never discard solid food scraps, used tea leaves, cotton, and sanitary items into the kitchen drain or toilet bowl?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Plumbing & Ecological Reasons:
1. Choking Drainage Conduits: Solid food scraps, viscous fats, tea leaves, and sanitary napkins do not dissolve in water. They agglomerate inside narrow pipe elbows and traps, trapping hair and debris to form stubborn blockages that cause foul sewer back-ups into homes.
2. Smothering Aerobic Microbes: In wastewater treatment systems, solid debris blankets bottom clarifiers, clogging intake pumps and reducing dissolved oxygen levels needed by aerobic bacteria to purify sewage.
Correct Practice: All solid organic refuse must be segregated and thrown into household dry/wet waste dustbins.

Page 165 — Intext Q2 (Boojho's Query)
Boojho asks: Why are eucalyptus trees planted along municipal wastewater treatment ponds and open sewage channels?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Phytoremediation / Bio-drainage by Eucalyptus:
1. High Transpiration Rate: Eucalyptus trees have deep root systems and extraordinarily high water-absorption rates, acting as natural biological water pumps.
2. Purification into Clean Water Vapor: The roots absorb surplus nutrient-rich wastewater rapidly, filtering out organic compounds to nourish tree biomass, and transpire massive volumes of clean, pure water vapor into the atmosphere.
3. Pollution & Odour Mitigation: By rapidly drying out waterlogged sewage lagoons, they prevent foul odor, eliminate stagnant mosquito-breeding swamps, and provide valuable timber without chemical expenses.

Q2
What is sewage? Explain why it is harmful to discharge untreated sewage into rivers or seas.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Definition of Sewage:
Sewage is wastewater carried away by a drainage network of sewers from homes, industries, agricultural fields, and public institutions. It is a complex liquid suspension containing dissolved organic and inorganic impurities, nutrients, toxic chemicals, and disease-causing microbes.
2. Hazards of Discharging Untreated Sewage into Water Bodies:
  • Outbreak of Waterborne Diseases: Untreated sewage contains dangerous pathogens that contaminate drinking water sources, sparking deadly epidemics of cholera, typhoid, jaundice (hepatitis), dysentery, and polio.
  • Severe Aquatic Oxygen Depletion (Eutrophication): Aerobic micro-organisms rapidly decompose organic wastes in sewage, consuming dissolved oxygen (DO). As oxygen plunges to near-zero levels, fish, crabs, and other aquatic life suffocate and die en masse.
  • Destruction of Aquatic Ecosystems: Toxic household detergents, industrial chemicals, and heavy metals bioaccumulate in marine life, devastating coral reefs and marine food webs.
  • Foul Odour & Environmental Degradation: Unchecked anaerobic decomposition emits toxic hydrogen sulphide (H₂S) and methane gases, making water bodies putrid, uninhabitable eyesores.

Q3
Why should oils and fats not be released in the drain? Explain.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Cooking oils, fats, and grease should never be poured down kitchen sinks or household drains for two major reasons:

  1. Clogging of Drainage Pipes: As liquid oils and melted butter flow through cold underground plumbing pipes, they cool down and solidify into thick, sticky fat deposits. These deposits trap dirt and other debris, narrowing the pipe diameter and eventually causing complete, foul blockage of household drains.
  2. Choking of Open Soil Pores: In open drains and soil seepage channels, oily residues coat the soil particles, clogging natural microscopic soil pores. This prevents normal water percolation and suffocates the soil's natural capacity to filter wastewater.

Eco-friendly Disposal: Used cooking oil and solid grease should always be discarded into the solid garbage dustbin.

Q4
Describe the steps involved in getting clarified water from wastewater.
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

At a Wastewater Treatment Plant (WWTP), raw sewage is transformed into clarified water through the following sequential physical and biological stages:

Stage 1: Filtration through Bar Screens:
Wastewater first flows through large vertical bar screens. These screens intercept and strain out large floating physical debris such as plastic rags, polythene bags, sticks, cans, sanitary napkins, and pebbles.
Stage 2: Grit and Sand Removal Chamber:
The water then passes through a grit and sand removal tank where the incoming flow velocity is deliberately slowed down. Heavy inorganic particles like coarse sand, gravel, and grit settle to the tank bottom by gravity and are mechanically removed.
Stage 3: Primary Sedimentation (Clarifier Tank):
The water is channeled into a large sedimentation tank sloped downwards towards the centre. The water remains undisturbed for several hours:
  • Heavy solid organic wastes (human faeces) settle at the bottom as sludge and are removed by a bottom mechanical scraper.
  • Light, floatable impurities such as oils and grease rise to the surface and are removed by a surface skimmer.
The remaining liquid separated from sludge and scum is called clarified water.
Stage 4: Aeration & Biological Purification:
Air is continuously pumped into the clarified water inside a massive aeration tank. The abundant oxygen triggers explosive growth of harmless aerobic bacteria, which consume residual dissolved organic impurities, food remnants, and soaps. After aeration, the microbes settle down as activated sludge, leaving clear, purified water ready for chemical disinfection.

Q5
What is sludge? Explain how it is treated.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Definition of Sludge:
Sludge is the solid, semi-liquid organic matter (primarily settled human faecal matter and particulate debris) that sinks and accumulates at the bottom of the primary sedimentation/clarifier tank during wastewater treatment.
2. Treatment of Sludge:
  1. Transfer to Anaerobic Digester: Sludge is continuously collected by mechanical scrapers and pumped into a sealed, oxygen-free chamber called an anaerobic digester.
  2. Anaerobic Digestion: Specialized anaerobic bacteria decompose the complex organic compounds in the sludge in the absence of oxygen.
  3. Biogas Production: This digestion process generates combustible biogas (a mixture of methane and carbon dioxide), which is captured and burned to generate electricity or used as fuel to power the treatment plant.
  4. Conversion to Organic Manure: After complete digestion, the remaining stabilized sludge is dehydrated on large sand drying beds. The dried solids yield an odorless, nutrient-rich organic manure that enriches agricultural soil.

Q6
Explain why untamed human excreta is a health hazard.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Untreated human excreta is a critical biological hazard for the following reasons:

  1. Severe Water Pollution: In open defecation areas, rain washes human faeces into nearby rivers, canals, ponds, and open wells. Furthermore, pathogens seep through porous soil into shallow aquifers, contaminating the groundwater that rural communities rely on for drinking and cooking.
  2. Transmission of Fatal Waterborne Diseases: A single gram of human faeces can contain millions of viruses, bacteria, and parasitic cysts. It is the primary vector for lethal waterborne epidemics, including:
    • Cholera (caused by Vibrio cholerae)
    • Typhoid (caused by Salmonella typhi)
    • Dysentery & Diarrhoea
    • Hepatitis A & Polio
  3. Vector Attraction (Houseflies & Pests): Exposed faecal mounds attract houseflies and cockroaches, which pick up microbial pathogens on their hairy legs and transfer them directly onto exposed human food, spreading infection.
  4. Soil Pollution & Worm Infections: Soil contaminated with faecal matter contains parasitic roundworm and hookworm eggs that penetrate barefoot walkers, causing chronic anaemia and malnutrition in children.

Q8
Explain the function of bar screens in a wastewater treatment plant.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Function of Bar Screens:
  • Physical Coarse Filtration: Bar screens act as the very first primary mechanical barrier at the entry point of a wastewater treatment plant.
  • Interception of Bulky Objects: As raw sewage flows through the grid of parallel vertical metal bars, all large, floating physical debris — such as plastic polythene bags, fabric rags, empty cans, plastic bottles, sticks, and sanitary items — are physically caught and strained out.
  • Protection of Downstream Equipment: By filtering out these large solids, bar screens prevent catastrophic clogging and damage to expensive high-pressure wastewater pumps, pipes, valves, and mechanical clarifier scrapers deeper within the facility.

Q9
Explain the relationship between sanitation and disease.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Sanitation and human health share a direct, inverse relationship: poor sanitation breeds disease, whereas robust sanitation protects public health.

Key Mechanisms:
  1. Breeding Ground for Pathogens: Poor sanitation — such as open defecation, overflowing unsealed sewers, stagnant wastewater, and accumulated garbage — provides the ideal breeding environment for disease-causing bacteria, viruses, and parasites.
  2. Vector Proliferation: Stagnant wastewater puddles serve as breeding grounds for mosquitoes (transmitting malaria, dengue, and chikungunya), while open excreta attracts houseflies that transfer microbes onto food.
  3. Contamination of Drinking Supplies: In areas with inadequate sanitation, untreated sewage leaches into shallow water tables and pipelines, triggering outbreaks of fatal waterborne illnesses like cholera, dysentery, and typhoid.
  4. Sanitation Breaks the Chain of Infection: Proper sanitation — including sanitary toilets, underground closed sewage systems, clean drinking water, and hygienic handwashing — breaks the faecal-oral transmission route, eradicating infectious diseases and saving millions of children's lives annually.

Q10
Outline your role as an active citizen in relation to sanitation.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

As a responsible and proactive citizen, one should maintain civic hygiene and sanitation through the following concrete actions:

  1. Responsible Household Waste Disposal: Never pour cooking oils, paints, grease, medicines, or chemicals down household sinks. Dispose of solid tea leaves, cotton, food scraps, and plastic wrappers in designated dry/wet dustbins rather than drains.
  2. Reporting Open Sewer Hazards: If an open sewer, choked manhole, or broken municipal water pipe is spotted in the neighbourhood, immediately alert the local municipal corporation or Gram Panchayat to avert disease outbreaks.
  3. Eradicating Open Defecation: Promote and use sanitary closed toilets; advocate against open defecation and urination in open public spaces.
  4. Community Cleanliness Campaigns: Actively participate in neighbourhood sanitation drives (like the Swachh Bharat Abhiyan), ensuring stagnant rainwater puddles are sprayed or drained to curb mosquito breeding.
  5. Spreading Awareness: Educate family, neighbours, and community workers about proper hand hygiene, water purification, and the health benefits of covered sewage networks.

Q12
Study the following statements about ozone:
(a) It is essential for breathing of living organisms.
(b) It is used to disinfect water.
(c) It absorbs ultraviolet rays.
(d) Its proportion in air is about 3%.
Which of these statements are correct?
(i) (a), (b) and (c)
(ii) (b) and (c)
(iii) (a) and (d)
(iv) All four
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Correct Option: (ii) (b) and (c)

Scientific Evaluation of Each Statement:
  • (a) Incorrect: Oxygen (O₂), not ozone (O₃), is essential for cellular respiration. Ozone is actually toxic and irritating to animal lungs if inhaled in significant quantities.
  • (b) Correct: Ozone is a potent germicide and powerful oxidizing agent used in municipal waterworks to disinfect and sterilize water.
  • (c) Correct: The stratospheric ozone layer shields Earth's biosphere by absorbing harmful ultraviolet (UV-B) solar radiation.
  • (d) Incorrect: Ozone is a trace gas making up less than 0.00006% of Earth's atmosphere, nowhere near 3%.

Case Study: Vermi-Processing Toilets & Low-Cost On-Site Sanitation
Biotechnology in Sanitation: In a remote rural village without underground sewerage infrastructure, an NGO introduced vermi-processing toilets. Human excreta is collected in a pit populated by red earthworms (tiger worms).

(i) What do the earthworms convert human excreta into?
(ii) Why are vermi-processing toilets considered ideal for rural communities with low water availability?
(iii) Name another traditional on-site sewage disposal system commonly used for isolated rural homes.

(i) Bio-conversion: Red worms ingest and thoroughly decompose human faeces, transforming pathogenic excreta into high-grade, pathogen-free vermicompost (vermi-cakes), which serves as nutrient-dense organic fertilizer for crops.

(ii) Advantages:
• Low Water Requirement: Requires minimal flushing water compared to standard cistern toilets.
• Simple & Zero Odour: Operates naturally without noxious odours, complex machinery, or expensive electrical connections.
• Resource Recovery: Directly converts hazardous waste into economic fertilizer right at the site.

(iii) Other On-site Systems: Septic tanks, biogas composting pits, and chemical toilets.

⚡ Assertion & Reason (HOTS)
Assertion (A): Cooking oils and solid food wastes should never be flushed down kitchen sinks into domestic drainage pipes.
Reason (R): Fats and oils solidify inside cold plumbing conduits, trapping food scraps and causing stubborn drain blockages, while choking soil filtration pores in open drains.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Explanation: Greasy hydrophobic lipids adhere to the walls of drainage pipes and harden as temperature drops, drastically constricting water flow. Discarding oils into solid waste receptacles preserves sewer line integrity.
