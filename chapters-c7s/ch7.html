📌 Key Scientific Concepts & Circulatory / Vascular Systems
  • Human Circulatory System:
    • Blood Composition: Liquid matrix called plasma containing Red Blood Cells (RBCs) with iron-rich haemoglobin for oxygen transport; White Blood Cells (WBCs) that destroy invading pathogens; and Platelets essential for blood clotting to prevent haemorrhage.
    • Blood Vessels: Arteries carry oxygenated blood away from the heart at high pressure (thick, elastic walls, no valves; exception: pulmonary artery); Veins carry deoxygenated blood towards the heart at low pressure (thin walls, possess internal pocket valves preventing backflow; exception: pulmonary vein); Capillaries are microscopic single-cell-thick vessels facilitating nutrient and gas exchange.
    • Heart: A hollow, muscular organ roughly the size of a fist, divided into 4 chambers (Right Atrium, Right Ventricle, Left Atrium, Left Ventricle) separated by a muscular septum to completely prevent mixing of oxygen-rich and carbon dioxide-rich blood.
  • Human Excretory System: Consists of a pair of bean-shaped Kidneys (filter blood), tube-like Ureters, a muscular Urinary Bladder (stores urine), and a terminal Urethra with an opening. Normal human urine composition: ~95% water, 2.5% urea, 2.5% other toxic metabolic salts. In kidney failure, blood is cleansed periodically using an artificial kidney through dialysis.
  • Vascular Transport in Plants:
    • Xylem: Continuous tubular network conducting water and dissolved mineral salts unidirectionally from roots upwards to the leaves.
    • Phloem: Living vascular tissue translocating food (sugars synthesized by leaves) bidirectionally to all parts of the plant.
    • Transpiration: Evaporation of water vapour from leaf stomata. Generates a powerful suction pull (transpirational pull) capable of lifting water columns over 100 metres high in giant trees, while cooling the plant.
NCERT Intext Questions & Scientific Enquiries
Page 81 — Intext Q1 (Boojho's Query)
Boojho wonders why primitive aquatic animals like sponges and Hydra do not possess blood or a circulatory system.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Scientific Reason:
1. Direct Water Circulation: Sponges and Hydra are simple multicellular organisms whose body wall consists of only two cell layers in direct contact with their aquatic environment.
2. The natural water in which they reside circulates continuously through their body cavity, bringing in dissolved oxygen and food particles directly to individual cells through simple diffusion.
3. As the water flows out, it carries away carbon dioxide and metabolic wastes directly. Because diffusion distance is microscopic, they require no internal blood or vascular pump.

Page 88 — Intext Q2 (Boojho's Query)
Boojho wonders why plants absorb such a large quantity of water through their roots if they give off most of it through transpiration.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Crucial Roles of Transpiration:
1. Generates Transpiration Pull: Evaporation of water from leaf stomata creates strong suction pressure (transpirational pull) that lifts water and essential dissolved soil minerals hundreds of feet to the crowns of tall trees.
2. Evaporative Cooling: Just as perspiration cools human skin, the continuous evaporation of water dissipates intense solar heat, preventing delicate leaf enzymes from denaturing under midday summer sun.
3. Turgidity & Mechanical Support: Continuous water flow maintains cell turgor, keeping herbaceous leaves upright and firm.

Q4
Why is transport of materials necessary in a plant or in an animal? Explain.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Transportation of materials is fundamentally essential for the survival and homeostasis of multicellular organisms for the following reasons:

1. Distribution of Nutrients and Water:
  • In Plants: Water and minerals absorbed by underground roots must reach leaves for photosynthesis. The glucose prepared by green leaves must be translocated to non-photosynthetic roots, fruits, and growing buds for energy.
  • In Animals: Digested nutrients from the intestines must be distributed by blood to trillions of cells across the entire body for cellular respiration and tissue growth.
2. Respiratory Gas Exchange: Oxygen inhaled by the lungs must be carried to all tissues to oxidise food, while carbon dioxide generated during metabolism must be transported back to lungs for expulsion.
3. Elimination of Toxic Waste Products: Cellular metabolism constantly generates toxic nitrogenous byproducts (such as urea and uric acid). An internal circulatory system carries these poisons to excretory organs (kidneys) for filtering and disposal. Without transport, cells would die from toxic accumulation.

Q5
What will happen if there are no platelets in the blood?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Inability to Clot: Blood platelets (thrombocytes) are specialised cellular fragments responsible for blood coagulation (clotting). When an injury or cut occurs, platelets rupture and release clotting factors that convert soluble fibrinogen into a dark meshwork of insoluble fibrin fibres, forming a dark red clot that seals the wound.
2. Fatal Consequence: If there are no platelets in the blood, bleeding from even a minor scratch or cut will not stop. Continuous, unchecked haemorrhage will cause severe blood loss, drop in blood pressure, organ failure, and eventual death.

Q6
What are stomata? Give two functions of stomata.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Definition: Stomata (singular: stoma) are tiny microscopic pores present predominantly on the epidermal surface of plant leaves. Each pore is flanked and regulated by a pair of kidney-shaped (or bean-shaped) guard cells.
Two Primary Functions:
  1. Exchange of Gases: They allow the diffusion of carbon dioxide (CO₂) from the atmosphere into the leaf for photosynthesis and the release of oxygen (O₂) into the atmosphere. They also facilitate respiration.
  2. Transpiration: They allow the evaporation of excess water in the form of water vapour, generating a powerful transpirational suction pull to transport water and cooling the plant.

Q7
Does transpiration serve any useful function in the plants? Explain.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Yes, although plants lose more than 95% of absorbed water through transpiration, it is a "necessary physiological process" serving several indispensable functions:

1. Generation of Transpirational Pull: As water evaporates through leaf stomata, it creates negative hydrostatic pressure (suction force) at the top of xylem columns. Similar to sucking liquid through a straw, this powerful transpirational pull lifts continuous columns of water and dissolved minerals from the roots to the highest tips of tall trees.
2. Mineral Uptake and Distribution: Mineral ions dissolved in soil water (nitrates, phosphates, potassium) are drawn upwards and distributed throughout the plant via transpirational stream.
3. Evaporative Cooling Effect: The evaporation of water absorbs latent heat from leaf tissues, lowering leaf temperatures by 2°C to 5°C. This prevents thermal shock and enzyme denaturing under scorching direct sunlight.
4. Maintaining Cell Turgidity: Transpiration ensures a steady water flow that keeps plant cells turgid, maintaining upright plant posture and leaf shape.

Q8
What are the components of blood?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Blood is a specialised fluid connective tissue composed of four primary components:

1. Plasma: The straw-coloured, fluid liquid matrix constituting about 55% of total blood volume. It consists of 90–92% water with dissolved nutrients (glucose, amino acids), proteins, salts, hormones, and waste materials (carbon dioxide, urea).
2. Red Blood Cells (RBCs / Erythrocytes): Biconcave disc-shaped cells packed with an iron-containing respiratory pigment called haemoglobin. Haemoglobin binds reversibly with oxygen to form oxyhaemoglobin, transporting oxygen from lungs to all body tissues.
3. White Blood Cells (WBCs / Leucocytes): Defence cells that form the body's immune system. They patrol the bloodstream, engulf foreign bacteria, and produce antibodies to neutralise pathogens and fight off infections.
4. Platelets (Thrombocytes): Tiny, disc-shaped cell fragments produced in bone marrow that initiate blood coagulation (clotting) at the site of a cut or injury to prevent fatal blood loss.

Q9
Why is blood needed by all the parts of a body?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Blood is the vital circulating river of life required by every living cell because:

1. Delivery of Oxygen: Every cell requires oxygen continuously to metabolise glucose during cellular respiration; blood carries oxygen from lungs to all peripheral organs.
2. Distribution of Digested Nutrients: It transports glucose, amino acids, fatty acids, vitamins, and minerals absorbed from the small intestine to nourish all body tissues.
3. Waste Removal: It picks up harmful carbon dioxide and nitrogenous wastes (urea) from cells and transports them to the lungs and kidneys for disposal.
4. Protection & Regulation: It carries antibodies and WBCs to fight infections and distributes heat to regulate uniform body temperature (37°C).

Q11
Describe the function of the heart.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

The human heart acts as a non-stop muscular pump maintaining systemic blood circulation through a double-circulation loop:

1. Four-Chambered Separation: The heart is partitioned into four chambers: two upper receiving atria (Right Atrium, Left Atrium) and two lower pumping ventricles (Right Ventricle, Left Ventricle). A central muscular partition (septum) completely isolates oxygen-rich blood from carbon dioxide-rich blood.
2. Pulmonary Circulation: Deoxygenated blood loaded with CO₂ collected from body tissues enters the Right Atrium, passes into the Right Ventricle, and is pumped via the pulmonary artery to the lungs to discharge CO₂ and absorb fresh oxygen.
3. Systemic Circulation: Freshly oxygenated blood from the lungs returns via pulmonary veins into the Left Atrium, enters the thick-walled Left Ventricle, and is pumped under high pressure through the main artery (Aorta) to all tissues and organs of the entire body.

Q12
Why is it necessary to excrete waste products?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Continuous Production of Toxins: During normal cellular metabolic processes (such as cellular respiration and protein breakdown), cells generate harmful, toxic metabolic byproducts like urea, uric acid, and carbon dioxide.
2. Toxicity & Cellular Poisoning: If these waste substances are not continuously filtered and eliminated, their concentration builds up in the bloodstream (uraemia). This causes chemical poisoning of vital organs, damages cell membranes, leads to coma, and can be fatal. Hence, excretion is mandatory to maintain physiological homeostasis.

Q13
Draw a diagram of the human excretory system and label the various parts.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

The human excretory system consists of four principal anatomical structures:

1. Pair of Kidneys: Two reddish-brown, bean-shaped organs located against the posterior abdominal wall on either side of the spine. Millions of microscopic nephron filters continuously purify blood, removing urea, excess water, and salts to form urine.
2. Pair of Ureters: Two thin, narrow muscular tubes (~25 cm long) emerging from the hilum of each kidney that conduct urine downwards by peristaltic action.
3. Urinary Bladder: A distensible, hollow muscular reservoir located in the pelvic cavity that temporarily stores urine (holding 300–500 mL) until pressure triggers the urge to urinate.
4. Urethra & Urinary Opening: A short muscular duct leading from the neck of the bladder to an external urinary aperture for micturition (expulsion of urine).

🎯 Competency-Based Questions (CBQ) & HOTS CBSE Board Exam Pattern
Case Study: Dialysis in Chronic Kidney Disease
Clinical Case: 55-year-old Mohan suffered from chronic uncontrolled hypertension, resulting in complete renal failure (both kidneys stopped functioning). His blood urea level surged dangerously, causing severe nausea, swelling of limbs, and breathlessness. His nephrologist scheduled him for haemodialysis twice a week.

(i) What is the medical term for artificial blood purification when kidneys fail?
(ii) How does a dialysis machine purify blood?
(iii) What permanent medical solution exists if long-term dialysis is no longer viable?

(i) Medical Term: Haemodialysis (Artificial Kidney).

(ii) Mechanism: Blood from an artery in the patient's arm is pumped through a dialysis machine containing thousands of semi-permeable cellophane tubes bathed in a temperature-controlled dialysing fluid (which has the exact same ionic composition as normal blood plasma but zero urea). As patient blood flows through, toxic urea, uric acid, and excess salts diffuse across the semi-permeable walls into the dialysing fluid. The cleansed blood is then warmed, mixed with an anti-coagulant reversal agent, and pumped back into the patient's vein.

(iii) Permanent Solution: Kidney Transplantation — surgically grafting a healthy matching kidney from a compatible living or deceased donor into the patient.

⚡ Assertion & Reason (HOTS)
Assertion (A): Veins have relatively thin walls with internal one-way valves, whereas arteries have thick, elastic walls without valves.
Reason (R): Blood in arteries is pumped directly from the heart under high, pulsating pressure, while blood in veins flows at low pressure and needs valves to prevent backward flow under gravity.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Explanation: Left ventricular contraction forcefully ejects blood into the aorta at high pressure (~120 mm Hg), requiring thick muscular walls in arteries to prevent bursting. In veins, blood pressure drops to only 5–10 mm Hg; hence, walls are thin, and crescent pocket valves are physiologically essential to ensure blood flows only towards the heart against gravity.
