NCERT Exercise Solutions — Complete Step-by-Step Answers with CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

📌 Key Scientific Concepts & Forest Ecosystem Architecture
  • Forest Structure & Stratification:
    • Canopy: The uppermost continuous roof formed by the crowns of tall mature trees over the forest floor.
    • Understorey: The distinct horizontal layers formed below the canopy, consisting of smaller trees, shrubs, and tall bushes.
    • Forest Floor: Covered with dead leaves, twigs, seeds, animal waste, and herbs; home to millipedes, beetles, fungi, and decomposers.
  • Ecosystem Balance & Food Chains:
    • Forests are self-sustaining, dynamic living entities (a "super-organism").
    • Food Chain: Energy flow from solar radiation to autotrophic producers (green plants) → primary consumers (herbivores like deer, grasshoppers) → secondary/tertiary consumers (carnivores like frogs, tigers) → decomposers.
    • Decomposers: Micro-organisms (fungi, bacteria) that decompose dead organic matter into dark organic humus, recycling nutrients back into the soil for root uptake. There is zero waste in a virgin forest.
  • Ecological & Hydrological Services:
    • "Green Lungs" of the Earth: Forests absorb atmospheric carbon dioxide (CO₂) through photosynthesis and replenish oxygen (O₂), preserving atmospheric gas equilibrium.
    • Natural Water Filter & Flood Barrier: Dense leaf canopies dissipate torrential rainfall impact, while extensive root networks bind the soil (preventing soil erosion) and facilitate rapid rainwater seepage into aquifers, maintaining the water table and preventing flash floods.
NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions
Q1
Explain how animals dwelling in the forest help it grow and regenerate.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Animals living in a forest play crucial symbiotic roles that stimulate forest growth, seed dispersal, and continuous regeneration:

  1. Seed Dispersal: Herbivores and frugivorous birds feed on forest fruits. The indigestible seeds pass through their digestive tracts unharmed and are deposited far away with manure, allowing seedlings to sprout across new territories without overcrowding. Hooked seeds (like Xanthium) cling to animal fur and are carried to distant clearings.
  2. Pollination of Flowers: Flying insects (butterflies, bees, moths), birds, and bats visit flowers for nectar, transferring pollen grains between blossoms to ensure cross-pollination and viable seed formation.
  3. Nutrient Enrichment (Natural Manure): Animal excreta (dung, droppings) and decomposing carcasses provide essential organic matter. Microorganisms decompose these wastes into nutrient-rich humus, providing essential nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium that fertilize germinating seedlings.
  4. Soil Aeration & Overgrowth Control: Burrowing animals (earthworms, ants, rodents) loosen the soil, improving root respiration and water infiltration, while grazing animals prevent aggressive undergrowth from suffocating young saplings.

Q2
Explain how forests prevent floods.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Forests act as natural giant sponges and flood control buffers through the following hydrological mechanisms:

  1. Canopy Interception: The dense overhead roof of interlocking tree crowns and leaves intercepts torrential downpours, breaking the kinetic force of falling raindrops and preventing direct high-velocity impact on bare soil.
  2. Slow Drip to Forest Floor: Rainwater trickles slowly down tree trunks and drips gently onto the porous, litter-covered forest floor rather than surging over the surface.
  3. Natural Sponge & Percolation: The thick spongy layer of decaying leaves, humus, and organic mulch absorbs vast quantities of water, allowing it to slowly percolate deep underground into aquifers, steadily recharging the groundwater table.
  4. Prevention of Surface Runoff & Erosion: Extensive tree root networks bind the soil particles tightly, preventing them from being washed away (preventing siltation of rivers and reservoirs). By moderating surface runoff, forests ensure rivers receive a steady, controlled supply of water throughout the year rather than sudden catastrophic flash floods.

Q3
What are decomposers? Name any two of them. What do they do in the forest?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Definition of Decomposers:
Decomposers are saprophytic micro-organisms that feed upon dead plant tissues, fallen leaves, rotten wood, and animal carcasses, breaking down complex organic molecules into simpler inorganic nutrients.
2. Examples of Decomposers:
• Bacteria
• Fungi (e.g., mushrooms, moulds)
3. Role in the Forest Ecosystem:
  • They convert dead plant and animal remains into a dark, crumbly organic substance known as humus.
  • Humus enriches the forest topsoil with essential mineral nutrients (nitrogen, carbon, potassium, phosphorus).
  • These released mineral nutrients are subsequently re-absorbed by tree roots, maintaining a continuous cyclic flow of nutrients in the ecosystem.
  • They prevent dead debris from piling up, serving as natural recyclers and cleaners of the forest floor.

Q4
Explain the role of forest in maintaining the balance between oxygen and carbon dioxide in the atmosphere.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Forests are globally recognized as the "green lungs of nature" because of their indispensable role in maintaining the atmospheric equilibrium of oxygen and carbon dioxide:

  1. Photosynthesis (Oxygen Generation & Carbon Sink):
    During daylight hours, trillions of leaves in a forest absorb immense volumes of carbon dioxide (CO₂) from the atmosphere to synthesize glucose, releasing massive quantities of life-sustaining oxygen gas (O₂) into the air:
    6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Solar Energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
  2. Respiration:
    All forest animals, micro-organisms, and plants inhale oxygen for cellular respiration and release carbon dioxide back into the atmosphere:
    C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy
  3. Net Ecological Balance:
    Because the net oxygen produced during daytime photosynthesis far exceeds the oxygen consumed during respiration by forest organisms, the surplus oxygen is dispersed globally to sustain animal life across the planet, while excess carbon dioxide is locked away in forest biomass, preventing runaway global warming.

Q5
Explain why there is no waste in a forest.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Answer: There is no waste in a forest because all biological by-products are naturally recycled within closed ecological food chains and cycles.

Key Reasons:
  • Scavengers Consume Carrion: Scavengers (vultures, crows, jackals, hyenas) quickly consume dead animal carcasses.
  • Decomposers Complete Mineralisation: Any remaining organic matter, fallen dry leaves, animal excreta, and decaying logs are attacked by millions of decomposers (fungi, bacteria, beetles, earthworms).
  • Transformation into Humus: Decomposers break down these organic residues into humus, releasing base minerals and nitrogen back into the topsoil.
  • Root Uptake: Plants immediately reabsorb these mineral nutrients through their root hairs to build new leaves, branches, and fruits.
  • Because everything produced is consumed and re-assimilated, the forest operates as a perfectly zero-waste circular ecosystem.

Q6
List five products we get from forests.
2.5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Five important economic and domestic products obtained from forests include:

  1. Timber and Firewood: High-grade structural wood (teak, sal, rosewood, deodar) used for furniture, buildings, boats, and paper pulp.
  2. Medicinal Herbs and Pharmaceuticals: Natural therapeutic plants (cinchona for quinine, neem, tulsi, amla, rauwolfia for blood pressure).
  3. Gums, Resins, and Turpentine: Industrial bonding agents, paints, varnishes, and aromatics extracted from tree bark.
  4. Honey and Wax: Wild forest honey harvested from wild honeybee hives, and natural beeswax used in cosmetics.
  5. Spices, Lac, and Catechu (Katha): Forest spices (cinnamon, cardamom), lac for sealing wax and bangles, and katha for betel leaves.

Q8
Why should we worry about the conditions and issues related to forests far from us?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Even if forests are located hundreds of kilometres away from our urban cities, their degradation directly impacts global planetary survival in the following ways:

  1. Global Warming & Climate Change: Forests act as planetary carbon sinks. Widespread deforestation releases stored carbon, increasing atmospheric CO₂ concentrations and accelerating global global warming, unpredictable monsoon cycles, and melting polar glaciers.
  2. Oxygen Depletion: Large tropical forests (like the Amazon and Western Ghats) generate substantial shares of the world's oxygen. Their loss threatens breathable atmospheric oxygen quality.
  3. Disruption of Rainfall & Water Cycles: Forests contribute massive volumes of water vapour to cloud formation through transpiration. Destroying forests leads to severe continental droughts, erratic rainfall, and shrinking rivers.
  4. Downstream Flooding & Siltation: Deforested mountain watersheds allow unchecked torrents of rainwater to wash down fertile topsoil into distant urban rivers, causing catastrophic silt build-up, flash flooding, and drinking water shortages in faraway plains.
  5. Loss of Global Biodiversity: Over 80% of terrestrial wildlife lives in forests. Deforestation drives species to permanent extinction, destroying potential future sources of life-saving medical compounds.

Q9
Explain why there is a need of variety of animals and plants in a forest.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

A high variety of plants and animals (biodiversity) is essential for the ecological resilience, stability, and survival of a forest:

  1. Interconnected Food Chains and Food Webs: Different herbivores feed on different species of plants (grasses, tender leaves, bark, fruits). A greater variety of herbivores provides abundant prey for diverse carnivores. This intricate food web ensures that no single species overpopulates or starves.
  2. Enhanced Nutrient Regeneration: A diverse mixture of tree species sheds varying types of foliage and produces complex roots, supporting diverse communities of soil microbes and decomposers that replenish a broader spectrum of soil minerals.
  3. Pest & Disease Resistance: In a diverse forest, individual trees of the same species are separated by other species. This spatial separation prevents insect infestations and plant epidemics from spreading unchecked across the entire landscape.
  4. Habitat Stratification: Different animal species occupy distinct vertical zones (canopy, understorey, forest floor, underground burrows), enabling the forest to maximize energy utilization without lethal inter-species competition.

Q11
Which of the following is not a forest product?
(i) Gum
(ii) Plywood
(iii) Sealing wax
(iv) Kerosene
1 Mark
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Correct Option: (iv) Kerosene

Scientific Reason:
  • Gum is a direct natural exudate tapped from forest trees (such as acacia).
  • Plywood is manufactured by gluing together thin veneers of forest timber.
  • Sealing wax is derived from lac, a resinous secretion produced by tiny insects dwelling on forest host trees.
  • Kerosene is a liquid hydrocarbon fuel obtained from the fractional distillation of underground crude petroleum (a fossil fuel), not a direct product harvested from forests.

🎯 Competency-Based Questions (CBQ) & HOTS CBSE Board Exam Pattern
Case Study: Deforestation & Flash Flood Catastrophe
Environmental Disaster Case: A hill slopes district cleared 500 hectares of virgin mountain forest for an infrastructure project. During the following monsoon, an intense rainfall event of 180 mm in 24 hours caused massive landslides, wiped out roads, and caused catastrophic flash flooding in river valleys 80 km downstream.

(i) What natural role of tree roots on the slope was lost due to deforestation?
(ii) Why did rainwater rush down into the river so rapidly instead of recharging aquifers?
(iii) How does the canopy of a forest protect the underlying soil from detachment?

(i) Loss of Soil Binding: Tree root systems act like biological reinforcing mesh that mechanically binds soil particles, prevents slope destabilization, and holds millions of tonnes of earth firmly against gravitational shear stress.

(ii) Loss of Infiltration: Without the spongy forest floor (humus and leaf litter) to absorb water and facilitate gradual underground percolation, rain became immediate high-speed surface runoff, causing catastrophic downstream torrents.

(iii) Canopy Shielding: An intact forest canopy absorbs the terminal velocity kinetic energy of large falling raindrops. Instead of forcefully impacting and dislodging topsoil particles (splash erosion), water gently drips down leaves and branches onto the ground.

⚡ Assertion & Reason (HOTS)
Assertion (A): Despite huge quantities of fallen leaves, dead animals, and droppings, a virgin forest floor never accumulates waste.
Reason (R): Decomposers (bacteria and fungi) convert all dead organic matter into fertile dark humus, which is rapidly recycled back into growing plants.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Explanation: Natural ecosystems operate on a perfect circular bio-economy. Microscopic decomposers and macro-scavengers recycle every gram of biomass into chemical nutrients, ensuring that nature generates zero waste.
