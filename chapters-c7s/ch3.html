📌 Key Scientific Concepts & Thermodynamics Summary
  • Heat vs Temperature:
    • Heat: A form of energy that flows spontaneously from a body at a higher temperature to a body at a lower temperature until thermal equilibrium is reached. Unit: Joule (J) or Calorie (cal).
    • Temperature: A reliable quantitative measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of an object. Measured using thermometers in Degree Celsius (°C), Fahrenheit (°F), or Kelvin (K).
  • Thermometers:
    • Clinical Thermometer: Designed specifically for human body temperature. Range: 35°C to 42°C (or 94°F to 108°F). Contains a narrow constriction (kink) just above the bulb to prevent mercury from falling back automatically before the reading is recorded.
    • Laboratory Thermometer: Used to measure temperature of chemical solutions and substances in labs. Range: -10°C to 110°C. Has no kink; reading must be taken while still in contact with the substance.
  • Three Modes of Heat Transfer:
    1. Conduction: Heat transfer in solids from the hotter end to the colder end through molecular vibrations without the actual movement of particles. Metals are good conductors; wood, plastic, glass, and air are insulators.
    2. Convection: Heat transfer in fluids (liquids and gases) where warmer, less dense fluid expands, rises, and cooler denser fluid sinks to replace it, setting up convection currents. Drives sea breeze (day) and land breeze (night) in coastal areas.
    3. Radiation: Heat transfer through electromagnetic infrared waves without requiring any physical medium. Can travel through vacuum (how the Sun's heat warms Earth). Dark, dull surfaces absorb and radiate heat best; light, polished surfaces reflect heat.
NCERT Textbook Exercise Questions
Q1
State similarities and differences between the laboratory thermometer and the clinical thermometer.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Similarities:
  • Both thermometers consist of a long, narrow, uniform glass capillary tube with a sealed liquid bulb at one end.
  • Both traditionally use mercury (or coloured alcohol) as the thermometric liquid that expands uniformly upon heating.
  • Both are graduated with the Celsius scale (°C) to measure temperature.
Differences:
ParameterClinical ThermometerLaboratory Thermometer
Measurement Range 35°C to 42°C (spans human body temperature variation). -10°C to 110°C (wide range for chemical experiments).
Constriction (Kink) Has a kink in the capillary tube near the bulb to prevent mercury from backflowing. Has no kink; mercury level falls immediately when removed from the medium.
Taking Readings Reading can be taken conveniently after removing it from the patient's mouth or armpit. Reading must be recorded while the thermometer bulb remains immersed in the liquid.
Jerking Must be given a few sharp jerks to reset mercury below 35°C before reuse. Does not require jerking; resets spontaneously to ambient temperature.

Q5
Discuss why wearing more layers of clothing during winter keeps us warmer than wearing just one thick piece of clothing.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Wearing multiple layers of clothing keeps us significantly warmer than wearing one thick garment due to the insulating properties of trapped air:

1. Trapped Air Layers: When we wear multiple thin layers of clothes, pockets of air become trapped in the microscopic spaces between adjacent layers of fabric.
2. Air is a Poor Conductor of Heat: Air is one of the poorest conductors (best insulators) of heat in nature. The motionless trapped air forms an effective thermal barrier that prevents body heat from conducting outwards into the freezing ambient surroundings.
Conclusion: Multiple trapped air interfaces provide far superior thermal resistance compared to a single thick piece of fabric, which has only two exterior air-contact surfaces.

Q6
Look at a vessel containing water boiling over a burner flame. Mark where the heat is being transferred by conduction, by convection and by radiation.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Conduction: Takes place through the solid metallic base and sides of the pan. Heat transfers from the outer bottom surface of the metal pan directly to its inner surface in contact with water, and along the pan handle.
2. Convection: Takes place within the water inside the pan. The bottom water layer heats up, expands, becomes lighter, and rises upwards. Cold, denser water from the top sinks to the bottom, creating circular convection currents that heat the entire body of water.
3. Radiation: Takes place through the air surrounding the flame and hot pan. Thermal infrared waves travel directly outward in all directions from the burner flame and hot outer pan surfaces without needing physical contact.

Q7
In places of hot climate, it is advised that the outer walls of houses be painted white. Explain.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. High Reflection: White and light-coloured surfaces are excellent reflectors of radiant heat. They reflect back most of the intense sunlight falling upon them.
2. Poor Absorption: White surfaces absorb a minimal fraction of solar infrared radiation compared to dark surfaces.
Conclusion: As a result, the outer walls absorb very little heat during scorching daytime hours, keeping the interior of the house substantially cooler, reducing room temperature and cooling costs.

Q8
One litre of water at 30°C is mixed with one litre of water at 50°C. The temperature of the mixture will be: (a) 80°C (b) more than 50°C but less than 80°C (c) 20°C (d) between 30°C and 50°C
1 Mark
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Correct Option: (d) between 30°C and 50°C

Scientific Principle of Heat Exchange: Heat flows spontaneously from a warmer body to a cooler body until thermal equilibrium is attained:
Heat Lost by Hot Water = Heat Gained by Cold Water
For equal masses of water: T_final = (30°C + 50°C) / 2 = 80 / 2 = 40°C. Since 40°C lies strictly between 30°C and 50°C, option (d) is correct.

Q9
An iron ball at 40°C is dropped in a mug containing water at 40°C. The heat will: (a) flow from iron ball to water (b) not flow from iron ball to water or from water to iron ball (c) flow from water to iron ball (d) increase the temperature of both
1 Mark
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Correct Option: (b) not flow from iron ball to water or from water to iron ball

Scientific Reason: The flow of thermal energy requires a temperature difference (ΔT > 0) between two contacting bodies. Since both the iron ball and water are at the exact same temperature (40°C), they are in a state of thermal equilibrium. Hence, no net heat transfer takes place between them.

Case Study: Coastal Breeze Convection Cycles
Scenario: Coastal cities like Mumbai and Chennai experience pleasant breezes during both day and night without drastic diurnal temperature extremes seen in desert cities like Jaisalmer.

(i) What is Sea Breeze? Explain its formation mechanism during daytime.
(ii) What is Land Breeze? Explain why it reverses during nighttime.
(iii) Why do windows of houses in coastal areas ideally face the sea?

(i) Sea Breeze (Daytime): Land heats up much faster than water due to its lower specific heat capacity. During the day, air above land heats up, expands, and rises. Cool, dense air from over the sea moves inland towards land to take its place. This cool onshore wind is the Sea Breeze.

(ii) Land Breeze (Nighttime): At night, land cools down much faster than sea water. The sea water remains relatively warm, causing the air above it to rise. Cooler, denser air from over the land blows seaward to replace it. This offshore wind is the Land Breeze.

(iii) Architectural Design: Windows are built facing the sea to directly receive the continuous, refreshing daytime sea breeze, naturally cooling the interior without requiring artificial air conditioning.

⚡ Assertion & Reason (HOTS)
Assertion (A): A clinical thermometer should never be sterilised by dipping in boiling water.
Reason (R): The maximum calibrated range of a clinical thermometer is 42°C, whereas boiling water reaches 100°C, causing extreme mercury expansion that can shatter the glass capillary.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Explanation: Clinical thermometers are specially engineered only for human physiological range (35°C–42°C). Dipping in boiling water (100°C) produces excessive hydrostatic pressure from expanding mercury, which will break the sealed glass tube and release toxic mercury vapor. Antiseptic solutions (like Dettol or alcohol wipes) should be used instead.
