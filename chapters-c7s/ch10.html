📌 Key Scientific Concepts & Circuit Fundamentals
  • Symbols of Circuit Components:
    • Electric Cell: Represented by two parallel lines — one long thin line (+ positive terminal) and one shorter thick line (- negative terminal).
    • Battery: A combination of two or more cells connected such that the positive terminal of one cell connects to the negative terminal of the next.
    • Switch: Shown open (OFF - incomplete circuit) or closed (ON - complete closed circuit).
    • Electric Bulb: A circle enclosing an incandescent filament loop.
  • Heating Effect of Electric Current (Joule Heating):
    • When an electric current flows through a high-resistance wire, electrical energy is converted into heat energy. The wire becomes hot.
    • Amount of heat produced depends on the wire's material, length, thickness, and the magnitude of current.
    • Heating Appliances: Electric iron, room heater, geyser, toaster use coils of high-resistance wire called elements (usually made of Nichrome alloy).
    • Electric Fuse: A safety device made of a special wire with a low melting point. If excessive current surges through the circuit due to overloading or short-circuiting, the fuse wire melts and breaks the circuit, preventing fires and device damage.
    • MCB (Miniature Circuit Breaker): Modern electromagnetic/bimetallic switch that automatically trips OFF when current exceeds safe limits; easily reset without wire replacement.
  • Magnetic Effect of Electric Current (Electromagnetism):
    • Discovered in 1820 by Danish physicist Hans Christian Oersted, who noticed that a magnetic compass needle deflects whenever current passes through a nearby wire.
    • Electromagnet: An insulated wire coil wound tightly around a soft iron core. It behaves like a magnet only when electric current flows through it. Magnetism disappears immediately when current is switched off.
    • Applications: Scrap-metal cranes, electric bells, telephone receivers, MRI machines, maglev trains.
    • Electric Bell: Operates on an electromagnet, an armature with a striker hammer, an adjustment contact screw, and a metallic gong in a self-breaking circuit loop.
NCERT Intext Questions & Scientific Enquiries
Page 124 — Intext Q1 (Paheli's Query)
Paheli asks: Why are Compact Fluorescent Lamps (CFLs) and Light Emitting Diodes (LEDs) preferred over traditional incandescent tungsten filament bulbs?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Energy Efficiency Comparison:
1. Wasted Thermal Energy in Incandescent Bulbs: An incandescent tungsten bulb functions by heating a filament to over 2500°C until it glows. Over 85–90% of electrical energy is wasted as heat, with only 10–15% converted into visible light.
2. Superior Efficiency of LEDs & CFLs: LEDs emit light via electroluminescence in semiconductor junctions, generating virtually zero excess heat. A 9-watt LED bulb produces the same brightness as a 60-watt incandescent bulb, saving over 80% electricity.
3. Longevity & Safety: LEDs last 25,000+ hours (compared to 1,000 hours for filament bulbs) and drastically reduce carbon footprints and electric utility bills.

Page 126 — Intext Q2 (Boojho's Query)
Boojho wants to know why a magnetic compass needle kept near an electric wire deflects ONLY when the switch is in the 'ON' position, but returns to normal North-South when 'OFF'.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Magnetic Effect of Current (Oersted's Principle):
1. When switch is 'OFF', no electric charges are flowing (I = 0). The wire has zero magnetic field. The compass needle responds only to Earth's natural geomagnetic field and points North-South.
2. When switch is moved to 'ON', electric current flows through the copper wire. Moving electrical charges generate an artificial magnetic field around the wire.
3. This magnetic field exerts a mechanical torque on the permanent magnetic compass needle, deflecting it away from North-South. When current is switched off, the magnetic field vanishes instantly, allowing the needle to swing back to Earth's North-South.

Q3
Fig. 10.22 shows four cells fixed on a board. Draw lines to indicate how you will connect their terminals with wires to make a battery of four cells.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

To construct a battery of four cells, the cells must be connected in series such that the positive terminal (+, metallic cap) of one cell is connected to the negative terminal (-, flat zinc base) of the adjacent cell.

Wiring Sequence for 4 Cells (labelled 1, 2, 3, 4):
  • Connect the negative terminal (-) of Cell 1 to the positive terminal (+) of Cell 2 using a connecting wire.
  • Connect the negative terminal (-) of Cell 2 to the positive terminal (+) of Cell 3 using a connecting wire.
  • Connect the negative terminal (-) of Cell 3 to the positive terminal (+) of Cell 4 using a connecting wire.
  • The positive terminal (+) of Cell 1 and the negative terminal (-) of Cell 4 serve as the two free external terminals of the combined 4-cell battery to power the circuit.
Key Rule: Never connect positive to positive (+ to +) or negative to negative (- to -), as their opposing electromotive forces will cancel each other out and no current will flow.

Q4

Identification of the Problem:
In the given circuit, the two cells are connected incorrectly: the positive terminal of the first cell is connected to the positive terminal of the second cell (+ to +). Because similar polarity terminals oppose each other, the net voltage across the circuit is zero, preventing electric current from flowing.
Necessary Changes:
Turn one of the cells around so that the positive terminal (+, brass cap) of the first cell connects to the negative terminal (-, zinc base) of the second cell.
Once the cells are connected in series with opposite polarities, a complete closed circuit is formed and the bulb will glow brightly.

Q5
Name any two effects of electric current.
1 Mark
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Two major effects produced by electric current are:

1. Heating Effect of Electric Current: Conversion of electrical energy into thermal energy when current passes through a resistive conductor (e.g., electric iron, water geyser, room heater, fuse wire).
2. Magnetic Effect of Electric Current: Production of an invisible magnetic field around a current-carrying conductor (e.g., electromagnets, electric bells, electric motors).

(Note: Electric current also exhibits a Chemical Effect, e.g., electroplating).

Q6
When the current is switched on through a wire, a compass needle kept nearby gets deflected from its north-south position. Explain.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Scientific Explanation:

  • A compass needle is a tiny, freely pivoted permanent magnet that aligns itself along Earth's geomagnetic North-South axis when no other magnetic field is present.
  • When electric current flows through the adjacent wire, the wire generates a magnetic field around itself (the magnetic effect of electric current, discovered by Hans Christian Oersted).
  • This newly generated magnetic field exerts a mechanical magnetic force on the compass needle.
  • The interaction between the magnetic field of the current-carrying wire and the permanent magnetic field of the compass needle forces the needle to deflect away from its natural North-South position.

Q7
Will the compass needle show deflection when the switch in the circuit shown by Fig. 10.24 is closed? Explain.

✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Answer: No, the compass needle will NOT show any deflection.

Scientific Reason:
  • The circuit shown in Fig. 10.24 consists only of a bulb, connecting wires, and a switch — it lacks an electric source (no electric cell or battery).
  • Without an electromotive power source (cell/battery), no potential difference exists to push electric charges through the circuit.

  • Since there is no electric current, no magnetic field is generated around the wire. Therefore, the magnetic compass needle remains completely undeflected in its resting North-South alignment.

Q10
Do you think an electromagnet can be used for separating plastic bags from a garbage heap? Explain.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Answer: No, an electromagnet cannot be used to separate plastic bags from a garbage heap.

Scientific Reason:
  • An electromagnet produces a magnetic field and can only attract magnetic substances such as iron, cobalt, nickel, and steel.
  • Plastic is a completely non-magnetic polymer. It does not possess magnetic dipoles and is unaffected by magnetic lines of force.
  • Therefore, an electromagnet will not exert any attractive force on plastic bags. Scrap heaps utilize electromagnets only to extract ferromagnetic scrap iron, not plastics.

Q11
An electrician is carrying out some repairs in your house. He wants to replace a fuse by a piece of wire. Would you agree? Give reasons for your response.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Answer: No, I would strictly disagree and forbid the electrician from replacing the fuse with an ordinary piece of wire.

Scientific Reasons:
  • Specific Low Melting Point: Standard fuse wire is manufactured from a specialized tin-lead alloy engineered with a low melting point and precise current rating.
  • Ordinary Wires Do Not Melt: Ordinary connecting copper or aluminium wires have very high melting points and can withstand very high electrical currents without breaking.
  • Severe Fire & Damage Hazard: If an electrical overload or short-circuit occurs, an ordinary copper wire will not melt. The heavy current will flow unchecked through household appliances, damaging expensive electronics, melting wall wiring insulation, and potentially causing an electrical building fire.
  • Safe Alternative: Only an ISI-marked fuse cartridge of the correct rated amperage (e.g., 5 A or 15 A) or a Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB) must be installed.

Q12
Zubeda made an electric circuit using a cell holder, a switch and a bulb. When she put the switch in the ‘ON’ position, the bulb did not glow. Help Zubeda in identifying the possible defects in the circuit.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

The bulb may fail to glow due to any of the following technical defects in the circuit:

  1. Incorrect Cell Polarity: The cells in the cell holder may be placed backwards, connecting like terminals together (+ to + or - to -) instead of opposite terminals (+ to -).
  2. Exhausted / Discharged Cells: The chemical energy of the dry cells might be completely depleted (dead cells).
  3. Fused Bulb: The tungsten filament inside the bulb might be broken, resulting in an open circuit.
  4. Loose Connections: Connecting wires may not be tightly fastened to the terminals of the cell holder, switch, or bulb socket.
  5. Oxidation / Dirt on Terminals: Corrosion or rust on the metal contacts of the cell holder or switch contacts may introduce high contact resistance, blocking current flow.
  6. Faulty Switch: The switch contacts might not make proper conductive contact when moved to the 'ON' position.

Q13
In the circuit shown in Fig. 10.25 (with bulbs A, B, and C connected in series along with a switch and a cell):
(i) Would any of the bulbs glow when the switch is in the ‘OFF’ position?
(ii) What will be the order in which the bulbs A, B and C will glow when the switch is moved to the ‘ON’ position?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) When switch is in 'OFF' position:
None of the bulbs will glow. When the switch is OFF, the electrical circuit is incomplete (open circuit). Electric current cannot flow across any part of an open circuit.
(ii) When switch is moved to 'ON' position:
All three bulbs (A, B, and C) will glow simultaneously at the exact same instant.
Scientific Justification: Electric current is the collective drift of free electrons present throughout the conducting wire loop. The moment the switch is closed, the electric field propagates through the circuit almost instantaneously at nearly the speed of light (~3 × 10⁸ m/s). Therefore, current starts flowing through every series component simultaneously, causing bulbs A, B, and C to illuminate together without any human-perceptible time delay.

Case Study: Modern Home Electrical Safety — Fuse vs MCB
Domestic Electrical Engineering: In an older apartment, a blown traditional porcelain fuse often plunged the house into darkness, requiring someone to search for fuse wire and rewire the cartridge in the dark. During renovation, an electrical engineer replaced all traditional fuses with Miniature Circuit Breakers (MCBs).

(i) On which two physical principles do MCBs operate to interrupt excessive current?
(ii) Why are MCBs considered far superior and safer than traditional rewirable fuses?
(iii) What should a homeowner inspect before resetting a tripped MCB back to the ON position?

(i) Operating Principles: MCBs utilize two trip mechanisms:
1. Thermal effect (Bimetallic strip): For gradual sustained overloads, resistive heating causes a bimetallic strip to bend and mechanically release the latch.
2. Magnetic effect (Solenoid coil): For dangerous instantaneous short circuits, high surge current energizes an internal electromagnet plunger that instantly trips the contact open in milliseconds.

(ii) Advantages of MCBs:
• Quick Reset: Simply flip the toggle switch back up — no messy wire handling or risk of touching live terminals.
• Tamper-proof Safety: Calibrated factory ratings cannot be bypassed by using an oversized wire.
• High Sensitivity: Trips in 2.5 milliseconds, minimizing fire risk and appliance damage.

(iii) Safety Inspection: The homeowner must unplug the faulty appliance or rectify the underlying short circuit / overload that triggered the trip before flicking the MCB back ON, otherwise it will trip again instantly.

⚡ Assertion & Reason (HOTS)
Assertion (A): An electromagnet can be switched ON and OFF at will and its magnetic strength can be varied by adjusting the electric current.
Reason (R): The magnetic field of an electromagnet is produced by the electric current flowing through its solenoid coil, and soft iron cores lose their magnetism as soon as current stops.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Explanation: Unlike permanent magnets whose strength and poles are fixed, electromagnets are temporary magnets. Increasing current or number of coil turns amplifies magnetic flux, and cutting current drops magnetization to zero immediately, making them indispensable in heavy crane hoists and relays.
