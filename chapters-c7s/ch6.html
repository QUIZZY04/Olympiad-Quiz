📌 Key Scientific Concepts & Respiratory Mechanisms
  • Cellular Respiration: The intracellular biochemical breakdown of glucose in the presence or absence of oxygen to release usable biological energy (stored as ATP):
    • Aerobic Respiration: Occurs in the presence of oxygen; completely oxidises glucose into carbon dioxide, water, and 36–38 ATP molecules:
      Glucose + Oxygen ⎯⎯→ Carbon dioxide (6CO₂) + Water (6H₂O) + Energy (38 ATP)
    • Anaerobic Respiration in Yeast (Fermentation): In the absence of oxygen, glucose is partially broken down into ethyl alcohol, carbon dioxide, and 2 ATP molecules:
      Glucose ⎯⎯[In absence of O₂]⎯⎯→ Ethyl alcohol (2C₂H₅OH) + Carbon dioxide (2CO₂) + Energy (2 ATP)
    • Anaerobic Respiration in Human Skeletal Muscles: Occurs during intense exercise when oxygen supply falls short of muscular demand; produces lactic acid:
      Glucose ⎯⎯[In absence of O₂]⎯⎯→ Lactic acid (2C₃H₆O₃) + Energy (2 ATP)
      Accumulation of lactic acid causes painful muscle cramps. Relief is obtained through a hot bath or massage, which improves blood circulation and oxygen supply to oxidise lactic acid into CO₂ and H₂O.
  • Breathing Mechanism in Humans:
    • Inhalation (Inspiration): Diaphragm contracts and flattens downwards; ribcage moves upwards and outwards. Chest cavity volume increases, lowering air pressure in lungs. Atmospheric air rushes in through nostrils → pharynx → trachea → bronchi → lungs.
    • Exhalation (Expiration): Diaphragm relaxes and curves upwards into dome shape; ribcage moves downwards and inwards. Chest cavity volume decreases, increasing lung air pressure, forcing air out.
  • Respiration in Diverse Organisms:
    • Insects (Cockroach): Network of air tubes called tracheae opening through small exterior holes called spiracles.
    • Earthworm: Breathes through moist, slimy, vascular skin (cutaneous respiration).
    • Fish: Breathes dissolved oxygen in water through comb-like, highly vascularised gills.
    • Frogs: Dual breathing: through moist skin in water and through a pair of lungs on land.
    • Plants: Stomata on leaves and lenticels on bark for gaseous exchange; root hairs absorb oxygen from air spaces between soil particles.
NCERT Intext Questions & Scientific Enquiries
Page 70 — Intext Q1 (Boojho's Query)
Boojho wondered why a person breathes much faster and deeper after running a 100-metre race.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Scientific Explanation:
1. High Energy Demand: Vigorous physical running requires immense muscular energy. To meet this demand, muscle cells consume oxygen at a rapid rate to break down glucose into ATP.
2. Oxygen Debt & Lactic Acid: Because oxygen supply cannot keep up with high consumption, muscles temporarily respire anaerobically, producing lactic acid, which causes muscle fatigue.
3. Breathing Rate Acceleration: To repay this 'oxygen debt' and oxidize the accumulated lactic acid into harmless CO₂ and water, the respiratory control center in the brain commands the diaphragm and rib muscles to breathe much faster and deeper.

Page 71 — Intext Q2 (Paheli's Query)
Paheli wants to know why we sneeze when we inhale a lot of dust-laden or pepper-filled air.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Protective Sneezing Reflex:
1. The nasal cavity is lined with fine hairs (cilia) and sticky mucus designed to trap foreign particulate matter.
2. When we inhale air heavily laden with fine dust, smoke particles, or irritant molecules (like capsaicin in pepper), some particles bypass the nasal hairs and irritate the sensitive mucosal lining of the nasal passage.
3. Sensory nerve receptors trigger a violent, involuntary reflex: the respiratory muscles suddenly contract and forcefully blast out a high-velocity jet of air through the nose and mouth.
4. This explosive expulsion clears the irritating foreign particles from the nasal airway, ensuring that only clean, filtered air reaches the delicate lungs.

Q1
Why does an athlete breathe faster and deeper than usual after finishing the race?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. High Energy & Oxygen Debt: During a fast running race, the athlete's leg muscle cells demand immense amounts of energy. Since the oxygen supplied through normal breathing is insufficient, muscle cells respire anaerobically, producing lactic acid.
2. Repaying the Oxygen Debt: After crossing the finish line, the athlete must breathe much faster and deeper to inhale extra oxygen (repaying the "oxygen debt"). This excess oxygen is transported by blood to the muscle cells to completely oxidise and breakdown the accumulated lactic acid into harmless carbon dioxide and water, relieving muscular fatigue.

Q2
List the similarities and differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Similarities:
  • In both types, glucose serves as the primary cellular fuel substrate to produce usable energy.
  • Both processes take place inside living cells through enzyme-catalysed biochemical reactions.
  • Both types generate energy stored chemically in ATP molecules.
Differences:
FeatureAerobic RespirationAnaerobic Respiration
Presence of Oxygen Occurs strictly in the presence of oxygen (O₂). Occurs in the absence of oxygen.
Breakdown of Glucose Complete oxidation of glucose molecules. Incomplete/partial breakdown of glucose.
End Products Carbon dioxide (CO₂) and Water (H₂O). Ethyl alcohol + CO₂ (in yeast) OR Lactic acid (in human muscles).
Energy Yield Releases a large amount of energy (~38 ATP per glucose molecule). Releases very little energy (only 2 ATP per glucose molecule).
Occurrence Most plants, animals, and human cells. Yeast, certain bacteria, and fatigued human muscle cells.

Q3
Why do we often sneeze when we inhale a lot of dust-laden air?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
1. Nasal Defence Mechanism: The human nasal cavity is lined with fine hairs (cilia) and sticky mucus designed to trap airborne dust particles, pollen, smoke, and microbes.
2. Irritation & Reflex Action: When we inhale air laden with heavy dust, some particles bypass the nasal hairs and directly irritate the delicate, sensitive inner mucosal lining of the nasal cavity.
3. Sneeze Expulsion: This irritation instantly triggers an involuntary protective reflex known as a sneeze. A forceful blast of air is expelled through the nose and mouth, violently dislodging and expelling the foreign irritant particles so that only clean, filtered air enters the delicate lungs.

Q4
Take three test-tubes. Fill 3/4th of each with water. Label them A, B and C. Keep a snail in test-tube A, a water plant in test-tube B and in C, keep snail and plant both. Which test-tube would have the highest concentration of CO₂?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Test-tube A will have the highest concentration of Carbon Dioxide (CO₂).

Scientific Analysis:
  • In Test-tube A (Snail only): The snail performs only respiration; it continuously absorbs dissolved oxygen (O₂) and releases carbon dioxide (CO₂) into the water. Since there is no green plant to utilise the CO₂, carbon dioxide accumulates progressively, reaching the highest concentration.
  • In Test-tube B (Water Plant only): The aquatic plant carries out both respiration and photosynthesis. In the presence of light, the rate of photosynthesis is far higher than respiration; the plant rapidly consumes CO₂ from the water to make food, resulting in the lowest CO₂ level.
  • In Test-tube C (Snail + Water Plant): The CO₂ exhaled by the snail is actively absorbed and utilised by the water plant for photosynthesis, while the O₂ released by the plant is used by the snail for respiration. A biological equilibrium is maintained, so CO₂ concentration remains moderate.

Q9
The mountaineers carry oxygen with them because: (a) At an altitude of more than 5 km there is no air. (b) The amount of air available to a person is less than that available on the ground. (c) The temperature of air is higher than that on the ground. (d) The pressure of air is higher than that on the ground.
1 Mark
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Correct Option: (b) The amount of air available to a person is less than that available on the ground.

Scientific Explanation: As altitude increases, atmospheric density and atmospheric pressure drop significantly. The air becomes thin (rarefied), meaning there are fewer oxygen molecules per unit volume of air. To prevent hypoxia (oxygen deficiency in tissues and brain), mountaineers must carry portable compressed oxygen cylinders.

Case Study: Investigating Exhaled Air with Lime Water
Experiment: Rohan set up a clean glass test tube filled with freshly prepared clear lime water [calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂]. Using a clean plastic straw, he gently blew his exhaled breath into the lime water for about 30 seconds.

(i) What change in appearance of the lime water is observed?
(ii) Write the chemical equation explaining this observation.
(iii) What does this test prove regarding the composition of exhaled air compared to inhaled air?

(i) Observation: The clear lime water turns cloudy and milky white.

(ii) Chemical Equation: Carbon dioxide in exhaled breath chemically reacts with calcium hydroxide to form an insoluble white precipitate of calcium carbonate (CaCO₃):
Ca(OH)₂ (aq) [Lime water] + CO₂ (g) [In exhaled breath] ⎯⎯→ CaCO₃ (s) [White precipitate] + H₂O (l)

(iii) Scientific Conclusion: Inhaled air contains only 0.04% carbon dioxide, whereas exhaled air contains approximately 4.4% carbon dioxide. This proves conclusively that cellular respiration produces CO₂ as an excretory metabolic byproduct released via the lungs.

⚡ Assertion & Reason (HOTS)
Assertion (A): Taking a hot water bath or getting an oil massage provides quick relief from painful muscle cramps after a strenuous gym workout.
Reason (R): Hot water baths improve systemic blood circulation, which enhances oxygen delivery to muscle cells to completely oxidise accumulated lactic acid into carbon dioxide and water.

Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Scientific Explanation: Muscular cramps stem from the intracellular accumulation of lactic acid under anaerobic conditions. Heat from a warm bath dilates blood vessels (vasodilation), increasing blood flow and flushing muscles with fresh oxygen-rich blood, enabling aerobic enzymes to convert lactic acid back into CO₂ and H₂O.
