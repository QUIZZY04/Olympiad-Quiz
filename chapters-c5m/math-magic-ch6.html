The Cat and the Mouse game: The mouse starts at 14 and jumps 2 steps at a time. The cat starts at 3 and jumps 3 steps at a time. If the mouse reaches 28, it can hide safely in its hole. (a) On which steps does the mouse jump? (b) On which steps does the cat jump? (c) Can the mouse reach safety without being caught?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
(a) Mouse's path (starting at 14, +2 each jump):
14, 16, 18, 20, 22, 24, 26, 28 (It takes 7 jumps to reach 28).

Q4 Textbook Exercise
Draw the factor tree for the number 60 in two different ways, and write down its prime factorization.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working

Prime Factorization of 60:

Method 1 (Starting with 6 × 10):
60 → $6 \times 10$
$6 → 2 \times 3$
$10 → 2 \times 5$
Prime factors: $\mathbf{2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5}$.

Method 2 (Starting with 4 × 15):
60 → $4 \times 15$
$4 → 2 \times 2$
$15 → 3 \times 5$
Prime factors: $\mathbf{2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5}$.

Conclusion: Both trees resolve to the unique prime factorization: $\mathbf{2^2 \times 3 \times 5}$.

CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct branch structure of Method 1 1.5 Marks
Correct branch structure of Method 2 1.5 Marks
Final prime factorization: 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 1 Mark

Q8 In-Text Activity • Investigation & Mathematical Patterns
In 'Be My Multiple, I'll be Your Factor', analyze the following arithmetic progression: The first term is 98 and each subsequent term increases by a fixed constant of 12. Find the next three terms (110, 122, 134) and determine the mathematical rule for the $n$-th term.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Investigation:
1. Given initial term $T_1 = \mathbf{98}$.
2. Common difference $d = \mathbf{12}$.
3. Term 2: 98 + 12 = 110.
4. Term 3: 110 + 12 = 122.
5. Term 4: 122 + 12 = 134.
6. General Rule: $T_n = 98 + (n - 1) \times 12$. Successive addition of 12 maintains uniform spacing on the number line.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate calculation of terms (110, 122, 134) 1.5 M
Valid statement of rule (+12) and progression formula 1.5 M

Q10 Measurement & Unit Conversion • Area, Perimeter & Volume
A rectangular courtyard described in 'Be My Multiple, I'll be Your Factor' has length $L = 11\text{ m}$ and width $B = 5\text{ m}$.
(a) Find the perimeter of the boundary fencing.
(b) Calculate the total area enclosed.
(c) If 1 square metre of turfing costs ₹25, what is the total cost of carpeting the floor?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Geometric Steps:
• (a) Perimeter: $P = 2 \times (L + B) = 2 \times (11 + 5) = 2 \times 16 = \mathbf{32\text{ m}}$.
• (b) Area: $A = L \times B = 11 \times 5 = \mathbf{55\text{ sq m}}$.
• (c) Cost of Turfing: Total area × Rate = 55 × ₹25 = ₹1375.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Formula & calculation of Perimeter = 32 m 1.0 M
Formula & calculation of Area = 55 sq m 1.0 M
Cost computation: 55 × ₹25 = ₹1375 1.0 M

Q12 HOTS & Mathematical Reasoning • Algebraic & Spatial Puzzles
HOTS Challenge from 'Be My Multiple, I'll be Your Factor': A mystery number $N$ is multiplied by 4. When 16 is subtracted from the product, the final value obtained is 148. Find the mystery number using inverse operations and verify your answer step-by-step.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Inverse Operations & Algebraic Proof:
1. Mathematical sentence: $(4 \times N) - 16 = 148$.
2. Undo subtraction by adding 16: 4 \times N = 148 + 16 = 164$.
3. Undo multiplication by dividing by 4: $N = \frac{164}{4} = \mathbf{41}$.
4. Verification: (41 × 4) - 16 = 164 - 16 = 148. Proof is verified!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Setting up inverse relation: 4N = 164 1.5 M
Correct mystery number N = 41 with full verification 1.5 M
