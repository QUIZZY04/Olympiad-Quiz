Q1 Textbook Exercise • The Mouse and the Cat Game
The cat is on step 3 and can jump 3 steps at a time. The mouse is on step 15 and can jump 2 steps at a time. (a) What steps will the cat land on? (b) What steps will the mouse land on? (c) Will the cat catch the mouse before the mouse reaches the safety hole at step 28?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Jump Sequences:
• (a) Cat steps (multiples of 3 starting from 3): 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27.
• (b) Mouse steps (adding 2 starting from 15): 15, 17, 19, 21, 23, 25, 27, 29 (safe in hole).
• (c) At step 21: The cat reaches 21 after 6 jumps (3→6→9→12→15→18→21). The mouse reaches 21 after 3 jumps (15→17→19→21). Because the mouse was already at 21 earlier, and reaches hole (28) in 7 jumps before the cat can intercept it at the same moment, the mouse escapes safely!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Cat steps list 1 M
Mouse steps list 1 M
Step comparison 1 M
Conclusion: Mouse escapes 1 M

Q4 Textbook Exercise • Tamarind Seeds Puzzle (LCM + Remainder)
Sunita has some tamarind seeds. When she makes groups of 4, 5, or 6 seeds, 1 seed is always left over. What is the smallest number of seeds Sunita can have?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
LCM Calculation:
1. Find the LCM of 4, 5, and 6:
- Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48, 52, 56, 60...
- Multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 55, 60...
- Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54, 60...
- LCM(4, 5, 6) = 60.
2. Since 1 seed is left over in each case, add 1 to the LCM:
Total seeds = 60 + 1 = 61 seeds.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
LCM of 4, 5, 6 is 60 2 M
Adding 1 remainder = 61 1 M
Q5 Textbook Exercise • Bangle Arrangements (Factors)
Ammini has 18 bangles. She wants to arrange them in equal rows. List all possible row sizes she can make such that no bangles are left over.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Factors of 18:
Any arrangement with equal rows must divide 18 completely without remainder.
• 1 row of 18 bangles (1 × 18)
• 2 rows of 9 bangles (2 × 9)
• 3 rows of 6 bangles (3 × 6)
• 6 rows of 3 bangles (6 × 3)
• 9 rows of 2 bangles (9 × 2)
• 18 rows of 1 bangle (18 × 1)
The possible group/row sizes are: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, and 18.

All 6 factor sizes listed 1.5 M
Q6 Textbook Exercise • Factor Tree

[3 Marks]

Factor Trees for 24:
• Tree 1: Start with 24 = 2 × 12 → 12 = 2 × 6 → 6 = 2 × 3.
• Tree 2: Start with 24 = 4 × 6 → 4 = 2 × 2 and 6 = 2 × 3.

Q8 Textbook Exercise • Tiling Path Problem (HCF)
A garden path is 90 feet long. Sheena wants to tile the path without cutting any tile. She has tiles of sizes 2 feet, 3 feet, and 5 feet. Which of these tile sizes can be used to tile the entire path completely?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Divisibility Test:
• 90 ÷ 2 = 45 (divides evenly, so 2-foot tiles can be used: 45 tiles).
• 90 ÷ 3 = 30 (divides evenly, so 3-foot tiles can be used: 30 tiles).
• 90 ÷ 5 = 18 (divides evenly, so 5-foot tiles can be used: 18 tiles).
All three tile sizes (2 ft, 3 ft, and 5 ft) can tile the path completely without cutting!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Divisibility by 2, 3, 5 checked 2 M
Conclusion that all three can be used 1 M
Q9 Textbook Exercise • Prime and Composite Numbers
(a) Define Prime Number and Composite Number. (b) Which is the smallest prime number? (c) Which is the only even prime number? (d) Is 1 a prime number or composite number?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Definitions and Facts:
• (a) Definitions: A Prime Number has exactly two distinct factors (1 and itself). A Composite Number has more than two factors.
• (b) Smallest prime number: 2.
• (c) Only even prime number: 2.
• (d) Number 1: 1 has only one factor (itself). Therefore, 1 is neither prime nor composite.

Q16 Textbook Exercise • Divisibility Rule for 3 and 9
(a) State the divisibility rule for 3. (b) Test if 4,572 is divisible by 3. (c) Is 4,572 also divisible by 9?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Divisibility Tests:
• (a) Rule for 3: A number is divisible by 3 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 3.
• (b) Sum of digits of 4,572: 4 + 5 + 7 + 2 = 18. Since 18 is divisible by 3 (18 ÷ 3 = 6), 4,572 is divisible by 3.
• (c) Divisible by 9: The sum of digits is 18. Since 18 is divisible by 9 (18 ÷ 9 = 2), 4,572 is also divisible by 9.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme

Divisibility by 9 verified 1 M

Q22 Textbook Exercise • Perfect Numbers
A number is called a 'perfect number' if the sum of all its proper factors (excluding itself) equals the number. Show that 6 is a perfect number.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Factors of 6:
• Factors of 6 are 1, 2, 3, and 6.
• Proper factors (excluding 6) = 1, 2, 3.
• Sum of proper factors = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6.
Since the sum equals the number itself, 6 is a perfect number.

Q24 Textbook Exercise • Square Numbers as Multiples
Write the first five square numbers and list the number of factors each has. What is unique about the number of factors of square numbers?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Square Numbers & Factor Count:
• 1² = 1 (1 factor: 1) → Odd count.
• 2² = 4 (3 factors: 1, 2, 4) → Odd count.
• 3² = 9 (3 factors: 1, 3, 9) → Odd count.
• 4² = 16 (5 factors: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16) → Odd count.
• 5² = 25 (3 factors: 1, 5, 25) → Odd count.
• Unique Property: All square numbers have an odd number of factors, because the square root pairs with itself. All other numbers have an even number of factors.
