Chapter 6: The Dairy Farm

दुग्धशाला (धारिता, लीटर-मिलीलीटर एवं व्यापारिक गणना) | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Standard units of liquid volume: 1 Litre (L) = 1,000 millilitres (mL).
  • Common fractional benchmarks: 1/2 L = 500 mL, 1/4 L = 250 mL, 3/4 L = 750 mL.
  • Adding and subtracting compound units of Litres and Millilitres.
  • Packaging milk into pouches and cartons; commercial billing at given rates per litre.
  • (a) True: Since $6 = 2 \times 3$, any number divisible by 6 is divisible by both of its factors (2 and 3).
  • (b) False: 1 has only one factor (itself). A prime number must have exactly two distinct factors (1 and the number itself).
  • (c) True: 1 is a factor of every integer, hence it is always the smallest common factor.

Q1 Textbook Exercise
A dairy cooperative farm has 12 cows. Each cow yields 8 litres 250 mL of milk daily.
(a) What is the daily milk yield of each cow in millilitres?
(b) What is the total milk collected from all 12 cows in one day (in litres and millilitres)?
(c) If milk is sold at ₹56 per litre, what is the farm's total daily income from milk?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
(a) Yield in Millilitres: 8 L 250 mL = 8,250 mL.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Part (a): 8,250 mL 1 Mark
Part (b): 99 Litres 1.5 Marks
Part (c): ₹5,544 1.5 Marks

Q6 In-Text Activity • Investigation & Mathematical Patterns
In 'The Dairy Farm', analyze the following arithmetic progression: The first term is 98 and each subsequent term increases by a fixed constant of 12. Find the next three terms (110, 122, 134) and determine the mathematical rule for the $n$-th term.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Investigation:
1. Given initial term $T_1 = \mathbf{98}$.
2. Common difference $d = \mathbf{12}$.
3. Term 2: 98 + 12 = 110.
4. Term 3: 110 + 12 = 122.
5. Term 4: 122 + 12 = 134.
6. General Rule: $T_n = 98 + (n - 1) \times 12$. Successive addition of 12 maintains uniform spacing on the number line.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate calculation of terms (110, 122, 134) 1.5 M
Valid statement of rule (+12) and progression formula 1.5 M

Q8 Measurement & Unit Conversion • Area, Perimeter & Volume
A rectangular courtyard described in 'The Dairy Farm' has length $L = 11\text{ m}$ and width $B = 5\text{ m}$.
(a) Find the perimeter of the boundary fencing.
(b) Calculate the total area enclosed.
(c) If 1 square metre of turfing costs ₹25, what is the total cost of carpeting the floor?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Geometric Steps:
• (a) Perimeter: $P = 2 \times (L + B) = 2 \times (11 + 5) = 2 \times 16 = \mathbf{32\text{ m}}$.
• (b) Area: $A = L \times B = 11 \times 5 = \mathbf{55\text{ sq m}}$.
• (c) Cost of Turfing: Total area × Rate = 55 × ₹25 = ₹1375.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Formula & calculation of Perimeter = 32 m 1.0 M
Formula & calculation of Area = 55 sq m 1.0 M
Cost computation: 55 × ₹25 = ₹1375 1.0 M

Q10 HOTS & Mathematical Reasoning • Algebraic & Spatial Puzzles
HOTS Challenge from 'The Dairy Farm': A mystery number $N$ is multiplied by 4. When 16 is subtracted from the product, the final value obtained is 148. Find the mystery number using inverse operations and verify your answer step-by-step.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Inverse Operations & Algebraic Proof:
1. Mathematical sentence: $(4 \times N) - 16 = 148$.
2. Undo subtraction by adding 16: 4 \times N = 148 + 16 = 164$.
3. Undo multiplication by dividing by 4: $N = \frac{164}{4} = \mathbf{41}$.
4. Verification: (41 × 4) - 16 = 164 - 16 = 148. Proof is verified!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Setting up inverse relation: 4N = 164 1.5 M
Correct mystery number N = 41 with full verification 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Competency Question: Community Milk Tea Stall Procurement

A tea vendor makes 1 cup of special masala chai using 75 mL of milk. On Sunday, he prepares 400 cups of tea. Milk is bought in 1-litre bottles costing ₹62 each.

Analytical Task: (a) How much milk in litres is needed for 400 cups?
(b) How many whole 1-litre bottles must he buy, and how much will he spend?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Suggested Model Solution:
(a) Total Milk: 400 × 75 mL = 30,000 mL = 30 Litres.
(b) Bottles and Cost: 30 bottles needed ⇒ 30 × ₹62 = ₹1,860.
