Chapter 12: Smart Charts

स्मार्ट चार्ट (चपाती चार्ट / पाई चार्ट, टैली चिह्न एवं पेड़ आरेख) | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Tally marks for rapid counting: 5 is represented by four vertical lines and one cross slash (||||).
  • Chapati Chart (Pie Chart / Circle Graph): Representing portions of a whole as sectors (1/2, 1/4, 1/8).
  • Growth bar graphs: Tracking seed/plant growth over consecutive days.
  • Family trees and generation tracking.
  • Total students: 40.
  • (a) Cricket: $\frac{1}{2} \times 40 = \mathbf{20\text{ students}}$.
  • (b) Football: $\frac{1}{4} \times 40 = \mathbf{10\text{ students}}$.
  • (c) Badminton: $\frac{1}{4} \times 40 = \mathbf{10\text{ students}}$.

Q1 Textbook Exercise • Tally Marks (Traffic Survey)
Yamini did a project 'Animals and Birds'. She stood on the road for half an hour and noted passing vehicles using tally marks: Cycle (28), Car (12), Auto-rickshaw (18), Bus (15), Cycle-rickshaw (24), Truck (6).
(a) Draw the tally marks for Cycle (28) and Bus (15) using NCERT box notation.
(b) Write the total number of vehicles that passed by in half an hour.
(c) Which vehicle was observed the most?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Survey Analysis:
• (a) Tally Marks (each complete 5-box is 4 sides + 1 diagonal):
- Cycle (28): 5 complete boxes of five + 3 lines (5 × 5 + 3 = 28).
- Bus (15): 3 complete boxes of five (5 × 3 = 15).
• (b) Total vehicles: 28 + 12 + 18 + 15 + 24 + 6 = 103 vehicles.
• (c) Most observed vehicle: Cycle (28).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Tally mark representation 1.5 M
Total count: 103 vehicles 1.5 M
Most observed: Cycle 1 M
Q2 Textbook Exercise • Chapati Chart (Pie Chart / Circle Graph)
In a class of 40 students, teacher made a circle chart of their favorite activities after school:
• 1/2 of the children like playing sports.
• 1/4 of the children like watching TV.
• 1/4 of the children like reading books.
(a) How many children like playing sports?
(b) How many children like watching TV?
(c) How many children like reading books?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Chapati Chart Calculations (Total = 40 students):
• (a) Sports (1/2): 1/2 of 40 = 40 ÷ 2 = 20 students.
• (b) Watching TV (1/4): 1/4 of 40 = 40 ÷ 4 = 10 students.
• (c) Reading books (1/4): 1/4 of 40 = 40 ÷ 4 = 10 students.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Sports: 20 1 M
TV: 10 1 M
Books: 10 1 M
Q3 Textbook Exercise • Rabbit Population Growth Table
Rabbits were brought to an island. The table shows their numbers each year:
• Year 0: 10 rabbits
• Year 1: 18 rabbits (almost double)
• Year 2: 32 rabbits
• Year 3: 58 rabbits
• Year 4: 108 rabbits
(a) Is the rabbit population growing by adding a fixed number or by multiplying? (b) Predict the approximate number of rabbits in Year 5.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Population Trend:
• (a) Growth nature: The population is multiplying / almost doubling each year (each year's count is a little less than double the previous year: 10×2-2=18, 18×2-4=32, 32×2-6=58, 58×2-8=108).
• (b) Year 5 prediction: Approximately double of 108 minus 10 ≈ 216 - 10 = around 200 to 210 rabbits.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Multiplicative / doubling trend identified 1.5 M
Year 5 prediction (approx 200-210) 1.5 M
Q4 Textbook Exercise • Plant Growth Bar Graph
A plant's height was measured every 4 days:
• Day 0: 0 cm
• Day 4: 1.4 cm
• Day 8: 5.3 cm
• Day 12: 9.5 cm
• Day 16: 10.9 cm
• Day 20: 11.1 cm
(a) Between which days did the plant grow the fastest? (b) What was the total growth of the plant in 20 days?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Growth Intervals:
• Day 0 to 4: 1.4 - 0 = 1.4 cm.
• Day 4 to 8: 5.3 - 1.4 = 3.9 cm.
• Day 8 to 12: 9.5 - 5.3 = 4.2 cm (fastest!).
• Day 12 to 16: 10.9 - 9.5 = 1.4 cm.
• Day 16 to 20: 11.1 - 10.9 = 0.2 cm.
• (a) Fastest growth: Between Day 8 and Day 12 (grew 4.2 cm).
• (b) Total growth: 11.1 - 0 = 11.1 cm.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Interval analysis 1 M
Fastest: Day 8 to 12 1 M
Total growth: 11.1 cm 1 M

Q7 Textbook Exercise • Favorite Ice Cream Pictograph
In a pictograph, one ice cream cone symbol 🍦 represents 10 students. The table shows: Vanilla: 3 symbols; Chocolate: 5 symbols; Mango: 4 symbols; Strawberry: 2 symbols.
(a) How many students prefer Chocolate ice cream?
(b) How many more students prefer Mango than Strawberry?
(c) What is the total number of students?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Pictograph Calculations (1 symbol = 10 students):
• (a) Chocolate: 5 × 10 = 50 students.
• (b) Mango vs Strawberry: (4 - 2) × 10 = 2 × 10 = 20 more students.
• (c) Total students: (3 + 5 + 4 + 2) × 10 = 14 × 10 = 140 students.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Chocolate: 50 1 M
Mango difference: 20 1 M
Total: 140 1 M

Q10 Textbook Exercise • Tree Plantation Data
Five houses planted saplings in 'Van Mahotsav': House 1 (12), House 2 (18), House 3 (15), House 4 (25), House 5 (10). (a) What is the total number of saplings planted? (b) What is the average number of saplings planted per house?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Calculations:
• (a) Total saplings: 12 + 18 + 15 + 25 + 10 = 80 saplings.
• (b) Average: Total ÷ Number of houses = 80 ÷ 5 = 16 saplings per house.

Q11 Textbook Exercise • Reading Pie Chart Fractions
A circular chart shows: 50% students walk to school, 25% take school bus, 25% ride bicycle. (a) What fraction of students walk? (b) If there are 200 students in the school, how many ride a bicycle?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Fractions & Counts:
• (a) Walking fraction: 50% = 50/100 = 1/2.
• (b) Bicycle riders: 25% = 1/4 of total. 1/4 of 200 = 200 ÷ 4 = 50 students.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Walking: 1/2 1 M
Bicycle: 50 students 1 M

Q14 Textbook Exercise • Constructing a Scale for a Bar Graph
The marks obtained by a student are: English 75, Hindi 80, Maths 95, Science 85, Social Science 70. If you draw a bar graph on a paper with maximum height 10 cm, what scale should you choose for the vertical axis?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Axis Scaling:
• Highest mark = 95 (round up to 100).
• Available height = 10 cm.
• Scale: 100 ÷ 10 = 10 marks per cm. So 1 cm = 10 marks is the ideal scale (the tallest bar for Maths will be 9.5 cm high).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Proposed scale: 1 cm = 10 marks 1 M
Height verification (9.5 cm for 95) 1 M
Q15 Textbook Exercise • Tally Box Notation
Explain how NCERT Class 5 tally marks are drawn using the 5-line box method.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
NCERT Box Tally Method:
• 1 = 1 vertical line (|)
• 2 = 2 lines forming a right angle (L)
• 3 = 3 sides of a square (U)
• 4 = A complete square (4 sides: 🗆)
• 5 = A square with a diagonal slash crossing it (🗎).
This creates easy-to-read bundles of 5.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
1 to 4 steps explained 1.5 M
5th diagonal slash completing bundle 1.5 M

Q21 Textbook Exercise • Class Strength Comparison
Section A has 35 students and Section B has 45 students. (a) What is the ratio of Section A to Section B in simplest form? (b) How many students should transfer from Section B to Section A to make both sections equal?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Calculations:
• (a) Ratio: 35 : 45 = (35÷5) : (45÷5) = 7 : 9.
• (b) Equal sections: Total = 35 + 45 = 80 students. Each section needs 80 ÷ 2 = 40 students.
Transfer needed = 45 - 40 = 5 students.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Ratio 7:9 1.5 M
Transfer 5 students 1.5 M

Q24 Textbook Exercise • Importance of Smart Charts
Why are charts and graphs called 'Smart Charts'? Give two reasons why displaying data in charts is better than raw number lists.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Advantages of Smart Charts:
1. Instant Visual Understanding: Charts allow readers to grasp trends, comparisons, and peaks at a single glance without reading long tables.
2. Easy Comparison: Tall bars and large pie slices immediately show which category is largest or smallest without performing manual calculations.

Visual clarity explained 1 M
