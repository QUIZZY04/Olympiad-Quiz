Chapter 9: Coconut Farm

नारियल का बाग (गुणन व्यूह, गुणनखंड वृक्ष एवं विभाजन) | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Rectangular arrays: Visualizing multiplication as Rows × Columns.
  • Box/Grid Multiplication Method: Breaking two-digit numbers by expanded form.
  • Factor Trees: Finding prime factorizations and factor pairs of composite numbers.
  • Division with remainder: Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder.
  • (a) Number of faces: An open box without a top lid has 1 base + 4 side walls = 5 square faces.
  • (b) Area of 1 face: $5\text{ cm} \times 5\text{ cm} = 25\text{ cm}^2$.
  • Total surface area: $5 \times 25\text{ cm}^2 = \mathbf{125\text{ cm}^2}$.

Q1 Coconut Orchard Planting • Multiplication Grid
A coconut grove has 24 rows of trees, with 18 trees in each row.
(a) Find the total number of coconut trees in the orchard using the box/grid multiplication method.
(b) Verify using the standard column multiplication algorithm.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Multiplication Working (24 × 18):
• (a) Box Method: Split 24 into 20 + 4, and 18 into 10 + 8:
- 20 × 10 = 200
- 4 × 10 = 40
- 20 × 8 = 160
- 4 × 8 = 32
- Sum = 200 + 40 + 160 + 32 = 432 trees.
• (b) Column Method: 24 × 8 = 192; 24 × 10 = 240; 192 + 240 = 432 trees. Both methods match!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Box method working 1.5 M
Column verification = 432 trees 1.5 M
Q2 Harvest Yield • Coconut Packing in Sacks
A farmer plucks 2,160 coconuts. He packs them into sacks of 45 coconuts each.
(a) How many full sacks are packed?
(b) If each sack is sold for ₹1,125, what is his total earnings from selling all the sacks?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Working:
• (a) Sacks Packed: 2,160 ÷ 45 = 48 sacks.
• (b) Total Earnings: 48 sacks × ₹1,125 = ₹54,000.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Sacks packed = 48 1.5 M
Total revenue = ₹54,000 1.5 M
Q3 Coconut Water Stalls • Daily Earnings
A roadside tender coconut vendor buys coconuts from the farm at ₹28 each and sells fresh coconut water at ₹45 each.

(b) If he sells 65 coconuts on Sunday, calculate his total sales revenue and net profit.

Profit & Revenue Calculations:

• (b) Sunday Sales (65 Coconuts):
- Total revenue = 65 × ₹45 = ₹2,925.
- Cost price = 65 × ₹28 = ₹1,820.
- Net profit = 65 × ₹17 = ₹1,105.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Profit per coconut = ₹17 1.0 M
Total revenue = ₹2,925 1.0 M
Net profit = ₹1,105 1.0 M
Q4 Sapling Nursery • Planting in Beds
A school gardener receives 576 plant saplings. He wants to plant them in 18 equal rows.
(a) How many saplings will be planted in each row?
(b) If he had 600 saplings instead, how many saplings would be in each row and how many would be left over?

• (b) 600 Saplings in 18 Rows:

576 ÷ 18 = 32 saplings 1.5 M
600 ÷ 18 = 33 per row with remainder 6 1.5 M

Q6 Multi-digit Multiplication • Distributive Property
Evaluate using the distributive property: $48 \times 25$. Show how you can compute this quickly by thinking of 25 as 100 ÷ 4.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Mental Arithmetic Strategies:
• Method 1 (Distributive): 48 × (20 + 5) = (48 × 20) + (48 × 5) = 960 + 240 = 1,200.
• Method 2 (Quarter of 100): 25 = 100 ÷ 4.
48 × 25 = (48 × 100) ÷ 4 = 4,800 ÷ 4 = 1,200.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme

Q10 Division Verification Algorithm
Divide 1,485 by 12. State the quotient and the remainder, and verify your answer using the formula: $\text{Dividend} = (\text{Divisor} \times \text{Quotient}) + \text{Remainder}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Division & Proof:
1. 1,485 ÷ 12:
• 12 × 100 = 1,200 (Remainder 285)
• 12 × 20 = 240 (Remainder 45)
• 12 × 3 = 36 (Remainder 9)
Quotient = 123, Remainder = 9.
2. Verification:
(12 × 123) + 9 = 1,476 + 9 = 1,485. The division is verified!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Quotient = 123, Remainder = 9 1.5 M
Verification formula step-by-step 1.5 M

Q12 Plantation Layout • Area and Spacing
Coconut trees are planted in a square grid with a spacing of 7 metres between trees.
(a) How many trees are in a row of length 84 metres (including trees at both ends)?
(b) If the orchard is a square of 84 m × 84 m, what is the total number of trees planted?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Grid Spacing:
• (a) Trees per Row: Number of 7-metre intervals = 84 ÷ 7 = 12 intervals.
- Trees per row = Intervals + 1 (posts and spaces rule) = 12 + 1 = 13 trees.
• (b) Total Trees in Square Orchard: 13 rows × 13 trees/row = 169 trees.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
12 intervals + 1 = 13 trees per row 1.5 M
Total trees = 13 × 13 = 169 1.5 M

Q17 Division with Zero in Quotient
Solve: $4,208 \div 4$. Show your long division clearly and explain why zero must be written in the hundreds/tens place.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Long Division Steps:
• Divide thousands: 4 ÷ 4 = 1 (Remainder 0).
• Bring down 2: 2 cannot be divided by 4 (2 < 4). We must write 0 in the quotient.
• Bring down 0 to make 20: 20 ÷ 4 = 5 (Remainder 0).
• Bring down 8: 8 ÷ 4 = 2 (Remainder 0).
Quotient = 1,052, Remainder = 0.
Explanation: Whenever the brought-down digit is smaller than the divisor, a 0 is placed in that place value of the quotient before bringing down the next digit.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct quotient = 1,052 1.5 M
Clear explanation for placing 0 1.5 M

Q19 HOTS • Coconut Division Riddle
A farmer divided a heap of coconuts equally among his 4 sons, and 2 coconuts were left over. When he divided the same heap among 5 neighbours, 2 coconuts were still left over. What is the smallest possible number of coconuts in the heap (greater than 10)?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
LCM and Remainder Principle:
1. The number leaves a remainder of 2 when divided by both 4 and 5.
2. LCM of 4 and 5 = 4 × 5 = 20.
3. The number must be of the form: $\text{LCM}(4, 5) \times k + 2 = 20k + 2$.
4. For $k = 1$: Number = 20 + 2 = 22 coconuts.
5. Verification:
- 22 ÷ 4 = 5 with remainder 2.
- 22 ÷ 5 = 4 with remainder 2.
The smallest possible number of coconuts is 22.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
LCM(4, 5) = 20 1.5 M
Smallest number = 22 with verification 1.5 M

Q23 True or False with Arithmetic Reason
State True or False with reasoning:
(a) If a number is divisible by 10, its ones digit must be 0.
(b) $25 \times 48$ is the same as $100 \times 12$.
(c) The remainder in any division problem can be equal to or greater than the divisor.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Justifications:
• (a) True: All multiples of 10 end with the digit 0.
• (b) True: $25 \times 48 = 25 \times (4 \times 12) = (25 \times 4) \times 12 = 100 \times 12 = 1,200$. Both equal 1,200.
• (c) False: The remainder must strictly be less than the divisor. If it were equal or greater, another division step could be carried out.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Part (a): True with place value reason 1.0 M
Part (b): True with associative property 1.0 M
Part (c): False (remainder must be < divisor) 1.0 M
Q24 Comprehensive Orchard Harvest Review

• (b) Market Sale:
- Domestic share = 10% of 11,400 = 1,140 coconuts.
- Coconuts sold = 11,400 - 1,140 = 10,260 coconuts.
• (c) Gross Revenue: 10,260 × ₹20 = ₹2,05,200.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Total = 11,400 coconuts 1.0 M
Coconuts sold = 10,260 1.0 M
Gross revenue = ₹2,05,200 1.0 M
