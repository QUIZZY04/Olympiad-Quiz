Chapter 2: Shapes and Angles

आकृतियाँ और कोण (माचिस की तीलियाँ, कोण टेस्टर एवं डिग्री घड़ी) | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Open and closed geometric shapes made of matchsticks (triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons).
  • Understanding that shapes with the same number of sides can have different shapes depending on their interior angles.
  • Angle tester (two strips joined by a split pin) for right angles, less than right angle (acute), and more than right angle (obtuse).
  • The Degree Clock: identifying 45°, 90°, 180° through hands and turns.
  • (a) 9:00: The hour hand is at 9 and the minute hand is at 12. Angle = $3 \times 30^\circ = 90^\circ$ → Right Angle.
  • (b) 3:30: The hour hand is halfway between 3 and 4, and minute hand is at 6. Angle = $75^\circ$ → Less than a right angle (Acute Angle).
  • (c) 7:15: The hour hand is just past 7 and minute hand is at 3. Angle = approx $127.5^\circ$ → More than a right angle (Obtuse Angle).
  • (d) 2:00: The hour hand is at 2 and minute hand is at 12. Angle = $2 \times 30^\circ = 60^\circ$ → Less than a right angle (Acute Angle).
  • (e) 6:00: The hour hand is at 6 and minute hand is at 12. Angle = $180^\circ$ → More than a right angle (Straight Angle).
  • M: 3 angles (all 3 acute, less than right angle).
  • E: 4 right angles (at the top, middle top, middle bottom, bottom intersections).
  • E: 4 right angles.
  • N: 2 angles (both acute, less than right angle).
  • A: 3 acute angles (at the apex and crossbar) + 2 obtuse angles (below crossbar).

Rohini and Mohini made shapes using 6 matchsticks each. Rohini's shape was a regular closed hexagon, while Mohini's shape had different side angles. (a) Is Mohini's shape a closed shape? Q5 Textbook Exercise
In the name 'MEENA', count the total number of: (a) Right angles, (b) Angles less than a right angle, (c) Angles more than a right angle.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working

Analyzing straight line segment angles in uppercase printed letters:

  • M: 3 angles (all 3 acute, less than right angle).
  • E: 4 right angles (at the top, middle top, middle bottom, bottom intersections).
  • E: 4 right angles.
  • N: 2 angles (both acute, less than right angle).
  • A: 3 acute angles (at the apex and crossbar) + 2 obtuse angles (below crossbar).

Total Counts:
(a) Right angles = $4 + 4 = \mathbf{8\text{ Right angles}}$.
(b) Angles less than right angle = $3 + 2 + 3 = \mathbf{8\text{ Acute angles}}$.
(c) Angles more than right angle = $\mathbf{2\text{ Obtuse angles}}$ (in A).

Q8 In-Text Activity • Investigation & Mathematical Patterns
In 'Shapes and Angles', analyze the following arithmetic progression: The first term is 38 and each subsequent term increases by a fixed constant of 8. Find the next three terms (46, 54, 62) and determine the mathematical rule for the $n$-th term.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Investigation:
1. Given initial term $T_1 = \mathbf{38}$.
2. Common difference $d = \mathbf{8}$.
3. Term 2: 38 + 8 = 46.
4. Term 3: 46 + 8 = 54.
5. Term 4: 54 + 8 = 62.
6. General Rule: $T_n = 38 + (n - 1) \times 8$. Successive addition of 8 maintains uniform spacing on the number line.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate calculation of terms (46, 54, 62) 1.5 M
Valid statement of rule (+8) and progression formula 1.5 M

Q10 Measurement & Unit Conversion • Area, Perimeter & Volume
A rectangular courtyard described in 'Shapes and Angles' has length $L = 13\text{ m}$ and width $B = 5\text{ m}$.
(a) Find the perimeter of the boundary fencing.
(b) Calculate the total area enclosed.
(c) If 1 square metre of turfing costs ₹25, what is the total cost of carpeting the floor?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Geometric Steps:
• (a) Perimeter: $P = 2 \times (L + B) = 2 \times (13 + 5) = 2 \times 18 = \mathbf{36\text{ m}}$.
• (b) Area: $A = L \times B = 13 \times 5 = \mathbf{65\text{ sq m}}$.
• (c) Cost of Turfing: Total area × Rate = 65 × ₹25 = ₹1625.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Formula & calculation of Perimeter = 36 m 1.0 M
Formula & calculation of Area = 65 sq m 1.0 M
Cost computation: 65 × ₹25 = ₹1625 1.0 M

Q12 HOTS & Mathematical Reasoning • Algebraic & Spatial Puzzles
HOTS Challenge from 'Shapes and Angles': A mystery number $N$ is multiplied by 4. When 16 is subtracted from the product, the final value obtained is 116. Find the mystery number using inverse operations and verify your answer step-by-step.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Inverse Operations & Algebraic Proof:
1. Mathematical sentence: $(4 \times N) - 16 = 116$.
2. Undo subtraction by adding 16: 4 \times N = 116 + 16 = 132$.
3. Undo multiplication by dividing by 4: $N = \frac{132}{4} = \mathbf{33}$.
4. Verification: (33 × 4) - 16 = 132 - 16 = 116. Proof is verified!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Setting up inverse relation: 4N = 132 1.5 M
Correct mystery number N = 33 with full verification 1.5 M
