Chapter 10: Symmetrical Designs

सममितीय डिज़ाइन (सममिति रेखाएँ, परावर्तन एवं रंगोली) | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Line of Symmetry (Mirror Line): An imaginary line dividing a figure into two identical mirror halves.
  • Vertical, Horizontal, and Diagonal lines of symmetry.
  • Number of lines of symmetry in regular polygons: Equilateral triangle (3), Square (4), Regular hexagon (6).
  • Rotational symmetry of order 2 (half turn), order 4 (quarter turn), order 6.

Q3 Textbook Exercise
Explain what happens when each of the following regular geometric shapes is rotated by 1/3 turn (120°) and 1/6 turn (60°): (a) Equilateral Triangle, (b) Regular Hexagon.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
  1. (a) Equilateral Triangle:
    Has 3 equal sides and 3 angles of 60°. Full turn $= 360^\circ$.
    • 1/3 Turn ($120^\circ$): The triangle aligns onto itself and looks exactly the same.
    • 1/6 Turn ($60^\circ$): The triangle rotates onto its side and does not look the same.
  2. (b) Regular Hexagon:
    Has 6 equal sides and 6-fold rotational symmetry.
    • 1/6 Turn ($60^\circ$): Aligns onto itself and looks identical.
    • 1/3 Turn ($120^\circ$): Multiple of $60^\circ$, hence also looks identical.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Equilateral triangle behavior at 1/3 and 1/6 turn 2 Marks
Regular hexagon behavior at 1/6 and 1/3 turn 2 Marks

Q5 In-Text Activity • Investigation & Mathematical Patterns
In 'Symmetrical Designs', analyze the following arithmetic progression: The first term is 158 and each subsequent term increases by a fixed constant of 16. Find the next three terms (174, 190, 206) and determine the mathematical rule for the $n$-th term.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Investigation:
1. Given initial term $T_1 = \mathbf{158}$.
2. Common difference $d = \mathbf{16}$.
3. Term 2: 158 + 16 = 174.
4. Term 3: 174 + 16 = 190.
5. Term 4: 190 + 16 = 206.
6. General Rule: $T_n = 158 + (n - 1) \times 16$. Successive addition of 16 maintains uniform spacing on the number line.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate calculation of terms (174, 190, 206) 1.5 M
Valid statement of rule (+16) and progression formula 1.5 M

Q7 Measurement & Unit Conversion • Area, Perimeter & Volume
A rectangular courtyard described in 'Symmetrical Designs' has length $L = 15\text{ m}$ and width $B = 5\text{ m}$.
(a) Find the perimeter of the boundary fencing.
(b) Calculate the total area enclosed.
(c) If 1 square metre of turfing costs ₹25, what is the total cost of carpeting the floor?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Geometric Steps:
• (a) Perimeter: $P = 2 \times (L + B) = 2 \times (15 + 5) = 2 \times 20 = \mathbf{40\text{ m}}$.
• (b) Area: $A = L \times B = 15 \times 5 = \mathbf{75\text{ sq m}}$.
• (c) Cost of Turfing: Total area × Rate = 75 × ₹25 = ₹1875.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Formula & calculation of Perimeter = 40 m 1.0 M
Formula & calculation of Area = 75 sq m 1.0 M
Cost computation: 75 × ₹25 = ₹1875 1.0 M

Q9 HOTS & Mathematical Reasoning • Algebraic & Spatial Puzzles
HOTS Challenge from 'Symmetrical Designs': A mystery number $N$ is multiplied by 4. When 16 is subtracted from the product, the final value obtained is 180. Find the mystery number using inverse operations and verify your answer step-by-step.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Inverse Operations & Algebraic Proof:
1. Mathematical sentence: $(4 \times N) - 16 = 180$.
2. Undo subtraction by adding 16: 4 \times N = 180 + 16 = 196$.
3. Undo multiplication by dividing by 4: $N = \frac{196}{4} = \mathbf{49}$.
4. Verification: (49 × 4) - 16 = 196 - 16 = 180. Proof is verified!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Setting up inverse relation: 4N = 196 1.5 M
Correct mystery number N = 49 with full verification 1.5 M

Competency Question: Traditional Diwali Rangoli Design Symmetry

A Rangoli artist creates an 8-pointed star mandala inside a circle. The motif repeated in each sector is reflected alternately.

Analytical Task: (a) What is the order of rotational symmetry of an 8-pointed star?
(b) By what smallest angle must the mandala be rotated to match itself exactly?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Suggested Model Solution:
(a) Order of Rotational Symmetry: 8.
(b) Smallest Angle: 360°/8 = 45°.
