📌 Key Biological Characteristics of Living Beings
  • Universal Hallmarks of Life:
    • Cellular Organization: All living organisms are composed of microscopic living units called cells (unicellular like Amoeba or multicellular like plants and animals).
    • Nutrition: All living beings need food for energy, growth, and repair. Green plants prepare their own food via photosynthesis (autotrophs); animals consume plants or other animals (heterotrophs).
    • Growth: Irreversible increase in size, mass, and developmental complexity throughout an organism's life cycle.
    • Respiration: The biochemical process of taking in oxygen, breaking down digested food inside cells to release usable energy, and expelling carbon dioxide.
    • Response to Stimuli: Changes in the external environment (light, heat, touch, sound, smell) are stimuli. Living organisms react to these stimuli (e.g., pulling hand away from a hot object, shoot bending towards sunlight, leaves of Mimosa pudica / touch-me-not curling when touched).
    • Excretion: The process of eliminating toxic metabolic waste products (urea, sweat, CO₂) from the body.
    • Reproduction: Producing offspring of their own kind to ensure species continuity across generations (sexual or asexual modes).
    • Definite Lifespan & Death: Every living organism is born, grows, matures, ages, and eventually dies.

NCERT Exercise 10.1: Life Processes & Organ Systems
Q1
Differentiate clearly between breathing and cellular respiration. Describe the specialized respiratory organs in (i) Humans, (ii) Fish, (iii) Earthworms, (iv) Insects, and (v) Green plants.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Breathing vs Cellular Respiration:
• Breathing (External Respiration): A physical process of inhaling oxygen-rich air and exhaling carbon dioxide-rich air using respiratory organs. No energy is released.
• Cellular Respiration (Internal Respiration): A biochemical process inside living cells where oxygen oxidizes glucose to release metabolic energy (ATP) with CO₂ and water as by-products.
Specialized Respiratory Organs across Species:
• (i) Humans: Lungs (gas exchange occurs across alveoli capillaries).
• (ii) Fish: Gills (feather-like vascular filaments extracting dissolved oxygen from water).
• (iii) Earthworms: Moist, slimy skin (oxygen diffuses directly into blood capillaries).
• (iv) Insects (e.g., Cockroach): Spiracles and Tracheal tubes (air enters small lateral pores and travels via internal tubes).
• (v) Green Plants: Microscopic pores called Stomata on leaf surfaces.

NCERT Exercise 10.2: Stimuli, Excretion & Reproduction
Q2
What is meant by 'Response to Stimulus'? Describe two examples each from the animal kingdom and the plant kingdom demonstrating this phenomenon.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Definition:
Any change in an organism's external or internal environment (like sudden light, temperature change, loud noise, touch, chemical scent) that triggers an active behavioural or physiological reaction is a stimulus. The resulting action is the response.
Examples in Animals:
• Cockroaches immediately scurry into dark crevices when kitchen lights are switched on at night (stimulus: bright light).
• A person reflexively withdraws their hand when touching a boiling utensil or stepping on a thorn (stimulus: sharp pain / intense heat).
Examples in Plants:
• Leaves of the Mimosa pudica (Touch-me-not / Chhui-mui) plant fold and droop downward instantly upon mechanical touch.
• Potted plant stems bend towards a window receiving sunlight (Phototropism).

CBSE Competency-Based Question (Biological Inquiry Case Study)
Case Study: Is a Stored Seed Living or Non-Living?
A sack of dry wheat grains remains in a farmer's godown for months without moving, growing, or showing any external activity.
(a) If a thermometer is inserted deep into the center of a large sack of wheat grains, it registers a slightly warmer temperature than ambient room air. Why?
(b) Is a dry seed classified as living or non-living? Explain scientifically.
(c) Why is a cloud or a car not considered a living thing even though both move across distances?
Detailed Analytical Solution & Marking Scheme

(a) Heat Production in Grains:
Dry seeds are in a state of dormancy, but they are not dead. The living embryo inside each seed continues very slow cellular respiration, breaking down stored starches and releasing trace thermal energy, which accumulates in the insulated center of the grain sack.

(b) Living Status of a Seed:
A dry seed is definitely living. It contains a viable embryo possessing cellular structure and genetic code. When provided with water, oxygen, and warmth, it breaks dormancy, respire vigorously, and germinates into a full-grown plant.

(c) Why Clouds and Cars are Non-Living:
To be classified as living, an entity must satisfy all fundamental criteria of life simultaneously. Although a car moves, it requires external fuel, cannot reproduce, lacks cellular organization, and does not grow. Similarly, a cloud moves by wind currents and grows by external accumulation of water droplets, not by internal biological cell division.
