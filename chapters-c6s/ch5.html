Chapter 5: Measurement of Length and Motion

NCERT Curiosity (Class 6) — SI Standard Units of Length, Precautions & Parallax Error, Measuring Curved Lines & Classification of Motion | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Physics Principles & Motion Classifications
  • Need for Standard Units: Ancient non-standard units (handspan, cubit, footstep) varied widely from person to person. To ensure universal consistency, the International System of Units (SI) was adopted in 1960. The SI standard unit of length is the metre (m).
  • Unit Conversions:
    • 1 kilometre (km) = 1,000 metres (m)
    • 1 metre (m) = 100 centimetres (cm)
    • 1 centimetre (cm) = 10 millimetres (mm)
  • Three Golden Rules for Ruler Measurement:
    • Alignment: Place the ruler strictly along the length of the object, parallel to its edge.
    • Handling Broken Edges / Non-zero Mark: If the 0-mark is broken or damaged, start measuring from any full clear mark (e.g., 1.0 cm) and subtract that reading from the final reading at the other end.
    • Correct Eye Position (Avoiding Parallax Error): The eye must be placed vertically directly above the point being measured, not tilted obliquely from the sides.
  • Types of Motion:
    • Rectilinear Motion: An object moving along a straight line path (e.g., Apple falling from tree, Marching soldiers on a straight parade).
    • Circular Motion: An object moving along a circular path at a constant distance from a fixed center (e.g., Blades of a ceiling fan, Hands of a clock, Stone tied to a whirled string).
    • Rotational Motion: An object spinning on its own central axis without shifting its overall location (e.g., Spinning top, Potter's wheel, Earth's daily rotation).
    • Periodic Motion: Motion that repeats itself at equal, regular intervals of time (e.g., Swing of a pendulum, Motion of a child on a swing, Vibration of a plucked guitar string).

NCERT Exercise 5.1: Precise Length Measurements
Q1
While measuring the length of a wooden knitting needle, the reading of the scale at one end is 3.0 cm and at the other end is 33.1 cm. What is the actual length of the needle? Express it in centimetres, millimetres, and metres.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Formula for Measurement with Broken/Shifted Zero:
Actual Length = Reading at final end - Reading at initial end
Calculation in Centimetres (cm):
Initial reading = 3.0 cm
Final reading = 33.1 cm
Actual Length = 33.1 cm - 3.0 cm = 30.1 cm.
Unit Conversions:
• In millimetres (1 cm = 10 mm): 30.1 × 10 = 301 mm.
• In metres (1 m = 100 cm): 30.1 / 100 = 0.301 m.

NCERT Exercise 5.2: Motion Types & Curved Line Measurement
Q2
Explain step-by-step how you can accurately measure the length of a curved line using a piece of thread and a standard centimetre ruler.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Method of Measuring a Curved Line:
1. Take a non-stretchable piece of cotton thread and tie a small knot near one of its ends to serve as the zero mark.
2. Place the knot on one end of the curved line (say Point A).
3. Using your thumb and forefinger, place small segments of the thread strictly along the curve, pressing gently to trace every bend.
4. Once you reach the end of the curved line (Point B), mark that exact point on the thread using a fine pen.
5. Straighten the thread along a standard ruler with the knot placed at 0 cm and read the length corresponding to the ink mark. This reading gives the exact length of the curved line.

Q3
Identify and explain the types of motion observed in: (i) A sewing machine in operation, and (ii) The motion of a moving bicycle on a straight road.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(i) Sewing Machine in Operation:
• Wheel of the machine: Exhibits Circular / Rotational Motion as it turns continuously around a central axle.
• Needle of the machine: Exhibits Periodic Motion (or rectilinear oscillatory motion) moving up and down continuously at regular intervals without changing position.
(ii) Moving Bicycle on a Straight Road:
• The entire bicycle and cyclist: Move forward along a straight path, exhibiting Rectilinear Motion.
• The wheels of the bicycle: Spin around their hub, exhibiting Rotational Motion, while simultaneously rolling forward. Thus, the wheels exhibit a combination of rectilinear and rotational motion.

CBSE Competency-Based Question (Sports & Engineering Case Study)
Case Study: Sports Track Surveying & Rolling Wheel Distance:
A sports coach uses an odometer surveyor's wheel to mark the 400 m running track at a stadium. The circular measuring wheel has a circumference of exactly 1 metre and clicks every time it completes one full rotation.
(a) How many full rotations will the wheel complete when surveying the 400 m track? What type of motion does the wheel execute?
(b) If an athlete runs one complete lap around the 400 m circular track in 60 seconds, what type of motion is the athlete performing?
(c) Why can a tailor's flexible measuring tape measure a person's chest girth accurately while a wooden meter scale cannot?
Detailed Analytical Solution & Marking Scheme

(a) Rotations & Motion Type:
Number of rotations = Total distance / Circumference of 1 rotation = 400 m / 1 m = 400 rotations.
The wheel simultaneously executes Rotational motion (spinning on its central hub) and Curvilinear / Rectilinear motion (advancing along the track).

(b) Athlete's Motion:
The athlete is executing Circular motion (moving along a closed curved trajectory around a center) and, if repeating laps in fixed intervals, Periodic motion.

(c) Reason for Flexible Measuring Tape:
The human chest and waist are curved surfaces. A wooden meter rod is rigid and straight, meaning it cannot bend around curved contours. A tailor's tape is made of flexible fabric/ribbon that conforms smoothly to the body's curved geometry without bending distortion.
