📌 Key Environmental & Physical Concepts of Water
  • Three Physical States of Water:
    • Solid State: Ice, snow, hail, frost (fixed shape and volume below 0 °C).
    • Liquid State: Flowing water in oceans, rivers, lakes, and raindrops (0 °C to 100 °C).
    • Gaseous State: Invisible water vapour in air, steam from boiling water (above 100 °C).
  • Phase Transformation Processes:
    • Evaporation: The slow change of liquid water into gaseous water vapour at any temperature below its boiling point. Rate increases with higher temperature, broader surface area, higher wind speed, and lower humidity.
    • Transpiration: The loss of excess water as water vapour through the microscopic stomata of plant leaves.
    • Condensation: The cooling of warm water vapour in the upper atmosphere into tiny liquid water droplets forming clouds.
    • Precipitation: When cloud droplets coalesce and grow too heavy to remain suspended in air, falling as rain, snow, or hail.
  • The Water Cycle: The continuous, cyclic movement of water from the Earth's surface (oceans, soil, plants) into the atmosphere through evaporation and transpiration, and its return to land through condensation and precipitation.
  • Rainwater Harvesting: Capturing, channeling, and storing monsoon rainwater from rooftops into underground recharge pits or tanks to replenish falling groundwater tables.

NCERT Exercise 8.1: States of Water & Atmospheric Processes
Q1
Explain the process of cloud formation. Why do water droplets condense in the upper atmosphere?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
1. Evaporation & Rising of Warm Air:
Water from water bodies (oceans, rivers, lakes) continuously evaporates due to solar heat. Warm air containing invisible water vapour and air particles expands, becomes less dense, and rises high into the sky.
2. Cooling at Higher Altitudes:
As warm air rises higher, atmospheric temperature and pressure drop significantly. The air cools down steadily.
3. Condensation into Cloud Droplets:
At sufficient heights, the air cools to its dew point. The water vapour condenses around tiny suspended dust particles in the air, transforming into millions of microscopic liquid water droplets. Clusters of these floating droplets appear in the sky as clouds.

NCERT Exercise 8.2: Everyday Water Phase Phenomena
Q2
Why do tiny droplets of water appear on the outer surface of a glass tumbler containing ice-cold water? Does the glass leak?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
No, the glass tumbler does NOT leak:
Glass is completely non-porous and impermeable to liquids.
Scientific Explanation (Condensation):
The atmosphere around us always contains invisible water vapour. When warm air in the room comes into contact with the chilled outer surface of the glass containing ice-cold water, thermal energy transfers from the air to the glass. As a result, the water vapour in the contact layer cools down rapidly and condenses into liquid water droplets on the outer glass surface.

CBSE Competency-Based Question (Water Conservation Case Study)
Case Study: Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting in Urban Housing:
A residential society in Delhi noticed that their borewell water dried up every summer because the water table dropped by 15 metres over three years. They constructed a rooftop rainwater harvesting system with collection pipes, filtration pits (sand, gravel, charcoal), and a groundwater recharge well.
(a) How does rooftop rainwater harvesting help recharge groundwater levels?
(b) Why is a filtration chamber necessary before channeling rainwater into the ground?
(c) Calculate how much water (in litres) can be collected from a 100 m² rooftop if 50 mm of rain falls in a shower. (Hint: 1 m³ = 1,000 litres).
Detailed Analytical Solution & Marking Scheme

(a) Groundwater Recharge Mechanism:
In modern cities, concrete roads, tiled pavements, and buildings prevent rainwater from naturally seeping into the soil, causing it to run off wasted into drains. Rooftop rainwater harvesting catches pure rainwater from roofs and leads it deep underground into porous water-bearing rock layers (aquifers), replenishing the groundwater table.

(b) Purpose of Filtration Chamber:
Rooftop runoff carries rooftop dust, dry bird droppings, dry leaves, and atmospheric soot. The multi-layered filtration pit (coarse sand, gravel, charcoal) traps suspended particulate contaminants, preventing the recharge pit from clogging and keeping groundwater clean.

(c) Numerical Water Volume Calculation:
• Rooftop Area = 100 m².
• Rainfall Depth = 50 mm = 50 / 1,000 m = 0.05 m.
• Volume of Rainwater = Area × Depth = 100 m² × 0.05 m = 5 m³.
• In Litres: 5 × 1,000 litres = 5,000 litres of clean water harvested.
