📌 Key Nutritional Concepts & Deficiency Diseases
  • Major Nutrients in Food:
    • Carbohydrates: Primary energy source for the body (e.g., Rice, Wheat, Potato, Banana, Sugar). Mainly present as starch and simple sugars.
    • Fats: Concentrated energy reserves yielding more energy per gram than carbohydrates; insulate body organs (e.g., Butter, Ghee, Mustard oil, Groundnuts).
    • Proteins: Body-building nutrients essential for cellular growth, muscle building, and tissue repair (e.g., Pulses, Milk, Paneer, Eggs, Fish, Soya chunks).
    • Vitamins & Minerals: Protective nutrients needed in minute amounts to defend against infections and maintain healthy eyes, bones, teeth, and blood.
    • Dietary Fibre (Roughage) & Water: Plant fibres from whole grains, salads, and fresh fruits that add bulk to digested food and prevent constipation. Water aids nutrient absorption and flushes out wastes as urine and sweat.
  • Standard Chemical Food Tests:
    • Test for Starch: Food extract + 2-3 drops of dilute Iodine solution → Appearance of a Blue-Black colour confirms starch.
    • Test for Protein: Food paste + 10 drops of water + 2 drops of Copper sulphate (CuSO₄) + 10 drops of Caustic soda (NaOH) → Solution turning Violet / Purple confirms protein.
    • Test for Fat: Rub food on a clean sheet of paper → An oily translucent patch visible when held against light confirms fat.

NCERT Exercise 3.1: Food Nutrients & Testing Procedures
Q1
Describe the chemical tests used to detect the presence of (i) Starch and (ii) Proteins in a given food sample. Include the reagents used and expected colour changes.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(i) Test for Starch (Iodine Test):
• Procedure: Take a small mashed food item (e.g., sliced potato or boiled rice paste) in a clean petri dish. Add 2 to 3 drops of dilute Iodine solution to it.
• Observation: The food sample turns blue-black in colour.
• Inference: The appearance of blue-black colour definitively confirms the presence of starch.
(ii) Test for Proteins (Biuret Test):
• Procedure: Grind the food item (e.g., powdered boiled dal or egg white) into a fine paste. Add 10 drops of water into a clean test tube and shake well. Add 2 drops of Copper Sulphate solution (CuSO₄) followed by 10 drops of Caustic Soda solution (NaOH).
• Observation: Shake the tube well and let it stand for 5 minutes. The mixture turns a vivid violet / purple colour.
• Inference: The change to violet colour confirms the presence of proteins.

NCERT Exercise 3.2: Balanced Diet & Deficiency Diseases
Q2
What is a balanced diet? Does a balanced diet require expensive food items? Justify with everyday examples.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Definition of a Balanced Diet:
A diet that provides all the essential nutrients (carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals) in appropriate, proportionate quantities required for normal growth, health, and activity, along with adequate roughage (dietary fibre) and water.
Balanced Diet without Expensive Foods:
No, a balanced diet does not require costly foods like almonds, exotic fruits, or dietary supplements. A completely wholesome and balanced meal can be easily prepared from affordable, locally available ingredients:
• Carbohydrates: Roti (wheat), boiled rice, jowar, or potatoes.
• Proteins: Sprouted moong/chana, dal, sattu, peanuts, curd, and soybean.
• Vitamins & Minerals: Green leafy vegetables (spinach, methi), seasonal bananas, guava, amla, and carrots.
• Fats: A small spoonful of mustard oil or sesame oil.

CBSE Competency-Based Question (Public Health & Nutrition Case Study)
Case Study: School Health Checkup & Mid-Day Meal Planning:
During an annual school health camp, the doctor diagnosed two specific issues among Class 6 pupils:
• Student X gets tired very quickly, looks pale, and his haemoglobin count was only 8.5 g/dL (normal: 12-15 g/dL).
• Student Y complains of bleeding gums while brushing teeth and recurrent skin infections.

(a) Identify the nutrient deficiency and disease affecting Student X and Student Y.
(b) Recommend specific changes to their daily diet to cure these conditions naturally.
(c) Why is iodised salt mandatory in government mid-day meals across India?
Detailed Analytical Solution & Marking Scheme

(a) Diagnosis:
• Student X: Suffers from Iron deficiency causing Anemia (low haemoglobin leads to reduced oxygen delivery to tissues, causing paleness and fatigue).
• Student Y: Suffers from Vitamin C deficiency causing Scurvy (vitamin C is required for collagen synthesis and capillary integrity, hence gums bleed).

(b) Nutritional Recommendations:
• For Student X (Iron-rich foods): Spinach, methi, jaggery (gur), beetroot, pomegranate, lentils, and sprouted beans.
• For Student Y (Vitamin C-rich foods): Fresh citrus fruits such as amla (Indian gooseberry), lemons, oranges, tomatoes, and guava (consumed raw because vitamin C is destroyed by heat).

(c) Reason for Mandatory Iodised Salt:
Iodine is indispensable for the thyroid gland to produce thyroxine hormone, which regulates physical growth and neurological development. Deficient iodine causes Goitre and irreversible mental disabilities in growing children. Iodised salt is the most universal and cost-effective method to prevent iodine deficiency disorders (IDD).
