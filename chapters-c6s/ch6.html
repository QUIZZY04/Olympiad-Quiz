📌 Key Chemical Principles & Material Properties
  • Need for Grouping (Classification): Grouping materials based on similarities and differences enables systematic study, quick identification, and convenient retrieval of objects in daily life and commerce.
  • Physical Properties for Classification:
    • Appearance & Lustre: Metals (Gold, Silver, Copper, Iron, Aluminium) possess a characteristic metallic shine called lustre on freshly cut surfaces. Non-metals (Wood, Chalk, Rubber) are non-lustrous / dull.
    • Hardness: Materials that cannot be scratched or compressed easily are hard (e.g., Iron, Diamond, Stone). Materials that can be easily scratched or compressed are soft (e.g., Cotton, Sponge, Wax).
    • Solubility in Water: Substances that dissolve completely and become invisible in water are soluble (e.g., Common salt, Sugar). Substances that do not dissolve even after vigorous stirring are insoluble (e.g., Sand, Sawdust, Chalk powder).
    • Floatation (Density relative to Water): Objects lighter than water float on its surface (e.g., Dry leaves, Cork, Oil, Plastic ball, Ice). Objects heavier than water sink to the bottom (e.g., Iron nail, Stone, Coin, Glass marble).
    • Optical Transparency:
      • Transparent: Materials through which light passes completely and objects can be seen clearly (e.g., Clean glass, Air, Pure water, Clear acrylic).
      • Translucent: Materials through which light passes partially and objects can be seen only hazily or blurrily (e.g., Butter paper, Frosted glass, Oiled sheet, Tracing paper).
      • Opaque: Materials that block light completely so nothing can be seen through them (e.g., Cardboard, Wood, Metals, Brick wall).

NCERT Exercise 6.1: Properties of Everyday Materials
Q1
Why cannot a tumbler (drinking cup) be made of cloth? Explain based on the properties of materials and suitability for purpose.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Scientific Principle:
A drinking tumbler is specifically designed to store and hold liquids like water, milk, or juice. To fulfill this functional purpose, the material chosen must be non-porous, rigid, and impermeable to water.
Why Cloth Fails:
Cloth is woven from yarn with microscopic spaces (pores) between threads. It is porous and permeable, meaning liquids instantly seep through and spill out. Therefore, tumblers are always made of impermeable, durable materials like glass, plastic, stainless steel, or ceramic.

NCERT Exercise 6.2: Sorting & Material Selection
Q2
Find the odd one out from each group and give a scientific reason:
(a) Chair, Bed, Table, Baby, Cupboard
(b) Rose, Jasmine, Boat, Marigold, Lotus
(c) Aluminium, Iron, Copper, Silver, Sand
(d) Sugar, Salt, Sand, Copper sulphate
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
(a) Baby is odd: Baby is a living human being, whereas all others are non-living wooden furniture items.
(b) Boat is odd: Boat is a man-made wooden transport vehicle, while all others are naturally blooming flowers.
(c) Sand is odd: Sand is a non-metallic, non-lustrous mineral mixture, while Aluminium, Iron, Copper, and Silver are lustrous metals.
(d) Sand is odd: Sand is completely insoluble in water, whereas Sugar, Salt, and Copper sulphate crystals are readily soluble in water.

CBSE Competency-Based Question (Industrial Material Selection Case Study)
Case Study: Selecting Materials for Everyday Consumer Products:
An industrial product designer has to choose materials for three products:
1. A transparent jar for a sweet shop owner.
2. The handle of a metal frying pan.
3. A rain protective raincoat.

(a) Why does the shopkeeper insist on transparent glass or clear PET plastic jars over metal tins?
(b) Why are frying pan handles made of Bakelite plastic or wood rather than steel?
(c) Which essential physical property must a raincoat material possess?
Detailed Analytical Solution & Marking Scheme

(a) Transparent Jars for Confectionery:
Transparent materials allow light to pass completely through without scattering. This lets customers instantly see the colourful sweets, biscuits, and toffees inside the jar without needing to open the lid, saving time and maintaining hygiene.

(b) Bakelite / Wooden Pan Handles:
Cooking pans are made of metals because metals are excellent conductors of heat. However, their handles must be made of Bakelite plastic or wood because these materials are poor conductors (insulators) of heat, keeping the handle cool so the cook can hold it safely without burning their fingers.

(c) Property of Raincoat Material:
The material must be waterproof and non-porous (synthetic polymers like PVC or nylon) so that falling rainwater rolls off the surface without soaking through to the wearer's clothes.
