H1

Three Questions

Honeycomb (Prose) • Author: Leo Tolstoy — A king searches for the answers to the three most vital questions in life

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
A certain king believed that he would never fail if he knew the right time to begin everything, the right people to listen to, and the most important thing to do. He proclaimed a great reward, but the answers from wise men were diverse and contradictory. Disappointed, the king visited a wise hermit living in a forest. While the king helped the frail hermit dig flower beds, an injured, bleeding man ran towards them. The king washed and bandaged his severe stomach wound, saving his life. The man revealed he was the king's sworn enemy who came to assassinate him, but was wounded by the king's bodyguard. Overwhelmed by the king's mercy, the enemy asked for forgiveness and pledged loyalty. The hermit then revealed the answers: The most important time is NOW (the present moment); the most important person is the one you are currently with; and the most important deed is to do good to that person.
🎯 Central Theme: The importance of living in the present moment, listening to conscience, and performing acts of benevolence to humanity.
📖 Intext Comprehension Checks
Comprehension Check — Page 10 (Q1)
Why did the king want to know answers to three questions?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution

The king wanted to know the answers to the three questions because he believed that if he always knew:

1. What was the right time to begin everything,
2. Who were the right people to listen to (and whom to avoid), and
3. What was the most important thing for him to do,
he would never fail in any undertaking or governance in his life.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Desire to achieve infallible governance and never fail 1 Mark
Identifying the 3 specific criteria (time, people, action) 1 Mark
Comprehension Check — Page 10 (Q2)
Messengers were sent throughout the kingdom:
(i) to fetch wise men.
(ii) to find answers to the questions.
(iii) to look for the wise hermit.
(iv) to announce a reward for those who could answer the questions.
Choose the correct option.
1 Mark
Detailed Solution

Correct Option: (iv) to announce a reward for those who could answer the questions.

Textual Proof: The king sent messengers throughout his kingdom promising a large sum of money to anyone who would answer these three questions satisfactorily.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct selection: option (iv) 1 Mark
Comprehension Check — Page 14 (Q1)
Who was the bearded man who came running out of the woods? Why did he ask for the king's forgiveness?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. Identity of the Bearded Man: The bearded man was the king's sworn enemy. The king had previously executed his brother and seized his ancestral property, so the man had resolved to ambush and assassinate the king on his return journey from the hermit's hut.
2. Reason for Asking Forgiveness: When the king's bodyguards recognized and wounded him, he escaped to the hermit's hut where he would have bled to death. The king, unaware of his identity, tirelessly washed and bandaged his severe wound and gave him fresh water, saving his life. Overwhelmed with remorse that the man he came to kill had saved his life, he asked for the king's forgiveness and promised lifelong faithful servitude.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification as sworn enemy seeking vengeance for brother and property 1.5 Marks
Remorse and gratitude because the king saved his life instead of killing him 1.5 Marks
✍️ Working with the Text
Working with the Text — Q1
Why was the king advised to go to magicians?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
In reply to the first question (the right time for every action), some wise men argued that to decide the right time for doing something, one must look into the future. Since only magicians possess the supernatural power to look into the future, they advised the king that he would have to consult magicians to know the right time for every action.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Need to know the future to decide the right time 1 Mark
Magicians being the only ones believed capable of foreseeing future 1 Mark
Working with the Text — Q2
In answer to the second question, whose advice did the people say would be important to the king?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
In response to the second question (who were the right people to listen to), the wise men gave varying opinions:
  • Some said the king's councillors were the most necessary advisors.
  • Others suggested the priests (clergy) were the most important.
  • A few recommended doctors for maintaining life and health.
  • Yet others claimed that his soldiers (warriors) were the most essential for defense and conquest.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Listing councillors, priests, doctors, and soldiers 2 Marks
Working with the Text — Q3
What suggestions were made in answer to the third question?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
In answer to the third question (what was the most important thing to do / occupation):
  • Some wise men asserted that the pursuit of science was the most important business.
  • Others chose skill in warfare and military strategy.
  • Still others maintained that religious worship and prayer was the highest priority.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Science, warfare, and religious worship accurately stated 2 Marks
Working with the Text — Q4
Did the wise men win the reward? If not, why not?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
No, none of the wise men won the reward.
Reason: Their answers were so diverse, contradictory, and conflicting that the king could not agree with any of them. Since no answer proved convincing or satisfying to his intellect, the king gave the reward to no one.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Negative response (No reward given) 1 Mark
Contradictory and unconvincing nature of the diverse answers 1 Mark
Working with the Text — Q5
How did the king and the hermit help the wounded man?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. The king unfastened the wounded man's clothing and discovered a large bleeding wound in his stomach.
2. The king washed the wound thoroughly and covered it with his own handkerchief and a towel provided by the hermit.
3. When blood continued to flow profusely, the king re-dressed the wound repeatedly until the bleeding finally ceased.
4. The king then brought fresh drinking water to revive the parched man.
5. As darkness fell and the air turned cool, the king and the hermit carried the wounded man inside the hut and laid him comfortably on the bed to rest safely overnight.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Washing and re-dressing the stomach wound with handkerchief/towel 1.5 Marks
Providing fresh water and carrying him to rest on hermit's bed 1.5 Marks
Working with the Text — Q6
What were the hermit's answers to the three questions? Write each answer separately. Which answer do you like most, and why?
5 Marks
Detailed Solution
The Hermit's Answers:
1. The Right Time: The most important time is NOW (the present moment). It is the only time over which human beings possess any actual control or power to act.
2. The Right Person: The most important person is the person you are with at any particular moment, for no one knows whether they will ever meet anyone else in the future.
3. The Most Important Thing to Do: The most important business is to do good to the person you are with, because humanity was sent into this earthly life for that sole purpose.
Personal Preference & Justification:
I like the answer regarding the present time (NOW) the most. People often waste precious life regretting the unchangeable past or worrying anxiously about the uncertain future. Focusing entirely on the present moment empowers us to act ethically, solve problems immediately, and live meaningful, productive lives.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
First question: Time is NOW (present moment) 1 Mark
Second question: Person you are currently with 1 Mark
Third question: Doing good to that person 1 Mark
Personal choice with thoughtful philosophical reasoning 2 Marks
💡 Competency-Based & HOTS Questions (CBSE 2026-27 Pattern) Analytical Thinking
Competency-Based Case Study: Practical Ethics
In modern corporate leadership and daily student life, how does Leo Tolstoy's philosophy of 'The Three Questions' help reduce anxiety and build empathetic relationships?

Tolstoy's philosophy provides two transformative life principles:

  1. Mindfulness over Multitasking: By realizing that the only real time is 'NOW', students avoid cognitive overload and procrastination. They focus deeply on the task at hand rather than future exam anxieties.
  2. Empathetic Human Relations: Treating the person standing in front of you as the most important individual fosters active listening, kindness, and deep mutual respect in families, friendships, and workplaces.
HOTS / Value-Based Question
How did the king turn an assassination attempt into an enduring lifelong alliance of friendship?

The king exemplified the transformative power of forgiveness and compassion. Instead of executing his enemy when he discovered his identity, the king treated his wounds with tenderness, promised his royal physicians would care for him, and restored his confiscated property. Compassion disarmed hatred, proving that love conquers enmity far more permanently than military force.

A Gift of Chappals

Honeycomb (Prose) • Author: Vasantha Surya — Children in Chennai show spontaneous, innocent generosity to a blistered beggar

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
Mridu, a young girl in Chennai, visits her aunt Rukku Manni and cousins Ravi, Meena, and Lalli. The children excitedly show Mridu a stray kitten hidden in the backyard, whom Ravi has grandly named 'Mahendravarma Pallava Poonai' (M.P. Poonai), explaining how hard it is to secretly bring milk for it without Grandma Patti noticing. While Lalli struggles clumsily with her violin lessons under an eccentric music-master, an exhausted beggar arrives at the gate with painful pink blisters on his bare feet from scorching midday road tar. Rukku Manni orders Ravi to send the beggar away. Heartbroken by the beggar's agony, the children search for footwear and give him the music-master's worn-out leather chappals. When the music-master discovers his footwear missing, Rukku Manni is compelled to give away Gopu Mama's brand new chappals. When scolded, Ravi innocently reminds his mother of the legend of Karna who gave away everything he had, exposing adult hypocrisy.
🎯 Central Theme: Pure, unfiltered empathy of children versus the hypocritical, rigid charity norms of adult society.

Meena shares the secret that they have found a tiny stray kitten that morning outside their front gate, meowing helplessly. They have hidden it secretly in the backyard behind a thick bitter-berry bush inside a torn football lined with sacking and filled with sand.

How does Ravi get milk for the kitten?
2 Marks

Ravi gets milk with great difficulty from the kitchen: 1. Grandma (Patti) caught him holding a glass of milk and asked him why he needed it. 2. Ravi pretended to be very hungry to avoid suspicion. 3. Patti insisted that he return the tumbler, so Ravi was forced to drink more than half of the milk right in front of her. 4. He then ran to the backyard, poured the remaining milk into a coconut shell for the kitten, ran back, washed the tumbler, and put it away before Patti grew suspicious.

No, the beggar had not come for the first time.

Working with the Text — Q3
Describe the condition of the beggar's feet. What prompted the children to give him chappals?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. Condition of the Feet: The beggar was completely barefoot. The road tar had melted under the blazing midday sun, and his bare feet were covered with large, painful, peeling pink blisters.
2. Children's Motivation: When the beggar stretched out his blistered feet to show why he was groaning in pain, Mridu and the children felt overwhelming pity. Realizing he had no money to buy footwear, their natural childhood compassion urged them to find any pair of chappals in the house to protect his burning feet.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Peeling pink blisters from burning melted road tar 1.5 Marks
Children's innocent empathy and desire to relieve his physical pain 1.5 Marks
Working with the Text — Q4
On getting Gopu Mama's chappals, the music-master tried not to look too happy. Why?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
The music-master's old chappals were shabby, dusty, and worn-out, whereas Gopu Mama's chappals were brand new, barely worn, and expensive. While the master was inwardly overjoyed to receive a superior new pair of footwear, he pretended to be displeased and stern so as to show that he was accepting them only under protest as compensation for his 'priceless' lost chappals.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Receiving brand new chappals in exchange for shabby old ones 1 Mark
Feigning anger to maintain moral superiority and dignity 1 Mark
Working with the Text — Q5
Why did Rukku Manni laugh after the music master left?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
Rukku Manni laughed because she suddenly realized the comic predicament awaiting her in the evening. Gopu Mama always hurried to take off his office shoes and slip into his beloved chappals the moment he arrived home. She wondered with amusement how he would react when she told him that she had gifted his brand-new chappals to the music-master!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Anticipating Gopu Mama's reaction upon discovering his chappals given away 2 Marks
💡 Competency-Based & HOTS Questions (CBSE 2026-27 Pattern) Analytical Thinking
Competency-Based Question: Social Analysis
How does the story highlight the contradiction between what adults teach children about kindness and how adults actually treat the impoverished?

Adults constantly preach to children to be kind, charitable, and compassionate towards all living beings. Yet, when a suffering beggar with blistered feet arrives at their doorstep, Rukku Manni harshly turns him away. When the children actually put lessons of compassion into practice by giving away slippers, they are reprimanded. Ravi highlights this moral hypocrisy by referencing Karna, proving that children's empathy is pure, while adult charity is selective and conditional.

Gopal and the Hilsa Fish

Honeycomb (Prose) • Author: Comic Folk Tale — A witty courtier proves that wisdom and humor can redirect public obsession

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
It was the monsoon season of Hilsa fish, and everyone in the kingdom — from fishermen to courtiers — could talk of nothing else. Even the King grew exasperated by the incessant chatter. To challenge Gopal, his cleverest courtier, the King dared him to buy a huge Hilsa fish from the market and bring it all the way to the palace without a single person asking him about it. Accepting the wager, Gopal smeared ash on his face, half-shaved his beard, and donned filthy, torn rags. When his alarmed wife asked why he looked like a madman, he simply said he was going to buy a Hilsa. In the market and on the streets, crowds and palace guards laughed and whispered about the 'lunatic' and 'mystic' rags-clad man. When Gopal was brought before the King, the King asked why he was dressed so absurdly. Gopal politely reminded the King of their challenge: throughout the market and palace corridors, not a single soul had noticed or spoken a word about the giant Hilsa fish in his hand! The King burst into laughter and congratulated Gopal on achieving the impossible yet again.
🎯 Central Theme: Intelligence, wit, and out-of-the-box thinking can triumph over any impossible challenge.

The King lost his temper because the constant fish chatter was distracting everyone from important royal administration.

The loud commotion created such a spectacle outside that the curious King ordered his guards to bring the crazy dancing man before him immediately.

Working with the Text — Q3
Explain why no one seemed to be interested in talking about the Hilsa-fish which Gopal had bought.
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
No one paid the slightest attention to the Hilsa fish because Gopal's bizarre, comic, and shocking appearance completely monopolized everyone's attention. Seeing a man with a half-shaven face, smeared with gray ash, and draped in filthy rags dancing along the street, people were fascinated and bewildered by his apparent lunacy. They debated whether he was a crazy madman or a mystical hermit, completely overlooking the giant fish swinging in his hand.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Gopal's comical and grotesque appearance drawing all attention 1.5 Marks

💡 Competency-Based & HOTS Questions (CBSE 2026-27 Pattern) Analytical Thinking
Competency-Based Question: Psychological Strategy
What psychological insight enabled Gopal to win the King's challenge so effortlessly?

Gopal understood human psychology: people's attention is naturally captured by what is visually dramatic, unusual, or shocking. By presenting himself in a comical, grotesque state, he shifted public focus from an external object (the fish) to his eccentric persona. In modern terms, he mastered the art of 'reframing attention' through unconventional creative thinking.

H4

The Ashes That Made Trees Bloom

Honeycomb (Prose) • Author: William Elliot Griffis — A Japanese folktale of fidelity, virtue, and magical blossoming cherry trees

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
In 19th-century Japan, an honest, kind-hearted old couple kept a pet dog named Muko, treating him like their own child. One day, Muko guided the kind old man to a spot in the garden and scratched the earth, unearthing a pile of glittering gold coins. The old couple gave generously to the poor and lived comfortably. Their envious, wicked neighbors dragged Muko into their garden, expecting gold, but found only a dead, foul-smelling kitten. In rage, the wicked couple beat Muko to death. Muko's spirit appeared to the good old man in a dream, instructing him to cut down a pine tree over his grave to make a wooden mortar and mill. When used to prepare New Year's rice pastries and bean sauce, the dough magically turned into showers of pure gold coins. The greedy neighbors borrowed the mortar, but their dough turned into a squirming mass of foul worms, so they chopped it into firewood. Muko's spirit appeared again, telling the good man to sprinkle the ashes of the mill onto withered trees. When the grand Daimio (feudal lord) paraded by, the good old man climbed a bare cherry tree, scattered a pinch of ash, and the tree burst into glorious pink blossoms. The delighted Daimio showered him with silk robes, cakes, and wealth. When the envious neighbor attempted the same, the wind blew the dry soot into the eyes and noses of the Daimio and his wife; the royal guards dragged the greedy man down and beat him soundly, leaving him to perish in the mud.
🎯 Central Theme: Virtue, kindness to animals, and benevolence bring prosperity, while greed, malice, and cruelty lead to destruction.

Detailed Solution

The wicked, envious neighbors killed Muko because they dragged him into their garden and forced him to find buried treasure for them. Enraged at the disappointment and foul odor, the wicked old man beat Muko brutally to death with a hoe.

The old farmer's extraordinary kindness is evident from several actions: 1. Compassion for Wildlife: While tilling his rice fields, the old farmer deliberately turned up sods of earth to provide grubs and worms for hungry white herons. 3. Charity to the Poor: When Muko led him to a pile of buried gold, the old couple bought land, hosted a feast for their friends, and distributed plentiful alms to their impoverished neighbors.

2 Marks

Muko ran towards the farmer, putting his paws against the farmer's legs and gesturing with his head towards a spot behind him. When the farmer followed him to see what the matter was, Muko began scratching the ground eagerly with his paws. Thinking there might be a bone or bit of fish, the farmer struck his hoe into the earth, unearthing a gleaming mound of gold coins.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Paw gestures and running back and forth to guide the farmer 1 Mark

Working with the Text — Q3
How did the spirit of the dog help the farmer first? How did it help him next?
4 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. First Help (Mortar & Mill): The spirit appeared in a dream and told the farmer to cut down the pine tree over his grave to carve a mortar for rice pastry and a hand-mill for bean sauce. When the farmer ground the rice and beans, the dough magically transformed into a sparkling fountain of gold coins.
2. Second Help (Magic Ashes): When the jealous neighbors burned the wooden mill, Muko's spirit instructed the farmer to collect the ashes of the mill and sprinkle them on withered, dead trees. The ashes miraculously caused withered cherry trees to burst into glorious, fragrant pink blossoms, earning the farmer immense royal honor from the Daimio.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
First instance: Wooden mortar/mill yielding gold coins 2 Marks
Second instance: Ashes blooming withered cherry trees before Daimio 2 Marks
Working with the Text — Q4
Why did the Daimio reward the farmer but punish his neighbor for the same act?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. The Good Farmer's Act: The kind farmer was gentle, reverent, and gifted with magical purity. When he scattered the ash, a withered cherry tree instantly burst into breathtaking pink blossoms, delighting the Daimio and filling the procession with sweet fragrance.
2. The Wicked Neighbor's Act: The greedy neighbor acted out of jealousy and malice. He climbed the tree greedily without bowing. When he threw the ash, not a single bud blossomed; instead, a sudden gust of wind blew the fine, choking soot directly into the eyes, noses, and royal palanquin of the Daimio and his wife, spoiling the dignity of the procession. The royal guards thrashed the wicked man and left him in the ditch.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Farmer's pure heart causing magical blooming to delight the lord 1.5 Marks
Neighbor's greedy soot blinding the Daimio causing swift retribution 1.5 Marks
💡 Competency-Based & HOTS Questions (CBSE 2026-27 Pattern) Analytical Thinking
Competency-Based Extract Question
How does the Japanese tale illustrate the universal moral law of Karma?

The tale is an allegory of karmic justice. Selfless love, kindness to voiceless animals, and humility are rewarded with prosperity and peace that outlives physical death. In contrast, selfishness, cruelty, and parasitic envy result in self-inflicted ruin and public disgrace.

H5

Quality

Honeycomb (Prose) • Author: John Galsworthy — A poignant portrait of an uncompromising craftsman crushed by mass industrial commercialization

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
In the West End of London, German shoemaker Mr. Gessler and his elder brother run a quiet, unpretentious boot shop. Mr. Gessler is a master craftsman who views shoemaking not merely as trade, but as an exalted art form. He never advertises, uses only the finest, softest leather, and crafts each boot entirely by hand to fit the customer's feet with absolute perfection. His boots last astonishingly long. However, mass-producing mechanized shoe corporations enter the market, capturing the public through flashy advertisements rather than craftsmanship. Mr. Gessler loses his brother, his health, and eventually his shop's frontage due to rising rents. Despite poverty, he refuses to compromise on quality, working late into the night without food. When the narrator visits years later, he learns from a young English shopkeeper that the proud old craftsman died of slow starvation, having spent every penny on high-grade leather and shop rent.
🎯 Central Theme: Dedication to artistic perfection and craftsmanship versus the soulless, profit-driven industrial market.
📖 Intext Comprehension Checks
Comprehension Check — Page 78 (Q1)
