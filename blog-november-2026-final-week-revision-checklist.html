Olympiad Exams in November 2026: Your Final-Week Revision Checklist (IEO, ISO, IMO)

📅 Published November 13, 2026 ⏱️ 7 min read ✍️ OlympiadQuiz Team

If you're reading this in mid-November 2026, you're in the busiest stretch of the entire Olympiad calendar. IEO falls on 17 November, ISO (NSO) on 19 November, IHO on 23 November, IMO on 26 November, and ISSO/ICO on 30 November - and that's before accounting for whichever date slot your own school actually picked. Several of these may be just days away for you right now.

This isn't the time to start a new round of full syllabus study. It's the time to convert what you already know into exam-ready recall. Here's exactly how to use whatever time you have left - whether that's three weeks or three days.

Note: Exact dates depend on which slot your school selected for each exam. Confirm your personal exam date with your school's coordinator - the dates above are the official SOF 2026-27 slots, not a guarantee of your specific test day. See our full exam schedule guide for every date and class eligibility.

The Final 7 Days: A Day-by-Day Plan

This plan assumes roughly a week left. If you have less time, compress it; if you have two or three weeks, repeat the "practice" days more and keep the last two days exactly as described.

  • Day 7-6: One full-length timed mock test in the real exam pattern. Don't review answers immediately - sleep on it, then review the next morning with a clear head.
  • Day 5-4: Go chapter by chapter through only the topics you got wrong in that mock, using Chapterwise practice. Skip chapters you're already scoring well in - this is recall time, not re-learning time.
  • Day 3: A second full mock test, ideally at the same time of day as your actual exam slot, to train your concentration for that specific window.
  • Day 2: Light revision only - formula sheets, key definitions, previous year question patterns. No new practice sets.
  • Day 1 (day before): No studying after the afternoon. Pack your admit card, stationery, and school ID the night before. Sleep on time - a rested mind outperforms a tired one that studied one extra hour.
  • Exam day: A quick 10-minute skim of your formula/fact sheet over breakfast, nothing more. Arrive early and calm.

Common Last-Minute Mistakes to Avoid

  • Starting a new chapter two days before the exam. At this stage, depth on known topics beats shaky coverage of a new one.
  • Pulling an all-nighter the night before. Working memory and accuracy both drop sharply on poor sleep - it costs you more marks than it gains.
  • Only re-reading notes instead of practicing. Recognition (reading and nodding along) feels like mastery but isn't the same as recall under time pressure. Always test yourself with timed questions instead.
  • Ignoring the Achievers/HOTS section. For IMO and ISO especially, a few tricky higher-order questions can swing your rank - don't spend all your remaining time on the easier sections only.

Already qualified for Level 2 from an earlier cycle? IEO, ISO, and IMO Level 2 exams (for students who cleared Level 1 last session) are a different, harder exam - use our HOTS Challenge section for that jump in difficulty rather than the standard Level 1 practice.

Exam Day Checklist

  1. Admit card / roll number slip, as instructed by your school.
  2. Two working pens or pencils, plus a spare.
  3. A simple watch if your exam hall doesn't allow phones (most don't).
  4. Arrive at least 15 minutes before the reporting time.
  5. Read every question once fully before answering - rushed misreads are one of the most common avoidable mistakes in OMR-based Olympiad exams.

After the Exam

Resist the urge to immediately start debating answers with friends outside the exam hall - it adds stress with zero benefit, since nothing changes at that point. If you have another Olympiad coming up in the same window (many students sit for two or three across November), give yourself a short break before switching subjects rather than diving straight into the next one exhausted.

Conclusion

With IEO, ISO, IHO, IMO, ISSO, and ICO all clustered into the same few weeks, this is less about learning something new and more about managing your remaining time well. Prioritize practice over passive reading, protect your sleep in the final 48 hours, and trust the preparation you've already put in.
