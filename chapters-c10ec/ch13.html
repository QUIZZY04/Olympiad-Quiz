Play 2: Julius Caesar

William Shakespeare — Political Idealism, Betrayal, Rhetoric & Crowd Psychology — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I. Literature Reader Textbook Solutions & Analysis — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
What ominous omens and nightmares preceded Caesar's assassination on the Ides of March?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

A tempest tossed the city with supernatural portents: graves yawned and yielded up their dead, a lion roamed the Capitol, men on fire walked unhurt, and a screech-owl hooted in the marketplace at noon. Caesar's wife, Calpurnia, had a terrifying nightmare where Caesar's statue spouted blood from a hundred spouts, and smiling Romans washed their hands in it, begging him not to leave the palace.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Supernatural omens: Graves open, roaming lion, fiery men, screech-owl at noon1½ Marks
Calpurnia's nightmare: Caesar's statue spouting blood with Romans bathing hands1½ Marks
Q2
How did Decius Brutus persuade Caesar to ignore Calpurnia's fears and go to the Senate House?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Decius Brutus skillfully manipulated Caesar's vanity through clever flattery:

  • He reinterpreted Calpurnia's nightmare favorably, claiming the spouting blood signified that Rome would draw new life and reviving influence from Caesar's sovereign reign.
  • He whispered that the Senate had resolved to offer Caesar the crown that day, and if he stayed home fearing a woman's dreams, senators would mock him as a coward.
CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Reinterpreted dream: Statues spouting blood means Rome draws reviving life1½ Marks
Appealed to pride: Senate offering crown; warned they would mock him as coward1½ Marks
Q3
How did the conspirators stage Caesar's assassination under the pretext of Metellus Cimber's petition?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Metellus Cimber knelt before Caesar, begging the repeal of his banished brother Publius. When Caesar arrogantly refused, declaring himself as constant and unmoving as the Northern Star, the conspirators crowded around him on their knees. Casca struck first from behind, shouting: 'Speak, hands, for me!' All conspirators stabbed him, and seeing his beloved friend Brutus deliver the final blow, Caesar uttered his tragic last words: 'Et tu, Brute?—Then fall, Caesar!' and died.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Pretext: Metellus Cimber's petition to repeal his brother's banishment1½ Marks
Assassination: Casca stabs first; Brutus stabs last; famous cry 'Et tu, Brute?'1½ Marks
Q4
What strategic blunder did Brutus commit regarding Mark Antony after the assassination?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Overruling Cassius's realistic warnings, Brutus committed two fatal blunders: (i) he spared Mark Antony's life, and (ii) he permitted Antony to deliver a funeral oration over Caesar's body in the marketplace, imposing the naive condition that Antony must speak only after Brutus and praise Caesar without condemning the conspirators. Brutus underestimated Antony's oratorical genius and deep loyalty.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Spared Antony's life despite Cassius's warnings of danger1½ Marks
Permitted Antony to speak at Caesar's funeral, assuming mob would remain calm1½ Marks
Q5
Compare the funeral orations of Brutus and Mark Antony. How does Antony masterfully sway the Roman plebeians from praising Brutus to demanding the conspirators' heads?
8 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Brutus's Speech (Intellectual Logic & Patriotism):
Brutus speaks in prose, appealing strictly to abstract reason, republican virtue, and civic duty. His central thesis is: 'Not that I loved Caesar less, but that I loved Rome more.' He justifies assassination by branding Caesar ambitious, demanding whether Romans would rather live as slaves under Caesar or free men in a republic. The mob, persuaded by his cold rhetoric, shouts in praise: 'Let him be Caesar!'

2. Mark Antony's Speech (Emotional Masterpiece & Irony):
Antony speaks in passionate blank verse, systematically dismantling Brutus's claim of Caesar's ambition using concrete evidence:

  • Caesar brought countless captives whose ransoms filled Roman public coffers.
  • When the poor cried, Caesar wept; ambition should be made of sterner stuff.
  • On the Lupercal, Caesar thrice refused the kingly crown.

3. Rhetorical Irony and Climax:
Antony repeats the biting refrain: 'Yet Brutus says he was ambitious; and Brutus is an honourable man.' He displays Caesar's blood-soaked mantle, pointing out each conspirator's dagger slash, highlighting Brutus's cut as 'the most unkindest cut of all'. Finally, he reads Caesar's will, which bequeathed seventy-five drachmas and private orchards to every Roman citizen. The mob erupts in mutinous fury, shouting: 'Revenge! About! Seek! Burn! Fire! Kill! Slay!', completely overthrowing the conspirators through the sheer power of rhetoric.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Brutus's speech: Abstract prose, logic, patriotism ('loved Rome more')2½ Marks
Antony's counter-arguments proving Caesar was not ambitious (ransom, tears, crown)2½ Marks
Antony's theatrical props: Mantle, dagger wounds, Caesar's will, incitement to mutiny3 Marks
Q7
Compare and contrast the rhetorical techniques used by Brutus and Mark Antony in their funeral orations in 'Julius Caesar'. How does Antony transform the mob into a raging instrument of vengeance?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Brutus's Speech: Brutus delivers an idealistic, stoic prose speech appealing strictly to abstract logic, patriotism, and honor ('Not that I loved Caesar less, but that I loved Rome more'). He assumes the Roman plebeians are rational philosophers who will accept his assertion that Caesar was ambitious.

Antony's Masterstroke: In contrast, Antony uses brilliant, emotive blank verse that appeals directly to the crowd's raw passions, grief, and pocketbooks. He repeatedly employs the devastating verbal irony 'For Brutus is an honourable man' while presenting tangible counter-proofs: Caesar brought captive ransoms, wept for the poor, and thrice refused the kingly crown at Lupercal. By displaying Caesar's blood-soaked mantle, pointing out Brutus's stab-wound, and reading Caesar's generous will bequeathing seventy-five drachmas and orchards to every citizen, Antony incites the mob to uncontrollable frenzy and civil war.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Brutus: Cold abstract logic, stoic prose, patriotism vs personal affection1½ Marks
Antony: Irony, emotional appeal, bloody mantle, and will inciting rebellion1½ Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 13CBSE 2026-27
Rhetoric & Crowd Psychology
Analysis: How does Shakespeare depict the fickleness and emotional volatility of the Roman mob in Julius Caesar?

Shakespeare presents the Roman mob as an unstable, easily swayed beast driven by emotion rather than political conviction. Within fifteen minutes, the plebeians switch from hailing Brutus as a hero and wanting to crown him, to screaming for his blood and burning the conspirators' homes after Antony shows them Caesar's wounds and reads his will. The scene is a timeless warning on how populist rhetoric can weaponize crowd hysteria against democratic order.
