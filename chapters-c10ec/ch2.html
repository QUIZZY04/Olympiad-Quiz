Q1
What compelled Mrs. Packletide to shoot a tiger? What was her primary motive?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Mrs. Packletide had no genuine passion for big-game hunting; her sole motive was poisonous jealousy towards her social rival, Loona Bimberton. Loona had recently been carried eleven miles in an airplane by an Algerian aviator and talked of nothing else. To outshine Loona, Mrs. Packletide resolved to shoot a tiger, procure a tiger-skin rug, wear a tiger-claw brooch, and splash her photograph across Parisian journals.

Q2
How did the villagers make the tiger hunt smooth and safe for Mrs. Packletide?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Tempted by the massive bounty of one thousand rupees, the villagers posted boys around to herd a senile, toothless, infirm old tiger that had given up hunting wild game and lived on domestic cattle. Children were warned not to disturb the tiger's sleep, cheap goats were tethered to supply easy food, and a comfortable platform (machan) was constructed on a tree.

Q3
How did the tiger actually die? What was the fatal error committed by Mrs. Packletide?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

When Mrs. Packletide fired her rifle, the bullet struck the tethered goat, killing it instantly. The old tiger was never touched by the bullet; it died of heart failure caused by the sudden, deafening report of the rifle at close quarters, collapsing dead from sheer cardiac shock.

Q4
Who was Miss Louisa Mebbin? How did she exploit Mrs. Packletide's secret?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Miss Louisa Mebbin was Mrs. Packletide's paid companion, possessed of an elder-sisterly attitude towards money. Having observed that the goat had the bullet hole and the tiger had none, she blackmailed Mrs. Packletide, threatening to expose the fraud unless Mrs. Packletide purchased her a lovely weekend cottage ('Les Fauves') at Dorking for six hundred and eighty pounds.

Q5
How does Saki satirize the hollow rivalry, artificiality, and pretentiousness of British high society in 'Mrs. Packletide's Tiger'?
8 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Satire on Social Competition:
Saki uses biting irony to mock the aristocracy's trivial rivalries. Mrs. Packletide risks thousands of rupees not for sport, but to humiliate Loona Bimberton. Loona, in turn, refuses to attend Mrs. Packletide's celebration dinner or look at the newspaper photographs on 'press of other engagements'.

2. The Mockery of Colonial Big-Game Hunting:
The heroic colonial safari is reduced to a commercial farce: an ancient, dying tiger pushed into place by bribed villagers, shot from an armchair platform with a missed bullet that hits a goat.

3. Poetic Justice & The Final Irony:
While Mrs. Packletide gains her public triumph over Loona, she is financially bled dry by the cunning Louisa Mebbin. When asked why she gave up hunting, Mrs. Packletide remarks wryly: 'The incidental expenses are so heavy.' Saki proves that vanity always carries an exorbitant price tag.

Irony & Humor
Analysis: How did Miss Mebbin name her weekend cottage, and what is the biting irony in that name?

Miss Mebbin named her cottage 'The Cedars' or 'Les Fauves' (meaning 'The Wild Beasts'). The supreme irony is that this idyllic country home was acquired through the 'wild beast' hunt—yet neither the tiger was wild, nor was the companion gentle, proving that the truly predatory beasts were the humans participating in the fraud.
