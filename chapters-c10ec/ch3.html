Q1
Who was Coachman Ali? How had his earlier life contrasted with his present existence?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In his youth, Coachman Ali was a renowned, ruthless shikari (hunter) who could spot a partridge hidden in yellow grass and derived sadistic pleasure from watching parent birds weep over slaughtered offspring. However, after his only daughter Miriam married a soldier and departed for Punjab, he experienced the agony of loneliness. Converted by grief, he renounced hunting, realizing that love and separation govern all living beings.

Q2
Describe Ali's five-year daily pilgrimage to the post office.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Every single morning at 4 a.m., wrapped in a tattered coat against the piercing cold, Ali walked through the sleeping town to the post office, treating it like a sacred shrine of pilgrimage. He sat silently in the same corner on a wooden bench, waiting patiently for a letter from Miriam. Over five agonizing years, not a single letter arrived, yet his faith remained unshakeable.

3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The clerks and peons viewed Ali as a standing village joke and an annoying lunatic. They would mockingly call out his name when sorting mail just to watch him leap up in vain hope. When Ali once dared to ask the haughty postmaster about his letter, the postmaster barked at him coldly: 'Do you think we are waiting here to deliver your daughter's letter? Go away, you pest!'

Q5
Trace the dramatic transformation of the postmaster from an unfeeling bureaucrat to an empathetic, grief-stricken father.
8 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Initial Apathy:
The postmaster was a textbook administrative automaton—cold, arrogant, and indifferent to human sentiment. He dismissed Ali's five-year devotion as lunacy, unable to perceive the suffering heart beating beneath Ali's ragged coat.

2. The Turning Point of Personal Suffering:
Poetic justice struck when the postmaster's own daughter fell critically ill in a distant town. Suddenly, the postmaster found himself in the exact same furnace of agonizing suspense. He spent the entire night pacing anxiously, waiting for a telegram, startled by every postal thud.

3. The Epiphany of Empathy:
When he picked up an envelope and saw the name 'Coachman Ali', his heart leaped with remorse. He realized that mail is not merely paper with stamps, but living human heartbeats connecting anxious souls. When he learned from Lakshmi Das that Ali had died three months prior, the postmaster was overwhelmed with guilt. Accompanied by Lakshmi Das, he visited Ali's grave to lay the letter, weeping in profound repentance.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 3CBSE 2026-27
Philosophical & Empathy Analysis
Analysis: Dhumaketu writes: 'The postmaster had never thought that letters were warm, pulsating human hearts.' Explain this profound revelation.

To bureaucrats, letters are sterile postal cargo sorted by pincodes and weight. It was only when personal grief struck the postmaster that he realized every envelope carries the tears, laughter, prayers, and anxieties of human relationships. Ali's tragic death taught him that without empathy, administrative efficiency is mere cold machinery.
