Q1
Why did John Hallock consider himself fortunate when writing ghost stories? What was Jenkins' demand?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

John Hallock, a ledger clerk by day, had no specialized skill or creative imagination for supernatural fiction, yet whenever Jenkins, the editor of a sensational magazine, demanded a spooky ghost story, ideas mysteriously flooded into his mind from nowhere. John considered himself lucky because ghost stories paid well, and Jenkins always insisted on something chilling and full of horrors.

Q2
Who was Helen the ghost? Why did ghosts organize the 'Writers' Inspiration Bureau'?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Helen was an eccentric female ghost who, in her mortal life, had been a terrible writer of bad magazine stories. In the afterlife, ghosts formed the Writers' Inspiration Bureau to help struggling human authors with plots. However, she announced that ghosts were going on strike because humans were pestering them day and night with modern Ouija boards, pulling them away from their ghostly haunts.

Q4
How did Lavinia's Ouija board party precipitate a domestic crisis for John?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

John's shopping-obsessed wife Lavinia bought an Ouija board at a bargain sale and hosted a party with her friends. Partnered with Miss Laura Hinkle, the Ouija indicator spelled out T-R-A-I-T-O-R and named H-E-L-E-N, accusing John of flirting with another woman. Lavinia was outraged, convinced her husband was conducting a secret affair with a woman named Helen, and threatened divorce.

Q5
Describe the comic climax of 'A Shady Plot'. How is John Hallock's marriage saved, and how does he obtain his ghost plot?
8 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Lavinia's Confrontation:
The next morning, Lavinia enters John's study with packed suitcases to bid him goodbye. She notices John desperately trying to conceal a figure behind him in the room, confirming her worst suspicions of an unfaithful husband.

2. Materialization of Helen:
Stepping forward to confront the intruder, Lavinia is astonished to find Helen the ghost fully materializing into thin air. Instead of shrieking in jealousy, Lavinia realizes that 'Helen' is literally a disembodied ghost, not a human mistress. Overwhelmed by relief that her husband was not cheating on her, Lavinia faints into John's arms.

3. Burning the Board & The Climax:
Gladys the cook enters demanding to leave because of eerie spirits; Lavinia pacifies her by feeding the cursed Ouija board into the fireplace. Helen smiles in gratitude at the destruction of the board and dissolves into thin air, leaving John with the complete, brilliant plot for his new ghost story.

Irony & Humor
Analysis: How does Elsie Brown make fun of supernatural fads and bourgeois shopping habits through Lavinia?

Lavinia represents the gullible consumer seduced by every passing bourgeois fad. She buys duck-shaped ash-trays and Ouija boards simply because they are 'ducky bargains'. Her obsession with calling spirits turns into an absurd parlor game, mocking how superficial middle-class fads create domestic chaos out of nothing.
