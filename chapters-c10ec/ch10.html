COMMUNICATIVE POETRY

Poem 4: The Rime of the Ancient Mariner

Samuel Taylor Coleridge — Sin, Retribution & Reverence for Nature — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

It is an ancient Mariner, And he stoppeth one of three. ‘By thy long grey beard and glittering eye, Now wherefore stopp’st thou me?... The ship was cheered, the harbour cleared, Merrily did we drop Below the kirk, below the hill, Below the lighthouse top... And now the STORM-BLAST came, and he Was tyrannous and strong: He struck with his o’ertaking wings, And chased us south along... And through the drifts the snowy clifts Did send a dismal sheen: Nor shapes of men nor beasts we ken— The ice was all between.
— Samuel Taylor Coleridge
I. Literature Reader Textbook Solutions & Analysis — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
How does the Ancient Mariner detain the Wedding Guest? Why does the guest listen?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The Ancient Mariner stops one of three guests on their way to a wedding. When the guest tries to brush him off, the mariner holds him with his 'glittering eye'. The Wedding Guest is spellbound by the hypnotic, supernatural intensity of the mariner's gaze, standing still like a three-year-old child, powerless to walk away.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Detained by hypnotic stare of the mariner's 'glittering eye'1½ Marks
Guest spellbound like a child, listening helplessly to the tale1½ Marks
Q2
How was the ship received by the South Pole ice, and how was the Albatross greeted by the crew?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Driven by a ferocious storm blast, the ship was trapped in a terrifying Antarctic realm of mist and snow where ice mast-high cracked, growled, and roared like demonic sounds. Suddenly, an Albatross appeared through the fog. The delighted crew greeted it as a Christian soul sent by God, feeding it food it had never eaten, as it split the ice and brought a favorable south wind.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Trapped in Antarctic ice roaring and cracking like monsters1½ Marks
Albatross hailed as a holy omen; ice split and favorable wind blew1½ Marks
Q3
What terrible crime did the Ancient Mariner commit? Why was it senseless and unprovoked?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In a moment of wanton cruelty and perverse madness, the Ancient Mariner shot the innocent, friendly Albatross with his crossbow. It was completely senseless and unprovoked because the noble bird had brought companionship, split the frozen ice pack, summoned favorable navigational winds, and never harmed a soul.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Crime: Shot the innocent Albatross with his crossbow1½ Marks
Senseless: Bird had brought good wind, cracked ice, and befriended sailors1½ Marks
Q4
Describe the curse that fell upon the ship in the silent equatorial sea.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The wind died suddenly and the sails collapsed. The ship was stuck motionless: 'Day after day, day after day, / We stuck, nor breath nor motion; / As idle as a painted ship / Upon a painted ocean.' Under a burning copper sun, water supplies evaporated, wooden boards shrank, rotting slimy creatures crawled upon the slimy sea, and sailors' tongues choked with thirst: 'Water, water, everywhere, / Nor any drop to drink.'

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Motionless ship compared to 'painted ship upon a painted ocean'1½ Marks
Extreme thirst ('nor any drop to drink') and rotting slimy sea1½ Marks
Q5
Analyze the moral, environmental, and spiritual themes of 'The Rime of the Ancient Mariner'. Why did the sailors hang the dead bird around the mariner's neck?
8 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Violation of the Sacred Covenant of Nature:
Coleridge's epic poem is an ecological parable. The Albatross is not merely a seabird, but God's creation embodying universal life. Killing it without motive violates the sacred bond between humanity and the natural world, bringing immediate cosmic retribution.

2. Burden of Guilt (The Hanging Albatross):
When water runs out and death looms, the angry sailors blame the mariner for their impending doom. Instead of a cross around his neck, they hang the heavy, dead Albatross as an outward badge of sin, guilt, and environmental curse.

3. Redemption Through Universal Love:
The mariner's redemption begins only when, watching the luminous water snakes under the moon, he overcomes his disgust, perceives their divine beauty, and blesses them unaware. The curse breaks, the dead bird falls from his neck into the sea, and he learns the eternal moral: 'He prayeth best, who loveth best / All things both great and small; / For the dear God who loveth us, / He made and loveth all.'

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Sacred bond with nature violated by shooting the Albatross2½ Marks
Dead Albatross hung around neck as physical manifestation of guilt2½ Marks
Spiritual redemption through loving all living creatures ('He prayeth best...')3 Marks
Q7
Explain the profound symbolic significance of the dead Albatross hung around the Ancient Mariner's neck. How does the mariner's sin against nature bring psychological and cosmic retribution?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The Albatross, a benign creature of God hailed as a Christian soul that brought favorable south winds out of polar ice, was killed by the Mariner without provocation in an act of callous, hubristic cruelty. When the ship stalled under a blistering copper sun and water ran out ('Water, water, everywhere, nor any drop to drink'), the dying crewmates hung the dead bird around the Mariner's neck in place of a cross. The dead Albatross serves as a visceral emblem of personal guilt, sin against ecological harmony, and spiritual curse. Only when the Mariner later experiences unconditional reverence for the water-snakes does the Albatross fall from his neck into the sea, initiating spiritual redemption.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Sin against nature: Killing harmless bird that brought blessing1½ Marks
Albatross as badge of guilt, cosmic retribution, and ecological penance1½ Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 10CBSE 2026-27
Supernatural Atmosphere & Imagery
Analysis: How does Coleridge use visual imagery and supernatural similes to evoke the terrifying stillness of the doldrums?

Coleridge creates eerie claustrophobia using vivid similes: the motionless ship is 'as idle as a painted ship upon a painted ocean', turning reality into an artificial frozen canvas. The 'bloody Sun at noon' in a 'copper sky' evokes suffocating heat, while 'slimy things did crawl with legs upon the slimy sea' stimulates visceral disgust, dramatizing the nightmare of spiritual alienation.
