Poem 3: Ozymandias

Percy Bysshe Shelley — Tyranny, Transience of Power & The Mockery of Time — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I met a traveller from an antique land Who said: Two vast and trunkless legs of stone Stand in the desert. Near them, on the sand, Half sunk, a shattered visage lies, whose frown, And wrinkled lip, and sneer of cold command, Tell that its sculptor well those passions read Which yet survive, stamped on these lifeless things, The hand that mocked them and the heart that fed; And on the pedestal these words appear: “My name is Ozymandias, King of Kings: Look on my Works, ye Mighty, and despair!” Nothing beside remains. Round the decay Of that colossal Wreck, boundless and bare The lone and level sands stretch far away.
— Percy Bysshe Shelley

Q1
What did the traveler from an antique land see in the desert?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The traveler saw the ruins of a colossal ancient monument: two vast, torso-less legs of stone standing isolated in the desert. Near them, half-buried in the shifting sand, lay a shattered stone face (visage) whose sculpted frown, wrinkled lip, and sneer of cold command revealed the tyrannical arrogance of the king.

Q2
How had the sculptor 'mocked' the passions of Ozymandias?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The word 'mocked' has a double meaning: (i) to copy or imitate realistically, and (ii) to ridicule or expose with subtle irony. The skilled sculptor read the despotic nature of Ozymandias so accurately that he permanently carved the king's cruel arrogance and haughty sneer onto cold, lifeless granite, exposing his hubris to the ridicule of all future generations.

Q3
What was inscribed on the pedestal? What is the supreme irony of the inscription?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. The Inscription: 'My name is Ozymandias, King of Kings: / Look on my Works, ye Mighty, and despair!'

2. Supreme Irony: The arrogant pharaoh wanted rival rulers to look at his eternal empire and despair at their own insignificance. The devastating irony is that absolutely nothing remains of his works; his palaces and empire have dissolved into nothingness, surrounded only by empty, desolate, rolling sands.

Q4
How does Shelley expose the vanity of political hubris and the triumph of nature and art in 'Ozymandias'?
6 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Vanity of Tyranny:
Ozymandias (Ramses II) believed his royal authority and stone monuments would withstand eternity. Shelley demonstrates that all political empires, dictatorships, and military conquests are transient dust before the relentless march of time.

2. Triumph of Art Over Tyranny:
While the King of Kings and his mighty works have vanished, the humble sculptor's artistic creation survives. The sculptor's perception of human pride outlasts the ruler who commanded it, proving that art preserves historical truth long after crowns turn to dust.

3. Nature's Supreme Majesty:
The final imagery—'boundless and bare, / The lone and level sands stretch far away'—is devastating. The vast, indifferent desert swallows the wreckage of human ego, proving that nature is the supreme sovereign of the cosmos.

Literary Devices & Alliteration
Analysis: Identify the alliterative phrases in the final lines: 'boundless and bare' and 'lone and level sands'. How do they reinforce the poem's atmosphere?

The alliteration in **'boundless and bare'** and **'lone and level sands'** produces a flat, elongated, whispering rhythm. The repetitive 'b' and 'l' sounds emphasize boundless emptiness, vastness, and eternal stillness, sonically evoking the infinite, uninterrupted horizon of the desert where human pride has vanished without a trace.
