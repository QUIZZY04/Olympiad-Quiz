COMMUNICATIVE POETRY

Poem 1: The Frog and the Nightingale

Vikram Seth — Exploitation, Flattery & Lack of Self-Confidence — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

Once upon a time a frog Croaked away in Bingle Bog Every night from dusk to dawn He croaked awn and awn and awn. Other creatures loathed his voice, But, alas, they had no choice, And the crass cacophony Blared out from the sumac tree... Neither stones nor prayers nor sticks, Insults or complained or bricks Stilled the frog’s determination To display his heart’s elation.
— Vikram Seth
I. Literature Reader Textbook Solutions & Analysis — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
How did the creatures of Bingle Bog react to the frog's song versus the nightingale's song?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The creatures of Bingle Bog utterly loathed the frog's loud, harsh, crass cacophony, hurling stones, sticks, and bricks in vain to silence him. In contrast, when the nightingale sang her melodious song, the creatures were spellbound; ducks and herons waded in to listen, toads wept with emotion, and everyone applauded with cries of 'Bravo! Too divine! Encore!'

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Reaction to frog: Disgusted, hurled sticks/stones to stop his crude noise1½ Marks
Reaction to nightingale: Spellbound, weeping with joy, shouting 'Encore!'1½ Marks
Q2
How did the cunning frog deceive the naive nightingale into accepting him as her music trainer?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The jealous frog introduced himself with boastful arrogance, claiming to be the owner of the sumac tree, a seasoned music maestro, and a famous critic for the 'Bog Trumpet'. While acknowledging her voice was sweet, he pointed out that it lacked 'force' and proper technique, offering to train her for an exorbitant fee. The gullible nightingale was flattered by praise from a 'maestro' and foolishly surrendered to his control.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Boasted of owning sumac tree, being music critic for 'Bog Trumpet'1½ Marks
Criticized her technique, offered training for a fee; nightingale flattered1½ Marks
Q3
How did the frog commercially exploit and systematically destroy the nightingale?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The frog turned her concerts into commercial ventures, charging entry fees and accumulating vast wealth while giving her nothing. He subjected her to cruel, exhausting training in bitter rain, made her sing for six hours without rest, insulted her constantly, and forced her to puff her lungs louder and sharper. Starved, exhausted, and terrified, the nightingale puffed up a desperate note until a vein in her head burst, and she died.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Charged admission fees, hoarded wealth without paying the bird1½ Marks
Forced extreme training in rain, caused exhaustion until vein burst and she died1½ Marks
Q4
'The Frog and the Nightingale' is an allegorical fable on how lack of self-confidence and uncritical subservience lead to destruction. Analyze the themes and character traits.
8 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. The Predator (The Frog):
The frog represents the untalented, loud, arrogant, and manipulative critic. Possessing no musical talent of his own, he maintains power through intimidation, commercial greed, and gaslighting. He cannot tolerate genuine genius and deliberately sabotages the nightingale to re-establish his monopoly over Bingle Bog.

2. The Victim (The Nightingale):
The nightingale represents gifted, sensitive artists who suffer from devastating self-doubt. Despite possessing a heavenly voice, she relies entirely on external validation. Her naive humility blinds her to the frog's jealousy. Even on her deathbed, she obeys his commands rather than trusting her own artistic instincts.

3. Moral Lesson:
At the end, the callous frog croaks: 'Your song must be your own.' Seth delivers a powerful warning: an individual must possess self-belief and independence. Relying blindly on envious critics and bowing to authoritarian manipulators will inevitably choke one's natural talent to death.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Analysis of frog as manipulative, untalented, greedy exploiter2½ Marks
Analysis of nightingale's fatal flaw: Lack of self-worth and gullibility3 Marks
Moral allegory: Warning against toxic mentorship and loss of authentic voice2½ Marks
Q6
What moral allegory does Vikram Seth convey through 'The Frog and the Nightingale'? How does the poem expose the vulnerability of uncritical artists to malicious critics?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Vikram Seth's poem is a poignant fable about the cutthroat creative world. The Frog represents arrogant, untalented, opportunistic mediocrity that dominates the cultural sphere through sheer bluster, monopolistic bullying, and commercial exploitation. The Nightingale represents pure, divine artistic genius that is tragically undermined by crippling insecurity, gullibility, and a desperate craving for external approval. Because she uncritically surrendered her artistic judgment to a malicious mentor, the Nightingale was worked to exhaustion until a vein burst in her throat. Seth cautions that genuine talent without critical self-confidence and independence will inevitably be destroyed by jealous charlatans.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Allegorical roles: Frog = untalented commercial exploiter; Nightingale = insecure genius1½ Marks
Moral lesson: Independence of spirit and self-belief required to survive exploitation1½ Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 7CBSE 2026-27
Extract & Irony Analysis
Analysis: Read the frog's final words: 'Well, she had no brain, you see / Every bird must sing its own song.' Explain the cruel dramatic irony in his statement.

The supreme irony is that the frog, who spent weeks brutally policing the nightingale's voice, altering her tempo, and forbidding her from singing naturally, hypocritically claims that 'every bird must sing its own song'. He murdered her through forced imitation and commercial exploitation, yet self-righteously blames her lack of intellect for her death.
