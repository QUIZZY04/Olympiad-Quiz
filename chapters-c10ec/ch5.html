Chapter 5: Patol Babu, Film Star

Satyajit Ray — Artistic Integrity, Dedication & The Unsung Hero — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I. Literature Reader Textbook Solutions & Analysis — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
Who offered Patol Babu a film role, and why was Patol Babu ecstatic?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Nishikanto Ghosh, Patol Babu's neighbor, arranged a small film role through his brother-in-law Naresh Dutt, who worked in film production. Patol Babu was ecstatic because fifty-two-year-old Patol had been a passionate, celebrated stage actor in his youth in pre-war Calcutta, and receiving a film role rekindled his dormant creative dreams.

Offer arranged by Nishikanto Ghosh through brother-in-law Naresh Dutt1½ Marks

Q2
What was the scene Patol Babu was hired for? What dialogue was he given?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The scene involved a pedestrian collision: as the lead hero Chanchal Kumar rushed out of an office car into the building, Patol Babu, playing a preoccupied pedestrian, was to collide with him, head-on. To Patol Babu's immense dismay, his dialogue was a solitary monosyllabic word: 'Oh!'

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Scene: Pedestrian colliding with hero Chanchal Kumar on sidewalk1½ Marks
Dialogue: Single monosyllabic exclamation ('Oh!')1½ Marks
Q3
What words of his theatrical mentor Gogon Pakrashi inspired Patol Babu when he felt insulted by the tiny dialogue?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Remembering his revered guru Gogon Pakrashi, Patol Babu recalled his golden advice: 'Remember one thing, Patol: however small a part you're offered, never consider it beneath your dignity to accept. An actor must squeeze the last drop of life and essence out of each single word.' This saintly advice banished his hurt ego and fired his artistic devotion.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Pakrashi's advice: Never consider any part beneath dignity1½ Marks
Artistic duty: Squeeze the last drop of essence and life out of every word1½ Marks
Q4
How did Patol Babu rehearse the monosyllable 'Oh!' to achieve perfection?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Standing near a quiet pan shop, Patol Babu practiced saying 'Oh!' with multiple emotional inflections—pain, surprise, sorrow, panic, and irritated shock. He calculated step-lengths, facial contortions, and timing. He even suggested wearing a butterfly mustache and carrying an open newspaper to make the pedestrian collision look natural.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Practiced multiple emotional inflections (sorrow, shock, pain, panic)1½ Marks
Added artistic details: Step calculations, newspaper prop, butterfly mustache1½ Marks
Q5
Why did Patol Babu leave the film set without collecting his payment? Discuss the triumph of artistic pride over commercial greed.
8 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Flawless Execution of Shot:
When the director yelled 'Action!', Patol Babu timed his collision with Chanchal Kumar to perfection, screaming 'Oh!' with a mix of anguish and surprise, landing gracefully. The director Baren Mullick praised him: 'Jolly good! A first-class shot!'

2. Financial Need vs Creative Sanctity:
Patol Babu desperately needed money; his wife struggled with household expenses every day. He knew Naresh Dutt would pay him twenty or twenty-five rupees for his day's work.

3. The Sublimity of Artistic Dignity:
Yet, standing in the shade, a wave of profound creative satisfaction washed over him. He realized that the pure joy of performing a role with absolute perfection was priceless. Receiving a petty twenty-rupee note would demean and commercialize the divine creative ecstasy he had just experienced. Choosing artistic dignity over pocket change, Patol Babu walked away toward the railway station, a true, triumphant artist.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Director's praise for flawless execution of collision and dialogue2½ Marks
Patol's dire financial need contrasted with supreme creative ecstasy3 Marks
Departure without payment: Choosing artistic dignity over commercialization2½ Marks
Q6
What inspiring words of his dramatic mentor, Gogon Pakrashi, flashed across Patol Babu's mind when he was disappointed by the one-word monosyllabic dialogue 'Oh!'? How did this transform his performance?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Initially humiliated to discover his entire role consisted of uttering a single monosyllable 'Oh!' after bumping into the hero Chanchal Kumar, Patol Babu was reminded of his theatrical guru Gogon Pakrashi's golden advice: 'Remember one thing, Patol; however small a part you're offered, never consider it beneath your dignity to accept it. As an artist your aim should be to squeeze the last drop of meaning out of your lines, for each word in a play has a purpose in the larger symphony.' This epiphany electrified Patol Babu. He practiced saying 'Oh' with dozens of emotional inflections—surprise, despair, irritation, agony—eventually delivering a flawless, synchronized collision that astounded director Baren Mullick.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Guru's precept: No role is small; squeeze every drop of meaning from line1½ Marks
Rehearsal & delivery: Experimenting with inflections and delivering masterpiece1½ Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 5CBSE 2026-27
Character & Ethical Analysis
Analysis: How does Satyajit Ray pay homage to millions of unsung background artists and daily-wage dreamers in cinema through Patol Babu?

Cinema glitters with glamorous stars, but relies on thousands of nameless extras who perform tiny background roles. Patol Babu represents the silent soul of art—those who pour heart, rehearsals, and passion into half-second appearances without expecting fame or wealth. Satyajit Ray proves that greatness is not determined by the size of the billboard, but by the sincerity of one's dedication.
