Chapter 1: Integers

NCERT Exercise 1.1 to 1.3 — Complete Solutions as per CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

📌 Key Concepts & Properties Summary
  • Integers (ℤ (Integers)): The set {..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...} containing whole numbers and their negatives.
  • Addition & Subtraction Properties:
    • Closure: a + b and a - b are always integers.
    • Commutative: a + b = b + a. (Note: a - b ≠ b - a).
    • Associative: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c).
    • Additive Identity: a + 0 = a = 0 + a. Additive inverse of a is -a.
  • Multiplication Properties:
    • Rules of Signs: (+) × (+) = (+), (-) × (-) = (+), (+) × (-) = (-), (-) × (+) = (-).
    • Distributive Property over Addition: a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c).
    • Distributive Property over Subtraction: a × (b - c) = (a × b) - (a × c).
    • Multiplicative Identity: a × 1 = a. Product with zero: a × 0 = 0.
  • Division of Integers:
    • (-a) ÷ b = a ÷ (-b) = -(a ÷ b) when b ≠ 0.
    • (-a) ÷ (-b) = a ÷ b.
    • Division by 0 is meaningless (a ÷ 0 is undefined); 0 ÷ a = 0 (a ≠ 0); a ÷ 1 = a.

An elevator descends into a mine shaft at the rate of 6 m/min. If the descent starts from 10 m above the ground level, how long will it take to reach −350 m?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Step 1: Determine total distance to descend:
Initial position = +10 m (above ground).
Final position = −350 m (below ground).
Total distance = 10 − (−350) = 10 + 350 = 360 m.
Step 2: Calculate time taken:
Speed of descent = 6 m/min.
Time taken = Distance/Speed = 360/6 = 60 minutes = 1 hour.

Final Answer: The elevator will take 1 hour (60 minutes) to reach −350 m.

Case Study: Temperature Variation
Scenario: In a cold storage plant, the temperature at 12:00 noon was 10°C above zero. It decreases at a rate of 2°C per hour until midnight. (i) At what time would the temperature be 8°C below zero? (ii) What would be the temperature at midnight?

(i) Time when temperature is 8°C below zero (−8°C):
Initial temp = +10°C; Target temp = −8°C.
Total drop in temp = 10 − (−8) = 18°C.
Rate of drop = 2°C/hour.
Time taken = 18 ÷ 2 = 9 hours.
Time = 12 noon + 9 hours = 9:00 PM.

(ii) Temperature at midnight (12:00 AM):
Time duration from 12 noon to 12 midnight = 12 hours.
Drop in temperature = 12 × 2°C = 24°C.
Temp at midnight = 10 − 24 = −14°C.

⚡ Assertion & Reason
Assertion (A): The product of an odd number of negative integers is always negative.
Reason (R): Two negative signs pair up to produce a positive sign, leaving one unpaired negative sign when the count is odd.
Choose: (a) Both A and R are true and R is correct explanation of A. (b) Both A and R are true, but R is not correct explanation. (c) A is true, R is false. (d) A is false, R is true.
Answer: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
Explanation: Every pair of negative integers gives a positive product: (−) × (−) = (+). For an odd count 2k + 1, the k pairs give (+), and multiplying with the remaining negative integer yields (+) × (−) = (−).

Chapter 2: Fractions and Decimals

NCERT Exercise 2.1 to 2.5 — Complete Solutions as per CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

📌 Key Concepts & Formulas Summary
  • Types of Fractions:
    • Proper Fraction: Numerator < Denominator (e.g. 3/5). Value < 1.
    • Improper Fraction: Numerator ≥ Denominator (e.g. 7/4). Value ≥ 1. Can be written as Mixed Fraction: 1 3/4.
  • Multiplication of Fractions: (Numerator₁ × Numerator₂)/(Denominator₁ × Denominator₂) The operator 'of' signifies multiplication: 1/2 of 24 = 1/2 × 24 = 12.
  • Reciprocal of a Fraction: The non-zero number obtained by interchanging numerator and denominator. Product with original fraction equals 1: a/b × b/a = 1.
  • Division of Fractions: Multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor: a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c.
  • Decimal Operations:
    • Multiplying by 10, 100, 1000: Shift decimal point to the right by as many places as there are zeros.
    • Dividing by 10, 100, 1000: Shift decimal point to the left by as many places as there are zeros.
    • Division by decimal: Multiply both dividend and divisor by suitable powers of 10 to make divisor a whole number.

Case Study: Uniform Tailoring
Scenario: A school needs 1.8 metres of cloth to stitch one uniform shirt. A roll of fabric contains 45 metres of cloth. (i) How many complete shirts can be tailored from this roll? (ii) What length of fabric will be left? (iii) If each shirt requires 6 buttons, how many buttons will be needed in total?

(i) Number of shirts:
Number of shirts = (Total cloth)/(Cloth per shirt) = 45 ÷ 1.8 = 450/18 = 25 complete shirts.

(ii) Leftover fabric:
Total used = 25 × 1.8 = 45 m. Leftover = 45 − 45 = 0 metres.

(iii) Total buttons needed:
25 × 6 = 150 buttons.

⚡ HOTS Question
Each side of a regular polygon is 2.5 cm in length. The perimeter of the polygon is 12.5 cm. How many sides does the polygon have?

Let number of sides be n.
In a regular polygon, all sides are equal. Perimeter = n × side.
n × 2.5 = 12.5 ⇒ n = 12.5/2.5 = 125/25 = 5.
Therefore, the polygon has 5 sides (it is a regular pentagon).

📌 Key Concepts & Formulas Summary
  • Data & Range: Range = Highest Value - Lowest Value
  • Arithmetic Mean (Average): Mean = (Sum of all observations)/(Number of observations)
  • Mode: The observation that occurs most frequently in the given data. A data set can have more than one mode (multimodal).

📌 Key Concepts & Angle Properties Summary
  • Complementary Angles: Two angles whose sum of measures equals 90°. Each angle is called the complement of the other.
  • Supplementary Angles: Two angles whose sum of measures equals 180°. Each angle is called the supplement of the other.
  • Adjacent Angles: Angles that have a common vertex, a common arm, and non-common arms on opposite sides of the common arm.
  • Linear Pair: A pair of adjacent angles whose non-common sides form opposite rays (a straight line). Sum = 180°.
  • Vertically Opposite Angles: When two lines intersect at a point, vertically opposite angles are formed and are always equal.
  • Parallel Lines and Transversal: When two parallel lines l ∥ m are cut by a transversal t:
    • Corresponding angles are equal (∠ 1 = ∠ 5, etc.).
    • Alternate interior angles are equal (∠ 3 = ∠ 5, ∠ 4 = ∠ 6).
    • Alternate exterior angles are equal (∠ 1 = ∠ 7, ∠ 2 = ∠ 8).
    • Co-interior angles (interior angles on same side of transversal) are supplementary (∠ 4 + ∠ 5 = 180°).

📌 Key Concepts & Properties Summary
  • Rational Number: A number that can be written in the form p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0.
    • Positive: Both p and q have the same sign (e.g. 2/3, (-5)/(-7)).
    • Negative: p and q have opposite signs (e.g. (-3)/8, 5/(-9)).
    • 0 is a rational number since 0 = 0/1.
  • Standard Form: A rational number p/q is in standard form if q > 0 and p, q have no common factor other than 1.
  • Equivalent Rational Numbers: Obtained by multiplying or dividing numerator and denominator by the same non-zero integer: p/q = (p × m)/(q × m)
  • Density Property: Between any two rational numbers, there exist infinitely many rational numbers.
  • Operations on Rational Numbers:
    • Addition/Subtraction: Take LCM of denominators and adjust numerators accordingly.
    • Multiplication: a/b × c/d = (a × c)/(b × d).
    • Division: a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c (multiplication by reciprocal of divisor).

Reasoning: We can multiply the numerator and denominator by any arbitrarily large integer k. For example, choosing k = 100 gives -100/100 and 0/100, yielding 99 rational numbers.   • Exponential Form: Repeated multiplication written concisely: a × a × a ... (n times) = aⁿ, where a is the base and n is the exponent.

  • Standard Form (Scientific Notation): Any number can be expressed as k × 10ⁿ, where 1 ≤ k < 10 and n is an integer. Used to write very large numbers compactly.

📌 Key Concepts & Symmetry Properties Summary
  • Line Symmetry: A figure has line symmetry if a line can be drawn dividing it into two identical halves that coincide exactly when folded along that line.
  • Lines of Symmetry for Regular Polygons: Each regular polygon of n sides has exactly n lines of symmetry:
    • Equilateral triangle: 3 lines
    • Square: 4 lines
    • Regular pentagon: 5 lines
    • Regular hexagon: 6 lines
    • Circle: Infinitely many lines (along any diameter)
  • Rotational Symmetry: When a figure rotated around a fixed point (centre of rotation) by an angle ≤ 360° looks exactly the same as in its original orientation.
  • Order of Rotational Symmetry: The number of times a figure fits onto itself in one full 360° rotation: Order = 360°/(Angle of rotation)
  • Dual Symmetry: Figures possessing both line symmetry and rotational symmetry of order > 1 (e.g. Equilateral triangle, square, rectangle, circle, and English letters H, I, O, X).

(a) A brick
(b) A round apple
(c) A die
(d) A circular pipe
(e) An ice cream cone
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) A brick (Cuboid):
(i) Vertical cut: Rectangle
(ii) Horizontal cut: Rectangle
(b) A round apple (Sphere):
(i) Vertical cut: Circle
(ii) Horizontal cut: Circle
(c) A die (Cube):
(i) Vertical cut: Square
(ii) Horizontal cut: Square
(d) A circular pipe (Cylinder):
(i) Vertical cut (along axis): Rectangle
(ii) Horizontal cut (parallel to base): Circular ring (or Circle)
(e) An ice cream cone (Cone):
(i) Vertical cut (through apex): Isosceles Triangle
(ii) Horizontal cut (parallel to base): Circle

Q2
Verify Euler’s formula (F + V − E = 2) for:
(a) A Cuboid
(b) A Triangular Prism
(c) A Square Pyramid
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Euler's formula: F + V − E = 2.

(a) Cuboid:
• Faces (F) = 6
• Vertices (V) = 8
• Edges (E) = 12
Verification: F + V − E = 6 + 8 − 12 = 14 − 12 = 2. Verified!
(b) Triangular Prism:
• Faces (F) = 5 (2 triangular + 3 rectangular)
• Vertices (V) = 6
• Edges (E) = 9
Verification: F + V − E = 5 + 6 − 9 = 11 − 9 = 2. Verified!
(c) Square Pyramid:
• Faces (F) = 5 (1 square + 4 triangular)
• Vertices (V) = 5 (1 apex + 4 base corners)
• Edges (E) = 8 (4 base edges + 4 slant edges)
Verification: F + V − E = 5 + 5 − 8 = 10 − 8 = 2. Verified!

Euler's Formula Polyhedron Test
Problem: Can a polyhedron have 10 faces, 20 edges and 15 vertices? Justify your answer using Euler’s formula.

Given parameters:
Faces, F = 10
Edges, E = 20
Vertices, V = 15.

By Euler's Formula for any polyhedron:
F + V − E = 2.

Substituting given values:
LHS = F + V − E = 10 + 15 − 20 = 25 − 20 = 5.

Since 5 ≠ 2, the given values violate Euler’s formula.

Conclusion: No, a polyhedron cannot have 10 faces, 20 edges, and 15 vertices.
