Question 1
A circus artist is climbing a $20\text{ m}$ long rope, which is tightly stretched and tied from the top of a vertical pole to the ground. Find the height of the pole, if the angle made by the rope with the ground level is $30^\circ$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

Figure: Pole AB, Ground BC, and Tight Rope AC at 30° Elevation
  1. Mathematical Modeling:
    • Let $AB$ represent the vertical pole of height $h\text{ metres}$.
    • Let $AC$ be the tightly stretched rope of length $20\text{ m}$.
    • The angle made by the rope with the ground is $\angle ACB = 30^\circ$.
    • The pole is vertical to the ground, so $\angle ABC = 90^\circ$.
  2. Applying Trigonometric Ratio:

    In right-angled triangle $\Delta ABC$:

    $$\sin 30^\circ = \frac{\text{Opposite side}}{\text{Hypotenuse}} = \frac{AB}{AC}$$ $$\frac{1}{2} = \frac{h}{20}$$ $$h = \frac{20}{2} = 10\text{ m}$$
  3. Conclusion: The height of the vertical pole is 10 metres.

Question 2
A tree breaks due to storm and the broken part bends so that the top of the tree touches the ground making an angle $30^\circ$ with it. The distance between the foot of the tree to the point where the top touches the ground is $8\text{ m}$. Find the height of the tree.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Mathematical Representation:
    • Let the original tree before breaking be $A'B$, where $B$ is the foot of the tree.
    • Let the tree break at point $A$. The broken portion $A'A$ bends over such that the top touches the ground at point $C$.
    • Therefore, broken part $AC = A'A$.
    • Total original height of tree = $AB + AC$.
    • Given: Distance from foot $B$ to ground contact point $C$ is $BC = 8\text{ m}$.
    • Angle made with the ground: $\angle ACB = 30^\circ$. Right angle at $B$ ($\angle ABC = 90^\circ$).
  2. Finding Standing Part ($AB$):

    In right $\Delta ABC$:

    $$\tan 30^\circ = \frac{AB}{BC} \implies \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{AB}{8} \implies AB = \frac{8}{\sqrt{3}}\text{ m}$$
  3. Finding Broken Part ($AC$): $$\cos 30^\circ = \frac{BC}{AC} \implies \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{8}{AC} \implies AC = \frac{16}{\sqrt{3}}\text{ m}$$
  4. Total Height of Tree: $$\text{Height} = AB + AC = \frac{8}{\sqrt{3}} + \frac{16}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{24}{\sqrt{3}}\text{ m}$$

    Rationalising the denominator:

    $$\text{Height} = \frac{24\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3}} = \frac{24\sqrt{3}}{3} = 8\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$$
  5. Conclusion: The total height of the tree is $8\sqrt{3}\text{ metres}$ (or $\approx 13.856\text{ m}$).

Question 3
A contractor plans to install two slides for children in a park. For children below 5 years, top is at height $1.5\text{ m}$ inclined at $30^\circ$ to the ground. For elder children, top is at height $3\text{ m}$ inclined at $60^\circ$. What should be the length of the slide in each case?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Case 1 (Children below 5 years):

    Let height of slide $AB = 1.5\text{ m}$, angle of inclination $\angle ACB = 30^\circ$, slide length $= AC$.

    $$\sin 30^\circ = \frac{AB}{AC} \implies \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1.5}{AC} \implies AC = 1.5 \times 2 = 3\text{ m}$$
  2. Case 2 (Elder children):

    Let height of slide $PQ = 3\text{ m}$, angle of inclination $\angle PRQ = 60^\circ$, slide length $= PR$.

    $$\sin 60^\circ = \frac{PQ}{PR} \implies \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{3}{PR} \implies PR = \frac{6}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{6\sqrt{3}}{3} = 2\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$$
  3. Conclusion:
    Length of slide for younger children is $3\text{ m}$.
    Length of slide for elder children is $2\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$ (approx $3.464\text{ m}$).

[2 Marks]

Step-by-step Solution:

  3. Conclusion: The height of the tower is $10\sqrt{3}\text{ metres}$.

Question 5
A kite is flying at a height of $60\text{ m}$ above the ground. The string attached to the kite is temporarily tied to a point on the ground. The inclination of the string with the ground is $60^\circ$. Find the length of the string, assuming that there is no slack in the string.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Given: Height of kite $AB = 60\text{ m}$, inclination $\angle ACB = 60^\circ$. Length of string = Hypotenuse $AC = l$.
  2. Trigonometric Formula: $$\sin 60^\circ = \frac{AB}{AC} \implies \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{60}{l}$$ $$l = \frac{120}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{120\sqrt{3}}{3} = 40\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$$
  3. Conclusion: The length of the string is $40\sqrt{3}\text{ metres}$.

Question 6
A $1.5\text{ m}$ tall boy is standing at some distance from a $30\text{ m}$ tall building. The angle of elevation from his eyes to the top of the building increases from $30^\circ$ to $60^\circ$ as he walks towards the building. Find the distance he walked towards the building.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Detailed Solution:

  1. Geometric Representation:
    • Let $PQ$ be the building of total height $30\text{ m}$.
    • Let $AB$ be the initial position of the boy of height $1.5\text{ m}$, with his eye at $A$.
    • Let $CD$ be his final position of height $1.5\text{ m}$, with eye at $C$.
    • Let horizontal line of sight from eye intersect building at point $R$.
    • Effective height of building above eye level: $$PR = PQ - RQ = 30 - 1.5 = 28.5\text{ m} = \frac{57}{2}\text{ m}$$
    • Angles of elevation: $\angle PAR = 30^\circ$ and $\angle PCR = 60^\circ$.
  2. Finding distance $CR$ in $\Delta PCR$: $$\tan 60^\circ = \frac{PR}{CR} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{28.5}{CR} \implies CR = \frac{28.5}{\sqrt{3}}\text{ m}$$
  3. Finding distance $AR$ in $\Delta PAR$: $$\tan 30^\circ = \frac{PR}{AR} \implies \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{28.5}{AR} \implies AR = 28.5\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$$
  4. Distance walked ($AC$): $$AC = AR - CR = 28.5\sqrt{3} - \frac{28.5}{\sqrt{3}} = 28.5 \left(\sqrt{3} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right) = 28.5 \left(\frac{3 - 1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)$$ $$= \frac{28.5 \times 2}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{57}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{57\sqrt{3}}{3} = 19\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$$
  5. Conclusion: The distance walked towards the building is $19\sqrt{3}\text{ metres}$.

Question 7
From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and the top of a transmission tower fixed at the top of a $20\text{ m}$ high building are $45^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ respectively. Find the height of the tower.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Given:
    • Height of building $BC = 20\text{ m}$.
    • Let height of transmission tower $AB = h\text{ m}$.
    • Let point of observation on ground be $D$. Distance $CD = x\text{ m}$.
    • Angle of elevation to bottom of tower (top of building): $\angle BDC = 45^\circ$.
    • Angle of elevation to top of tower: $\angle ADC = 60^\circ$.
  2. In right $\Delta BCD$: $$\tan 45^\circ = \frac{BC}{CD} \implies 1 = \frac{20}{x} \implies x = 20\text{ m}$$
  3. In right $\Delta ACD$: $$\tan 60^\circ = \frac{AC}{CD} = \frac{AB + BC}{CD}$$ $$\sqrt{3} = \frac{h + 20}{20} \implies h + 20 = 20\sqrt{3}$$ $$h = 20\sqrt{3} - 20 = 20(\sqrt{3} - 1)\text{ m}$$
  4. Conclusion: The height of the transmission tower is $20(\sqrt{3} - 1)\text{ metres}$ (or $\approx 14.64\text{ m}$).

Question 8
A statue, $1.6\text{ m}$ tall, stands on the top of a pedestal. From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of the statue is $60^\circ$ and from the same point the angle of elevation of the top of the pedestal is $45^\circ$. Find the height of the pedestal.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Given: Height of statue $AB = 1.6\text{ m}$. Let height of pedestal $BC = h\text{ m}$. Point of observation $D$ on ground at distance $CD = x$.
  2. In right $\Delta BCD$: $$\tan 45^\circ = \frac{BC}{CD} \implies 1 = \frac{h}{x} \implies x = h$$
  3. In right $\Delta ACD$: $$\tan 60^\circ = \frac{AC}{CD} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{h + 1.6}{h}$$ $$h\sqrt{3} = h + 1.6 \implies h(\sqrt{3} - 1) = 1.6$$ $$h = \frac{1.6}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = \frac{1.6(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{(\sqrt{3} - 1)(\sqrt{3} + 1)} = \frac{1.6(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{3 - 1} = 0.8(\sqrt{3} + 1)\text{ m}$$
  4. Conclusion: The height of the pedestal is $0.8(\sqrt{3} + 1)\text{ metres}$.

Question 9
The angle of elevation of the top of a building from the foot of the tower is $30^\circ$ and the angle of elevation of the top of the tower from the foot of the building is $60^\circ$. If the tower is $50\text{ m}$ high, find the height of the building.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Given: Let tower be $AB = 50\text{ m}$ and building be $CD = h\text{ m}$. Ground distance between bases $B$ and $D$ is $BD = d$.
    From $B$, angle to top of building: $\angle CBD = 30^\circ$.
    From $D$, angle to top of tower: $\angle ADB = 60^\circ$.
  2. In right $\Delta ABD$: $$\tan 60^\circ = \frac{AB}{BD} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{50}{d} \implies d = \frac{50}{\sqrt{3}}\text{ m}$$
  3. In right $\Delta CDB$: $$\tan 30^\circ = \frac{CD}{BD} \implies \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{h}{d} \implies h = \frac{d}{\sqrt{3}}$$ $$h = \frac{\frac{50}{\sqrt{3}}}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{50}{3} = 16\frac{2}{3}\text{ m}$$
  4. Conclusion: The height of the building is $16\frac{2}{3}\text{ m}$ (or $\approx 16.67\text{ m}$).

Question 10
Two poles of equal heights are standing opposite each other on either side of the road, which is $80\text{ m}$ wide. From a point between them on the road, the angles of elevation of the top of the poles are $60^\circ$ and $30^\circ$ respectively. Find the height of the poles and the distances of the point from the poles.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Detailed Solution:

  1. Given:
    • Width of road $BD = 80\text{ m}$.
    • Two poles of equal height: $AB = CD = h\text{ m}$.
    • Let point on the road be $P$. Let $BP = x\text{ m}$, then $PD = (80 - x)\text{ m}$.
    • Angle of elevation from $P$ to top $A$: $\angle APB = 60^\circ$.
    • Angle of elevation from $P$ to top $C$: $\angle CPD = 30^\circ$.
  2. In right $\Delta ABP$: $$\tan 60^\circ = \frac{AB}{BP} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{h}{x} \implies h = x\sqrt{3} \quad \text{--- (1)}$$
  3. In right $\Delta CDP$: $$\tan 30^\circ = \frac{CD}{PD} \implies \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{h}{80 - x} \implies h = \frac{80 - x}{\sqrt{3}} \quad \text{--- (2)}$$
  4. Equating (1) and (2): $$x\sqrt{3} = \frac{80 - x}{\sqrt{3}}$$ $$3x = 80 - x \implies 4x = 80 \implies x = 20\text{ m}$$
  5. Finding Distances and Height:
    • Distance from first pole $BP = 20\text{ m}$.
    • Distance from second pole $PD = 80 - 20 = 60\text{ m}$.
    • Height of each pole $h = x\sqrt{3} = 20\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$.
  6. Conclusion: Height of poles is $20\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$, and distances from the poles are $20\text{ m}$ and $60\text{ m}$.

From another point $20\text{ m}$ away from this point on the line joining this point to the foot of the tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is $30^\circ$. Find the height of the tower and the width of the canal.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Given: Height of tower $AB = h\text{ m}$. Canal width $BC = x\text{ m}$. Distance $CD = 20\text{ m}$.
    Angles: $\angle ACB = 60^\circ$ and $\angle ADB = 30^\circ$.
  2. In right $\Delta ABC$: $$\tan 60^\circ = \frac{AB}{BC} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{h}{x} \implies h = x\sqrt{3} \quad \text{--- (1)}$$

  4. Total Height of Tower: $$CD = CE + ED = 7\sqrt{3} + 7 = 7(\sqrt{3} + 1)\text{ m}$$
  5. Conclusion: The height of the cable tower is $7(\sqrt{3} + 1)\text{ metres}$.

Question 14
A $1.2\text{ m}$ tall girl spots a balloon moving with the wind in a horizontal line at a height of $88.2\text{ m}$ from the ground. The angle of elevation of the balloon from the eyes of the girl at any instant is $60^\circ$. After some time, the angle of elevation reduces to $30^\circ$. Find the distance travelled by the balloon during the interval.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Effective Height above Eye Level:

    Total height $= 88.2\text{ m}$, Height of girl $= 1.2\text{ m}$.

    $$H = 88.2 - 1.2 = 87\text{ m}$$
  2. Positions of Balloon:

    Let initial balloon position be $P$ and final position be $Q$.

    In right $\Delta PAC$ (initial position, angle $60^\circ$):

    $$\tan 60^\circ = \frac{H}{AC} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{87}{AC} \implies AC = \frac{87}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{87\sqrt{3}}{3} = 29\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$$
  3. In right $\Delta QAD$ (final position, angle $30^\circ$): $$\tan 30^\circ = \frac{H}{AD} \implies \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{87}{AD} \implies AD = 87\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$$
  4. Distance travelled by Balloon: $$\text{Distance} = CD = AD - AC = 87\sqrt{3} - 29\sqrt{3} = 58\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$$
  5. Conclusion: The distance travelled by the balloon is $58\sqrt{3}\text{ metres}$.

Question 15
A straight highway leads to the foot of a tower. A man standing at the top of the tower observes a car at an angle of depression of $30^\circ$, which is approaching the foot of the tower with a uniform speed. Six seconds later, the angle of depression of the car is found to be $60^\circ$. Find the time taken by the car to reach the foot of the tower from this point.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Given: Height of tower $AB = h$. Let uniform speed of car be $v\text{ m/s}$.
    Let car be at $C$ initially, and reaches $D$ after 6 seconds.
    Distance $CD = \text{speed} \times \text{time} = 6v$.
    Angles of depression: from top $A$, $\angle ACB = 30^\circ$ and $\angle ADB = 60^\circ$.
  2. In right $\Delta ABD$: $$\tan 60^\circ = \frac{AB}{BD} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{h}{BD} \implies BD = \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}} \quad \text{--- (1)}$$
  3. In right $\Delta ABC$: $$\tan 30^\circ = \frac{AB}{BC} \implies \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{h}{BC} \implies BC = h\sqrt{3} \quad \text{--- (2)}$$
  4. Relating Distances: $$CD = BC - BD = h\sqrt{3} - \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{3h - h}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{2h}{\sqrt{3}}$$

    Since $CD = 6v$:

    $$6v = \frac{2h}{\sqrt{3}} \implies \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}} = 3v$$
  5. Finding Time to reach foot from $D$:

    Distance remaining is $BD = \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}} = 3v$.

    $$\text{Time taken} = \frac{\text{Distance } BD}{\text{Speed } v} = \frac{3v}{v} = 3\text{ seconds}$$
  6. Conclusion: The time taken by the car to reach the foot of the tower from point $D$ is 3 seconds.

Question 16
The angles of elevation of the top of a tower from two points at a distance of $4\text{ m}$ and $9\text{ m}$ from the base of the tower and in the same straight line with it are complementary. Prove that the height of the tower is $6\text{ m}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Proof:

  1. Given: Let $AB = h\text{ m}$ be the tower.
    Points $C$ and $D$ lie in the same straight line with base $B$, where $BC = 4\text{ m}$ and $BD = 9\text{ m}$.
    Let $\angle ACB = \theta$. Since angles are complementary, $\angle ADB = 90^\circ - \theta$.
  2. In right $\Delta ABC$: $$\tan \theta = \frac{AB}{BC} = \frac{h}{4} \quad \text{--- (1)}$$
  3. In right $\Delta ABD$: $$\tan(90^\circ - \theta) = \frac{AB}{BD} \implies \cot \theta = \frac{h}{9} \quad \text{--- (2)}$$
  4. Multiplying Equations (1) and (2): $$\tan \theta \cdot \cot \theta = \left(\frac{h}{4}\right) \times \left(\frac{h}{9}\right)$$

    Since $\tan \theta \cdot \cot \theta = 1$:

    $$1 = \frac{h^2}{36} \implies h^2 = 36$$ $$h = \sqrt{36} = 6\text{ m} \quad (\text{height is always positive})$$
  5. Conclusion: Hence proved, the height of the tower is 6 metres.

Case-Study Competency Question
A surveillance drone hovers directly above a highway checkpost at an altitude of $120\text{ m}$. The drone camera spots two suspicious cars traveling in opposite directions along the straight road. The angles of depression of the two cars are $45^\circ$ and $30^\circ$ respectively.
(i) Find the distance of the first car from the checkpost base.
(ii) Find the distance of the second car from the checkpost base.
(iii) What is the total straight-line distance between the two cars? (Take $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$)

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) In $\Delta$ with angle $45^\circ$: $\tan 45^\circ = \frac{120}{d_1} \implies 1 = \frac{120}{d_1} \implies d_1 = 120\text{ m}$.
  2. (ii) In $\Delta$ with angle $30^\circ$: $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{120}{d_2} \implies \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{120}{d_2} \implies d_2 = 120\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$.
  3. (iii) Since cars are in opposite directions from the checkpost:
    $$\text{Total distance} = d_1 + d_2 = 120 + 120\sqrt{3} = 120(1 + \sqrt{3}) = 120(2.732) = 327.84\text{ m}.$$

Assertion (A): As an observer moves closer to the base of a vertical tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the tower increases.
Reason (R): For an acute angle $\theta$, the value of $\tan \theta$ increases as $\theta$ increases from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$.

CBSE Board Solution:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Mathematical Explanation: Let height be $h$ and horizontal distance be $x$. Then $\tan \theta = \frac{h}{x} \implies x = \frac{h}{\tan \theta}$. As the observer moves closer, $x$ decreases, which requires $\tan \theta$ to increase. Since $\tan \theta$ is strictly increasing in $(0, 90^\circ)$, $\theta$ must increase.
