Theorem 6.1: Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales Theorem)
Question 1
State and prove the Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales Theorem).
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Statement of the Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales Theorem):

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, then the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.

Mathematical Proof:

  1. Given: A triangle $\Delta ABC$ in which a line $DE$ parallel to side $BC$ intersects sides $AB$ and $AC$ at points $D$ and $E$ respectively ($DE \parallel BC$).
  2. To Prove: $$\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$$
  3. Construction:
    • Join $B$ to $E$ and $C$ to $D$.
    • Draw $DM \perp AC$ and $EN \perp AB$.
    4. Geometric Construction for Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales Theorem)
  4. Proof Steps:

    Recall that $\text{Area of a triangle} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$.

    In $\Delta ADE$, taking $AD$ as the base, the corresponding altitude is $EN$:

    $$\text{ar}(\Delta ADE) = \frac{1}{2} \times AD \times EN \quad \text{--- (1)}$$

    In $\Delta BDE$, taking $DB$ as the base, the altitude is also $EN$ (since $\Delta BDE$ is an obtuse-angled triangle):

    $$\text{ar}(\Delta BDE) = \frac{1}{2} \times DB \times EN \quad \text{--- (2)}$$

    Dividing equation (1) by equation (2):

    $$\frac{\text{ar}(\Delta ADE)}{\text{ar}(\Delta BDE)} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \times AD \times EN}{\frac{1}{2} \times DB \times EN} = \frac{AD}{DB} \quad \text{--- (3)}$$

    Similarly, taking $AE$ as the base of $\Delta ADE$, the altitude is $DM$:

    $$\text{ar}(\Delta ADE) = \frac{1}{2} \times AE \times DM \quad \text{--- (4)}$$

    In $\Delta CDE$, taking $EC$ as the base, the altitude is $DM$:

    $$\text{ar}(\Delta CDE) = \frac{1}{2} \times EC \times DM \quad \text{--- (5)}$$

    Dividing equation (4) by equation (5):

    $$\frac{\text{ar}(\Delta ADE)}{\text{ar}(\Delta CDE)} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \times AE \times DM}{\frac{1}{2} \times EC \times DM} = \frac{AE}{EC} \quad \text{--- (6)}$$
  5. Geometric Identity on Same Base & Between Same Parallels:

    Note that $\Delta BDE$ and $\Delta CDE$ lie on the same base $DE$ and between the same parallel lines $DE$ and $BC$.

    $$\text{ar}(\Delta BDE) = \text{ar}(\Delta CDE) \quad \text{--- (7)}$$
  6. Conclusion:

    From equations (3), (6), and (7), the left-hand sides are equal:

    $$\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$$

    Hence, the theorem is proved.

Question 3
In the given figure, $LM \parallel CB$ and $LN \parallel CD$, prove that $\frac{AM}{AB} = \frac{AN}{AD}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Given: In the given quadrilateral/triangle figure, $LM \parallel CB$ and $LN \parallel CD$.

To Prove: $\frac{AM}{AB} = \frac{AN}{AD}$.

Figure: Trapezium ABCD with Diagonals AC and BD Intersecting at O

Proof:

  1. In $\Delta ABC$, since $LM \parallel CB$, by Basic Proportionality Theorem (BPT):
    $$\frac{AM}{MB} = \frac{AL}{LC}$$ Taking reciprocal and adding 1 to both sides (or using corollary $\frac{AM}{AM + MB} = \frac{AL}{AL + LC}$):
    $$\frac{AM}{AB} = \frac{AL}{AC} \quad \text{--- (1)}$$
  2. In $\Delta ADC$, since $LN \parallel CD$, by BPT:
    $$\frac{AN}{ND} = \frac{AL}{LC}$$ Similarly, using the corollary:
    $$\frac{AN}{AD} = \frac{AL}{AC} \quad \text{--- (2)}$$
  3. Comparing equations (1) and (2):
    Both $\frac{AM}{AB}$ and $\frac{AN}{AD}$ are equal to $\frac{AL}{AC}$.
    $$\frac{AM}{AB} = \frac{AN}{AD}$$

Hence proved.

Question 4
$ABCD$ is a trapezium in which $AB \parallel DC$ and its diagonals intersect each other at the point $O$. Show that $\frac{AO}{BO} = \frac{CO}{DO}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Given: $ABCD$ is a trapezium in which $AB \parallel DC$ and diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ intersect at point $O$.

To Prove: $\frac{AO}{BO} = \frac{CO}{DO}$ (or $\frac{AO}{CO} = \frac{BO}{DO}$).

Construction: Through point $O$, draw a line $OE \parallel AB$ intersecting $AD$ at $E$. (Since $AB \parallel DC$, $OE \parallel DC$ as well).

Figure: Trapezium ABCD with Diagonals AC and BD Intersecting at O

Proof:

  1. In $\Delta ADC$, since $OE \parallel DC$, by BPT:
    $$\frac{AE}{ED} = \frac{AO}{OC} \quad \text{--- (1)}$$
  2. In $\Delta DAB$, since $EO \parallel AB$, by BPT:
    $$\frac{ED}{AE} = \frac{DO}{OB}$$ Taking reciprocal on both sides:
    $$\frac{AE}{ED} = \frac{BO}{OD} \quad \text{--- (2)}$$
  3. From equations (1) and (2):
    $$\frac{AO}{OC} = \frac{BO}{OD}$$ Cross-multiplying or rearranging:
    $$\frac{AO}{BO} = \frac{CO}{DO}$$

Hence proved.

NCERT Exercise 6.3: Criteria for Similarity of Triangles
Question 5
State which pairs of triangles are similar. Write the similarity criterion used and write the pairs of similar triangles in symbolic form: (i) $\angle A=\angle P=60^\circ, \angle B=\angle Q=80^\circ$, (ii) Triangle with sides 2, 2.5, 3 and triangle with sides 6, 4, 5.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Criteria for Similarity of Triangles:

  • AAA / AA: If two angles of one triangle are respectively equal to two angles of another triangle, the two triangles are similar.
  • SSS: If the corresponding sides of two triangles are proportional, then the triangles are similar.
  • SAS: If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of the other and the sides including these angles are proportional, then the triangles are similar.

Analysis of Problems:

  1. (i) $\Delta ABC$ and $\Delta PQR$ with $\angle A = \angle P = 60^\circ, \angle B = \angle Q = 80^\circ, \angle C = \angle R = 40^\circ$:
    By AAA similarity criterion, $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta PQR$.
  2. (ii) $AB=2, BC=2.5, CA=3$ and $PQ=6, QR=4, PR=5$:
    $$\frac{AB}{QR} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \frac{BC}{PR} = \frac{2.5}{5} = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \frac{CA}{PQ} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$$ By SSS similarity criterion, $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta QRP$.

Question 6
A girl of height 90 cm is walking away from the base of a lamp-post at a speed of 1.2 m/s. If the lamp is 3.6 m above the ground, find the length of her shadow after 4 seconds.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Understand the Geometric Model

  • Let $AB$ be the lamp-post: $AB = 3.6\text{ m}$.
  • Let $CD$ be the girl: $CD = 90\text{ cm} = 0.9\text{ m}$.
  • Speed of the girl $= 1.2\text{ m/s}$.
  • Time walked $= 4\text{ seconds}$.
  • Distance walked by the girl away from the lamp-post: $$BD = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} = 1.2 \times 4 = 4.8\text{ m}$$
  • Let the length of her shadow after 4 seconds be $DE = x\text{ metres}$.
Figure: Similarity Model for Lamp-Post, Girl, and Shadow (AA Criterion)

Step 2: Prove Triangles Similar

In $\Delta ABE$ and $\Delta CDE$:

  • $\angle B = \angle D = 90^\circ$ (both the lamp-post and the girl stand vertically upright).
  • $\angle E = \angle E$ (common angle of elevation to the top of the lamp).

Therefore, by AA Similarity Criterion:

$$\Delta ABE \sim \Delta CDE$$

Step 3: Equate Corresponding Sides & Solve for $x$

$$\frac{BE}{DE} = \frac{AB}{CD}$$ $$\frac{BD + DE}{DE} = \frac{3.6}{0.9}$$ $$\frac{4.8 + x}{x} = 4$$ $$4.8 + x = 4x \implies 3x = 4.8 \implies x = \frac{4.8}{3} = 1.6\text{ m}$$

Final Answer: The length of the girl's shadow after 4 seconds is $1.6\text{ metres}$ (or $160\text{ cm}$).

Question 7
A vertical pole of length 6 m casts a shadow 4 m long on the ground and at the same time a tower casts a shadow 28 m long. Find the height of the tower.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Geometric Setup

  • Vertical pole $AB = 6\text{ m}$, shadow $BC = 4\text{ m}$.
  • Tower $PQ = h\text{ m}$, shadow $QR = 28\text{ m}$.
  • Both observations are taken at the same time, meaning the sun's altitude (angle of elevation) $\angle C = \angle R$.

Step 2: Proving Similarity

In $\Delta ABC$ and $\Delta PQR$:

  • $\angle B = \angle Q = 90^\circ$ (vertical structures to the horizontal ground).
  • $\angle C = \angle R$ (angular elevation of the sun is identical at the same instant).

By AA Similarity Criterion: $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta PQR$.

Step 3: Calculate Height of Tower

$$\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{BC}{QR}$$ $$\frac{6}{h} = \frac{4}{28} = \frac{1}{7}$$ $$h = 6 \times 7 = 42\text{ m}$$

Final Answer: The height of the tower is $42\text{ metres}$.

Question 8
$D$ is a point on the side $BC$ of a triangle $ABC$ such that $\angle ADC = \angle BAC$. Show that $CA^2 = CB \times CD$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Given: $D$ is a point on side $BC$ of $\Delta ABC$ such that $\angle ADC = \angle BAC$.

To Prove: $CA^2 = CB \times CD$.

Figure: Trapezium ABCD with Diagonals AC and BD Intersecting at O

Proof:

  1. In $\Delta ABC$ and $\Delta DAC$:
    • $\angle BAC = \angle ADC$ (Given)
    • $\angle C = \angle C$ (Common angle to both triangles)
  2. Therefore, by AA Similarity Criterion: $$\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DAC$$
  3. Since corresponding sides of similar triangles are proportional: $$\frac{CA}{CD} = \frac{CB}{CA}$$
  4. Cross-multiplying gives: $$CA \times CA = CB \times CD \implies CA^2 = CB \times CD$$

Hence proved.

(i) Using Theorem 6.1 (BPT), prove that a line drawn through the mid-point of one side of a triangle parallel to another side bisects the third side. (ii) Prove that the line joining the mid-points of any two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side.

✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

By Converse of BPT: $\mathbf{DE \parallel BC}$. Hence proved.

A surveyor uses a 1.5 m tall sighting rod to measure the height of a monument. When placed 12 m from the base of the monument, the top of the rod and the top of the monument align with the line of sight from the ground 3 m behind the rod. Find the height of the monument.

  4. $H = 5 \times 1.5 = 7.5\text{ metres}$.
  5. Answer: The height of the monument is 7.5 metres.
