NCERT Exercise 14.1: Theoretical Probability & Experiments
Question 1 to 4
Fundamental Concepts & MCQs:
1. Complete the statements: (i) $P(E) + P(\text{not } E) = \dots$, (ii) Probability of impossible event, (iii) Probability of sure event, (iv) Sum of probabilities of all elementary events, (v) Bounds of probability.
2. Which of the following cannot be the probability of an event?
(A) $\frac{2}{3}$ (B) $-1.5$ (C) $15\%$ (D) $0.7$
3. If $P(E) = 0.05$, what is the probability of 'not $E$'?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solutions:

  1. 1. Statement Completions:
    • (i) $P(E) + P(\text{not } E) = \mathbf{1}$.
    • (ii) The probability of an event that cannot happen is $\mathbf{0}$. Such an event is called an impossible event.
    • (iii) The probability of an event that is certain to happen is $\mathbf{1}$. Such an event is called a sure or certain event.
    • (iv) The sum of the probabilities of all the elementary events of an experiment is $\mathbf{1}$.
    • (v) The probability of an event is greater than or equal to $\mathbf{0}$ and less than or equal to $\mathbf{1}$ ($0 \le P(E) \le 1$).
  2. 2. Valid Probability Bounds:

    Probability of any event $E$ satisfies $0 \le P(E) \le 1$. Probability cannot be negative.

    Therefore, (B) $-1.5$ cannot be the probability of an event.

  3. 3. Finding $P(\text{not } E)$:

    Using complementary event identity:

    $$P(E) + P(\bar{E}) = 1$$ $$P(\text{not } E) = 1 - P(E) = 1 - 0.05 = 0.95$$

Question 8 & 9 [Marbles and Balls]
(i) A bag contains 3 red balls and 5 black balls. A ball is drawn at random from the bag. What is the probability that the ball drawn is (a) red? (b) not red?
(ii) A box contains 5 red marbles, 8 white marbles and 4 green marbles. One marble is taken out of the box at random. What is the probability that the marble taken out will be (a) red? (b) white? (c) not green?
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Detailed Solutions:

  1. (i) Bag with Red and Black Balls:
    • Total balls $= 3 + 5 = 8$. Total possible outcomes $n(S) = 8$.
    • (a) Favourable outcomes for red $n(R) = 3$: $$P(\text{Red}) = \frac{n(R)}{n(S)} = \frac{3}{8}$$
    • (b) Probability of not red: $$P(\text{not Red}) = 1 - P(\text{Red}) = 1 - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{5}{8}$$
  2. (ii) Box with Red, White, and Green Marbles:
    • Total marbles $= 5 + 8 + 4 = 17$. $n(S) = 17$.
    • (a) Red marble: $$P(\text{Red}) = \frac{5}{17}$$
    • (b) White marble: $$P(\text{White}) = \frac{8}{17}$$
    • (c) Not green marble:

      Favourable outcomes = Red + White $= 5 + 8 = 13$ (or $1 - P(\text{Green}) = 1 - \frac{4}{17}$):

      $$P(\text{not Green}) = \frac{13}{17}$$

Question 13 [Single Die Problems]
A die is thrown once. Find the probability of getting:
(i) a prime number
(ii) a number lying between 2 and 6
(iii) an odd number.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Detailed Solution:

  1. Sample Space of a Single Die: $$S = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\} \implies n(S) = 6$$
  2. (i) Getting a prime number:

    Prime numbers on a die are: $\{2, 3, 5\}$ (Note: 1 is neither prime nor composite).

    $$n(E_1) = 3$$ $$P(\text{Prime number}) = \frac{n(E_1)}{n(S)} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$$
  3. (ii) Getting a number lying between 2 and 6:

    Numbers strictly between 2 and 6 are: $\{3, 4, 5\}$.

    $$n(E_2) = 3$$ $$P(\text{Between 2 and 6}) = \frac{n(E_2)}{n(S)} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$$
  4. (iii) Getting an odd number:

    Odd numbers are: $\{1, 3, 5\}$.

    $$n(E_3) = 3$$ $$P(\text{Odd number}) = \frac{n(E_3)}{n(S)} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$$
  5. Conclusion: In each case, the probability is $\frac{1}{2}$.

Question 14 & 15 [Deck of 52 Cards]
One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. Find the probability of getting:
(i) a king of red colour
(ii) a face card
(iii) a red face card
(iv) the jack of hearts
(v) a spade
(vi) the queen of diamonds.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Complete Solutions:

    Total cards $n(S) = 52$ (26 Red: Hearts, Diamonds; 26 Black: Spades, Clubs).

    Face cards (Kings, Queens, Jacks) $= 3 \times 4 = 12$ cards.

  2. Calculations:
    • (i) King of red colour: King of hearts and King of diamonds $\implies 2$ cards. $$P = \frac{2}{52} = \frac{1}{26}$$
    • (ii) A face card: Total 12 face cards. $$P = \frac{12}{52} = \frac{3}{13}$$
    • (iii) A red face card: 3 hearts $+ 3$ diamonds $= 6$ cards. $$P = \frac{6}{52} = \frac{3}{26}$$
    • (iv) The jack of hearts: Exactly 1 card. $$P = \frac{1}{52}$$
    • (v) A spade: Total 13 cards in spades suit. $$P = \frac{13}{52} = \frac{1}{4}$$
    • (vi) The queen of diamonds: Exactly 1 card. $$P = \frac{1}{52}$$

Question 24 [Two Dice Experiment]
A die is thrown twice. What is the probability that:
(i) 5 will not come up either time?
(ii) 5 will come up at least once?
[Hint: Throwing a die twice and throwing two dice simultaneously are treated as the same experiment.]
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Detailed Solution:

  1. Total Sample Space:

    When a die is thrown twice, total number of outcomes:

    $$n(S) = 6 \times 6 = 36$$ $$S = \{(1,1), (1,2), \dots, (6,6)\}$$
  2. Outcomes where 5 comes up at least once ($A$):

    These outcomes have 5 on the first throw or second throw (or both):

    $$A = \{(1,5), (2,5), (3,5), (4,5), (5,5), (6,5), (5,1), (5,2), (5,3), (5,4), (5,6)\}$$ $$n(A) = 11$$
  3. (ii) Probability that 5 will come up at least once: $$P(A) = \frac{n(A)}{n(S)} = \frac{11}{36}$$
  4. (i) Probability that 5 will not come up either time:

    This is the complement of event $A$:

    $$P(\bar{A}) = 1 - P(A) = 1 - \frac{11}{36} = \frac{36 - 11}{36} = \frac{25}{36}$$

    Verification: For each die, there are 5 outcomes other than 5. Total pairs $= 5 \times 5 = 25$. Probability $= \frac{25}{36}$.

  5. Conclusion:
    (i) $P(5\text{ will not come up}) = \mathbf{\frac{25}{36}}$
    (ii) $P(5\text{ will come up at least once}) = \mathbf{\frac{11}{36}}$

NCERT Exercise 14.1 • Q2
Which of the following experiments have equally likely outcomes? Explain. (i) A driver attempts to start a car. The car starts or does not start. (ii) A player attempts to shoot a basketball. She/he shoots or misses the shot. (iii) A trial is made to answer a true-false question. The answer is right or wrong. (iv) A baby is born. It is a boy or a girl.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution
  1. (i) Not equally likely: Whether a car starts depends on its mechanical condition, fuel, battery, etc. It is not an equally likely 50-50 chance.
  2. (ii) Not equally likely: A player's success depends on skill, practice, and defense. The chances are not equally likely.
  3. (iii) Equally likely: For a true-false question with only two possible alternatives, the chances of being right or wrong are exactly equal ($1/2$).
  4. (iv) Equally likely: A newborn baby has an equal natural likelihood of being a boy or a girl ($1/2$).

NCERT Exercise 14.1 • Q3 & Q4
(i) Why is tossing a coin considered to be a fair way of deciding which team should get the ball at the beginning of a football game? (ii) Which of the following cannot be the probability of an event? (a) $2/3$, (b) $-1.5$, (c) $15\%$, (d) $0.7$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

(i) Fairness of Coin Toss: When a fair coin is tossed, the only two possible outcomes—head and tail—are mutually exclusive and equally likely ($P(H) = P(T) = 0.5$). The outcome cannot be predicted beforehand, making it completely fair and unbiased.

(ii) Probability bounds: The probability of any event must satisfy $0 \le P(E) \le 1$.
Therefore, (b) -1.5 cannot be the probability of an event, because probability can never be negative.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Area of entire rectangular region: $A_{\text{rectangle}} = l \times b = 3 \times 2 = \mathbf{6\text{ m}^2}$
  2. Diameter of circle $= 1\text{ m} \implies$ radius $r = 1/2\text{ m} = 0.5\text{ m}$.
    Area of circular region: $A_{\text{circle}} = \pi r^2 = \pi \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \mathbf{\frac{\pi}{4}\text{ m}^2}$
  3. Probability that die lands inside circle: $P = \frac{A_{\text{circle}}}{A_{\text{rectangle}}} = \frac{\pi / 4}{6} = \mathbf{\frac{\pi}{24}} \approx 0.1309$

Answer: The probability is $\pi/24$.

A lot consists of 144 ball pens of which 20 are defective and the others are good. Nuri will buy a pen if it is good, but will not buy if it is defective. The shopkeeper draws one pen at random and gives it to her. What is the probability that: (i) She will buy it? (ii) She will not buy it?

  2. Defective pens $= 20$.
  3. Good pens $= 144 - 20 = 124$.
  4. (i) Probability that she will buy it (pen is good): $P(\text{buys}) = \frac{124}{144} = \mathbf{\frac{31}{36}}$

NCERT Exercise 14.1 • Q22 & Q23
(i) Two dice are thrown at the same time. Find the probability of getting sum of numbers as: (a) 2, (b) 8, (c) 13. (ii) A game consists of tossing a one rupee coin 3 times and noting its outcome each time. Hanif wins if all the tosses give the same result (three heads or three tails), and loses otherwise. Calculate the probability that Hanif will lose the game.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

(i) Two dice sum ($6 \times 6 = 36$ outcomes):
(a) Sum $= 2$: pair $(1, 1) \implies P = \mathbf{\frac{1}{36}}$.
(b) Sum $= 8$: pairs $(2, 6), (3, 5), (4, 4), (5, 3), (6, 2) \implies P = \frac{5}{36} = \mathbf{\frac{5}{36}}$.
(c) Sum $= 13$: impossible $\implies P = \mathbf{0}$.

(ii) Hanif's coin toss game (8 outcomes):
$S = \{HHH, HHT, HTH, THH, HTT, THT, TTH, TTT\} \implies n(S) = 8$.
Wins: $\{HHH, TTT\}$ (2 outcomes).
Loses: remaining 6 outcomes.
$P(\text{Hanif loses}) = \frac{6}{8} = \mathbf{\frac{3}{4}}$

NCERT Exercise 14.1 • Q24 & Q25
(i) A die is thrown twice. What is the probability that: (a) 5 will not come up either time? (b) 5 will come up at least once? (ii) Which of the following arguments are correct and which are not? Give reasons: (a) If two coins are tossed simultaneously there are three possible outcomes—two heads, two tails or one of each. Therefore, each has probability 1/3. (b) If a die is thrown, there are two possible outcomes—an odd number or an even number. Therefore, probability of odd number is 1/2.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

(i) Die thrown twice (36 outcomes):
Outcomes with at least one 5: $(5, 1), (5, 2), (5, 3), (5, 4), (5, 5), (5, 6), (1, 5), (2, 5), (3, 5), (4, 5), (6, 5)$ (11 outcomes).
(a) $P(\text{5 not either time}) = \frac{36 - 11}{36} = \mathbf{\frac{25}{36}}$.
(b) $P(\text{5 at least once}) = \mathbf{\frac{11}{36}}$.

(ii) Arguments:
(a) Incorrect: The sample space $\{HH, HT, TH, TT\}$ has 4 equally likely outcomes, so "one of each" has probability $2/4 = 1/2$, not $1/3$.
(b) Correct: There are 3 odd $\{1, 3, 5\}$ and 3 even $\{2, 4, 6\}$ numbers, giving equal probability $3/6 = 1/2$.

Case-Study Competency Question
A quality control laboratory tests a consignment of 400 rechargeable lithium-ion battery cells. 32 cells are found to have a defective internal electrolyte seal.
(i) If a cell is drawn at random, what is the probability that it is defective?
(ii) What is the probability that it is good (non-defective)?
(iii) If 4 defective cells are discarded and 24 new good cells are added to the batch, find the updated probability of drawing a non-defective cell.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Total cells $N = 400$. Defective $n(D) = 32$.
    $$P(\text{Defective}) = \frac{32}{400} = \frac{2}{25} = 0.08.$$
  2. (ii) Non-defective cells $n(G) = 400 - 32 = 368$.
    $$P(\text{Non-defective}) = \frac{368}{400} = \frac{23}{25} = 0.92.$$
  3. (iii) New total cells $= 400 - 4 + 24 = 420$.
    New good cells $= 368 + 24 = 392$.
    $$P'(\text{Non-defective}) = \frac{392}{420} = \frac{14}{15} \approx 0.933.$$
Assertion & Reason HOTS Question
Assertion (A): When two coins are tossed simultaneously, the probability of getting at most one head is $\frac{3}{4}$.
Reason (R): The sample space of tossing two coins simultaneously has 4 outcomes: $\{HH, HT, TH, TT\}$.

CBSE Board Solution:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: The sample space consists of 4 equally likely outcomes $\{HH, HT, TH, TT\}$ (Reason R). The event 'at most one head' includes 0 heads or 1 head: $\{HT, TH, TT\}$, giving 3 favorable outcomes. Therefore, $P(\text{at most 1 head}) = \frac{3}{4}$ (Assertion A).
