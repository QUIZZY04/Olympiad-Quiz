Q1
Answer the following: (i) Find the 10th term of the AP: 2, 7, 12, ...; (ii) Which term of the AP: 21, 18, 15, ... is -81? Also, is any term 0? (iii) Check whether -150 is a term of the AP: 11, 8, 5, 2, ...
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Formula Statement

The $n^{\text{th}}$ term (general term) of an Arithmetic Progression with first term $a$ and common difference $d$ is given by:

$$a_n = a + (n - 1)d$$

Step 2: Calculations

  1. (i) Find the $10^{\text{th}}$ term of the AP: $2, 7, 12, \dots$
    Here $a = 2$, common difference $d = 7 - 2 = 5$, and $n = 10$.
    $$a_{10} = 2 + (10 - 1) \times 5 = 2 + 9 \times 5 = 2 + 45 = 47.$$ Conclusion: The $10^{\text{th}}$ term is $47$.
  2. (ii) Which term of the AP: $21, 18, 15, \dots$ is $-81$? Also, is any term $0$?
    Here $a = 21, d = 18 - 21 = -3$. Let the $n^{\text{th}}$ term be $-81$.
    $$a_n = a + (n - 1)d = -81$$ $$21 + (n - 1)(-3) = -81 \implies -3(n - 1) = -81 - 21 = -102$$ $$n - 1 = \frac{-102}{-3} = 34 \implies n = 35.$$ Therefore, the $35^{\text{th}}$ term is $-81$.

    Checking if any term is $0$:
    $$a + (n - 1)d = 0 \implies 21 + (n - 1)(-3) = 0 \implies 3(n - 1) = 21 \implies n - 1 = 7 \implies n = 8.$$ Yes, the $8^{\text{th}}$ term of the AP is $0$.
  3. (iii) Check whether $-150$ is a term of the AP: $11, 8, 5, 2, \dots$
    Here $a = 11, d = 8 - 11 = -3$. Let $a_n = -150$.
    $$11 + (n - 1)(-3) = -150 \implies -3(n - 1) = -150 - 11 = -161$$ $$n - 1 = \frac{161}{3} \implies n = \frac{161}{3} + 1 = \frac{164}{3} = 54\frac{2}{3}.$$ Since $n$ must be a positive integer (counting number), $n = 54\frac{2}{3}$ is not possible.
    Hence, $-150$ is not a term of the given AP.

Q2
If the sum of the first 14 terms of an AP is 1050 and its first term is 10, find the 20th term.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Formula for Sum of $n$ Terms

The sum of the first $n$ terms of an AP is given by:

$$S_n = \frac{n}{2} [2a + (n - 1)d] \quad \text{or} \quad S_n = \frac{n}{2} [a + l]$$

where $a$ is the first term, $d$ is common difference, and $l$ is the last term.

Step 2: Solving for $d$ from Given Sum

  • Given: $n = 14$, $S_{14} = 1050$, and first term $a = 10$.
  • Substituting into the sum formula:
    $$S_{14} = \frac{14}{2} [2(10) + (14 - 1)d] = 1050$$ $$7 [20 + 13d] = 1050$$ $$20 + 13d = \frac{1050}{7} = 150$$ $$13d = 150 - 20 = 130 \implies d = \frac{130}{13} = 10$$
  • Therefore, the common difference is $d = 10$.

Step 3: Calculating the $20^{\text{th}}$ Term

Using the $n^{\text{th}}$ term formula $a_n = a + (n - 1)d$ for $n = 20$:

$$a_{20} = a + (20 - 1)d = 10 + 19 \times 10 = 10 + 190 = 200$$

Conclusion: The $20^{\text{th}}$ term of the AP is 200.

Q3
How many terms of the AP: 9, 17, 25, ... must be taken to give a sum of 636?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Identify Given Data

  • Given AP: $9, 17, 25, \dots$
  • First term $a = 9$
  • Common difference $d = 17 - 9 = 8$
  • Sum of $n$ terms $S_n = 636$
  • To find: The number of terms $n$.

Step 2: Set Up the Quadratic Equation

Using the sum formula $S_n = \frac{n}{2} [2a + (n - 1)d]$:

$$636 = \frac{n}{2} [2(9) + (n - 1)8]$$ $$636 = \frac{n}{2} [18 + 8n - 8] = \frac{n}{2} [8n + 10] = n [4n + 5]$$ $$4n^2 + 5n - 636 = 0$$

Step 3: Solve Using the Quadratic Formula

Comparing with $An^2 + Bn + C = 0$: $A = 4, B = 5, C = -636$.

$$D = B^2 - 4AC = 5^2 - 4(4)(-636) = 25 + 10176 = 10201$$ $$\sqrt{D} = \sqrt{10201} = 101$$

Now, applying the quadratic formula:

$$n = \frac{-B \pm \sqrt{D}}{2A} = \frac{-5 \pm 101}{2 \times 4} = \frac{-5 \pm 101}{8}$$
  • $n_1 = \frac{-5 + 101}{8} = \frac{96}{8} = 12$
  • $n_2 = \frac{-5 - 101}{8} = \frac{-106}{8} = -\frac{53}{4}$

Step 4: Feasibility and Conclusion

Since the number of terms $n$ cannot be a fraction or negative, we reject $n = -\frac{53}{4}$.
Therefore, $n = 12$.
Hence, 12 terms must be taken to give a sum of 636.

Q4
A manufacturer of TV sets produced 600 sets in the third year and 700 sets in the seventh year. Assuming that the production increases uniformly by a fixed number every year, find: (i) the production in the 1st year, (ii) the production in the 10th year, (iii) the total production in first 7 years.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Mathematical Modelling as an Arithmetic Progression

Since the production increases uniformly by a fixed number every year, the production in $1^{\text{st}}, 2^{\text{nd}}, 3^{\text{rd}}, \dots$ years forms an AP with first term $a$ and common difference $d$.

Step 2: Formulate Equations from Given Data

  • Production in the $3^{\text{rd}}$ year = $600 \implies a_3 = a + 2d = 600 \quad \text{--- (1)}$
  • Production in the $7^{\text{th}}$ year = $700 \implies a_7 = a + 6d = 700 \quad \text{--- (2)}$

Step 3: Solve for $a$ and $d$

Subtracting equation (1) from equation (2):

$$(a + 6d) - (a + 2d) = 700 - 600 \implies 4d = 100 \implies d = 25$$

Substitute $d = 25$ into equation (1):

$$a + 2(25) = 600 \implies a + 50 = 600 \implies a = 550$$

Step 4: Answering Required Questions

  1. (i) The production in the $1^{\text{st}}$ year:
    $a = 550$ TV sets.
  2. (ii) The production in the $10^{\text{th}}$ year:
    $$a_{10} = a + 9d = 550 + 9(25) = 550 + 225 = 775\text{ TV sets}.$$
  3. (iii) The total production in first 7 years ($S_7$):
    Using $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[a + l]$ where $l = a_7 = 700$:
    $$S_7 = \frac{7}{2} [550 + 700] = \frac{7}{2} \times 1250 = 7 \times 625 = 4375\text{ TV sets}.$$

Competency Case Study (Real-Life Savings Plan)
Question: Kanika was given her pocket money on Jan 1st, 2026. She puts ₹1 on day 1, ₹2 on day 2, ₹3 on day 3, and continued doing so till the end of the month. She also spent ₹204 of her pocket money and found that at the end of the month she still had ₹100 left with her. How much was her pocket money for the month?

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. January has 31 days. Her daily savings form an AP: $1, 2, 3, \dots, 31$.
  2. Here $a = 1, d = 1, n = 31$.
    Total savings in January:
    $$S_{31} = rac{31}{2} [1 + 31] = rac{31}{2} imes 32 = 31 imes 16 = ₹496.$$
  3. Let her total pocket money be ₹$M$.
    Money left = Total pocket money - Expenditure + Savings
    $$ ext{Remaining amount} = M - 204 = 100 \implies M = 100 + 204 = ₹304.$$
    Total money saved from pocket money was ₹496, meaning initial pocket money provided was $496 + 204 + 100 - 496 = ₹304$ (or $M = ₹304$ spent and saved).
  4. Answer: Her pocket money for the month was ₹800 (where Total Pocket Money = ₹496 + ₹204 + ₹100 = ₹800).
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): The sum of the first $n$ positive integers is given by $S_n = rac{n(n + 1)}{2}$.
Reason (R): The first $n$ positive integers form an AP where $a = 1$, $d = 1$, and $l = n$.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Board Solution:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Proof: For sequence $1, 2, 3, \dots, n$, applying $S_n = rac{n}{2}[a + l] = rac{n}{2}[1 + n] = rac{n(n + 1)}{2}$. Hence Assertion (A) directly derives from Reason (R).
