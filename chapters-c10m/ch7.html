Q1
Find the distance between the following pairs of points: (i) (2, 3), (4, 1); (ii) (-5, 7), (-1, 3); (iii) (a, b), (-a, -b).
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Formula Statement

The distance between two points $P(x_1, y_1)$ and $Q(x_2, y_2)$ is given by the Distance Formula:

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$

Step 2: Calculations

  1. (i) Distance between $(2, 3)$ and $(4, 1)$:
    Here $x_1 = 2, y_1 = 3, x_2 = 4, y_2 = 1$.
    $$d = \sqrt{(4 - 2)^2 + (1 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{2^2 + (-2)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 4} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}\text{ units}.$$
  2. (ii) Distance between $(-5, 7)$ and $(-1, 3)$:
    Here $x_1 = -5, y_1 = 7, x_2 = -1, y_2 = 3$.
    $$d = \sqrt{[-1 - (-5)]^2 + (3 - 7)^2} = \sqrt{(-1 + 5)^2 + (-4)^2} = \sqrt{4^2 + 16} = \sqrt{16 + 16} = \sqrt{32} = 4\sqrt{2}\text{ units}.$$
  3. (iii) Distance between $(a, b)$ and $(-a, -b)$:
    Here $x_1 = a, y_1 = b, x_2 = -a, y_2 = -b$.
    $$d = \sqrt{(-a - a)^2 + (-b - b)^2} = \sqrt{(-2a)^2 + (-2b)^2} = \sqrt{4a^2 + 4b^2} = \sqrt{4(a^2 + b^2)} = 2\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\text{ units}.$$

Q2
Determine if the points (1, 5), (2, 3) and (-2, -11) are collinear.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Condition for Collinearity

Three points $A, B,$ and $C$ are collinear if the sum of the lengths of any two line segments is equal to the length of the third line segment (i.e., $AB + BC = AC$).

Step 2: Distance Calculations

Let the given points be $A(1, 5)$, $B(2, 3)$, and $C(-2, -11)$.

  1. Calculate $AB$:
    $$AB = \sqrt{(2 - 1)^2 + (3 - 5)^2} = \sqrt{1^2 + (-2)^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4} = \sqrt{5}$$
  2. Calculate $BC$:
    $$BC = \sqrt{(-2 - 2)^2 + (-11 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{(-4)^2 + (-14)^2} = \sqrt{16 + 196} = \sqrt{212} = \sqrt{4 \times 53} = 2\sqrt{53}$$
  3. Calculate $AC$:
    $$AC = \sqrt{(-2 - 1)^2 + (-11 - 5)^2} = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + (-16)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 256} = \sqrt{265}$$

Step 3: Comparison and Conclusion

Comparing the lengths:

$$AB + BC = \sqrt{5} + 2\sqrt{53} \neq \sqrt{265} = AC$$ $$AB + AC \neq BC \quad \text{and} \quad BC + AC \neq AB$$

Since the sum of no two distances is equal to the third distance, the points $A(1, 5)$, $B(2, 3)$, and $C(-2, -11)$ do not lie on a single straight line.
Hence, the given points are NOT collinear.

Q3
Find the coordinates of the points of trisection of the line segment joining (4, -1) and (-2, -3).
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Understanding Points of Trisection

Points of trisection divide a line segment into three equal parts. Let $P$ and $Q$ be the points of trisection of the line segment joining $A(4, -1)$ and $B(-2, -3)$:

$$AP = PQ = QB$$
  • Point $P$ divides $AB$ internally in the ratio $1 : 2$.
  • Point $Q$ divides $AB$ internally in the ratio $2 : 1$ (or $Q$ is the midpoint of $PB$).

Step 2: Finding Coordinates of Point $P$ using Section Formula

The Section Formula for coordinates of a point dividing the segment joining $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ in ratio $m_1 : m_2$ is:

$$(x, y) = \left(\frac{m_1x_2 + m_2x_1}{m_1 + m_2}, \frac{m_1y_2 + m_2y_1}{m_1 + m_2}\right)$$

For $P$, $m_1 = 1, m_2 = 2$, and $A(4, -1)$, $B(-2, -3)$:

$$x_P = \frac{1(-2) + 2(4)}{1 + 2} = \frac{-2 + 8}{3} = \frac{6}{3} = 2$$ $$y_P = \frac{1(-3) + 2(-1)}{1 + 2} = \frac{-3 - 2}{3} = -\frac{5}{3}$$

Therefore, coordinates of point $P$ are $\left(2, -\frac{5}{3}\right)$.

Step 3: Finding Coordinates of Point $Q$

For $Q$, $m_1 = 2, m_2 = 1$:

$$x_Q = \frac{2(-2) + 1(4)}{2 + 1} = \frac{-4 + 4}{3} = \frac{0}{3} = 0$$ $$y_Q = \frac{2(-3) + 1(-1)}{2 + 1} = \frac{-6 - 1}{3} = -\frac{7}{3}$$

Therefore, coordinates of point $Q$ are $\left(0, -\frac{7}{3}\right)$.

Conclusion: The coordinates of the points of trisection are $\left(2, -\frac{5}{3}\right)$ and $\left(0, -\frac{7}{3}\right)$.

Q4
Find the ratio in which the y-axis divides the line segment joining the points (5, -6) and (-1, -4). Also find the point of intersection.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Condition for Points on the y-axis

Any point lying on the y-axis has its x-coordinate equal to zero. Let the point on the y-axis be $P(0, y)$, and let it divide the line segment joining $A(5, -6)$ and $B(-1, -4)$ in the ratio $k : 1$.

Step 2: Apply Section Formula for the x-coordinate

$$x = \frac{m_1x_2 + m_2x_1}{m_1 + m_2}$$ $$0 = \frac{k(-1) + 1(5)}{k + 1}$$ $$0 = \frac{-k + 5}{k + 1} \implies -k + 5 = 0 \implies k = 5$$

Therefore, the ratio is $k : 1 = 5 : 1$.
The y-axis divides the line segment internally in the ratio $5 : 1$.

Step 3: Find the y-coordinate of the Point of Intersection

Now substitute $k = 5$ into the section formula for $y$:

$$y = \frac{k(-4) + 1(-6)}{k + 1} = \frac{5(-4) + 1(-6)}{5 + 1} = \frac{-20 - 6}{6} = \frac{-26}{6} = -\frac{13}{3}$$

Conclusion:

  • The required ratio is $5 : 1$.
  • The point of intersection on the y-axis is $\left(0, -\frac{13}{3}\right)$.

Competency Sports Ground Case Study
Question: To conduct Sports Day activities in a rectangular school ground $ABCD$, lines have been drawn with chalk powder at a distance of 1 m each. 100 flower pots have been placed at a distance of 1 m from each other along $AD$. Niharika runs $\frac{1}{4}^{\text{th}}$ the distance $AD$ on the $2^{\text{nd}}$ line and posts a green flag. Preet runs $\frac{1}{5}^{\text{th}}$ the distance $AD$ on the $8^{\text{th}}$ line and posts a red flag. What is the distance between both flags? If Rashmi has to post a blue flag exactly halfway between the line segment joining the two flags, where should she post her flag?

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Position of Green flag posted by Niharika ($N$):
    x-coordinate = 2; y-coordinate = $\frac{1}{4} \times 100 = 25$. So $N = (2, 25)$.
  2. Position of Red flag posted by Preet ($P$):
    x-coordinate = 8; y-coordinate = $\frac{1}{5} \times 100 = 20$. So $P = (8, 20)$.
  3. Distance between flags ($NP$):
    $$NP = \sqrt{(8 - 2)^2 + (20 - 25)^2} = \sqrt{6^2 + (-5)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 25} = \sqrt{61}\text{ m}.$$
  4. Midpoint Formula for Blue Flag ($R$):
    $$R = \left(\frac{2 + 8}{2}, \frac{25 + 20}{2}\right) = \left(\frac{10}{2}, \frac{45}{2}\right) = (5, 22.5).$$
    Rashmi should post her blue flag on the $5^{\text{th}}$ line at a distance of $22.5\text{ m}$ along $AD$.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): The point $(0, 4)$ lies on the y-axis.
Reason (R): The x-coordinate of any point on the y-axis is zero.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Board Solution:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Explanation: In a 2D Cartesian plane, the equation of the y-axis is $x = 0$. Since the point $(0, 4)$ has an x-coordinate of 0, it lies squarely on the y-axis, exactly 4 units above the origin.
