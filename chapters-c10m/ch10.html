CBSE Core Board Theorems: Theorem 10.1 & Theorem 10.2
Theorem 10.1 [Direct Proof]
Prove that the tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Detailed Proof:

  1. Given: A circle with centre $O$ and a tangent $XY$ to the circle at a point $P$.
  2. To Prove: $OP \perp XY$.
  3. Construction: Take any point $Q$ other than $P$ on the tangent line $XY$. Join $OQ$. Let $OQ$ intersect the circle at point $R$.
  4. Proof:
    • Since $XY$ is a tangent touching the circle only at point $P$, any point $Q$ on $XY$ other than $P$ must lie outside the circle.
    • Therefore, the segment $OQ$ is greater than the radius of the circle: $$OQ > OR$$
    • Since $OR = OP$ (radii of the same circle): $$OQ > OP$$
    • This inequality holds for every point on the line $XY$ except the point of contact $P$.
    • Therefore, $OP$ is the shortest distance of the centre $O$ from the tangent line $XY$.
    • In geometry, the shortest distance from a given point to a given line is always the perpendicular distance.
  5. Conclusion: Hence, $OP \perp XY$. (Hence Proved).

Theorem 10.2 [Direct Proof]
Prove that the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Detailed Proof:

  1. Given: A circle with centre $O$, an external point $P$, and two tangents $PQ$ and $PR$ drawn from $P$ touching the circle at points $Q$ and $R$ respectively.
  2. To Prove: $PQ = PR$.
  3. Construction: Join $OP$, $OQ$, and $OR$.
  4. Proof:

    By Theorem 10.1, the radius through the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent:

    $$OQ \perp PQ \implies \angle OQP = 90^\circ$$ $$OR \perp PR \implies \angle ORP = 90^\circ$$

    Now, compare right-angled triangles $\Delta OQP$ and $\Delta ORP$:

    • $\angle OQP = \angle ORP = 90^\circ$ (Each $90^\circ$)
    • $OP = OP$ (Common hypotenuse)
    • $OQ = OR$ (Radii of the same circle)

    Therefore, by RHS (Right angle - Hypotenuse - Side) Congruence Criterion:

    $$\Delta OQP \cong \Delta ORP$$

    By Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles (CPCTC):

    $$PQ = PR$$
  5. Conclusion: Hence proved, the lengths of tangents drawn from an external point are equal.

NCERT Exercise 10.1: Basics of Tangents and Secants
Question 1 to 4
Answer the following NCERT Exercise 10.1 questions:
(i) How many tangents can a circle have?
(ii) Fill in the blanks: Tangent intersects at ..., Secant intersects at ..., Max parallel tangents...
(iii) A tangent $PQ$ at a point $P$ of a circle of radius $5\text{ cm}$ meets a line through the centre $O$ at a point $Q$ so that $OQ = 12\text{ cm}$. Length $PQ$ is: (A) 12 cm, (B) 13 cm, (C) 8.5 cm, (D) $\sqrt{119}\text{ cm}$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Complete Solutions:

  1. (i) Tangents to a Circle:

    A circle has infinitely many points on its circumference, and at each point exactly one unique tangent can be drawn. Therefore, a circle can have infinitely many tangents.

  2. (ii) Fill in the blanks:
    • A tangent to a circle intersects it in one (1) point.
    • A line intersecting a circle in two points is called a secant.
    • A circle can have two (2) parallel tangents at the most (at opposite ends of a diameter).
    • The common point of a tangent to a circle and the circle is called the point of contact.
  3. (iii) Finding $PQ$:

    By Theorem 10.1, radius $OP \perp PQ$, so $\Delta OPQ$ is right-angled at $P$.

    $$OQ^2 = OP^2 + PQ^2 \implies 12^2 = 5^2 + PQ^2$$ $$144 = 25 + PQ^2 \implies PQ^2 = 144 - 25 = 119 \implies PQ = \sqrt{119}\text{ cm}$$

    Correct option is (D) $\sqrt{119}\text{ cm}$.

NCERT Exercise 10.2: Tangent Lengths, Quadrilaterals & Circle Proofs
Question 1, 2, 3
Choose the correct option and justify:
1. From a point $Q$, length of tangent is $24\text{ cm}$ and distance of $Q$ from centre is $25\text{ cm}$. The radius is (A) 7 cm, (B) 12 cm, (C) 15 cm, (D) 24.5 cm.
2. If $TP$ and $TQ$ are tangents to circle with centre $O$ such that $\angle POQ = 110^\circ$, then $\angle PTQ$ is: (A) 60°, (B) 70°, (C) 80°, (D) 90°.
3. If tangents $PA$ and $PB$ from $P$ to circle with centre $O$ are inclined at angle $80^\circ$, then $\angle POA$ is: (A) 50°, (B) 60°, (C) 70°, (D) 80°.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Justifications:

  1. Question 1:

    Radius $r = \sqrt{OQ^2 - PQ^2} = \sqrt{25^2 - 24^2} = \sqrt{625 - 576} = \sqrt{49} = 7\text{ cm}$.

    Correct option: (A) $7\text{ cm}$.

  2. Question 2:

    In quadrilateral $OPTQ$, radius is perpendicular to tangent: $\angle OPT = 90^\circ, \angle OQT = 90^\circ$.

    $$\angle PTQ + \angle POQ = 180^\circ \implies \angle PTQ + 110^\circ = 180^\circ \implies \angle PTQ = 70^\circ$$

    Correct option: (B) $70^\circ$.

  3. Question 3:

    $\angle APB = 80^\circ \implies \angle AOB = 180^\circ - 80^\circ = 100^\circ$.

    Line $OP$ bisects $\angle AOB$, therefore $\angle POA = \frac{1}{2} \angle AOB = \frac{100^\circ}{2} = 50^\circ$.

    Correct option: (A) $50^\circ$.

Question 4 & 5
(i) Prove that the tangents drawn at the ends of a diameter of a circle are parallel.
(ii) Prove that the perpendicular at the point of contact to the tangent to a circle passes through the centre.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Detailed Proofs:

  1. Proof of (i) Parallel Tangents at Diameter Ends:
    • Let $AB$ be a diameter of a circle with centre $O$.
    • Let lines $l$ and $m$ be tangents drawn at endpoints $A$ and $B$ respectively.
    • By Theorem 10.1, radius is perpendicular to the tangent at point of contact: $$OA \perp l \implies \angle 1 = 90^\circ$$ $$OB \perp m \implies \angle 2 = 90^\circ$$
    • Since $\angle 1 = 90^\circ$ and $\angle 2 = 90^\circ$, we have $\angle 1 = \angle 2$.
    • These angles form a pair of alternate interior angles for lines $l$ and $m$ intersected by transversal $AB$.
    • When alternate interior angles are equal, the lines are parallel. Therefore, $l \parallel m$. (Hence Proved).
  2. Proof of (ii) Perpendicular passes through Centre:
    • Let $AB$ be a tangent to circle with centre $O$ at point of contact $P$.
    • We know by Theorem 10.1 that radius $OP \perp AB$, so $\angle OPB = 90^\circ$.
    • If possible, let another perpendicular $X P \perp AB$ be drawn at $P$ which does not pass through centre $O$. Then $\angle XPB = 90^\circ$.
    • Comparing the two gives $\angle XPB = \angle OPB$, which is only possible if line segment $XP$ coincides with $OP$.
    • Therefore, the perpendicular at the point of contact must pass through the centre $O$. (Hence Proved).

Question 6 & 7
(i) The length of a tangent from a point $A$ at distance $5\text{ cm}$ from the centre of the circle is $4\text{ cm}$. Find the radius of the circle.
(ii) Two concentric circles are of radii $5\text{ cm}$ and $3\text{ cm}$. Find the length of the chord of the larger circle which touches the smaller circle.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solutions:

  1. (i) Radius calculation:

    Let centre be $O$, point of contact be $P$. Then $\angle OPA = 90^\circ$, $OA = 5\text{ cm}$, $AP = 4\text{ cm}$.

    $$OP = \sqrt{OA^2 - AP^2} = \sqrt{5^2 - 4^2} = \sqrt{25 - 16} = \sqrt{9} = 3\text{ cm}$$

    Radius of the circle is $3\text{ cm}$.

  2. (ii) Length of chord in concentric circles:
    • Let $O$ be the common centre of the two concentric circles.
    • Let $AB$ be the chord of the larger circle (radius $R = 5\text{ cm}$) touching the smaller circle (radius $r = 3\text{ cm}$) at point $P$.
    • Since $AB$ is tangent to the inner circle at $P$, $OP \perp AB$, and $OP = 3\text{ cm}$.
    • Join $OA$. In right $\Delta OPA$, $OA = 5\text{ cm}$.
    • By Pythagoras Theorem: $$AP = \sqrt{OA^2 - OP^2} = \sqrt{5^2 - 3^2} = \sqrt{25 - 9} = \sqrt{16} = 4\text{ cm}$$
    • The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord: $AB = 2 \times AP = 2 \times 4 = 8\text{ cm}$.

    The length of the chord is $8\text{ cm}$.

Question 8
A quadrilateral $ABCD$ is drawn to circumscribe a circle. Prove that $AB + CD = AD + BC$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Proof:

  1. Given: A quadrilateral $ABCD$ circumscribing a circle with centre $O$. Let the circle touch sides $AB, BC, CD, DA$ at points $P, Q, R, S$ respectively.
  2. To Prove: $AB + CD = AD + BC$.
  3. Property Used: By Theorem 10.2, lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal.
    • From vertex $A$: $AP = AS$ \quad --- (1)
    • From vertex $B$: $BP = BQ$ \quad --- (2)
    • From vertex $C$: $CR = CQ$ \quad --- (3)
    • From vertex $D$: $DR = DS$ \quad --- (4)
  4. Adding Equations (1), (2), (3), and (4): $$(AP + BP) + (CR + DR) = (AS + DS) + (BQ + CQ)$$
  5. Grouping terms by line segments:
    • $AP + BP = AB$
    • $CR + DR = CD$
    • $AS + DS = AD$
    • $BQ + CQ = BC$
    $$AB + CD = AD + BC$$
  6. Conclusion: Hence Proved, $AB + CD = AD + BC$.

Question 9
In the given figure, $XY$ and $X'Y'$ are two parallel tangents to a circle with centre $O$ and another tangent $AB$ with point of contact $C$ intersecting $XY$ at $A$ and $X'Y'$ at $B$. Prove that $\angle AOB = 90^\circ$.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Detailed Proof:

  1. Given: Parallel tangents $XY$ and $X'Y'$ touching circle at $P$ and $Q$ respectively. Tangent $AB$ touches at $C$.
  2. To Prove: $\angle AOB = 90^\circ$.
  3. Construction: Join $OC$.
  4. Proof:

    Compare $\Delta OPA$ and $\Delta OCA$:

    • $OP = OC$ (Radii of the same circle)
    • $AP = AC$ (Tangents from external point $A$)
    • $OA = OA$ (Common side)

    By SSS Congruence: $\Delta OPA \cong \Delta OCA \implies \angle POA = \angle COA$.

    $$\angle POC = 2\angle COA \implies \angle COA = \frac{1}{2}\angle POC \quad \text{--- (1)}$$

    Similarly, comparing $\Delta OQB$ and $\Delta OCB$:

    $$\Delta OQB \cong \Delta OCB \implies \angle COB = \frac{1}{2}\angle QOC \quad \text{--- (2)}$$
  5. Sum of Angles on Diameter $POQ$:

    Since $POQ$ is a straight line diameter:

    $$\angle POC + \angle QOC = 180^\circ$$

    Dividing by 2:

    $$\frac{1}{2}\angle POC + \frac{1}{2}\angle QOC = \frac{180^\circ}{2} = 90^\circ$$ $$\angle COA + \angle COB = 90^\circ$$ $$\angle AOB = 90^\circ$$
  6. Conclusion: Hence proved, $\angle AOB = 90^\circ$.

Question 10 & 11
(i) Prove that the angle between the two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle is supplementary to the angle subtended by the line-segment joining the points of contact at the centre.
(ii) Prove that the parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Proofs:

  1. Proof of (i) Supplementary Angles:
    • Let tangents from external point $P$ touch circle at $A$ and $B$. Centre is $O$.
    • By Theorem 10.1, $OA \perp PA \implies \angle OAP = 90^\circ$, and $OB \perp PB \implies \angle OBP = 90^\circ$.
    • In quadrilateral $OAPB$, sum of interior angles is $360^\circ$: $$\angle APB + \angle AOB + \angle OAP + \angle OBP = 360^\circ$$ $$\angle APB + \angle AOB + 90^\circ + 90^\circ = 360^\circ$$ $$\angle APB + \angle AOB = 360^\circ - 180^\circ = 180^\circ$$
    • Thus, $\angle APB$ and $\angle AOB$ are supplementary. (Hence Proved).
  2. Proof of (ii) Circumscribing Parallelogram is Rhombus:
    • Let $ABCD$ be a parallelogram circumscribing a circle.
    • Opposite sides of parallelogram are equal: $AB = CD$ and $AD = BC$.
    • From Question 8, for any circumscribing quadrilateral: $$AB + CD = AD + BC$$
    • Substituting $CD = AB$ and $AD = BC$: $$AB + AB = BC + BC \implies 2AB = 2BC \implies AB = BC$$
    • Since adjacent sides are equal ($AB = BC = CD = DA$), parallelogram $ABCD$ has all four sides equal.
    • A parallelogram with all sides equal is a rhombus. Hence Proved.

Question 12
A triangle $ABC$ is drawn to circumscribe a circle of radius $4\text{ cm}$ such that the segments $BD$ and $DC$ into which $BC$ is divided by the point of contact $D$ are of lengths $8\text{ cm}$ and $6\text{ cm}$ respectively. Find the sides $AB$ and $AC$.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Detailed Solution:

  1. Given & Geometry:
    • Circle with centre $O$ and inradius $r = 4\text{ cm}$.
    • $D, E, F$ are points of contact on sides $BC, CA, AB$ respectively.
    • $BD = 8\text{ cm} \implies BF = BD = 8\text{ cm}$ (tangents from $B$).
    • $CD = 6\text{ cm} \implies CE = CD = 6\text{ cm}$ (tangents from $C$).
    • Let $AE = AF = x\text{ cm}$ (tangents from $A$).
    • Sides of $\Delta ABC$: $$a = BC = 6 + 8 = 14\text{ cm}$$ $$b = AC = (x + 6)\text{ cm}$$ $$c = AB = (x + 8)\text{ cm}$$
  2. Area using Heron's Formula: $$s = \frac{a + b + c}{2} = \frac{14 + (x + 6) + (x + 8)}{2} = \frac{2x + 28}{2} = (x + 14)\text{ cm}$$ $$s - a = (x + 14) - 14 = x$$ $$s - b = (x + 14) - (x + 6) = 8$$ $$s - c = (x + 14) - (x + 8) = 6$$ $$\text{Area}(\Delta ABC) = \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)} = \sqrt{(x + 14)(x)(8)(6)} = \sqrt{48x(x + 14)}$$
  3. Area as Sum of Sub-triangles: $$\text{Area}(\Delta ABC) = \text{Area}(\Delta OBC) + \text{Area}(\Delta OCA) + \text{Area}(\Delta OAB)$$ $$= \frac{1}{2} \times 14 \times 4 + \frac{1}{2} \times (x + 6) \times 4 + \frac{1}{2} \times (x + 8) \times 4$$ $$= 28 + 2(x + 6) + 2(x + 8) = 28 + 2x + 12 + 2x + 16 = 4x + 56 = 4(x + 14)$$
  4. Equating the Two Expressions for Area: $$\sqrt{48x(x + 14)} = 4(x + 14)$$

    Squaring both sides:

    $$48x(x + 14) = 16(x + 14)^2$$

    Since $x + 14 \ne 0$, divide both sides by $16(x + 14)$:

    $$3x = x + 14 \implies 2x = 14 \implies x = 7\text{ cm}$$
  5. Lengths of sides $AB$ and $AC$: $$AB = x + 8 = 7 + 8 = 15\text{ cm}$$ $$AC = x + 6 = 7 + 6 = 13\text{ cm}$$
  6. Conclusion: $AB = 15\text{ cm}$ and $AC = 13\text{ cm}$.

Question 13
Prove that opposite sides of a quadrilateral circumscribing a circle subtend supplementary angles at the centre of the circle.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Proof:

  1. Given: Quadrilateral $ABCD$ circumscribes a circle with centre $O$, touching sides $AB, BC, CD, DA$ at $P, Q, R, S$ respectively.
  2. To Prove: $\angle AOB + \angle COD = 180^\circ$ and $\angle BOC + \angle AOD = 180^\circ$.
  3. Construction: Join $OP, OQ, OR, OS$, and vertices $OA, OB, OC, OD$.
  4. Proof:

    In $\Delta OAP$ and $\Delta OAS$:

    • $AP = AS$ (Tangents from $A$)
    • $OP = OS$ (Radii)
    • $OA = OA$ (Common)

    By SSS Congruence: $\Delta OAP \cong \Delta OAS \implies \angle 1 = \angle 8$.

    Similarly, $\angle 2 = \angle 3$, $\angle 4 = \angle 5$, and $\angle 6 = \angle 7$.

  5. Sum of Angles at Centre: $$\angle 1 + \angle 2 + \angle 3 + \angle 4 + \angle 5 + \angle 6 + \angle 7 + \angle 8 = 360^\circ$$ $$2(\angle 1 + \angle 2 + \angle 5 + \angle 6) = 360^\circ$$ $$(\angle 1 + \angle 2) + (\angle 5 + \angle 6) = 180^\circ$$ $$\angle AOB + \angle COD = 180^\circ$$

    Similarly, $\angle BOC + \angle AOD = 180^\circ$.

  6. Conclusion: Opposite sides subtend supplementary angles at the centre. (Hence Proved).

Case-Study Competency Question
A mechanical transmission system uses a circular gear of radius $6\text{ cm}$. Two belt tensioners act as tangents $PA$ and $PB$ meeting at external adjustment point $P$. The distance from the gear axle $O$ to point $P$ is $10\text{ cm}$.
(i) Find the length of each tangent belt section $PA$ and $PB$.
(ii) Find the perimeter of quadrilateral $OAPB$.
(iii) Determine the exact value of $\sin(\angle OPA)$.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Since radius $\perp$ tangent, in right $\Delta OAP$ ($OA = 6, OP = 10$):
    $$PA = \sqrt{OP^2 - OA^2} = \sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = \sqrt{64} = 8\text{ cm}.$$
    By Theorem 10.2, $PB = PA = 8\text{ cm}$.
  2. (ii) $\text{Perimeter of } OAPB = OA + AP + PB + BO = 6 + 8 + 8 + 6 = 28\text{ cm}$.
  3. (iii) In right $\Delta OAP$ with hypotenuse $OP = 10$ and opposite side $OA = 6$:
    $$\sin(\angle OPA) = \frac{OA}{OP} = \frac{6}{10} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6.$$
Assertion & Reason HOTS Question
Assertion (A): If two tangents are drawn to a circle from an external point, they subtend equal angles at the centre.
Reason (R): The tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal in length.

CBSE Board Solution:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is part of the congruence proof that explains Assertion (A).

Explanation: In triangles $\Delta OPA$ and $\Delta OPB$, $PA = PB$ (Reason R), $OA = OB$ (radii), and $OP = OP$ (common). By SSS (or RHS) congruence, $\Delta OPA \cong \Delta OPB$, which directly gives $\angle AOP = \angle BOP$ (Assertion A).
