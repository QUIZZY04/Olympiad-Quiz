Q1
On comparing the ratios a1/a2, b1/b2 and c1/c2, find out whether the lines representing the following pairs of linear equations intersect at a point, are parallel or coincident: (i) 5x - 4y + 8 = 0, 7x + 6y - 9 = 0; (ii) 9x + 3y + 12 = 0, 18x + 6y + 24 = 0; (iii) 6x - 3y + 10 = 0, 2x - y + 9 = 0.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Criteria for Consistency and Nature of Solutions:

For a pair of linear equations $a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0$ and $a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0$:

Ratio Comparison Graphical Representation Algebraic Interpretation Consistency
$ rac{a_1}{a_2} eq rac{b_1}{b_2}$ Intersecting lines Exactly one unique solution Consistent
$ rac{a_1}{a_2} = rac{b_1}{b_2} = rac{c_1}{c_2}$ Coincident lines Infinitely many solutions Consistent (dependent)
$ rac{a_1}{a_2} = rac{b_1}{b_2} eq rac{c_1}{c_2}$ Parallel lines No solution Inconsistent

Calculations:

  1. (i) $5x - 4y + 8 = 0$ and $7x + 6y - 9 = 0$:
    $a_1 = 5, b_1 = -4, c_1 = 8$ and $a_2 = 7, b_2 = 6, c_2 = -9$.
    $$ rac{a_1}{a_2} = rac{5}{7}, \quad rac{b_1}{b_2} = rac{-4}{6} = - rac{2}{3}$$ Since $ rac{a_1}{a_2} eq rac{b_1}{b_2}$, the lines intersect at a single point (Consistent with unique solution).
  2. (ii) $9x + 3y + 12 = 0$ and $18x + 6y + 24 = 0$:
    $$ rac{a_1}{a_2} = rac{9}{18} = rac{1}{2}, \quad rac{b_1}{b_2} = rac{3}{6} = rac{1}{2}, \quad rac{c_1}{c_2} = rac{12}{24} = rac{1}{2}$$ Since $ rac{a_1}{a_2} = rac{b_1}{b_2} = rac{c_1}{c_2} = rac{1}{2}$, the lines are coincident (Consistent with infinitely many solutions).
  3. (iii) $6x - 3y + 10 = 0$ and $2x - y + 9 = 0$:
    $$ rac{a_1}{a_2} = rac{6}{2} = 3, \quad rac{b_1}{b_2} = rac{-3}{-1} = 3, \quad rac{c_1}{c_2} = rac{10}{9}$$ Since $ rac{a_1}{a_2} = rac{b_1}{b_2} eq rac{c_1}{c_2}$, the lines are parallel (Inconsistent, No solution).

Q2
Solve the following pairs of linear equations: (i) x + y = 14 and x - y = 4 by substitution method; (ii) 3x + 4y = 10 and 2x - 2y = 2 by elimination method.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

(i) By Substitution Method:

Solve the pair of linear equations:

$$x + y = 14 \quad ext{--- (1)}$$ $$x - y = 4 \quad ext{--- (2)}$$
  1. From equation (1), express $x$ in terms of $y$:
    $$x = 14 - y \quad ext{--- (3)}$$
  2. Substitute $x = 14 - y$ into equation (2):
    $$(14 - y) - y = 4 \implies 14 - 2y = 4$$ $$-2y = 4 - 14 = -10 \implies y = rac{-10}{-2} = 5$$
  3. Substitute $y = 5$ into equation (3):
    $$x = 14 - 5 = 9$$
  4. Solution: $x = 9, y = 5$.

(ii) By Elimination Method:

Solve the pair of equations:

$$3x + 4y = 10 \quad ext{--- (1)}$$ $$2x - 2y = 2 \quad ext{--- (2)}$$
  1. Multiply equation (2) by 2 to make the coefficients of $y$ equal and opposite:
    $$2 imes (2x - 2y = 2) \implies 4x - 4y = 4 \quad ext{--- (3)}$$
  2. Add equation (1) and equation (3):
    $$(3x + 4y) + (4x - 4y) = 10 + 4$$ $$7x = 14 \implies x = rac{14}{7} = 2$$
  3. Substitute $x = 2$ in equation (1):
    $$3(2) + 4y = 10 \implies 6 + 4y = 10 \implies 4y = 4 \implies y = 1$$
  4. Solution: $x = 2, y = 1$.

Q3
A fraction becomes 1 if we add 1 to the numerator and subtract 1 from the denominator. It becomes 1/2 if we only add 1 to the denominator. What is the fraction?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Define Variables

Let the numerator of the fraction be $x$ and the denominator be $y$.
Then the original fraction is $ rac{x}{y}$ (where $y eq 0$).

Step 2: Formulate Equations from Given Conditions

  • Condition 1: If we add 1 to the numerator and subtract 1 from the denominator, a fraction reduces to 1.
    $$ rac{x + 1}{y - 1} = 1$$ $$x + 1 = y - 1 \implies x - y = -2 \quad ext{--- (Equation 1)}$$
  • Condition 2: It becomes $ rac{1}{2}$ if we only add 1 to the denominator.
    $$ rac{x}{y + 1} = rac{1}{2}$$ $$2x = y + 1 \implies 2x - y = 1 \quad ext{--- (Equation 2)}$$

Step 3: Solve the Linear System using Elimination Method

Subtract Equation (1) from Equation (2):

$$(2x - y) - (x - y) = 1 - (-2)$$ $$2x - y - x + y = 1 + 2$$ $$x = 3$$

Step 4: Find $y$ and State the Fraction

Substitute $x = 3$ into Equation (1):

$$3 - y = -2 \implies -y = -5 \implies y = 5$$

Step 5: Verification and Final Answer

  • Condition 1: $ rac{3 + 1}{5 - 1} = rac{4}{4} = 1$ (Verified)
  • Condition 2: $ rac{3}{5 + 1} = rac{3}{6} = rac{1}{2}$ (Verified)

Hence, the required fraction is $ rac{3}{5}$.

Competency Case-Study Problem (Age Word Problem)
Question: Five years hence, the age of Jacob will be three times that of his son. Five years ago, Jacob's age was seven times that of his son. What are their present ages?

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Let the present age of Jacob be $x$ years and that of his son be $y$ years.
  2. Case 1 (5 years hence):
    Jacob's age = $x + 5$; Son's age = $y + 5$.
    $$x + 5 = 3(y + 5) \implies x + 5 = 3y + 15 \implies x - 3y = 10 \quad ext{--- (1)}$$
  3. Case 2 (5 years ago):
    Jacob's age = $x - 5$; Son's age = $y - 5$.
    $$x - 5 = 7(y - 5) \implies x - 5 = 7y - 35 \implies x - 7y = -30 \quad ext{--- (2)}$$
  4. Subtract (2) from (1):
    $$(x - 3y) - (x - 7y) = 10 - (-30) \implies 4y = 40 \implies y = 10$$
  5. Substitute $y = 10$ into (1):
    $$x - 3(10) = 10 \implies x - 30 = 10 \implies x = 40$$
  6. Conclusion: Present age of Jacob = 40 years, and present age of his son = 10 years.
Assertion & Reason Assessment
Assertion (A): The pair of equations $x + 2y - 5 = 0$ and $-4x - 8y + 20 = 0$ has infinitely many solutions.
Reason (R): If $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}$, the pair of linear equations is consistent with infinitely many coincident solutions.

Evaluate whether Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct and if (R) explains (A).

CBSE Board Solution:

Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Calculation:
$$a_1 = 1, b_1 = 2, c_1 = -5 \quad \text{and} \quad a_2 = -4, b_2 = -8, c_2 = 20$$
$$\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{1}{-4} = -\frac{1}{4}, \quad \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{2}{-8} = -\frac{1}{4}, \quad \frac{c_1}{c_2} = \frac{-5}{20} = -\frac{1}{4}$$
Since $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2} = -\frac{1}{4}$, the lines are coincident and have infinitely many solutions.
