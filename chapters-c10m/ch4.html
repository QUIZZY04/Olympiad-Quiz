NCERT Exercise 4.1: Checking Quadratic Equations & Word Formulations
Question 1
Check whether the following are quadratic equations: (i) $(x + 1)^2 = 2(x - 3)$, (ii) $x^2 - 2x = (-2)(3 - x)$, (iii) $(x - 2)(x + 1) = (x - 1)(x + 3)$, (iv) $(x + 2)^3 = 2x(x^2 - 1)$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Definition:

An equation of the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}$ and $a \neq 0$, is called a quadratic equation in variable $x$ (degree 2).

Checking the Equations:

  1. (i) $(x + 1)^2 = 2(x - 3)$:
    $$x^2 + 2x + 1 = 2x - 6 \implies x^2 + 2x - 2x + 1 + 6 = 0 \implies x^2 + 7 = 0$$ Since the highest power of $x$ is 2 and the coefficient of $x^2$ is $1 \neq 0$, it is a quadratic equation.
  2. (ii) $x^2 - 2x = (-2)(3 - x)$:
    $$x^2 - 2x = -6 + 2x \implies x^2 - 4x + 6 = 0$$ It is of the standard form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with $a = 1 \neq 0$. Hence, it is a quadratic equation.
  3. (iii) $(x - 2)(x + 1) = (x - 1)(x + 3)$:
    $$x^2 - x - 2 = x^2 + 2x - 3 \implies -x - 2x - 2 + 3 = 0 \implies -3x + 1 = 0 \implies 3x - 1 = 0$$ The term containing $x^2$ cancels out ($a = 0$). Hence, it is NOT a quadratic equation (it is a linear equation).
  4. (iv) $(x + 2)^3 = 2x(x^2 - 1)$:
    $$x^3 + 3(x)^2(2) + 3(x)(2)^2 + 2^3 = 2x^3 - 2x$$ $$x^3 + 6x^2 + 12x + 8 = 2x^3 - 2x \implies -x^3 + 6x^2 + 14x + 8 = 0$$ The highest degree is 3 (cubic). Hence, it is NOT a quadratic equation.

Question 2
Represent the following situations in the form of quadratic equations: (i) Area of rectangular plot is $528\text{ m}^2$, length is $1\text{ m}$ more than twice its breadth, (ii) Product of two consecutive positive integers is 306, (iii) Rohan's mother is 26 years older than him; product of ages 3 years hence is 360.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Mathematical Formulation of Situations:

  1. (i) Rectangular Plot:
    Let the breadth of the plot be $x\text{ metres}$.
    Then its length is $(2x + 1)\text{ metres}$.
    $$\text{Area} = \text{Length} \times \text{Breadth} = (2x + 1)x = 2x^2 + x$$ Given Area $= 528\text{ m}^2$, so the equation is: $$2x^2 + x - 528 = 0$$
  2. (ii) Consecutive Positive Integers:
    Let two consecutive positive integers be $x$ and $x + 1$.
    Their product is $x(x + 1) = 306$.
    $$x^2 + x - 306 = 0$$
  3. (iii) Rohan's Mother's Age:
    Let Rohan's present age be $x\text{ years}$. Mother's present age $= (x + 26)\text{ years}$.
    After 3 years: Rohan's age $= x + 3$, Mother's age $= x + 29$.
    Product of their ages: $(x + 3)(x + 29) = 360$
    $$x^2 + 32x + 87 = 360 \implies x^2 + 32x - 273 = 0$$

NCERT Exercise 4.2: Solving Quadratic Equations by Factorisation
Question 3
Find the roots of the following quadratic equations by factorisation: (i) $x^2 - 3x - 10 = 0$, (ii) $2x^2 + x - 6 = 0$, (iii) $\sqrt{2}x^2 + 7x + 5\sqrt{2} = 0$, (iv) $2x^2 - x + \frac{1}{8} = 0$, (v) $100x^2 - 20x + 1 = 0$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Factorisation Solutions:

  1. (i) $x^2 - 3x - 10 = 0$:
    Product $= -10$, sum $= -3$. Factors are $-5$ and $+2$.
    $$x^2 - 5x + 2x - 10 = 0 \implies x(x - 5) + 2(x - 5) = 0 \implies (x - 5)(x + 2) = 0$$ Roots: $x = 5, -2$.
  2. (ii) $2x^2 + x - 6 = 0$:
    Product $= 2 \times (-6) = -12$, sum $= 1$. Factors are $+4$ and $-3$.
    $$2x^2 + 4x - 3x - 6 = 0 \implies 2x(x + 2) - 3(x + 2) = 0 \implies (2x - 3)(x + 2) = 0$$ Roots: $x = \frac{3}{2}, -2$.
  3. (iii) $\sqrt{2}x^2 + 7x + 5\sqrt{2} = 0$:
    Product $= \sqrt{2} \times 5\sqrt{2} = 5 \times 2 = 10$, sum $= 7$. Factors are $+5$ and $+2$.
    $$\sqrt{2}x^2 + 2x + 5x + 5\sqrt{2} = 0$$ $$\sqrt{2}x(x + \sqrt{2}) + 5(x + \sqrt{2}) = 0 \implies (\sqrt{2}x + 5)(x + \sqrt{2}) = 0$$ $$\sqrt{2}x + 5 = 0 \implies x = -\frac{5}{\sqrt{2}} = -\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{2}, \quad x + \sqrt{2} = 0 \implies x = -\sqrt{2}$$ Roots: $x = -\sqrt{2}, -\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
  4. (iv) $2x^2 - x + \frac{1}{8} = 0$:
    Multiply by 8: $16x^2 - 8x + 1 = 0 \implies (4x - 1)^2 = 0$.
    Roots: $x = \frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{4}$.
  5. (v) $100x^2 - 20x + 1 = 0$:
    $$(10x - 1)^2 = 0 \implies 10x - 1 = 0$$ Roots: $x = \frac{1}{10}, \frac{1}{10}$.

Question 4
The altitude of a right triangle is 7 cm less than its base. If the hypotenuse is 13 cm, find the other two sides.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Set Up Geometric Variables

  • Let the base of the right triangle be $x\text{ cm}$ ($x > 0$).
  • Then its altitude (height) $= (x - 7)\text{ cm}$.
  • Hypotenuse $= 13\text{ cm}$.

Step 2: Apply Pythagoras Theorem

$$\text{Base}^2 + \text{Altitude}^2 = \text{Hypotenuse}^2$$ $$x^2 + (x - 7)^2 = 13^2$$ $$x^2 + (x^2 - 14x + 49) = 169$$ $$2x^2 - 14x + 49 - 169 = 0$$ $$2x^2 - 14x - 120 = 0$$

Dividing the entire equation by 2:

$$x^2 - 7x - 60 = 0$$

Step 3: Factorise and Solve

Find two numbers whose product is $-60$ and sum is $-7$. These are $-12$ and $+5$:

$$x^2 - 12x + 5x - 60 = 0$$ $$x(x - 12) + 5(x - 12) = 0 \implies (x - 12)(x + 5) = 0$$ $$x = 12 \quad \text{or} \quad x = -5$$

Step 4: Check Validity and Conclude

Since the side length of a geometric figure cannot be negative, $x \neq -5$. Therefore, $x = 12\text{ cm}$.

  • $\text{Base} = 12\text{ cm}$.
  • $\text{Altitude} = 12 - 7 = 5\text{ cm}$.

Verification: $12^2 + 5^2 = 144 + 25 = 169 = 13^2$. (Verified)

Final Answer: The other two sides are $12\text{ cm}$ and $5\text{ cm}$.

NCERT Exercise 4.3: Quadratic Formula & Nature of Roots
Question 5
Find the nature of the roots of the following quadratic equations. If the real roots exist, find them: (i) $2x^2 - 3x + 5 = 0$, (ii) $3x^2 - 4\sqrt{3}x + 4 = 0$, (iii) $2x^2 - 6x + 3 = 0$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

1. Theory of the Discriminant and Nature of Roots:

For a quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with $a \neq 0$, the Discriminant is defined as:

$$D = b^2 - 4ac$$
  • If $D > 0$: The equation has two distinct real roots given by $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}$.
  • If $D = 0$: The equation has two equal real roots given by $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$.
  • If $D < 0$: The equation has no real roots (roots are non-real complex numbers).

2. (i) For $2x^2 - 3x + 5 = 0$:

  • Here $a = 2, b = -3, c = 5$.
  • Discriminant: $$D = b^2 - 4ac = (-3)^2 - 4(2)(5) = 9 - 40 = -31$$
  • Since $D < 0$, this equation has no real roots.

3. (ii) For $3x^2 - 4\sqrt{3}x + 4 = 0$:

  • Here $a = 3, b = -4\sqrt{3}, c = 4$.
  • Discriminant: $$D = b^2 - 4ac = (-4\sqrt{3})^2 - 4(3)(4) = (16 \times 3) - 48 = 48 - 48 = 0$$
  • Since $D = 0$, the equation has two equal real roots.
  • Evaluating the roots: $$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a} = \frac{-(-4\sqrt{3}) \pm 0}{2(3)} = \frac{4\sqrt{3}}{6} = \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$$
  • Roots: $x = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}, \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$.

4. (iii) For $2x^2 - 6x + 3 = 0$:

  • Here $a = 2, b = -6, c = 3$.
  • Discriminant: $$D = b^2 - 4ac = (-6)^2 - 4(2)(3) = 36 - 24 = 12 > 0$$
  • Since $D > 0$, the equation has two distinct real roots.
  • Evaluating the roots: $$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a} = \frac{-(-6) \pm \sqrt{12}}{2(2)} = \frac{6 \pm 2\sqrt{3}}{4} = \frac{2(3 \pm \sqrt{3})}{4} = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{3}}{2}$$
  • Roots: $x = \frac{3 + \sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $x = \frac{3 - \sqrt{3}}{2}$.

Question 6
Find the values of $k$ for each of the following quadratic equations, so that they have two equal roots: (i) $2x^2 + kx + 3 = 0$, (ii) $kx(x - 2) + 6 = 0$.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Condition for Equal Roots:

A quadratic equation has two equal real roots if and only if its discriminant $D = b^2 - 4ac = 0$.

(i) $2x^2 + kx + 3 = 0$:

  • Here $a = 2, b = k, c = 3$.
  • $D = b^2 - 4ac = k^2 - 4(2)(3) = k^2 - 24$.
  • For equal roots: $k^2 - 24 = 0 \implies k^2 = 24 \implies k = \pm\sqrt{24} = \pm 2\sqrt{6}$.
  • Answer: $k = 2\sqrt{6}$ or $k = -2\sqrt{6}$.

(ii) $kx(x - 2) + 6 = 0$:

  • Expand the equation: $kx^2 - 2kx + 6 = 0$.
  • Here $a = k, b = -2k, c = 6$. For a quadratic equation, $a = k \neq 0$.
  • $D = b^2 - 4ac = (-2k)^2 - 4(k)(6) = 4k^2 - 24k$.
  • For equal roots: $$4k^2 - 24k = 0 \implies 4k(k - 6) = 0$$ $$k = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad k = 6$$
  • If $k = 0$, the $x^2$ and $x$ terms vanish ($6 = 0$, impossible). Hence $k \neq 0$.
  • Answer: $k = 6$.

Question 7
A train travels 360 km at a uniform speed. If the speed had been 5 km/h more, it would have taken 1 hour less for the same journey. Find the speed of the train.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Define Variables & Travel Parameters

  • Total distance of the journey = $360\text{ km}$.
  • Let the uniform speed of the train be $x\text{ km/h}$ ($x > 0$).
  • Time taken at normal speed: $T_1 = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}} = \frac{360}{x}\text{ hours}$.
  • If the speed is increased by $5\text{ km/h}$, the new speed $= (x + 5)\text{ km/h}$.
  • Time taken at increased speed: $T_2 = \frac{360}{x + 5}\text{ hours}$.

Step 2: Formulate the Algebraic Equation

According to the given condition, the journey takes 1 hour less at the increased speed:

$$T_1 - T_2 = 1$$ $$\frac{360}{x} - \frac{360}{x + 5} = 1$$

Step 3: Simplify into Standard Quadratic Form

$$360 \left(\frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{x + 5}\right) = 1$$ $$360 \left[\frac{(x + 5) - x}{x(x + 5)}\right] = 1$$ $$360 \left[\frac{5}{x^2 + 5x}\right] = 1$$ $$\frac{1800}{x^2 + 5x} = 1 \implies x^2 + 5x = 1800$$ $$x^2 + 5x - 1800 = 0$$

Step 4: Solve the Quadratic Equation

Find two numbers whose product is $-1800$ and sum is $+5$. These are $+45$ and $-40$:

$$x^2 + 45x - 40x - 1800 = 0$$ $$x(x + 45) - 40(x + 45) = 0$$ $$(x + 45)(x - 40) = 0$$ $$x = -45 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 40$$

Step 5: Verify and Conclude

Speed of a train cannot be negative ($x > 0$), so $x \neq -45$. Thus, $x = 40\text{ km/h}$.

Check: Normal time $= 360 / 40 = 9\text{ hours}$. Increased speed time $= 360 / 45 = 8\text{ hours}$. Difference $= 9 - 8 = 1\text{ hour}$.

Final Answer: The uniform speed of the train is $40\text{ km/h}$.

Question 8
Two water taps together can fill a tank in $9\frac{3}{8}$ hours. The tap of larger diameter takes 10 hours less than the smaller one to fill the tank separately. Find the time in which each tap can separately fill the tank.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step 1: Problem Modeling

  • Total time taken by both taps working together $= 9\frac{3}{8}\text{ hours} = \frac{75}{8}\text{ hours}$.
  • In 1 hour, the fraction of tank filled by both taps together $= \frac{1}{75/8} = \frac{8}{75}$.
  • Let the tap of smaller diameter take $x\text{ hours}$ to fill the tank alone ($x > 10$).
  • Then the tap of larger diameter takes $(x - 10)\text{ hours}$ to fill the tank alone.

Step 2: Formulate Equation

Fraction filled by smaller tap in 1 hour $= \frac{1}{x}$.
Fraction filled by larger tap in 1 hour $= \frac{1}{x - 10}$.

$$\frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{x - 10} = \frac{8}{75}$$

Step 3: Simplify Equation

$$\frac{(x - 10) + x}{x(x - 10)} = \frac{8}{75}$$ $$\frac{2x - 10}{x^2 - 10x} = \frac{8}{75} \implies \frac{2(x - 5)}{x^2 - 10x} = \frac{8}{75} \implies \frac{x - 5}{x^2 - 10x} = \frac{4}{75}$$ $$75(x - 5) = 4(x^2 - 10x)$$ $$75x - 375 = 4x^2 - 40x$$ $$4x^2 - 40x - 75x + 375 = 0$$ $$4x^2 - 115x + 375 = 0$$

Step 4: Solve by Factorisation

Product $= 4 \times 375 = 1500$, sum $= -115$. The factors are $-100$ and $-15$:

$$4x^2 - 100x - 15x + 375 = 0$$ $$4x(x - 25) - 15(x - 25) = 0 \implies (4x - 15)(x - 25) = 0$$ $$x = 25 \quad \text{or} \quad x = \frac{15}{4} = 3.75$$

Step 5: Check Feasibility

If $x = 3.75\text{ hours}$, then the time for larger tap would be $x - 10 = 3.75 - 10 = -6.25\text{ hours}$, which is impossible since time cannot be negative.
Therefore, $x = 25\text{ hours}$.

  • Time taken by smaller tap $= 25\text{ hours}$.
  • Time taken by larger tap $= 25 - 10 = 15\text{ hours}$.

Final Answer: The smaller tap takes 25 hours and the larger tap takes 15 hours separately to fill the tank.

Case Study Question • Environmental Landscape Design
A park designer wants to fence a rectangular garden along a river bank (so only 3 sides require fencing). The total fencing wire available is 100 metres and the desired area is 1200 m². Can such a garden be designed? If yes, find its dimensions.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Let the two perpendicular sides be $x\text{ metres}$ each.
  2. Then the side parallel to the river $= 100 - 2x\text{ metres}$.
  3. Area equation: $x(100 - 2x) = 1200 \implies 100x - 2x^2 = 1200$.
  4. Divide by 2: $-x^2 + 50x - 600 = 0 \implies x^2 - 50x + 600 = 0$.
  5. Discriminant: $D = (-50)^2 - 4(1)(600) = 2500 - 2400 = 100 > 0$.
    Since $D > 0$, real solutions exist! The garden can be designed.
  6. Solve: $(x - 30)(x - 20) = 0 \implies x = 20\text{ or } x = 30$.
    If $x = 20\text{ m}$: Dimensions are $20\text{ m} \times 60\text{ m}$.
    If $x = 30\text{ m}$: Dimensions are $30\text{ m} \times 40\text{ m}$.
