📌 Key Commercial Mathematics Formulas
  • Ratio & Percentage: Ratio compares two quantities of the same unit. Percentage is a fraction with denominator 100. \text{Percentage} = \frac{\text{Value}}{\text{Total}} \times 100\%.
  • Discount & Marked Price: \text{Discount} = \text{Marked Price (MP)} - \text{Sale Price (SP)}.
    \text{Discount \%} = \frac{\text{Discount}}{\text{MP}} \times 100.
  • Taxes (Sales Tax / VAT / GST): Calculated on the selling price and added to bill amount: \text{Bill Amount} = \text{Cost} + \text{GST}.
  • Compound Interest (CI): A = P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^n, where A = Amount, P = Principal, R = Annual Rate, n = Time period in years.
    \text{CI} = A - P.
    Compounded Half-Yearly: Rate = R/2, Time = 2n.
  • Growth & Depreciation: Growth: P_n = P_0 \left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^n. Depreciation: P_n = P_0 \left(1 - \frac{R}{100}\right)^n.
NCERT Exercise 7.1
Q1
Find the ratio of the following:
(a) Speed of a cycle 15 km/h to the speed of scooter 30 km/h.
(b) 5 m to 10 km.
(c) 50 paise to ₹ 5.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) Cycle (15 km/h) to Scooter (30 km/h):
Both are in km/h. \text{Ratio} = \frac{15}{30} = \frac{1}{2} = 1 : 2.
(b) 5 m to 10 km:
Convert km to m: 10\text{ km} = 10 \times 1000\text{ m} = 10000\text{ m}.
\text{Ratio} = \frac{5}{10000} = \frac{1}{2000} = 1 : 2000.
(c) 50 paise to ₹ 5:
Convert ₹ to paise: ₹ 5 = 5 \times 100\text{ paise} = 500\text{ paise}.
\text{Ratio} = \frac{50}{500} = \frac{1}{10} = 1 : 10.

72% of 25 students are interested in mathematics. How many are not interested in mathematics?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Total students = 25.
Percentage not interested = 100\% - 72\% = 28\%.
Number of students not interested in mathematics:
= 28\% \text{ of } 25 = \frac{28}{100} \times 25 = \frac{28}{4} = 7 students.

Final Answer: 7 students are not interested in mathematics.

Q4
A football team won 10 matches out of the total number of matches they played. If their win percentage was 40, then how many matches did they play in all?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Let the total number of matches played be x.
According to question:
40\% \text{ of } x = 10
\frac{40}{100} \times x = 10 \implies \frac{2}{5}x = 10
x = 10 \times \frac{5}{2} = 5 \times 5 = 25.

Final Answer: The team played 25 matches in all.

NCERT Exercise 7.2
Q5
During a sale, a shop offered a discount of 10% on the marked prices of all items. What would a customer have to pay for a pair of jeans marked at ₹ 1450 and two shirts marked at ₹ 850 each?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Total Marked Price (MP):
MP of 1 pair of jeans = ₹ 1450
MP of 2 shirts = 2 \times ₹ 850 = ₹ 1700
Total MP = ₹ 1450 + ₹ 1700 = ₹ 3150.
Discount rate = 10%.
Total Discount = 10\% \text{ of } 3150 = \frac{10}{100} \times 3150 = ₹ 315.
Amount customer has to pay = \text{MP} - \text{Discount} = ₹ 3150 - ₹ 315 = ₹ 2835.

Q6
Arun bought a pair of roller skates at a sale where the discount given was 20%. If the amount he pays is ₹ 1600, find the marked price.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Let the Marked Price (MP) be x.
Discount = 20% of x.
Sale Price (SP) = x - 0.20x = 0.80x (or 80\% \text{ of } x).
Given \text{SP} = ₹ 1600:
\frac{80}{100} \times x = 1600
x = \frac{1600 \times 100}{80} = 20 \times 100 = ₹ 2000.

Final Answer: The marked price of the roller skates was ₹ 2,000.

Q7
An article was purchased for ₹ 1239 including GST of 18%. Find the price of the article before GST was added.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Let the original price before GST be ₹ x.
GST = 18% of x.
Price with GST = x + \frac{18}{100}x = \frac{118}{100}x = 1.18x.
Given total cost = ₹ 1239:
\frac{118}{100}x = 1239
x = \frac{1239 \times 100}{118} = 10.5 \times 100 = ₹ 1050.

Final Answer: The price before GST was ₹ 1,050.

Q8
The population of a place increased to 54,000 in 2003 at a rate of 5% per annum.
(i) Find the population in 2001.
(ii) What would be its population in 2005?
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) Population in 2001:
Let population in 2001 be P. Time gap = 2 years, Rate R = 5\%.
54000 = P \left(1 + \frac{5}{100}\right)^2 = P \left(\frac{21}{20}\right)^2 = P \times \frac{441}{400}
P = \frac{54000 \times 400}{441} = \frac{21600000}{441} \approx 48,980 (approx).
(ii) Population in 2005:
Base population in 2003 = 54,000. Time = 2 years.
\text{Population}_{2005} = 54000 \left(1 + \frac{5}{100}\right)^2 = 54000 \times \frac{441}{400}
= 135 \times 441 = 59,535.

Q9
A scooter was bought at ₹ 42,000. Its value depreciated at the rate of 8% per annum. Find its value after one year.
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Principal cost P = ₹ 42000, Depreciation rate R = 8\%, Time n = 1 year.
\text{Depreciation amount} = 8\% \text{ of } 42000 = \frac{8}{100} \times 42000 = ₹ 3360.
Value after 1 year = 42000 - 3360 = ₹ 38,640.
(Using formula: 42000 \left(1 - \frac{8}{100}\right) = 42000 \times 0.92 = ₹ 38640).

Case Study: Banking & Fixed Deposits
Scenario: Radhika invests ₹ 10,000 in a fixed deposit for 1 year at 10% per annum. Compare the interest she receives if it is: (i) Compounded annually (ii) Compounded half-yearly.

(i) Compounded Annually:
A = 10000 \left(1 + \frac{10}{100}\right)^1 = 10000 \times 1.10 = ₹ 11,000.
Interest = ₹ 11,000 - ₹ 10,000 = ₹ 1,000.

(ii) Compounded Half-Yearly:
Rate = 10/2 = 5\% per half-year; Time = 2 \times 1 = 2 half-years.
A = 10000 \left(1 + \frac{5}{100}\right)^2 = 10000 \times 1.05 \times 1.05 = ₹ 11,025.
Interest = ₹ 11,025 - ₹ 10,000 = ₹ 1,025.

Observation: Compounding half-yearly yields ₹ 25 more interest due to interest-on-interest after 6 months.

⚡ Successive Discounts (HOTS)
Is offering two successive discounts of 20% and 10% the same as offering a single flat discount of 30%? Justify mathematically.

No. Let the Marked Price be ₹ 100.

After first discount of 20%: Price = 100 - 20 = ₹ 80.
Second discount of 10% is calculated on ₹ 80: 10\% \text{ of } 80 = ₹ 8.
Final Price = 80 - 8 = ₹ 72.
Effective single discount = 100 - 72 = 28\%, which is strictly less than 30%.
