📌 Key Concepts & Formulas Summary
  • Rational Number: Any number that can be expressed in the form p/q, where p, q ∈ ℤ and q ≠ 0.
  • Closure Property: Rational numbers are closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication. They are not closed under division because division by zero is undefined.
  • Commutative Property: For any two rational numbers a and b:
    • a + b = b + a (Addition is commutative)
    • a × b = b × a (Multiplication is commutative)
    • Subtraction and division are not commutative.
  • Associative Property: For rational numbers a, b, c:
    • (a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
    • (a × b) × c = a × (b × c)
    • Subtraction and division are not associative.
  • Distributive Property: a(b + c) = ab + ac and a(b − c) = ab − ac.
  • Identities & Inverses:
    • Additive Identity: 0, because a + 0 = 0 + a = a.
    • Additive Inverse: For rational number a, its additive inverse is -a, such that a + (−a) = 0.
    • Multiplicative Identity: 1, because a × 1 = 1 × a = a.
    • Multiplicative Inverse (Reciprocal): For non-zero rational number a/b, its reciprocal is b/a, such that (a/b) × (b/a) = 1. Note: 0 has no reciprocal.

Name the property under multiplication used in each of the following:
(i) -4/5 × 1 = 1 × -4/5 = -4/5
(ii) -13/17 × -2/7 = -2/7 × -13/17
(iii) -19/29 × 29/-19 = 1
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) 1 is the Multiplicative Identity.
Reason: For any rational number a, a × 1 = 1 × a = a.
(ii) Commutative Property of Multiplication (Commutativity).
Reason: For any two rational numbers a and b, a × b = b × a.
(iii) Multiplicative Inverse Property (or Reciprocal Property).
Reason: For any non-zero rational number a, a × 1/a = 1.

Q2
Tell what property allows you to compute 1/3 × (6 × 4/3) as (1/3 × 6) × 4/3?
1 Mark
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Property: Associative Property of Multiplication (Associativity).

Explanation: For any three rational numbers a, b, and c, the grouping does not affect the product:
a × (b × c) = (a × b) × c.
Here, a = 1/3, b = 6, and c = 4/3.

Q3

Explanation: This follows directly from the Closure Property of Multiplication for rational numbers. If a/b and c/d are rational numbers (b, d ≠ 0), then their product (ac)/(bd) is also a rational number because ac ∈ ℤ and bd ∈ ℤ ≠ 0.

Q4
Using appropriate properties, find:
(i) -2/3 × 3/5 + 5/2 − 3/5 × 1/6
(ii) 2/5 × (−3/7) − 1/6 × 3/2 + 1/14 × 2/5
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) Given: -2/3 × 3/5 + 5/2 − 3/5 × 1/6
Step 1 (Commutativity): Rearrange terms to bring terms with the common factor 3/5 together:
= -2/3 × 3/5 − 3/5 × 1/6 + 5/2
Step 2 (Distributivity): Take out common factor 3/5:
= 3/5 (−2/3 − 1/6) + 5/2
Step 3: Simplify bracket: -2/3 − 1/6 = (−4 − 1)/6 = -5/6.
= 3/5 × (−5/6) + 5/2 = -1/2 + 5/2 = (−1 + 5)/2 = 4/2 = 2.
(ii) Given: 2/5 × (−3/7) − 1/6 × 3/2 + 1/14 × 2/5
Step 1 (Commutativity): Rearrange terms containing 2/5:
= 2/5 × (−3/7) + 1/14 × 2/5 − (1/6 × 3/2)
Step 2 (Distributivity): Factor out 2/5:
= 2/5 (−3/7 + 1/14) − 1/4
Step 3: Simplify bracket: -3/7 + 1/14 = (−6 + 1)/14 = -5/14.
= 2/5 × (−5/14) − 1/4 = -1/7 − 1/4 = (−4 − 7)/28 = -11/28.

Q6
Verify that -(−x) = x for:
(i) x = 11/15
(ii) x = -13/17
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(i) Given x = 11/15:
The additive inverse of x is -x = -11/15.
Since 11/15 + (−11/15) = 0, the additive inverse of -11/15 is -(−11/15).
Therefore, -(−11/15) = 11/15 = x.
Hence verified: LHS = RHS.
(ii) Given x = -13/17:
Additive inverse of x is -x = -(−13/17) = 13/17.
Now, -(−x) = -(13/17) = -13/17 = x.
Hence verified: LHS = RHS.

Q9
Is 8/9 the multiplicative inverse of -1 1/8? Why or why not?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Condition: A number a is the multiplicative inverse of b if and only if a × b = 1.
Convert mixed fraction to improper fraction:
-1 1/8 = -(8 × 1 + 1)/8 = -9/8.
Calculate their product:
8/9 × (−9/8) = -1 ≠ 1.

Conclusion: No, 8/9 is not the multiplicative inverse of -1 1/8 because their product is -1 and not +1.

Q10
Is 0.3 the multiplicative inverse of 3 1/3? Why or why not?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Condition: Product of two numbers must be equal to 1.
Convert decimal and mixed fraction to rational form:
0.3 = 3/10
3 1/3 = (3 × 3 + 1)/3 = 10/3
Now compute the product:
0.3 × 3 1/3 = 3/10 × 10/3 = 30/30 = 1.

Conclusion: Yes, 0.3 is the multiplicative inverse of 3 1/3 because their product is exactly 1.

Q11
Find five rational numbers between 2/3 and 4/5.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Step 1: Make denominators equal by finding the LCM of 3 and 5.
LCM(3, 5) = 15.
2/3 = (2 × 5)/(3 × 5) = 10/15
4/5 = (4 × 3)/(5 × 3) = 12/15
Step 2: To find five numbers, multiply numerator and denominator of both by a suitable factor, say 4 (or 5):
(10 × 4)/(15 × 4) = 40/60
(12 × 4)/(15 × 4) = 48/60
Step 3: The integers between 40 and 48 are 41, 42, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47.
Hence, five rational numbers between 2/3 and 4/5 are:
41/60, 42/60, 43/60, 44/60, 45/60 (or simplified: 41/60, 7/10, 43/60, 11/15, 3/4).

Case Study: Stock Market Variations
Scenario: An investor notices the price of a green energy stock fluctuating daily. On Monday it changed by +₹7/4, on Tuesday by -₹5/6, and on Wednesday by +₹1/2.
(i) What is the net price change over the three days?
(ii) What is the additive inverse of the net change, and what does it represent?

(i) Net Price Change:
Net change = 7/4 + (−5/6) + 1/2
LCM of 4, 6, 2 is 12.
= (7 × 3 − 5 × 2 + 1 × 6)/12 = (21 − 10 + 6)/12 = 17/12 = +₹1 5/12.
The stock gained ₹1.42 (or ₹17/12).

(ii) Additive Inverse:
The additive inverse of +17/12 is -17/12.
It represents the exact price drop required on Thursday to bring the stock back to its opening value on Monday.

⚡ Assertion & Reason
Assertion (A): Between any two distinct rational numbers, there are infinitely many rational numbers.
Reason (R): The mean or average (a+b)/2 of two rational numbers a and b always lies strictly between a and b.
Choose: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A. (b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation. (c) A is true, R is false. (d) A is false, R is true.
Answer: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
Explanation: By repeatedly applying the mean method m = (a+b)/2, between a and m, and between m and b, we generate an endless sequence of rational numbers. This density property proves there are infinitely many rational numbers between any two rational numbers.
