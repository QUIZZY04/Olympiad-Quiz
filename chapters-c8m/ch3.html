📌 Key Geometric Theorems & Properties
  • Polygons: A simple closed curve made up of only line segments.
    • Convex Polygon: All diagonals lie completely inside the interior; every interior angle < 180°.
    • Concave Polygon: At least one diagonal lies in the exterior; at least one interior angle > 180° (reflex angle).
    • Regular Polygon: Both equiangular (all angles equal) and equilateral (all sides equal).
  • Angle Sum Formulas:
    • Sum of interior angles of an n-sided polygon = (n - 2) \times 180^\circ.
    • Each interior angle of a regular n-sided polygon = \frac{(n - 2) \times 180^\circ}{n}.
    • Sum of measures of external angles of any convex polygon = 360^\circ.
    • Each exterior angle of a regular n-sided polygon = \frac{360^\circ}{n}.
    • Number of sides = \frac{360^\circ}{\text{Measure of each exterior angle}}.
  • Quadrilateral Special Types:
    • Parallelogram: Opposite sides are equal & parallel; opposite angles are equal; adjacent angles are supplementary (180^\circ); diagonals bisect each other.
    • Rhombus: All 4 sides equal; opposite angles equal; diagonals bisect each other perpendicularly (90^\circ).
    • Rectangle: Parallelogram with four right angles (90^\circ); diagonals are equal and bisect each other.
    • Square: Rectangle with all sides equal; diagonals are equal and perpendicular bisectors of each other.
    • Trapezium: Exactly one pair of opposite sides is parallel.
    • Kite: Two distinct pairs of adjacent equal sides; diagonals are perpendicular; one diagonal bisects the other.

Q2
How many diagonals does each of the following have?
(a) A convex quadrilateral    (b) A regular hexagon    (c) A triangle
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer

Formula: Number of diagonals of an n-sided polygon = \frac{n(n - 3)}{2}.

(a) Convex quadrilateral (n = 4):
\text{Diagonals} = \frac{4(4 - 3)}{2} = \frac{4 \times 1}{2} = 2.
(b) A regular hexagon (n = 6):
\text{Diagonals} = \frac{6(6 - 3)}{2} = \frac{6 \times 3}{2} = 9.
(c) A triangle (n = 3):
\text{Diagonals} = \frac{3(3 - 3)}{2} = \frac{3 \times 0}{2} = 0.

Q3
What is the sum of the measures of the angles of a convex quadrilateral? Will this property hold if the quadrilateral is not convex?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Angle sum of convex quadrilateral:
A quadrilateral can be divided into two triangles by drawing one diagonal.
Angle sum = 2 \times 180^\circ = 360^\circ (or (4 - 2) \times 180^\circ = 360^\circ).
Non-convex (concave) quadrilateral:
Yes, this property holds even if the quadrilateral is concave (not convex). By drawing an internal diagonal between vertices, the concave quadrilateral also divides into two non-overlapping triangles, whose angle sum is 180^\circ + 180^\circ = 360^\circ.

Q6
Find the angle measure x in the following figures:
(a) Quadrilateral with angles 50^\circ, 130^\circ, 120^\circ, x
(b) Quadrilateral with right-angle exterior, 60^\circ, 70^\circ, x
(c) Pentagon with top angle 30^\circ, two equal angles x, and base exterior angles 70^\circ and 60^\circ
(d) Regular pentagon with 5 equal angles x
4 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) Quadrilateral: Angle sum = 360^\circ.
50^\circ + 130^\circ + 120^\circ + x = 360^\circ
300^\circ + x = 360^\circ \implies x = 60^\circ.
(b) Quadrilateral: Exterior angle = 90^\circ \implies adjacent interior angle = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ.
90^\circ + 60^\circ + 70^\circ + x = 360^\circ
220^\circ + x = 360^\circ \implies x = 140^\circ.
(c) Pentagon: Angle sum = (5 - 2) \times 180^\circ = 540^\circ.
Base interior angles via linear pair: 180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ and 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ.
30^\circ + x + x + 110^\circ + 120^\circ = 540^\circ
2x + 260^\circ = 540^\circ \implies 2x = 280^\circ \implies x = 140^\circ.
(d) Regular pentagon: All 5 angles are equal to x.
5x = 540^\circ \implies x = \frac{540^\circ}{5} = 108^\circ.

Q5
(a) Is it possible to have a regular polygon with measure of each exterior angle as 22^\circ?
(b) Can it be an interior angle of a regular polygon? Why?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) Exterior angle = 22^\circ:
For a regular polygon, the number of sides n = \frac{360^\circ}{22^\circ} = \frac{180}{11} = 16.36, which is not a whole number.
Since the number of sides must be an integer \ge 3, No, it is not possible.
(b) Interior angle = 22^\circ:
If interior angle = 22^\circ, then exterior angle = 180^\circ - 22^\circ = 158^\circ.
Then n = \frac{360^\circ}{158^\circ} = \frac{180}{79}, which is not an integer.
Therefore, No, 22^\circ cannot be an interior angle of a regular polygon.

Q6
(a) What is the minimum interior angle possible for a regular polygon? Why?
(b) What is the maximum exterior angle possible for a regular polygon?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
(a) Minimum interior angle:
The regular polygon with the fewest possible sides is an equilateral triangle (n = 3).
Each interior angle of an equilateral triangle = \frac{(3 - 2) \times 180^\circ}{3} = 60^\circ.
As n increases, the interior angles increase. Hence, the minimum interior angle is 60^\circ.
(b) Maximum exterior angle:
Since Interior Angle + Exterior Angle = 180^\circ, the maximum exterior angle occurs when the interior angle is at its minimum:
\text{Max exterior angle} = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ.

Q3
The measures of two adjacent angles of a parallelogram are in the ratio 3:2. Find the measure of each of the angles of the parallelogram.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer
Let the two adjacent angles be 3x and 2x.
Property: Adjacent angles of a parallelogram are supplementary (180^\circ):
3x + 2x = 180^\circ
5x = 180^\circ \implies x = \frac{180^\circ}{5} = 36^\circ.
The two adjacent angles are:
3x = 3 \times 36^\circ = 108^\circ
2x = 2 \times 36^\circ = 72^\circ

Since opposite angles are equal, the four angles of the parallelogram are 108^\circ, 72^\circ, 108^\circ, 72^\circ.

🎯 Competency-Based Questions (CBQ) & HOTS CBSE Board Exam Pattern
Case Study: Tessellation in Architecture
Scenario: An architect is designing floor tiles for a museum using regular polygons. Can a floor be tessellated (tiled without gaps or overlaps) using only regular pentagons? Justify mathematically.

For regular polygons to tessellate a flat surface around a vertex, the sum of interior angles meeting at each vertex must be exactly 360^\circ (so 360^\circ must be divisible by the interior angle).

Each interior angle of a regular pentagon = \frac{(5 - 2) \times 180^\circ}{5} = \frac{540^\circ}{5} = 108^\circ.

Checking divisibility: \frac{360^\circ}{108^\circ} = 3.33, which is not an integer.

Conclusion: No, regular pentagons cannot tessellate a floor by themselves because 108^\circ does not divide 360^\circ evenly.

⚡ Proof Challenge (HOTS)
ABC is a right-angled triangle, right-angled at B, and O is the mid-point of the hypotenuse AC. Prove that O is equidistant from vertices A, B, and C.

Construction: Produce BO to D such that BO = OD. Join AD and DC.

In quadrilateral ABCD:
AO = OC (given) and BO = OD (by construction).
Since diagonals AC and BD bisect each other, ABCD is a parallelogram.

Since \angle B = 90^\circ, parallelogram ABCD is a rectangle.

In a rectangle, diagonals are equal: AC = BD.
Taking halves: \frac{1}{2}AC = \frac{1}{2}BD \implies OA = OC = OB.
Hence, O is equidistant from A, B, and C.
