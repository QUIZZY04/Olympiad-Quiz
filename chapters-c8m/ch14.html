📌 Key Mensuration Formulas & 3D Solid Geometry
  • Area of 2D Polygons:
    • Trapezium: Area = 1/2 × (a + b) × h, where a and b are parallel sides, and h is the perpendicular distance between them.
    • General Quadrilateral: Area = 1/2 × d × (h₁ + h₂), where d is a diagonal, and h₁, h₂ are perpendicular offsets from opposite vertices.
    • Rhombus: Area = 1/2 × d₁ × d₂, where d₁ and d₂ are diagonal lengths.
  • Surface Area & Volume of 3D Solids:
    • Cube (edge l):
      • Lateral Surface Area (LSA) = 4l²
      • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 6l²
      • Volume = l³
    • Cuboid (length l, breadth b, height h):
      • Lateral Surface Area (Area of 4 walls) = 2(l + b)h
      • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2(lb + bh + hl)
      • Volume = l × b × h
    • Right Circular Cylinder (radius r, height h):
      • Curved Surface Area (CSA) = 2πrh
      • Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2πr(r + h)
      • Volume = πr²h
  • Metric Capacity Conversions:
    • 1 cm³ = 1 mL
    • 1 L = 1,000 cm³ = 1,000 mL
    • 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³ = 1,000 Litres = 1 kL

Q1
The shape of the top surface of a table is a trapezium. Find its area if its parallel sides are 1 m and 1.2 m and perpendicular distance between them is 0.8 m.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Trapezium Area Formula: Area = 1/2 × (a + b) × h.

1. Given: Parallel sides a = 1 m, b = 1.2 m, and height h = 0.8 m.

2. Substituting values:
Area = 1/2 × (1 + 1.2) × 0.8
Area = 1/2 × 2.2 × 0.8
Area = 1.1 × 0.8 = 0.88 m².

Hence, the area of the top surface of the table is 0.88 m².

Q2
The area of a trapezium is 34 cm² and the length of one of the parallel sides is 10 cm and its height is 4 cm. Find the length of the other parallel side.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
1. Let the length of the unknown parallel side be b cm.
Given: Area = 34 cm², side a = 10 cm, height h = 4 cm.

2. Using formula: Area = 1/2 × (a + b) × h
34 = 1/2 × (10 + b) × 4
34 = (10 + b) × 2

3. Solving for b:
10 + b = 34 / 2 = 17
b = 17 − 10 = 7 cm.

Therefore, the length of the other parallel side is 7 cm.

1. Given: Diagonal d = 24 m, perpendicular offsets h₁ = 13 m and h₂ = 8 m.

2. Substituting values:
Area = 1/2 × 24 × (13 + 8)
Area = 12 × 21 = 252 m².

Hence, the area of the quadrilateral field is 252 m².

Q4
The diagonals of a rhombus are 7.5 cm and 12 cm. Find its area. If the perimeter of another rhombus is 40 cm and its altitude is 5 cm, find its area.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
• Part 1: Rhombus with diagonals d₁ = 7.5 cm, d₂ = 12 cm:
Area = 1/2 × d₁ × d₂
Area = 1/2 × 7.5 × 12 = 7.5 × 6 = 45 cm².

• Part 2: Rhombus with Perimeter = 40 cm and Altitude = 5 cm:
Since all 4 sides of a rhombus are equal:
Side = Perimeter / 4 = 40 / 4 = 10 cm.
Since a rhombus is also a parallelogram:
Area = Base × Altitude = Side × Altitude
Area = 10 × 5 = 50 cm².

Q5
There are two cuboidal boxes. Box A has dimensions 60 cm × 40 cm × 50 cm. Box B is a cube of side 50 cm. Which box requires the lesser amount of material to make?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Criterion: The material required to make a closed box corresponds to its Total Surface Area (TSA).

• For Cuboidal Box A (l = 60 cm, b = 40 cm, h = 50 cm):
TSA = 2(lb + bh + hl)
TSA = 2[(60 × 40) + (40 × 50) + (50 × 60)]
TSA = 2[2400 + 2000 + 3000] = 2[7400] = 14,800 cm².

• For Cubic Box B (side l = 50 cm):
TSA = 6l²
TSA = 6 × 50² = 6 × 2500 = 15,000 cm².

• Comparison:
Since 14,800 cm² < 15,000 cm², Box A requires lesser material to make (by 200 cm²).

Q6
A suitcase with measures 80 cm × 48 cm × 24 cm is to be covered with a tarpaulin cloth. How many meters of tarpaulin of width 96 cm is required to cover 100 such suitcases?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
1. Total Surface Area of 1 suitcase (l = 80, b = 48, h = 24):
TSA = 2(lb + bh + hl)
= 2[(80 × 48) + (48 × 24) + (24 × 80)]
= 2[3840 + 1152 + 1920] = 2[6912] = 13,824 cm².

2. Total Surface Area of 100 suitcases:
Total Area = 100 × 13,824 = 1,382,400 cm².

3. Tarpaulin cloth is rectangular with width = 96 cm and unknown length L:
Area of cloth = Width × Length
1,382,400 = 96 × L
L = 1,382,400 / 96 = 14,400 cm.

4. Converting to meters:
Length in meters = 14,400 / 100 = 144 meters.

Therefore, 144 meters of tarpaulin cloth is required.

Q7
A road roller takes 750 complete revolutions to move once over to level a road. Find the area of the road if the diameter of the road roller is 84 cm and length is 1 m. (Take π = 22/7)
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Principle: In one revolution, a cylinder levels an area equal to its Curved Surface Area (CSA = 2πrh).

1. Dimensions of the cylindrical roller:
Diameter = 84 cm ⇒ Radius r = 84 / 2 = 42 cm = 0.42 m.
Length (height h) = 1 m.

2. Curved Surface Area (CSA):
CSA = 2πrh = 2 × (22/7) × 0.42 × 1
CSA = 2 × 22 × 0.06 × 1 = 2.64 m².

3. Total area leveled in 750 revolutions:
Total Area = 750 × CSA = 750 × 2.64 = 1,980 m².

Hence, the area of the road is 1,980 m².

Q8
A cuboidal water tank is 6 m long, 5 m wide and 4.5 m deep. How many litres of water can it hold? (1 m³ = 1000 litres)
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
1. Dimensions: Length l = 6 m, Breadth b = 5 m, Depth (height) h = 4.5 m.

2. Volume of the cuboidal tank:
Volume = l × b × h
Volume = 6 × 5 × 4.5 = 30 × 4.5 = 135 m³.

3. Capacity in litres:
Since 1 m³ = 1,000 litres:
Capacity = 135 × 1,000 = 1,35,000 litres.

Hence, the tank can hold 1,35,000 litres of water.

Q9
Find the height of a cylinder whose radius is 7 cm and the total surface area is 968 cm². (Take π = 22/7)
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Formula: Total Surface Area (TSA) = 2πr(r + h).

1. Given: Radius r = 7 cm, TSA = 968 cm².

2. Substituting into formula:
968 = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × (7 + h)
968 = 44 × (7 + h)

3. Solving for h:
7 + h = 968 / 44 = 22
h = 22 − 7 = 15 cm.

Therefore, the height of the cylinder is 15 cm.

Q10
If each edge of a cube is doubled: (i) how many times will its surface area increase? (ii) how many times will its volume increase?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
1. Let the original edge of the cube be l.
Original Surface Area: S₁ = 6l².
Original Volume: V₁ = l³.

2. When the edge is doubled, new edge = 2l.

• (i) New Surface Area (S₂):
S₂ = 6(2l)² = 6(4l²) = 4 × (6l²) = 4 × S₁.
Therefore, the surface area increases by 4 times (or 2² times).

• (ii) New Volume (V₂):
V₂ = (2l)³ = 8l³ = 8 × V₁.
Therefore, the volume increases by 8 times (or 2³ times).

(i) Surface area increases 4 times (2² factor)1 Mark
(ii) Volume increases 8 times (2³ factor)1 Mark

Context — Municipal Rainwater Harvesting Cistern Architecture: A residential society constructs a cylindrical concrete rainwater harvesting tank with an internal radius of 3.5 meters and depth (height) of 8 meters.
To prevent seepage and algae buildup, the society decides to waterproof both the interior curved wall and the base circular floor with an epoxy coating.

(a) Calculate the total surface area that needs to be coated (curved cylindrical wall + flat bottom circular floor).
(b) If epoxy waterproofing costs ₹ 120 per square meter, calculate the total cost of coating the tank.
(c) Calculate the maximum water storage capacity of the tank in cubic meters and in litres. (Take π = 22/7).
Detailed Analytical Solution & Marking Scheme

(a) Surface Area to be Coated:
Area = Curved Surface Area (CSA) + Base Circle Area
= 2πrh + πr² = πr(2h + r)
= (22/7) × 3.5 × [2(8) + 3.5]
= 11 × [16 + 3.5] = 11 × 19.5 = 214.5 m².

(b) Total Cost of Waterproofing:
Cost = Area × Rate = 214.5 × ₹ 120 = ₹ 25,740.

(c) Maximum Water Storage Capacity:
Volume = πr²h = (22/7) × (3.5)² × 8
= (22/7) × 12.25 × 8 = 22 × 1.75 × 8 = 308 m³.
In litres = 308 × 1,000 = 3,08,000 litres.
