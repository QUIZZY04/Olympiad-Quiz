The Voice of the Rain

NCERT Rationalised 2026–27 Syllabus • All 6 Verbatim Questions with Official Step-by-Step Marking Rubrics

The Eternal Cycle: Parallel Between Rain & Song

NCERT Exercises: Think It Out & Scientific Parallels
Question 1
Think It Out
There are two voices in the poem. Who do they belong to? Which lines indicate this?
[2]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The poem is structured as an imaginary, philosophical dialogue between two distinct voices:

  1. The Voice of the Poet:
    Indicated in the opening line: “And who art thou? said I to the soft-falling shower,”
  2. The Voice of the Rain:
    Indicated in lines 3 and 4: “I am the Poem of Earth, said the voice of the rain,”

Whitman uses personification to give the shower of rain a vocal consciousness capable of translating its ecological mission into human language.

CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Identification of the poet and rain voices with exact textual quotes1 Mark
Explanation of personification giving conscious speech to rain1 Mark
Question 2
Think It Out
What does the phrase “strange to tell” mean?
[2]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The phrase “strange to tell” conveys the poet’s sheer wonder, amazement, and disbelief.

When the poet casually addresses the falling shower with the inquiry "Who art thou?", he never expected an answer from an inanimate meteorological phenomenon. To his pleasant astonishment, the rain responded in an articulate, poetic voice that the poet felt compelled to transcribe and translate into human language.

CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Poet’s surprise and wonder at an unexpected reply from nature1 Mark
Rain responding with articulate voice requiring translation1 Mark
Question 3
Think It Out
There is a parallel drawn between rain and music. Which words indicate this? Explain the similarity between the two.
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The parallel between rain and music is explicitly established when rain proclaims herself to be the “Poem of Earth”, and elaborated in the final bracketed lines:

“(For song, issuing from its birth-place, after fulfilment, wandering / Reck’d or unreck’d, duly with love returns.)”

Similarities between Rain and Song (Music):

  • Origin: A song originates from the soul or heart of a poet/singer; rain originates from the land and bottomless ocean depths.
  • Wandering & Transformation: A song travels across the world, touching diverse listeners; rain rises into the skies as invisible vapor, condensates into clouds, and wanders through atmospheric heavens.
  • Disregard for Recognition: Whether heard and appreciated (“reck’d”) or ignored (“unreck’d”), neither ceases its mission.
  • Return with Love: Both return with love to replenish their birthplace—song returns to the singer's heart as joy and artistic fulfillment, while rain returns to the parched earth to revive seeds and wash away dust layers.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Textual quote indicating parallel (Poem of Earth / bracketed song lines)1 Mark
Shared origins (poet’s soul vs earth and water bodies)1 Mark
Wandering whether reck’d or unreck’d1 Mark
Final return with love to purify and beautify their birthplace1 Mark
Question 4
Think It Out
How is the cyclic movement of rain brought out in the poem? Compare it with what you have learnt in science.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Walt Whitman poeticizes the scientific hydrological (water) cycle through evocative verses:

  1. Evaporation (Ascent): The poem states that rain rises “impalpable out of the land and the bottomless sea” into the heavens. In science, water evaporates invisibly from water bodies as vapor due to solar heating.
  2. Condensation (Formation): In the heavens, it is “vaguely form’d, altogether changed, and yet the same.” Scientifically, invisible vapor cools and condenses around microscopic dust particles to form clouds; its physical state changes from gas to droplets, yet its chemical nature ($H_2O$) remains identical.
  3. Precipitation (Descent): Rain descends to “lave the droughts, atomies, dust-layers of the globe” and germinate “seeds that had been only latent, unborn.” Scientifically, precipitation brings moisture back to earth, recharging aquifers and stimulating plant germination.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Ascent: Impalpable rising mapped to scientific evaporation1 Mark
Condensation: "Vaguely form’d, altogether changed yet same" mapped to cloud formation1 Mark
Descent: Laving droughts and germinating seeds mapped to precipitation1 Mark
Question 5
Think It Out
Why are the last two lines put within brackets?
[2]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The last two lines—“(For song, issuing from its birth-place, after fulfilment, wandering / Reck’d or unreck’d, duly with love returns.)”—are enclosed in brackets because:

  • Shift in Speaker: The previous lines represent the direct dialogue spoken by the Voice of the Rain. The bracketed lines are not spoken by the rain; they are the poet’s personal philosophical reflection and concluding commentary.
  • Literary Aside: The brackets function as an intellectual "aside," drawing a conceptual analogy between the ecological cycle of rain and the artistic lifecycle of human music and poetry.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Distinction between the rain’s speech and the poet’s internal contemplation1 Mark
Enclosure marks a philosophical aside comparing art and nature1 Mark
Question 6
Think It Out
List the pairs of opposites found in the poem.
[2]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Walt Whitman employs multiple binary oppositions to reflect the comprehensive reach of nature:

  • Rise and Descend: Upward motion into heaven vs. downward shower onto the globe.
  • Day and Night: “And for ever, by day and night, I give back life to my own origin.”
  • Reck’d and Unreck’d: Cared for / noticed vs. uncared for / unnoticed.
  • Latent and Unborn: Dormant under soil vs. brought to flourishing life through rain.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Rise and descend / Day and night identified1 Mark
Reck’d and unreck’d / Latent and unborn identified1 Mark
