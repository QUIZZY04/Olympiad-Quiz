The Summer of the Beautiful White Horse

The Moral Dilemma: Garoghlanian Honor vs Childhood Passion

NCERT Exercises: Reading with Insight
Question 1
Reading with Insight
You will probably agree that this story does not have breathless adventure and exciting action. Then what in your opinion makes it interesting?
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

While the story lacks high-octane violence, espionage, or perilous physical adventures, it is profoundly engaging, warm, and delightful for several literary reasons:

  1. Nostalgic Childhood Perspective: Narrated from the perspective of nine-year-old Aram, the story captures the enchanting innocence of a world that was “full of every imaginable kind of magnificence, and life was still a delightful and mysterious dream.”
  2. Authentic Child Psychology: The boys’ childish rationalization—that stealing a horse to ride was not the same as stealing money because they never intended to sell it—rings psychologically true and humorous.
  3. Eccentric and Colorful Characters: The "crazy streak" in the family provides rich humor, particularly through Uncle Khosrove with his booming temper and universal dismissal: “It is no harm; pay no attention to it!”, even when his own house was burning down.
  4. Poignant Moral Conflict: The quiet tension between the boys’ desperate longing for horses and their unyielding pride in their tribe’s eleven centuries of unblemished honesty creates a subtle, emotionally satisfying climax.

Question 2
Reading with Insight
Did the boys return the horse because they were conscience-stricken or because they were afraid?
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The boys returned the horse because they were conscience-stricken, not out of fear of punishment or detection.

Detailed Literary Evidence:

  • No Fear of Discovery: The boys had successfully hidden the beautiful white horse in the deserted vineyard of farmer Fetvajian for weeks, riding it every morning at daybreak. Nobody suspected them because the Garoghlanian tribe was universally renowned for honesty.
  • The Encounter with John Byro: When the boys met the horse’s rightful owner, John Byro, on the road, Byro examined the horse closely, looked into its mouth tooth by tooth, and remarked: “I would swear it is my horse if I didn’t know your parents. The fame of your family for honesty is well known to me. Yet the horse is the twin of my horse.”
  • The Prick of Conscience: John Byro’s noble refusal to accuse them, solely out of profound respect for their family’s proud heritage of integrity, pierced their conscience deeply. They realized that keeping the horse would permanently tarnish their family's unblemished eleven-hundred-year reputation. The very next morning, they took the horse to John Byro’s vineyard and tied it in the barn.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Clear assertion that return was driven by conscience, not fear1 Mark
Proof that the horse was securely hidden and suspicion was zero1 Mark
John Byro’s unconditional trust in their family honesty acting as catalyst1 Mark
Conscious decision to protect tribal honor over personal pleasure1 Mark
Question 3
Reading with Insight
“One day back there in the good old days when I was nine and the world was full of every imaginable kind of magnificence, and life was still a delightful and mysterious dream...” The story begins in a mood of nostalgia. Can you narrate some incident from your childhood that brings back memories like these?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

This opening line evokes the timeless magic of early childhood, when reality is bathed in wonder and simple adventures assume epic grandeur.

Personal Reflective Narrative (Sample Model Response):

“This line transports me back to my seventh summer, when my cousins and I spent our holidays at my grandfather’s ancestral village home. One twilight evening, we discovered an abandoned, overgrown orchard behind the cow sheds. To our young imaginations, that tangled thicket of guava trees was an uncharted enchanted forest. We fashioned wooden swords from fallen branches, christened an old rusty bullock cart as our royal chariot, and spent hours chasing fireflies across the moonlit fields. There were no cellphones, no academic pressures, and no anxieties about the future—the world was infinitely large, safe, and brimming with innocence. Just as Aram and Mourad felt flying through walnut orchards in the cool dawn air, that summer remains an indelible dream of pure happiness.”

Question 4
Reading with Insight
Describe the distinctive traits of Mourad and Uncle Khosrove that show they shared the family’s “crazy streak”.
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

In the Garoghlanian tribe, individual character was believed to descend directly from spiritual ancestors rather than biological fathers. Both Mourad and Uncle Khosrove were living embodiments of the family’s eccentric "crazy streak":

  • Uncle Khosrove’s Eccentricities: An enormous man with a roaring voice and a bushy black moustache. He was notoriously impatient, irritable, and furious, silencing all human dialogue with his trademark roar: “It is no harm; pay no attention to it!” When his son Arak ran eight blocks to tell him their house was on fire, Khosrove simply roared: “Enough! It is no harm, I say!” When John Byro mourned his stolen horse, Khosrove roared that losing a horse was trivial compared to losing their homeland.
  • Mourad’s Crazy Streak: Viewed as the natural descendant of Khosrove’s mania. He lived with wild, fearless abandon, sang or roared lustily while galloping bareback on the horse at 4 a.m., and claimed to have “a way with horses, a way with dogs, and a way with birds.” He nursed an injured young robin's broken wing back to health and talked to animals with serene confidence.
