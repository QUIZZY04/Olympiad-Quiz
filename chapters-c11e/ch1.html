The Portrait of a Lady

Thematic Concept: Three Phases of the Relationship

NCERT Exercises: Understanding the Text & Talking About the Text
Question 1
Understanding the Text
Mention the three phases of the author’s relationship with his grandmother before he left the country to study abroad.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The author, Khushwant Singh, details three distinct, emotional phases in his relationship with his grandmother over the course of his early life:

  1. Phase 1: Childhood in the Village (Intimate Companionship): The author lived alone with his grandmother while his parents settled in the city. She was his sole caregiver, waking him up, bathing him, plastering his slate, reciting morning prayers in a monotonous sing-song, and escorting him to the temple school. They were inseparable companions, and on their return, she fed stale chapattis to village dogs.
  2. Phase 2: Boyhood in the City (The Turning Point): When called to the city by his parents, their bond weakened. Though they still shared a room, the grandmother no longer accompanied him to school as he traveled by an English motor bus. She could neither assist him with his Western science curriculum (law of gravity, Archimedes' principle) nor approve of the lack of scriptural education and the inclusion of music. They saw significantly less of each other.
  3. Phase 3: University Life (Snapped Friendship & Seclusion): When the author enrolled in university, he was allocated a room of his own. The last physical link of their companionship was formally severed. The grandmother stoically accepted her seclusion, spending her days at the spinning wheel, reciting prayers on her rosary, and feeding hundreds of sparrows in the courtyard.

Question 2
Understanding the Text
Mention three reasons why the author’s grandmother was disturbed when he started going to the city school.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The grandmother was deeply agitated and distressed when the author began attending the city English school because:

  1. Inability to Assist in Studies: The curriculum was taught in English and covered Western science (Archimedes' principle, gravity, the earth being round). Since she did not know English or modern science, she felt completely sidelined and unable to guide him.
  2. Absence of Moral and Religious Instruction: She was distressed to learn that there was no teaching about God, holy scriptures, or spiritual values in the city school curriculum, which she considered the core foundation of true education.
  3. Aversion to Music Lessons: When he announced he was receiving music lessons, she was appalled. To her traditional mindset, music had "lewd associations"—she believed it was the exclusive monopoly of harlots and beggars and had no place in the upbringing of respectable gentlefolk. Her disapproving silence conveyed her deep displeasure.

Question 3
Understanding the Text
Mention three ways in which the author’s grandmother spent her days after he grew up.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

After the author grew up and was assigned a separate room at university, the grandmother spent her days in three fixed routines:

  1. Spinning Wheel: From sunrise to sunset, she sat by her spinning wheel spinning thread, rarely pausing to speak to anyone in the household.
  2. Continuous Prayer and Rosary: Her lips constantly moved in an inaudible, silent prayer while her fingers ceaselessly told the beads of her rosary.
  3. Feeding the Courtyard Sparrows: Only in the afternoon did she relax for half an hour to feed sparrows in the verandah. She broke bread into little crumbs, and hundreds of birds perched on her legs, shoulders, and head. She smiled gently but never shooed them away; this was consistently the happiest half-hour of her day.

Question 4
Understanding the Text
Mention the odd way in which the author’s grandmother behaved just before she died.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Just before her death, the grandmother exhibited strange and uncharacteristic behavior:

  • Sudden Break in Lifelong Habit: On the evening the author returned from abroad, she did not pray—a first in her entire life. Instead, she collected women from the neighborhood, acquired an old dilapidated drum, and thudded its sagging skin for hours, singing celebratory songs of warriors returning home.
  • Pre-monition of Impending End: The next morning she was taken ill with a mild fever. Although the doctor assured the family it would pass, she declared with absolute conviction that her end was near.
  • Refusal to Converse: She stated that since she had omitted her prayers in the last chapter of her life, she would not waste another moment talking to her family.
  • Peaceful Departure in Prayer: Ignoring all familial protests, she lay peacefully in bed, reciting prayers and telling her beads until her lips stopped moving and the rosary fell from her lifeless fingers.

Question 5
Understanding the Text
Mention the way in which the sparrows expressed their sorrow when the author’s grandmother died.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The sparrows paid an extraordinary, solemn tribute to the departed grandmother, displaying emotional empathy that transcended animal instinct:

  • Silent Gathering: When the grandmother's shroud-wrapped body lay on the verandah floor, thousands of sparrows flew in and sat scattered all over the courtyard.
  • Total Absence of Chirping: Unlike their usual lively chatter and chirruping, there was complete, mournful silence across the courtyard.
  • Disregard for Food: Feeling pity, the author’s mother threw small pieces of bread crumbs to them, exactly as the grandmother used to. The sparrows took absolutely no notice of the bread, proving they had not assembled for food but to mourn their human benefactor.
  • Quiet Departure: As soon as the corpse was carried out for cremation, the sparrows flew away silently. The next morning, the sweeper swept the untouched bread crumbs into the dustbin.

Question 6
Talking About the Text
The author’s grandmother was a religious person. What are the different ways in which we come to know this?
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Khushwant Singh’s narrative paints the portrait of an intensely pious, spiritual woman whose existence was anchored in religious faith:

  1. Inaudible Prayers & Rosary: Her lips were perpetually moving in an inaudible, meditative prayer, and her hands were continuously occupied telling the beads of her rosary.
  2. Temple Visits in the Village: She chose to accompany the author to the village school every morning specifically because the school was attached to a temple, allowing her to sit inside and read holy scriptures while he studied the alphabet.
  3. Morning Hymns: While washing and dressing the author, she recited morning hymns in a monotonous sing-song voice, hoping he would absorb them by heart.
  4. Discontent with Secular Curriculum: In the city, her primary resentment against the modern school was its lack of religious and moral instruction.
  5. Compassion for God's Creatures: Feeding stale chapattis to village dogs and bread crumbs to sparrows was an extension of her religious duty and dharmic compassion.
  6. Devotion at the Hour of Death: On her deathbed, she refused to waste precious minutes in conversation, dedicating her final moments purely to God until the rosary slipped from her fingers.

Question 7
Talking About the Text
Describe the mutual love and bond between the grandmother and the author. Did the emotional distance change their feelings for each other?
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The relationship between the author and his grandmother is one of profound, unspoken tenderness, mutual respect, and emotional steadfastness:

  • Deep Childhood Affection: In early childhood, the grandmother was the author's world. She tended to his physical and emotional needs with unconditional maternal warmth, fostering deep mutual trust.
  • Acceptance of Changing Circumstances: As the author grew into adolescence and adulthood, physical distance inevitably grew—first through city schooling, then university separation, and eventually five years of foreign study. However, the grandmother never harbored bitterness, never demanded attention, and gracefully accepted her seclusion without guilt-tripping the author.
  • Unfading Emotional Bond: When the author left for abroad, she kissed his forehead silently at the railway station—a moist touch the author cherished as possibly the last physical imprint of their love. When he returned five years later, she clasped him in her arms, whispering her prayers.
  • Conclusion: Although modernization and adulthood created physical distance, their underlying emotional connection remained pristine and untarnished until the very end.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Early childhood companionship and tender nurturing described1 Mark
Dignified acceptance of inevitable separation due to higher studies1 Mark
Railway station farewell kiss and affectionate homecoming reunion1 Mark
Concluding analysis confirming enduring mutual love despite physical distance1 Mark
