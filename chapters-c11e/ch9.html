The Evolution of Maturity: Loss of Innocence

NCERT Exercises: Think It Out & Philosophical Analysis
Question 1
Think It Out
Identify the stanza that talks of each of the following: (i) Individuality, (ii) Rationalism, (iii) Hypocrisy.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Mapping the philosophical themes to the corresponding stanzas:

  • (i) Rationalism — Stanza 1:
    Reflected when the poet realizes at age eleven that “Hell and Heaven / Could not be found in Geography,” concluding through scientific logic that they could not physically exist.
  • (ii) Hypocrisy — Stanza 2:
    Reflected when the poet observes that adults “were not all they seemed to be. / They talked of love and preached of love, / But did not act so lovingly.”
  • (iii) Individuality — Stanza 3:
    Reflected when the poet discovers that his mind is completely his own: “To use whichever way I choose, / Producing thoughts that were not those of other people / But my own, and mine alone.”

Question 2
Think It Out
What, according to the poem, is involved in the process of growing up?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Markus Natten portrays growing up not merely as biological aging, but as a three-stage psychological transformation accompanied by the loss of naive innocence:

  1. Emergence of Rational Thinking: The child abandons blind belief in folklore, myths, and religious dogmas (like geographical hell and heaven) and begins evaluating reality through empirical logic and science.
  2. Recognition of Adult Hypocrisy: The growing adolescent sees through the facade of adult morality, realizing that elders preach lofty sermons about love and compassion but frequently practice hostility and deception.
  3. Assertion of Individuality: The individual develops intellectual autonomy, forming unique personal convictions rather than slavishly following herd mentality.

Question 3
Think It Out
What are the poet’s feelings towards childhood?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The poet views childhood with deep nostalgia, reverence, and a sense of wistful loss:

  • Pristine Sanctuary of Innocence: He regards childhood as a magical, uncorrupted period of existence free from deceit, cynicism, and inner contradictions.
  • Wistful Yearning: While he values the rational intellect and individuality that accompany adulthood, he mourns the irrevocable loss of childlike simplicity and wonder.
  • Consolation: He takes comfort in knowing that childhood has not vanished into absolute nothingness; it remains immortalized in the pure, untainted smile on an infant’s face.

Question 4
Think It Out
Which, do you think, are the most poetic lines? Why?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The concluding lines of the poem are widely regarded as the most poetic and evocative:

“It went to some forgotten place,
That’s hidden in an infant’s face,
That’s all I know.”

Critical Rationale:

  • Shift from ‘When’ to ‘Where’: Across the first three stanzas, the poet repeatedly asks the temporal question: “When did my childhood go?” In this final stanza, he pivots to the spatial question: “Where did my childhood go?”
  • Lyrical Imagery: Instead of offering a dry philosophical answer, the poet captures the essence of innocence through the tender, concrete image of an infant’s face.
  • Universal Truth: It highlights that childhood is not lost forever; it is reborn continuously in every new generation of infants, leaving the reader with a poignant, lingering sense of peace.
