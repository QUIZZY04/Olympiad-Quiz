The Psychological Burden of Possessions: Number 46, Marconi Street

NCERT Exercises: Reading with Insight
Question 1
Reading with Insight
“Have you come back?” said the woman, “I thought that no one had come back.” Does this statement give some clue about the story? If yes, what is it?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Yes, this blunt, inhospitable opening statement provides profound clues about the story’s socio-historical context and human drama:

  • The Context of the Holocaust: The setting is Holland during and after World War II. The narrator and her mother were Dutch Jews persecuted by the Nazi regime. The woman, Mrs. Dorling, had assumed that the entire family had perished in Nazi concentration camps, as happened to millions of European Jews.
  • Human Greed and Betrayal: Mrs. Dorling took custody of all the valuable antique furniture, crockery, cutlery, and silverware under the pretext of "safekeeping." Her disappointment upon seeing the daughter alive betrays her covetousness—she had usurped these treasures as her own and felt threatened that someone had survived to reclaim them.
  • Sets the Emotional Tone: It establishes the cold indifference and lack of basic human sympathy that greeted surviving Jewish refugees upon returning to their homelands.

Clue identifying World War II, Nazi occupation and Holocaust extermination1 Mark
Exposure of Mrs. Dorling’s greed assuming the owners were dead1 Mark

Question 2
Reading with Insight
The story is divided into pre-war and post-war times. What hardships do you think the girl underwent during these times?
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Marga Minco’s understated prose reveals intense psychological and physical trauma across both eras:

  • Pre-War Hardships:
    The girl and her mother lived under the terrifying shadow of Nazi persecution, facing curfew restrictions, yellow star mandates, and the constant threat of deportation. They suffered the agonizing distress of seeing their cherished ancestral home dismantled piece by piece, as Mrs. Dorling carried away heavy suitcases filled with silver and antiques, while the mother lived in constant dread of an impending raid.
  • Post-War Hardships:
    The narrator survived the war entirely alone—her mother, family, and former life were eradicated. She lived in a bleak, tiny rented room with blackout paper peeling from the windows. When she attempted to reconnect with her past by visiting Number 46, Marconi Street, she was met with cold hostility by Mrs. Dorling. Entering the house on a second visit, she found her mother’s prized belongings displayed tasteless and misused in an alien living room, re-traumatizing her with grief and alienation.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Pre-war fear of arrest, persecution and seeing home dismantled2 Marks
Post-war total bereavement, loneliness in rented room, and traumatic reunion2 Marks
Question 3
Reading with Insight
Why did the narrator of the story want to forget the address?
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The narrator resolved to erase Number 46, Marconi Street from her memory because holding onto it prolonged futile pain:

  • Severed Emotional Connection: Possessions derive their emotional soul and beauty from the living context of the loved ones who used them. Stripped of her mother’s gentle touch and placed in Mrs. Dorling’s tasteless, stuffy room, the objects looked mutilated and severed from their past.
  • Impracticality of Material Possessions: The narrator lived in a tiny rented room with narrow cutlery drawers. She had no space or practical use for heavy silver sets, pewter plates, or elaborate tea services.
  • Psychological Liberation: Reclaiming the objects could never bring back her mother or resurrect her lost youth. To cling to the address meant remaining imprisoned by grief, resentment, and bitterness. Forgetting the address was an act of mature emotional liberation, enabling her to make a clean break with the past and move forward.

Question 4
Reading with Insight
“The Address” is a story of human predicament that follows war. Comment.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Minco’s narrative is a poignant exploration of the profound, invisible wounds inflicted by war on the human psyche:

  • Beyond Physical Destruction: While war history often documents battle deaths and ruined cities, it rarely records the quiet tragedy of the survivors—individuals left uprooted, emotionally bankrupt, and isolated in empty landscapes.
  • Moral Erosion: War brings out predatory selfishness in acquaintances (like Mrs. Dorling), who feign friendship to opportunistically loot the possessions of vulnerable families facing extermination.
  • The Futility of Clinging to the Past: The survivor realizes that material relics cannot substitute for living relationships. True healing begins only when the victim courageously relinquishes physical attachments and accepts that the pre-war world is irretrievably gone.
