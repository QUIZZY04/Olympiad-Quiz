A Photograph

Thematic Structure: Three Stages of Time & Mourning

NCERT Exercises: Think It Out & Poetic Appreciation
Question 1
Think It Out
What does the word ‘cardboard’ denote in the poem? Why has this word been used?
[2]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The word ‘cardboard’ denotes the stiff, thick backing on which the vintage black-and-white photograph of the poet’s mother and her cousins is mounted.

The poet specifically uses the word ‘cardboard’ for multiple literary reasons:

  • Frail and Insubstantial Medium: Unlike marble or enduring stone monuments, cardboard is a cheap, perishable material that inevitably yellows and degrades over time, underscoring the fragility of human records.
  • Stark Contrast between Inanimate Matter & Mortal Life: The lifeless, stiff piece of cardboard has survived for decades, outlasting the vibrant, living human being it portrays. It ironically emphasizes the transience of human life compared to ordinary, inanimate objects.

Question 2
Think It Out
What has the camera captured?
[2]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The camera captured an innocent, sunlit beach holiday from the distant past:

  • The Subjects: The poet's mother when she was about twelve years old, flanked by her two younger cousins, Betty and Dolly. Each cousin was holding one of her hands as they were paddling in the coastal shallows.
  • The Moment: The three girls stood smiling with their sea-blown hair framing their faces, looking directly into the camera held by their uncle.
  • The Contrast: It captures a youthful, carefree moment of laughter frozen in time before adulthood, motherhood, and mortality overtook them.

Question 3
Think It Out
What has not changed over the years? Does this suggest something to you?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The sea has not changed over the years. Its restless waves wash the shore today in precisely the same manner as they did when the three young girls stood there decades ago.

Philosophical Suggestion:

  • Permanence vs. Transience: This creates a poignant contrast between the immortal, unchanging permanence of Nature and the fleeting, perishable nature of human life.
  • The Transferred Epithet: The poet underscores this through the memorable poetic phrase “terribly transient feet.” While the mortal human feet undergo growth, aging, decay, and death, the sea remains immortal and unblemished by the passage of time.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Identification of the sea as the unchanging element over time1 Mark

Question 4
Think It Out
The poet’s mother laughed at the snapshot. What did this laugh indicate?
[2]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The mother’s laugh, occurring some twenty to thirty years after the beach holiday, indicates a complex mixture of nostalgia, amusement, and bittersweet acceptance:

  • Amusement at Childhood Dress: She was amused by the old-fashioned beach clothes that Betty, Dolly, and she were made to wear by their parents for the seaside holiday.
  • Nostalgia for Lost Innocence: The laughter expressed fondness for a carefree, innocent childhood that was gone forever. It demonstrated her ability to look back on lost youth with grace and cheerful resignation.

Question 5
Think It Out
What is the meaning of the line “Both wry with the laboured ease of loss”?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

This profound line encapsulates the central emotional core of the poem through an oxymoron (“laboured ease”):

  • Two Different Losses:
    The Mother’s Loss: The sea holiday of her youth was the mother's past, which she mourned with a nostalgic smile.
    The Poet’s Loss: Her deceased mother’s laughter is the poet's past, which the poet now mourns in grief.
  • The Paradox of ‘Laboured Ease’: Both the mother and daughter have had to struggle intensely (“laboured”) over years to come to terms with their respective irrevocable losses, until time forces an outward calm or acceptance (“ease”). Yet, the lingering pain leaves a twisted, bittersweet, or ironic expression (“wry”) on the face.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Identification of the two parallel losses (mother’s childhood & poet’s mother)1 Mark
Explanation of the oxymoron "laboured ease" (struggle yielding forced acceptance)1 Mark
Significance of "wry" as bittersweet, ironic sorrow1 Mark
Question 6
Think It Out
What does “this circumstance” refer to? Explain: “Its silence silences.”
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

“This circumstance” refers to the present state of bereavement—the permanent absence and death of the poet’s mother, who has now been dead nearly as many years as the twelve-year-old girl in the photograph had lived.

Explanation of “Its silence silences”:

  • Alliteration & Personification: The repetition of the soft 's' sound mimics the quiet, heavy vacuum of grief.
  • Absolute Speechlessness: The circumstance of death is so final and absolute that there are no words left to express or console the profound ache of loss. The silence of the dead mother and the grave envelopes the living daughter, rendering her completely speechless. Death’s final silence silences all human expression.

"This circumstance" defined as the mother’s death and permanent absence1 Mark
