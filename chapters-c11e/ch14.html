The Pearson Revolution: Personality Swap & Domestic Reform

NCERT Exercises: Reading with Insight
Question 1
Reading with Insight
This play, written in the 1950s, is a humorous and satirical depiction of the status of the mother in a family. What are the issues it raises? Do you think it has outlived its utility?
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

J.B. Priestley’s witty play addresses persistent social issues surrounding domestic gender inequality:

  • Unpaid and Unappreciated Domestic Labor: Mrs. Pearson works eight hours a day, seven days a week, cooking, ironing, cleaning, and waiting on her family hand and foot without receiving a single word of thanks, financial compensation, or a weekend off.
  • Taking Mothers for Granted: Her husband George and children Doris and Cyril treat the house as a luxury hotel, dropping in only to demand tea, demanding clothes be mended, and rushing out to their social clubs while abandoning their mother in solitary neglect.
  • Has it Outlived its Utility?
    No, it has certainly NOT outlived its utility. Even in contemporary 21st-century society, working and homemaking mothers alike carry an inequitable "second shift" of domestic chores and mental labor. The systemic expectation that mothers must selflessly sacrifice their personal leisure, dignity, and autonomy remains painfully prevalent worldwide.

Question 2
Reading with Insight
Is drama a good medium for conveying a social message? Discuss.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Drama is uniquely powerful and persuasive for delivering sharp social critiques:

  • Immediate Emotional & Visual Impact: Unlike a dry essay or political speech, drama embodies ideas through living human characters. Watching Doris burst into tears or George cower in embarrassment makes the audience viscerally experience the absurdity of domestic arrogance.
  • The Disarming Power of Satire and Humor: Priestley uses comedy and the fantastical body-swap device to make serious social injustices palpable. Humor disarms the audience’s defenses, allowing them to laugh at the Pearsons while simultaneously recognizing their own selfish domestic behavior.
  • Cathartic Resolution: Drama allows the audience to witness an immediate before-and-after transformation. Seeing the reformed family humbly serve their mother tea and play rummy provides a clear, practical blueprint for domestic reform.

Question 3
Reading with Insight
The play portrays the situation in a British suburban home. Has Indian society changed enough for Indian mothers to relate to Mrs. Pearson?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

An exploration of cross-cultural relevance in contemporary Indian society:

  • Profound Resonance with Indian Mothers: The plight of Mrs. Pearson resonates deeply with the traditional reality of millions of Indian households, where the mother is often eulogized as a selfless deity (“Annapurna” or “Devi”) precisely to justify her domestic servitude. Indian mothers frequently wake up earliest, sleep latest, eat after everyone has eaten, and rarely get personal leisure time.
  • Signs of Gradual Evolution: In modern urban educated homes, there is growing feminist awareness. Husbands and children increasingly share kitchen chores, clean dishes, and support mothers pursuing independent careers and hobbies.
  • Persistent Reality: Despite surface changes, the deep-seated patriarchal conditioning that household management is fundamentally the woman’s responsibility remains prevalent in many Indian households, making Mrs. Pearson’s awakening relatable to Indian audiences.

Deep resonance with traditional Indian homemakers eulogized into servitude1 Mark

Conclusion confirming strong cultural relatability in contemporary India1 Mark
