NCERT Exercises: Understanding the Text & Talking About the Text
Question 1
Understanding the Text
Give reasons for the following: (i) The article has been titled ‘Silk Road’, (ii) Tibetan mastiffs were popular in China’s imperial courts, (iii) The author’s experience at Hor was in stark contrast to earlier accounts of the place.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Detailed reasons grounded in geography, cultural history, and travel literature:

(i) Why the article is titled ‘Silk Road’:

The journey retraces parts of the ancient Silk Road—the historic trading network connecting China, Central Asia, India, and the Mediterranean. The author, a physical geographer, traveled along these ancient high-altitude trade corridors across the Tibetan plateau towards Mount Kailash to undertake the sacred Buddhist pilgrimage (Kora).

(ii) Why Tibetan mastiffs were popular in China’s imperial courts:

Tibetan mastiffs were enormous, ferocious black guard dogs with massive heads, explosive barks, and absolute fearlessness. They were trained by Tibetan nomads (drokbas) to guard encampments and fiercely chase away any intruder. Because of their fierce loyalty and hunting prowess, they were brought along the Silk Road as royal tributes to Chinese emperors to serve as guard dogs.

(iii) Author’s experience at Hor in contrast to earlier accounts:

Earlier accounts by travelers like Japanese monk Ekai Kawaguchi (who wept at the sublime purity of Lake Manasarovar) and Swedish explorer Sven Hedin depicted Hor as a place of spiritual transcendence. In stark contrast, Nick Middleton found Hor to be a desolate, wretched, and grim settlement strewn with dust, rocks, and decades of rusted tin cans and garbage, devoid of any natural vegetation.

(i) Traversal of historic trade routes towards Mount Kailash kora1 Mark
(ii) Enormous ferocity, loyalty and tribute status in imperial courts1 Mark
(iii) Disillusionment: dirty, barren, trash-strewn settlement vs spiritual accounts1 Mark
Question 2
Understanding the Text
Give reasons for the following: (i) The author was disappointed with Darchen, (ii) The author thought that his positive thinking strategy worked well after all.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Detailed psychological and situational analysis:

(i) The author was disappointed with Darchen:

The author reached Darchen dusty and physically exhausted, only to fall acutely ill with severe sinus blockage and nocturnal breathing difficulties at the high altitude. Furthermore, Darchen was drab, dusty, and full of garbage heaps. To his immense dismay, he had arrived early in the season—there were no other pilgrims, the town had a derelict feel, and he found nobody who could speak English to help him organize his Kora.

(ii) The author thought his positive thinking strategy worked well:

Feeling stranded, lonely, and medically drained in Darchen's solitary cafe, Middleton resolved to maintain a positive mental outlook. Almost immediately, he encountered Norbu, a jovial Tibetan scholar working at the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences in Beijing. Norbu spoke fluent English, was also carrying an English academic book, and was visiting to perform the Kora himself. The two struck an instant friendship and decided to team up, hiring yaks to carry their luggage, proving to the author that optimism had yielded an ideal outcome.

(i) Acute altitude sickness, lack of pilgrims, and language barrier at Darchen1.5 Marks
(ii) Serendipitous meeting with English-speaking academic partner Norbu1.5 Marks
Question 3
Understanding the Text
Briefly comment on: (i) The purpose of the author’s journey to Mount Kailash, (ii) The author’s physical condition in Darchen, (iii) The author’s meeting with Norbu.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Commentary on central narrative facets:

(i) Purpose of author’s journey to Mount Kailash:

As an academic geographer, Nick Middleton wished to experience the extreme geography of Tibet and complete the traditional Kora (circumambulation) around sacred Mount Kailash. While motivated partly by cultural appreciation, his drive was primarily that of an intrepid travel writer and geographer exploring remote terrains.

(ii) Physical condition in Darchen:

At an altitude of over 4,500 meters, Middleton suffered from acute mountain sickness. His nasal passages were congested, his chest felt heavy, and whenever he lay flat on his mattress, his breath choked, causing heart palpitations. He spent a sleepless night sitting upright in terror before Tsetan took him to the local Tibetan medical college, where a five-day herbal concoction restored his health.

(iii) Meeting with Norbu:

Sitting alone in Darchen’s rustic cafe, Middleton met Norbu, who noticed the author reading an English novel. Norbu turned out to be an unconventional pilgrim—an urban Tibetan academic who wrote papers on the Kailash Kora but had never actually undertaken it. Both were slightly out of shape and had no desire to prostrate themselves around the mountain, making them well-matched trekking companions.

Question 4
Understanding the Text
Briefly comment on: (i) Tsetan’s support to the author during the journey, (ii) “As a Buddhist, he told me, he knew that it didn’t really matter if I passed away, but he thought it would be bad for business.”
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Analysis of character dynamics and cultural worldview:

(i) Tsetan’s support during the journey:

Tsetan was an exceptional driver, mechanic, and companion. He skillfully maneuvered his four-wheel-drive vehicle across treacherous iced tracks and high passes (5,515 m), throwing dirt on snow to gain traction. When the author fell sick in Darchen, Tsetan took him directly to the medical college, translated his symptoms to the Tibetan doctor, and ensured he was on the road to recovery before departing.

(ii) Meaning of Tsetan’s Buddhist remark:

This humorous yet profound statement reflects a pragmatic collision of Buddhist philosophy and commercial livelihood. As a practicing Tibetan Buddhist, Tsetan viewed bodily death as merely a transition of the soul to its next incarnation, hence "it didn't really matter." However, as a professional travel guide and driver, having a Western tourist die under his care in Darchen would be devastating for his tourism business reputation.

Question 5
Talking About the Text
Discuss the sensitive and generous behaviour of hill-folk as observed in the chapter.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The author’s travelogue highlights the warm, hospitable nature of high-altitude Himalayan communities:

  • Lhamo’s Parting Gift: Before the author left Ravu, Lhamo gave him a traditional long-sleeved sheepskin coat (chuba), recognizing that he would freeze in the biting winds of the high passes. She demonstrated genuine, thoughtful maternal concern for a departing guest.
  • Tsetan’s Unfailing Loyalty: Tsetan cared for the author beyond commercial duty. When Middleton struggled to breathe, Tsetan personally escorted him to the medical college in the middle of the night and waited until he showed improvement.
  • Innocence Uncorrupted by Commercialism: Despite living in harsh, impoverished environments, the hill folk maintain simplicity, spiritual calm, and genuine human warmth.

Question 6
Talking About the Text
What precautions should travelers take when visiting high-altitude regions like Tibet or Ladakh?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Essential health and logistical guidelines for high-altitude expeditions:

  1. Gradual Acclimatization: Ascend slowly to allow the body to adapt to lower oxygen levels and barometric pressure, preventing Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) or High Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE).
  2. Medical Preparedness: Carry altitude sickness medication (e.g., Diamox), nasal decongestants, portable oxygen cans, and essential pain relievers.
  3. Adequate Hydration & Nutrition: Drink copious amounts of water and warm fluids to counter extreme atmospheric dryness, and consume calorie-rich, easily digestible food.
  4. Layered Thermal Clothing: Wear windproof and waterproof insulated gear, thermal inners, UV-protective sunglasses, and heavy fleece gloves to guard against severe hypothermia and frostbite.
  5. Experienced Local Guides: Travel with licensed local drivers familiar with unpredictable mountain blizzards, black ice, and river crossings.

Gradual acclimatization and pacing of altitude gain1 Mark
Medical kit (Diamox, oxygen, decongestants) and hydration1 Mark
Thermal clothing layers and relying on experienced local drivers1 Mark
Question 7
Talking About the Text
Describe the author’s encounter with the Tibetan medical practitioner and the treatment prescribed.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The author’s encounter with Tibetan traditional medicine in Darchen was surprisingly effective:

  • The Setting: The Tibetan Medical College in Darchen was rustic, resembling a monastery courtyard with plain stone buildings and no white coats or diagnostic machines.
  • The Diagnosis: The doctor wore traditional Tibetan garb. Instead of a stethoscope, he carefully felt the author’s pulse at the wrists, observing his eyes and tongue. He diagnosed the condition as a severe cold compounded by the effects of extreme altitude.
  • The Treatment: The doctor provided a five-day supply of Tibetan herbal remedies packed in fifteen small brown paper packets. The morning dose was a brown powder taken with hot water (tasting like cinnamon), and the lunch and bedtime doses were small brown pellets. The remedies proved miraculously effective—Middleton slept soundly that very night.

Rustic clinic setting without modern diagnostic machinery1 Mark
Pulse diagnosis by Tibetan physician identifying cold & altitude shock1 Mark
Fifteen brown herbal powder packets providing immediate, restful relief1 Mark
