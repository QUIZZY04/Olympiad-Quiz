The Resuscitation Protocol: Dr. Andrew Manson’s Miracle at Midnight

NCERT Exercises: Reading with Insight
Question 1
Reading with Insight
“I have done something; oh, God! I’ve done something real at last.” Why does Andrew say this? What does it mean?
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Dr. Andrew Manson exclaims these immortal words at daybreak following an agonizing, miraculous battle for life:

  • Why Andrew Says This:
    Called to attend Joe Morgan’s wife Susan delivering their first child after twenty years of marriage, Andrew faced an acute crisis. The mother was collapsing from post-partum shock, and the infant was born lifeless, pale, and limp—a victim of asphyxia pallida. After stabilizing the mother, Andrew pulled the seemingly dead child from beneath dirty sodden sods under the bed. For nearly an hour, in freezing sweat and physical exhaustion, he plunged the baby alternately between basins of steaming hot and ice-cold water, rubbed it with a rough towel, and applied artificial respiration until a sudden heave filled the lungs and the baby let out a hearty cry.
  • What it Means:
    This exclamation marks Andrew's transition from a naive, textbook medical graduate into a true doctor. Previously plagued by self-doubt, romantic disillusionment, and professional insecurity, saving two human lives through instinctive brilliance gave his existence genuine meaning and purpose. He had achieved something profoundly "real" and noble that transcended theoretical examinations.

Question 2
Reading with Insight
There lies a great difference between textbook medicine and the world of a practising physician. Discuss.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The chapter illustrates the vast chasm between theoretical medical textbooks and practical clinical realities:

  • Limitations of Theory in Emergencies: Medical textbooks provide sterile, step-by-step diagnostic algorithms under ideal laboratory conditions. In practical life, doctors confront chaotic, under-equipped environments (like the cramped, dim bedroom of an impoverished Welsh miner's cottage with no electricity or modern incubators).
  • Split-Second Clinical Instinct: Andrew faced an agonizing moral dilemma—save the dying mother or attempt to revive the lifeless child. No textbook could make that choice for him; clinical instinct dictated saving the mother first, then fighting for the infant.
  • Improvisation & Sheer Will: When orthodox methods failed, Andrew recalled an experimental method of treating asphyxia pallida he had once observed in a Samaritan hospital. Using two kitchen basins of hot and cold water and rough towels, his physical endurance and refusal to concede to death achieved what textbook protocol deemed impossible.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Contrast between sterile academic textbook theory and messy emergency conditions1 Mark
Necessity of moral decision-making and prioritizing between two critical patients1 Mark
Role of clinical improvisation, observation and sheer physical endurance1 Mark
Question 3
Reading with Insight
Describe the precise steps taken by Dr. Andrew Manson to revive the lifeless baby born to Susan Morgan.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Dr. Andrew Manson executed a heroic, methodical resuscitation protocol:

  1. Immediate Diagnosis: Recognizing that the baby's whiteness and limpness indicated asphyxia pallida (lack of oxygen in the blood), he knew standard slapping would not suffice.
  2. Clearing Airways: He wiped mucus from the infant’s nose and mouth to open the respiratory passages.
  3. Hydrotherapy Shock Treatment: He ordered two basins—one filled with water as hot as his hand could bear, the other with ice-cold water. He plunged the limp, slippery body alternately into the cold water, then the steaming water, repeating this thermal shock cycle for fifteen grueling minutes.
  4. Vigorous Friction & Chest Compression: Wrapping the baby in a rough towel, he crushed and released the tiny rib cage rhythmically with his hands, blowing breath into the tiny lungs.
  5. The Miracle: Just as despair gripped the room and the midwife declared it useless, a bubble of mucus issued from the infant’s nostril, the chest convulsed, and the child cried aloud, its skin turning flushed pink.

Correct identification of asphyxia pallida and clearing of airways1 Mark
Alternate hot and cold water immersion shock therapy described1 Mark
Rough towel friction, chest compression and successful respiratory revival1 Mark
