The Laburnum Top

The Dynamic Cycle of Life in "The Laburnum Top"

NCERT Exercises: Find Out & Think It Out
Question 1
Find Out
What is the Laburnum tree called in your language? Which local bird is like the goldfinch?
[2]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Language Equivalent:

In Hindi and most Indian vernacular languages, the Laburnum tree is called the ‘Amaltaas’ (अमलतास) tree. It is characterized by drooping yellow flower clusters, medicinal pods, and poisonous seeds.

Similar Local Bird:

The local bird closely resembling the goldfinch in plumage and size is the Indian Lutino Ringneck or the Yellow-throated Bulbul / Greenfinch (or Eurasian Siskin), which exhibits vibrant yellow-streaked plumage, brisk flight, and melodious whistling calls.

Question 2
Think It Out
What do you notice about the beginning and the ending of the poem?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Ted Hughes crafts a striking circular structure connecting the poem’s opening and conclusion:

  • The Beginning: The poem opens with quiet desolation: “The Laburnum top is silent, quite still” in the yellow September afternoon sunlight, with yellowing leaves and fallen seeds. It resembles a lifeless corpse.
  • The Ending: After the goldfinch launches away into the infinite blue sky, the poem concludes with the exact same quietude: “and the laburnum subsides to empty.”
  • Significance: The bird’s energetic arrival is a transient burst of maternal energy that temporarily awakens the inanimate tree into an engine of life before returning it to primeval silence.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Beginning describes motionless, silent, death-like laburnum top1 Mark
Ending returns to primeval emptiness once goldfinch flies away1 Mark
Significance of circular structure emphasizing transience of vital energy1 Mark
Question 3
Think It Out
To what is the bird’s movement compared? What is the basis for the comparison?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The bird’s entry into the thick foliage is compared to the movement of a lizard (“Sleek as a lizard, and alert, and abrupt”).

Basis for Comparison:

  • Sleek & Smooth: Like a lizard gliding silently over a surface without creating a rustle, the goldfinch slips into the dense yellow foliage with effortless grace.
  • Alert & Cautious: Both creatures display hyper-vigilance, looking around sharply to ensure predators are not observing their movements.
  • Abrupt & Rapid: A lizard makes sudden, darting stops and quick dashes; similarly, the bird makes sudden, sharp movements before vanishing into the interior of the tree to feed her young.

Question 4
Think It Out
Why is the image of the engine evoked by the poet?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The poet invokes the industrial metaphor of an engine because of how energy and movement are generated within the quiet tree:

  • Source of Energy & Power: Just as an engine is the beating heart that sets a lifeless machine or locomotive into roaring motion, the goldfinch is the vital source that sets her hidden brood into frenzied activity.
  • Sonic Symphony: When the mother bird enters with food, the nestlings erupt into a mechanical chorus of “chitterings, and a tremor of wings, and trillings.” The entire tree vibrates and shivers like a roaring motor.
  • Fueling the Engine: Hughes writes: “She stokes that engine full.” The mother bird feeds her offspring with nutrient-rich food (stoking fuel) before flitting out to a branch end.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Metaphor of engine as the source of kinetic power for a machine1 Mark
Nestlings’ eruption of chitterings and wing-tremors compared to machine noise1 Mark

Question 5
Think It Out
What does the phrase “her barred face identity mask” mean?
[2]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The phrase refers to the unique facial markings of the goldfinch and their symbolic role in nature:

  • Camouflage & Disguise: The goldfinch’s body has yellowish plumage that blends seamlessly into the yellowing leaves and yellow blossoms of the Laburnum tree, rendering her invisible to the external world.
  • The Barred Identification: Her face carries distinctive dark bars or black markings across the eyes. While the rest of her body remains camouflaged, this striped, barred face acts as her "identity badge" or "mask" by which her hungry chicks instantly recognize their mother amidst the dense foliage.

Yellow plumage camouflaging her body within yellow laburnum leaves1 Mark
