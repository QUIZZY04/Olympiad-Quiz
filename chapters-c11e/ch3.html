Chronology of Modern Investigations on King Tut’s Mummy

NCERT Exercises: Understanding the Text & Talking About the Text
Question 1
Understanding the Text
Give reasons for the following: (i) King Tut’s body has been subjected to repeated scrutiny, (ii) Howard Carter’s investigation was resented, (iii) Carter had to chisel away the solidified resins to raise the king’s remains.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Detailed reasons for each of the archaeological milestones:

(i) King Tut’s body subjected to repeated scrutiny:

King Tutankhamun was the last youthful heir of a magnificent royal dynasty that had ruled Egypt for centuries. He died unexpectedly as a teenager under mysterious circumstances. In addition to the immense gold treasures buried with him, historians and forensic experts were desperate to determine two unanswered questions: How did he die? and How old was he at the time of his death?

(ii) Howard Carter’s investigation was resented:

British archaeologist Howard Carter’s 1922 investigation was heavily resented because of his invasive, damaging methods. In his obsession to recover the solid gold coffin and priceless artifacts, Carter severed the mummy’s head and hacked off nearly every major joint to free the body from solidified ritual resins, permanently disfiguring Tut’s physical remains.

(iii) Carter chiseling away solidified resins:

The ritual funerary resins used during Tut’s embalming had hardened over 3,300 years, cementing the mummy firmly to the bottom of the solid gold inner coffin. Carter initially attempted to soften the resins by setting the coffin outside in the blazing Egyptian sun at 149°F (65°C), but nothing budged. To prevent thieves from ripping the mummy apart for gold, Carter had no choice but to chisel the hardened resins away from beneath the limbs and torso.

Question 2
Understanding the Text
Give reasons for the following: (i) Tut’s body was buried along with gilded treasures, (ii) The boy king changed his name from Tutankhaten to Tutankhamun.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Reasons grounded in ancient Egyptian theology and historical context:

(i) Why Tut’s body was buried with gilded treasures:

The ancient Egyptian royals were unimaginably wealthy and firmly believed in the afterlife (resurrection). They believed the deceased king could take his riches into the next world. Therefore, Tut was buried with dazzling pure-gold artifacts—collars, inlaid neckpieces, bracelets, rings, amulets, sandals, and gold inner coffins—believed to guarantee immortality, alongside everyday necessities like board games, a bronze razor, linen garments, and cases of food and wine.

(ii) Why the boy king changed his name from Tutankhaten to Tutankhamun:

Tut’s predecessor, the eccentric pharaoh Amenhotep IV (Akhenaten), had outlawed the worship of Egypt's major deity Amun, smashed his temples, closed his shrines, and promoted the sun disc god, Aten. When young Tut took power, he restored the ancient religion, reopened the temples of Amun, and changed his name from Tutankhaten ("Living Image of Aten") to Tutankhamun ("Living Image of Amun") to signal a religious restoration across Egypt.

Question 3
Talking About the Text
Scientific intervention is necessary to unearth buried mysteries. Discuss in groups/pairs.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Modern archaeology relies on non-invasive scientific technologies rather than destructive physical excavations:

  • Non-Invasive Diagnostics: Traditional 19th-century archaeology focused on treasure-hunting and destructive physical extraction (e.g., Howard Carter). Modern scientific interventions—such as computed tomography (CT scanning), digital X-rays, carbon dating, and DNA fingerprinting—allow researchers to examine fragile antiquities without inflicting physical damage.
  • High-Resolution Forensic Reconstruction: King Tut’s 2005 CT scan captured 1,700 digital cross-sectional X-ray images, reconstructing his skull, vertebrae, and ribs in microscopic millimeter slices, verifying that his breastbone and front ribs were indeed missing (likely from an ancient chest trauma).
  • Conclusion: Science rescues historical truth from speculative mythology while preserving the cultural sanctity of ancient relics for posterity.

Question 4
Talking About the Text
Advanced technology gives us conclusive evidence of past events. Discuss in groups/pairs.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

A balanced critical evaluation of advanced technological tools in historical inquiry:

  • Points in Favor (High Precision Clues):
    Advanced tools provide incontrovertible anatomical and chronological data. In Tut’s case, the CT scan definitively disproved rumors that his head had been crushed by an execution weapon, verified his age at death as roughly 19 years, and confirmed that a broken leg had occurred shortly before death, possibly leading to a fatal infection.
  • Limitations (Need for Interpretive Context):
    While technology provides high-resolution anatomical cross-sections, it cannot reconstruct human intent, political conspiracies, or historical motives. For instance, whether Tut's chest injury occurred in battle, a chariot accident, or a court assassination remains open to historical deduction. Technology supplies the evidence; human historians must supply the contextual interpretation.

Arguments supporting technological precision in physical and chronological facts1.5 Marks
Arguments highlighting boundaries where historical interpretation is still needed1.5 Marks
Question 5
Talking About the Text
Traditions are paramount / Scientific research on ancient remains should be prohibited. What are the opposing viewpoints on this debate?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

An exploration of the ethical debate between cultural sanctity and scientific progress:

  • In Favor of Preserving Traditions (Prohibiting Research on Remains):
    Every civilization has sacred funeral rituals designed to allow the dead to rest in eternal peace. Desecrating royal tombs, stripping shrouds, and transporting mummified human bodies through diagnostic scanners degrades sacred human remains into biological specimens. The fear of "the pharaoh’s curse" reflects humanity’s deep-seated ethical boundary against disturbing the repose of the deceased.
  • In Favor of Scientific Research (Unearthing Historical Truth):
    Historical inquiry is a vital human enterprise. Studying ancient remains sheds light on ancestral human genetics, prehistoric pathologies, medical practices, and societal collapses. Without scientific research, misconceptions and myths prevail over truth. Modern scans honor historical figures by documenting their lives accurately.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Arguments for traditional respect and ethical inviolability of graves1.5 Marks
Arguments for scientific illumination, genetic history and medical archaeology1.5 Marks
Question 6
Talking About the Text
What were the dramatic atmospheric and supernatural elements that accompanied King Tut’s CT scan in 2005?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The narrative highlights an eerie coincidence of nature and myth during the CT scan:

  • Violent Desert Weather: An angry, dark wind whipped up desert sand into "dark-bellied clouds" tumbling across the Valley of the Kings, veiling the evening stars in a casket of grey haze.
  • Machine Malfunction: When Tut’s body was placed inside the multimillion-dollar Siemens CT scanner, the cooling fan abruptly stopped working due to fine desert sand entering the mechanism. A nervous guard joked, "Curse of the pharaoh!"
  • Relief upon Completion: The diagnostic team successfully deployed two white substitute plastic fans to complete the scan. Once the data was secured and Tut was returned to his wooden coffin, the wind stopped completely, and a peaceful silence settled over the valley, with Orion—the ancient god of the afterlife—watching over the boy king.
