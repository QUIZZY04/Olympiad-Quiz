The Physics of Alternate Reality: Catastrophe Theory & Quantum Parallel Worlds

NCERT Exercises: Understanding the Text & Talking About the Text
Question 1
Understanding the Text
Tick whether each statement is True or False: (i) The story is an account of real events, (ii) The story hinges on a particular historical event, (iii) Rajendra Deshpande was a historian, (iv) The places mentioned in the story are all imaginary, (v) The story tries to relate history to science.
[5]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Evaluation of each statement based on the text:

  • (i) The story is an account of real events. — [FALSE] (It is a science-fiction narrative exploring speculative alternate history and quantum theories).
  • (ii) The story hinges on a particular historical event. — [TRUE] (It pivots on the outcome of the Third Battle of Panipat fought in 1761 between the Marathas and Ahmad Shah Abdali).
  • (iii) Rajendra Deshpande was a historian. — [FALSE] (Rajendra Deshpande is an eminent physicist and mathematician; Professor Gangadharpant Gaitonde is the historian).
  • (iv) The places mentioned in the story are all imaginary. — [FALSE] (Real geographical locations are featured, including Pune, Bombay/Mumbai, Victoria Terminus, Azad Maidan, and the Town Hall Asiatic Society Library).
  • (v) The story tries to relate history to science. — [TRUE] (It correlates historical branch points with Catastrophe Theory and the Many-Worlds interpretation of Quantum Mechanics).
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
(i) False identified with scientific fiction justification1 Mark
(ii) True identified with Third Battle of Panipat1 Mark
(iii) False identified with Deshpande as physicist1 Mark
(iv) False identified with real geographical locations1 Mark
(v) True identified with Quantum & Catastrophe theories1 Mark
Question 2
Understanding the Text
Briefly explain the following statements from the text: (i) “You neither travelled to the past nor the future. You were in the present experiencing a different world.” (ii) “You have passed through a fantastic experience: or more correctly, a catastrophic experience.”
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Detailed explanations provided by Rajendra Deshpande to Professor Gaitonde:

(i) “You neither travelled to the past nor the future...”

Rajendra clarifies that Gaitonde did not engage in physical time travel. He was not in the 18th century; he was in contemporary, modern-day Bombay, but in an alternate parallel reality. In quantum physics, multiple versions of reality can coexist simultaneously. Due to his subconscious fixation during a collision, Gaitonde made a quantum leap into another branch of reality existing right now in the present.

(ii) “You have passed through a fantastic experience: or more correctly, a catastrophic experience.”

Rajendra uses the term "catastrophic" in a mathematical sense, invoking Catastrophe Theory. In non-linear systems, a tiny variation or tipping point can trigger a massive divergence of outcomes. In the Battle of Panipat, a bullet grazing Vishwasrao’s ear rather than killing him was a bifurcation point that completely reversed the outcome of the battle, altering the subsequent destiny of India.

CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Explanation of quantum parallel world existing in present time1.5 Marks
Explanation of Catastrophe Theory with historical bifurcation point1.5 Marks
Question 3
Understanding the Text
Briefly explain the following statements from the text: (i) Gangadharpant could not help comparing the country he knew with what he was witnessing around him, (ii) “The lack of determinism in quantum theory!” (iii) “You need some interaction to cause a transition.”
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Explanation of the theoretical and observational insights:

(i) Gangadharpant comparing the two Indias:

In his known reality, India endured British colonization, partition, and administrative dependency. In the alternate Bombay he entered, India was a proud, self-reliant democratic nation where the East India Company operated only on leased commercial terms, the Marathas ruled from Delhi, and Indian technology and railways were modern and efficient.

(ii) “The lack of determinism in quantum theory!”:

In classical physics, an object’s trajectory is deterministic and predictable. In quantum mechanics, an electron does not have a single fixed path; it can jump to different energy states unpredictably. Similarly, macroscopic reality is not deterministic—alternative outcomes do not disappear; they manifest in parallel quantum worlds.

(iii) “You need some interaction to cause a transition.”:

Just as an electron requires an interaction (such as absorbing a photon) to transition between orbits, Gaitonde’s brain needed an external catalyst. The violent physical shock of the truck accident, synchronized with his intense mental pondering on "what if the Marathas had won Panipat," triggered a neurological quantum transition into the parallel reality.

Comparison between colonized India and prosperous sovereign alternate India1 Mark
Explanation of quantum non-determinism and electron transitions1 Mark
Explanation of accident + thoughts acting as catalytic transition1 Mark
Question 4
Talking About the Text
A single event may change the course of the history of a nation. Discuss taking opposite points of view.
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Opposing philosophical and historical perspectives on historical turning points:

  • Arguments FOR (The "Great Man" / Tipping Point Theory):
    Proponents argue that pivotal moments often balance on knife-edge accidents. In the story, Vishwasrao surviving the gunshot preserved Maratha army morale, crushed Abdali’s troops, and blocked European colonial dominance. Real history exhibits similar critical events—the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand sparking World War I, or the miraculous retreat at Dunkirk changing WWII.
  • Arguments AGAINST (Socioeconomic Determinism):
    Critics argue that history is driven by massive economic currents, technological advances, and demographic forces rather than isolated accidents. Even if Vishwasrao had survived, Maratha internal factionalism and European naval and industrial superiority might still have eventually altered India's balance of power. Isolated incidents may alter timelines, but underlying historical momentum dictates long-term destiny.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Viewpoint FOR: Detailed tipping point examples and military morale impacts2 Marks
Viewpoint AGAINST: Socioeconomic determinism and macro-historical currents2 Marks
Question 5
Talking About the Text
Reality is what is directly experienced through the senses. Discuss taking opposite points of view.
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

An exploration of sensory perception versus scientific and cognitive reality:

  • Arguments FOR (Empirical Realism):
    Human beings perceive existence through their senses—sight, sound, touch, smell, and taste. For Gaitonde, the alternate Bombay was undeniable: he held printed currency, ate in restaurants, read historical volumes in the Town Hall library, and was physically assaulted by an angry crowd at Azad Maidan. The torn page of the Bhausahebanchi Bakhar in his pocket was tangible, physical proof of his experience.
  • Arguments AGAINST (Quantum & Cognitive Science):
    Modern quantum physics demonstrates that reality is far vaster than human sensory apparatus. Phenomena like electromagnetic spectrums, quantum superposition, and subatomic particles cannot be perceived by naked senses yet are fundamentally real. Furthermore, human consciousness is vulnerable to hallucinations, coma states, and neural misfiring. Sensory experience is merely an interpretation generated by the brain, not absolute reality itself.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Arguments FOR: Tangible sensory proof, physical receipt and library documents2 Marks
Arguments AGAINST: Limitations of human senses, quantum physics and neural states2 Marks
Question 6
Talking About the Text
What tangible evidence convinced Rajendra Deshpande that Professor Gaitonde was not simply hallucinating?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

Rajendra was initially skeptical, treating Gaitonde’s story as a traumatic concussion hallucination, until physical proof was presented:

  • The Missing Page of the Bakhar: When Gaitonde reached into his pocket to retrieve his notes, he inadvertently pulled out a torn page from the Bhausahebanchi Bakhar that he had absentmindedly slipped into his pocket at the alternate Town Hall library.
  • The Discrepancy in Text: Rajendra compared Gaitonde’s torn page with the accepted historical version of the Bakhar. In the real-world historical edition, the text read: "...and then Vishwasrao was guided to the battle and a bullet hit him; he fell dead." But on Gaitonde’s page, the printed Marathi text declared: "...and then Vishwasrao was guided to the battle. A bullet brushed past his ear. By the grace of God, he was saved!"
  • Irrefutable Physical Reality: A hallucination cannot deposit a physically printed, ink-on-paper page from an alternate book inside a person's real jacket pocket. This physical anomaly proved Gaitonde had genuinely crossed quantum dimensional thresholds.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Torn page from the Bhausahebanchi Bakhar produced from pocket1 Mark
Textual proof: bullet grazing ear vs killing Vishwasrao1 Mark
Physical ink-and-paper evidence refuting pure psychological hallucination1 Mark
