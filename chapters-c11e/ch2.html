We're Not Afraid to Die... if We Can All Be Together

NCERT Rationalised 2026–27 Syllabus • All 6 Verbatim Questions with Official Step-by-Step Marking Rubrics

Voyage Map & Crisis Timeline: Wavewalker’s Heroic Journey

NCERT Exercises: Understanding the Text & Talking About the Text
Question 1
Understanding the Text
List the steps taken by the captain: (i) to protect the ship when rough weather began, (ii) to check the flooding of water in the ship.
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The captain took critical precautionary and damage-control measures:

(i) Steps to protect the ship when rough weather began:

  • Slowing the Boat: Dropped the storm jib and looped a heavy mooring rope across the stern to reduce the vessel's speed.
  • Double-Lashing Everything: Securely fastened all gear and equipment on deck to prevent shifting.
  • Life-Raft Drill: Attached lifelines to life rafts, donned waterproof oilskins, and strapped on life jackets.

(ii) Steps to check the flooding of water in the ship:

  • Waterproofing Gaping Holes: Stretched canvas and secured waterproof hatch covers across the shattered starboard side to deflect incoming waves over the side.
  • Managing Hand Pumps: When primary hand pumps clogged with debris and the main electric pump short-circuited under water, the captain located an auxiliary electric pump under the chartroom floor and connected it to an out-pipe.
  • Continuous Pumping: Larry, Herb, and the narrator pumped out water relentlessly for 36 hours until the water level was reduced to a few centimeters.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Steps to protect ship: storm jib, mooring rope, lifelines and life jackets2 Marks
Steps to check flooding: canvas hatch covers, spare electric pump, non-stop pumping2 Marks
Question 2
Understanding the Text
Describe the mental condition of the voyagers on 4 and 5 January.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The emotional and psychological condition of the voyagers fluctuated dramatically across these 48 hours:

  • January 4 (Temporary Respite & Fragile Hope): After 36 hours of continuous pumping, they managed to keep pace with the incoming seawater. They consumed their first meal in nearly two days (corned beef and cracker biscuits). The atmosphere was tinged with exhausted relief, although the captain knew the boat’s structural frame was critically weakened.
  • January 5 (Grim Despair & Impending Doom): Their respite was short-lived. By 4 p.m., black storm clouds gathered again, winds whipped up to 40 knots, and waves towered menacingly. When the narrator went in to comfort the children, Jonathan asked if they were going to die. The children’s bravery deeply moved him, but when night fell, water seeped in steadily through broken timber. By dawn, the situation was utterly hopeless, and the adult voyagers sat holding hands, convinced that their end was very near.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Jan 4: First meal, temporary relief after 36 hours pumping1 Mark
Jan 5: Return of ferocious storm, rising water and grim despair1 Mark
Emotional tension between adults facing death and children displaying courage1 Mark
Question 3
Understanding the Text
Describe the shifts in the narration of the events as indicated in the three sections of the text. Give a subtitle to each section.
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The narrative undergoes distinct tonal and thematic shifts across three main sections:

  1. Section 1: The Joyous Voyage (Subtitle: "Smooth Sailing and Cheerful Ambition")
    Covers the cheerful departure from Plymouth in July 1976 to round the world like Captain James Cook. The tone is buoyant, confident, and optimistic. The 105,000-kilometer leg to Cape Town passes smoothly, and they celebrate a joyous Christmas 3,500 km east of Cape Town despite rough seas.
  2. Section 2: The Catastrophic Struggle (Subtitle: "Into the Jaws of the Tempest")
    Begins on January 2 with gigantic waves and a deafening explosion that slams Wavewalker. The narrative turns breathless, intense, and visceral—detailing broken bones, punctured lungs, flooded cabins, failing pumps, and desperate repairs amidst freezing gale winds.
  3. Section 3: The Triumph of Resilience (Subtitle: "Pinpricks in the Vast Ocean: Safe Haven")
    Covers January 6 onward, characterized by precise navigation calculations, sheer mental determination, and overwhelming emotional relief when the captain spots the volcanic rock of Ile Amsterdam. The tone transforms into gratitude, celebrating human endurance and family unity.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Section 1: Cheerful start with appropriate subtitle1 Mark
Section 2: Violent crisis and survival battle with subtitle1 Mark
Section 3: Mathematical rescue and island triumph with subtitle1 Mark
Question 4
Talking About the Text
What difference did you notice between the reaction of the adults and the children when faced with danger?
[4]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The story contrasts adult responsibility with childlike heroism when confronted by catastrophic danger:

  • The Adults’ Reaction: The adults (the narrator, Mary, Larry Vigil, and Herb Seigler) reacted with pragmatic grit and intense dedication. They experienced terror and physical anguish, yet channeled it into relentless, disciplined action—steering, pumping, navigating, and repairing. When hope slipped away, they succumbed quietly to sorrow, holding hands in resigned acceptance of death.
  • The Children’s Reaction: The children (seven-year-old Suzanne and six-year-old Jonathan) exhibited astonishing maturity, fearlessness, and emotional consideration:
    • Suzanne: Suffered a severely bumped forehead, black eyes, and a deep cut on her arm, yet concealed the full extent of her injuries so as not to distract her father while he fought to keep the ship afloat. Later, she presented a cheerful handmade card with caricatures of her parents to lift their spirits.
    • Jonathan: Uttered the immortal words that gave the chapter its title: "We aren’t afraid of dying if we can all be together—you and Mummy, Sue and all of us." This innocent display of unity renewed the father’s will to battle the ocean.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Adult reaction: Practical action, disciplined pumping, psychological vulnerability2 Marks
Children reaction: Suzanne hiding head injury & Jonathan declaring fearlessness2 Marks
Question 5
Talking About the Text
How does the story suggest that optimism helps to endure “the direst stress”?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The narrative is a testament to the transformative power of optimism when confronted by lethal circumstances:

  • Unshakable Crew Morale: Despite icy seas pouring through shattered wood, Larry Vigil and Herb Seigler pumped cheerfully like madmen, refusing to surrender to despair.
  • The Captain’s Mathematical Grit: Even with broken ribs, a damaged compass, and missing navigational equipment, the narrator continued calculating drift, leeway, and magnetic variation, pursuing a tiny 65-kilometer-wide island in 150,000 square kilometers of ocean.
  • Emotional Anchor of Family: Suzanne’s humorous card and Jonathan’s inspiring fearlessness revitalized the father’s sinking spirits whenever exhaustion threatened to break his resolve.
  • Conclusion: Optimism provided the voyagers with the mental fortitude to convert catastrophic panic into constructive action, ultimately steering them to safety at Ile Amsterdam.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Cheerfulness and sustained pumping by Larry and Herb under icy conditions1 Mark
Father’s continuous navigation calculations despite severe injuries1 Mark
Children’s optimism acting as emotional fuel to overcome dire stress1 Mark
Question 6
Talking About the Text
What lessons can be drawn from the voyagers’ experience for handling crisis and life-threatening situations?
[3]
✅ CBSE Step-by-Step English Core Solution

The harrowing experience of Wavewalker offers timeless lessons in crisis management:

  1. Thorough Preparation: The family spent 16 years honing their seafaring skills and rigorously tested Wavewalker in the roughest British weather before undertaking the voyage. Preparedness is the first line of defense.
  2. Teamwork and Role Division: Everyone performed their assigned role without panic—Mary held the wheel, the crewmen operated pumps, and the captain sealed deck leaks.
  3. Mental Composure under Pressure: In life-or-death crises, panic accelerates disaster. Retaining calm composure allowed the captain to think rationally, find an auxiliary pump, and chart navigation coordinates.
  4. Mutual Empathy and Love: Caring for one another elevates human spirit above physical pain, generating superhuman endurance.
CBSE Marking Scheme Rubric (Session 2026–27)
Pre-voyage preparation and technical mastery of skills1 Mark
Seamless teamwork and calm rational thinking during disaster1 Mark
Mutual empathy, emotional resilience and positive mental outlook1 Mark
