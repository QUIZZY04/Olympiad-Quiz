Chapter 3: The Midnight Visitor

Robert Arthur — Wit, Presence of Mind & Subversion of Stereotypes — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I. Textbook Exercise Solutions & Important Questions — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
How is Ausable different from conventional secret agents depicted in spy thrillers?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Ausable shatters every Hollywood stereotype of a dashing, glamorous secret agent. He is exceptionally fat, sloppy, and clumsy. He speaks French and German with difficulty and retains a heavy American accent brought from Boston twenty years ago. Instead of operating from exotic, high-tech mansions with mysterious femmes fatales, he occupies a gloomy, musty room on the sixth and top floor of a rundown French hotel.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Physical contrast: Exceptionally fat, sloppy, heavy American accent1½ Marks
Setting contrast: Gloomy, cheap room on 6th floor of a rundown French hotel1½ Marks
Q2
Who is Fowler, and why did he feel disillusioned upon meeting Ausable?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Fowler is a young, romantic writer who had envisioned espionage as a world of mysterious figures in the night, pistol shots, and drugs in the wine. He felt profoundly let down and bored when he found Ausable to be a fat, mundane man spending an ordinary evening in a dim music hall discussing reports.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Identity: Young, romantic mystery writer seeking thrills1 Mark
Disillusionment: Ausable's ordinary, fat appearance and dull routine1 Mark
Q3
Who was the midnight intruder inside Ausable's room, and what did he demand?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The intruder was Max, a slender, crafty rival secret agent with the face of a fox. Standing in the center of the room with an automatic pistol pointed directly at Ausable, he demanded the top-secret military report concerning new missiles that Ausable was scheduled to receive that night, boasting that it would be safer in his hands.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Identity: Rival secret agent Max, slender with fox-like cunning1½ Marks
Mission: Stealing confidential missile report at gunpoint1½ Marks
Q4
How did Ausable fabricate the story of the nonexistent balcony to trap Max?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Without showing a trace of panic, Ausable coolly sat down in an armchair and complained bitterly with convincing irritation that this was the second time in a month that someone had entered his room through that 'nuisance of a balcony' belonging to the next apartment. His calm, detailed description convinced Max that a real balcony extended directly beneath his window sill.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Feigned irritation about management failing to block the balcony1½ Marks
Psychological deception: Made Max believe a balcony existed under window1½ Marks
Q5
'Presence of mind and sharp intellect are far deadlier than a loaded gun.' How does Ausable outwit and eliminate Max without firing a single shot?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Psychological Composure Under Gunpoint:
When confronted by an armed assassin, Ausable neither screamed nor pleaded. He assessed Max's psychological makeup—cunning yet jittery—and immediately seized narrative control of the room.

2. The Double Deception:

  • Fabricating the Balcony: He planted the false idea of an external balcony beneath the sixth-floor window.
  • Weaponizing the Knock: When a sudden knock sounded at the door, Max panicked and asked who it was. Ausable smiled calmly and claimed it was the police, whom he had requested for extra protection for the secret missile paper.

3. The Fatal Plunge:
Terrified of the 'police', Max stepped backwards onto the window sill, threatening Ausable with his gun, and dropped blindly into what he believed was a balcony. In reality, there was no balcony; Max fell six floors to his death on the pavement below. The door opened to reveal Henry the waiter with a tray, drinks, and glasses—proving that Ausable's mind had orchestrated the entire fatal trap.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Cool psychological control under gunpoint without physical weapon1½ Marks
Balcony fabrication + claiming waiter's knock was police2 Marks
Climax: Max jumping into the void from 6th floor; waiter enters with drinks1½ Marks
Q6
How did Ausable exploit the sound of knocking on the door to outsmart Max? What was the true identity of the person knocking?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

When sudden, sharp knocking sounded at the hotel room door, Ausable calmly smiled and claimed it was the police, whom he had supposedly requested to provide extra protection for the vital secret missile report. Terrified of arrest, Max panicked and scrambled backward over the windowsill onto what he believed was a balcony, dropping to his death. In reality, the knocker was merely Henry, the hotel waiter, delivering the bottle of wine and two glasses that Ausable had ordered when entering the hotel.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Ausable's bluff: Fabricating story that police arrived to guard missile report1½ Marks
Truth: Henry the waiter bringing drinks ordered earlier1½ Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 22CBSE 2026-27
Narrative Technique
Analysis: How does Robert Arthur use dramatic irony and twist ending to construct a brilliant suspense narrative in 'The Midnight Visitor'?

Arthur sets up expectations through Fowler's romantic notions of espionage, only to immediately dismantle them with Ausable's mundane reality. Suspense peaks when Max holds them at gunpoint, but Arthur reverses power dynamics through Ausable's dialogue. The dramatic irony lies in the fact that neither Max nor Fowler realizes that the 'balcony' and 'police' are figments of Ausable's quick wit until Max plunges to his death and the harmless waiter walks in with drinks.
