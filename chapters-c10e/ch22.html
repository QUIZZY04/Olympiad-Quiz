Q1
How is Ausable different from conventional secret agents depicted in spy thrillers?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Ausable shatters every Hollywood stereotype of a dashing, glamorous secret agent. He is exceptionally fat, sloppy, and clumsy. He speaks French and German with difficulty and retains a heavy American accent brought from Boston twenty years ago. Instead of operating from exotic, high-tech mansions with mysterious femmes fatales, he occupies a gloomy, musty room on the sixth and top floor of a rundown French hotel.

Q5
'Presence of mind and sharp intellect are far deadlier than a loaded gun.' How does Ausable outwit and eliminate Max without firing a single shot?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Psychological Composure Under Gunpoint:
When confronted by an armed assassin, Ausable neither screamed nor pleaded. He assessed Max's psychological makeup—cunning yet jittery—and immediately seized narrative control of the room.

2. The Double Deception:

  • Fabricating the Balcony: He planted the false idea of an external balcony beneath the sixth-floor window.
  • Weaponizing the Knock: When a sudden knock sounded at the door, Max panicked and asked who it was. Ausable smiled calmly and claimed it was the police, whom he had requested for extra protection for the secret missile paper.

3. The Fatal Plunge:
Terrified of the 'police', Max stepped backwards onto the window sill, threatening Ausable with his gun, and dropped blindly into what he believed was a balcony. In reality, there was no balcony; Max fell six floors to his death on the pavement below. The door opened to reveal Henry the waiter with a tray, drinks, and glasses—proving that Ausable's mind had orchestrated the entire fatal trap.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 22CBSE 2026-27
Narrative Technique
Analysis: How does Robert Arthur use dramatic irony and twist ending to construct a brilliant suspense narrative in 'The Midnight Visitor'?

Arthur sets up expectations through Fowler's romantic notions of espionage, only to immediately dismantle them with Ausable's mundane reality. Suspense peaks when Max holds them at gunpoint, but Arthur reverses power dynamics through Ausable's dialogue. The dramatic irony lies in the fact that neither Max nor Fowler realizes that the 'balcony' and 'police' are figments of Ausable's quick wit until Max plunges to his death and the harmless waiter walks in with drinks.
