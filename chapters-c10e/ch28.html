Q1
When is the play set, and what era does the Historian describe in the Museum of Ancient History?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The play is framed in the twenty-fifth century, where the Historian addresses visitors at the Department of Twenty-First Century in the Museum of Ancient History. She explains that the twentieth century was famously known as the 'Era of the Book'—a time when books taught people everything from how to, when to, where to, and why to. The most remarkable book of all was a book of nursery rhymes that saved the Earth from a Martian invasion in 2040.

Q2
Who is Think-Tank? Describe his physical appearance and overbearing personality.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Think-Tank is the Supreme Commander of Mars Space Control. He is a pompous, arrogant, and absurdly vain ruler who has a huge, balloon-shaped egghead and wears a magnificent robe decorated with stars and circles. He demands to be saluted by his subordinates as 'Think-Tank, Great and Mighty, Commander-in-Chief, most powerful and intelligent creature in the whole universe', constantly admiring his reflection in a mirror.

Q3
Where does the Martian probe crew land on Earth, and what do they mistake books for?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The Martian crew—Captain Omega, Lieutenant Iota, and Sergeant Oop—lands inside the Centerville Public Library. Never having seen a book, they are completely baffled by the thousands of books on shelves. When they consult Think-Tank via video communication, Think-Tank makes three hilarious blunders:

  1. First, he identifies books as 'sandwiches' (Earthling dietary staple).
  2. Second, he identifies them as 'communication sandwiches' for listening.
  3. Third, he identifies them as 'eye communication devices' for viewing.

Q4
What nursery rhymes does the Martian crew read from 'Mother Goose'? How does Think-Tank misinterpret them?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

After taking chemical vitamins to decipher the printed codes of 'Mother Goose', the crew reads three rhymes:

  • 'Mistress Mary': Mentions cockle shells and silver bells grown in gardens. Think-Tank panics, believing Earthlings have discovered how to combine agriculture and mining to grow rare metals and explosives.
  • 'Hey Diddle Diddle': The cow jumping over the moon makes him believe Earthlings have trained domesticated animals for interplanetary space warfare.
  • 'Humpty Dumpty': Seeing the picture of the egghead falling off the wall, Think-Tank is convinced Earthlings know him, have drawn his picture, and are plotting to topple his balloon head and conquer Mars!

Q5
How does a simple nursery rhyme book save planet Earth from a Martian invasion? What does the play satirize?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. The Panic and Flight:
Terrified by the picture of Humpty Dumpty, Think-Tank orders Captain Omega and the crew to evacuate Earth immediately without leaving a trace. He orders his space fleet to prepare a capsule for his own escape to the planet Alpha Centauri, a hundred million miles away, to escape the 'terrifying' Earthlings. A humble children's book of nursery rhymes ('Mother Goose') successfully repelled a full-scale extraterrestrial invasion without firing a single weapon.

2. Satire on Arrogance and Half-Knowledge:
Claire Boiko's play is a delightful satire on imperialist arrogance and intellectual vanity. Think-Tank claims to be the most supreme intellect in the cosmos, yet his total ignorance of literature and context leads him to literal misinterpretation. The play demonstrates that literal thinking without contextual wisdom turns power into absurdity.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 28CBSE 2026-27
Science Fiction & Satirical Analysis
Analysis: How does 'The Book That Saved the Earth' pay a creative tribute to the value of human libraries and cultural literacy?

The play celebrates libraries as the ultimate vaults of human civilization. The Martians possessed spaceships, vitamins for intelligence, and advanced communication transmitters, yet they were completely defenseless against human cultural imagination. Books contain poetry, metaphor, nursery humor, and emotional depth that technological algorithms and literalist invaders cannot decode, proving that reading literacy is humanity's greatest superpower.
