Chapter 2: Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom

Nelson Rolihlahla Mandela — Dignity, Courage & Liberation — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I. Textbook Exercise Solutions & Important Questions — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
Where did the inauguration ceremony take place? Why is 10 May described as an 'autumn day' in South Africa?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Location: The historic inauguration ceremonies took place in the Union Buildings amphitheatre in Pretoria, carved out of lovely sandstone, which had been the seat of white supremacy for decades.

2. 'Autumn Day' in South Africa: Because South Africa lies in the Southern Hemisphere, its seasonal cycle is opposite to the Northern Hemisphere, making May an autumn month. Metaphorically, autumn signifies harvest and the shedding of old withered leaves—here symbolizing the harvest of South Africa's hard-won freedom and the shedding of centuries of apartheid oppression.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Location: Union Buildings amphitheatre in Pretoria (sandstone structure)1½ Marks
Southern hemisphere autumn season + metaphorical harvest of freedom1½ Marks
Q2
What does Mandela mean when he mentions 'an extraordinary human disaster' and 'a glorious human achievement'?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. 'Extraordinary Human Disaster': Mandela refers to the brutal practice of Apartheid—a racially discriminatory system wherein black South Africans were disenfranchised, segregated, dehumanized, and subjected to atrocities on their own land for over three centuries.

2. 'Glorious Human Achievement': He refers to the birth of a non-racial, democratic, and egalitarian South African republic where every citizen, regardless of skin color or ethnicity, enjoys equal human dignity, rights, and constitutional liberty.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Disaster: System of Apartheid, racial oppression, segregation1½ Marks
Achievement: Democratic, non-racial government upholding human rights1½ Marks
Q3
Why were two national anthems sung on the day of the inauguration?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Two national anthems were sung to signify the harmonious reconciliation and equality of both races: the whites sang 'Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika' (God Bless Africa) and the blacks sang 'Die Stem' (The Voice of South Africa), the old republican anthem. Singing both honored the cultural identities of both communities, laying the bedrock of racial unity.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Names of anthems: 'Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika' (Whites) and 'Die Stem' (Blacks)1 Mark
Significance: Symbol of racial reconciliation, equality, and unity1 Mark
Q4
How had the attitude of the military generals changed towards Mandela, and why?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The highest military generals and police chiefs, whose chests were decorated with ribbons and medals, saluted Nelson Mandela with solemn allegiance. Mandela recalled with sober irony that not so many years ago, these very generals would not have saluted him but arrested and imprisoned him as a rebel. Their attitude changed because a free, democratic election had legitimately appointed Mandela as the President of South Africa, replacing white supremacy with constitutional democracy.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Contrast: Previously would have arrested him; now saluting in homage1½ Marks
Reason: Democratic transition, Mandela elected constitutional Commander-in-Chief1½ Marks
Q5
What does courage mean to Nelson Mandela? Which does he consider more natural: love or hate?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Meaning of Courage: Mandela learned that courage was not the absence of fear, but the triumph over it. A brave man is not someone who feels no fear, but one who conquers and overcomes that fear.

2. Love vs Hate: Mandela firmly asserts that love comes more naturally to the human heart than its opposite. No one is born hating another person because of skin color, religion, or background; hatred is taught, and if people can learn to hate, they can much more easily be taught to love.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Courage defined: Triumph over fear, not the absence of fear1½ Marks
Love is natural: Born without hate; love touches human heart organically1½ Marks
Q6
What 'twin obligations' does Mandela mention in the chapter?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Mandela explains that every man in a civil society has twin obligations:

  • First Obligation: To his personal family—parents, wife, and children.
  • Second Obligation: To his larger community, his people, and his country.

Under the apartheid regime, a man of color attempting to fulfill his duty to his people was ripped from his family and forced to live an isolated life of rebellion and exile.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
First obligation: Personal duties to parents, wife, and children1½ Marks
Second obligation: Social duties to community, people, and nation1½ Marks
Q7
How did Mandela's 'hunger for freedom' change his life? Why does he state that the oppressor must be liberated just as surely as the oppressed?
6 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Transformation from Boyhood to Freedom Fighter:
As a boy, Mandela believed he was born free—free to run in the fields, swim in clear streams, and roast mealies under the stars. In youth, he realized these were merely illusions of 'transitory freedoms'. His growing hunger for the real, honorable freedom of his people transformed him completely:

  • It turned a frightened young man into a bold revolutionary.
  • It drove a law-abiding attorney to become an outlaw living on the run.
  • It forced a family-loving husband to live like a monk in prison for 27 years.

2. Liberation of Both Oppressor and Oppressed:
Mandela presents a sublime philosophical insight: 'The oppressed and the oppressor alike are robbed of their humanity.' A man who deprives another man of his freedom is himself a prisoner of hatred, locked behind the bars of prejudice and narrow-mindedness. True emancipation requires liberating the oppressed from chains and freeing the oppressor from bigotry.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Evolution of freedom: Boyhood illusions -> adult realization of collective denial2 Marks
Personal cost: Law-abiding lawyer to criminal; family man to solitary prisoner2 Marks
Philosophy of mutual liberation: Oppressor locked in bigotry, robbed of humanity2 Marks
Q8
What does Nelson Mandela say about the natural mineral wealth of South Africa compared to its people?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Mandela proudly acknowledges that South Africa is richly endowed with minerals, gold, and precious gems that lie beneath its soil. However, he emphatically asserts that the country's greatest and truest wealth is its people, whom he describes as finer, truer, and purer than the purest diamonds.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
South Africa's mineral richness: Gold, gems, minerals in soil1 Mark
Comparison: People are the supreme wealth, finer than purest diamonds1 Mark
Q9
How did Mandela's understanding of freedom change with age and experience? Contrast his boyhood illusions of freedom with his adult hunger for freedom.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

As a boy, Mandela thought he was born free to run in the fields, swim in the stream, roast mealies under the stars, and ride bulls, so long as he obeyed his father and customs. As a student, he yearned for transitory personal freedoms—staying out late and reading chosen books. But as a young man in Johannesburg, he realized that his boyhood freedom was an illusion, because the basic freedoms of all black people were brutally curtailed. His personal desire evolved into a transcendent hunger for the collective freedom, dignity, and self-respect of his entire oppressed nation.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Boyhood/student phase: Transitory, self-centered illusions of freedom1½ Marks
Adult realization: Collective hunger for freedom and dignity for all Africans1½ Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 2CBSE 2026-27
Competency-Based Evaluation
Analysis: Mandela observed: 'The depths of oppression create heights of character.' How did the apartheid regime inadvertently produce towering leaders like Oliver Tambo, Walter Sisulu, and Chief Luthuli?

Mandela explains that extreme adversity, systemic cruelty, and ruthless persecution act as a fiery crucible that tempers human willpower. The decades of brutal apartheid oppression demanded extraordinary courage, sacrifice, wisdom, and resilience from resistance leaders, thereby cultivating towering moral giants whose stature might never have been summoned under ordinary conditions. It proved that human spirit shines brightest against the darkest tyranny.
