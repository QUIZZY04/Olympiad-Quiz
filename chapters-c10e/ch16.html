FIRST FLIGHT POETRY

Poem 7: The Trees

Adrienne Rich — Ecological Liberation, Eco-Feminism & Subversion of Domesticity — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

The trees inside are moving out into the forest, the forest that was empty all these days where no bird could sit no insect hide no sun bury its feet in shadow the forest that was empty all these nights will be full of trees by morning. All night the roots work to disengage themselves from the cracks in the veranda floor. The leaves strain toward the glass small twigs stiff with exertion long-cramped boughs shuffling under the roof like newly discharged patients half-dazed, moving to the clinic doors.
— Adrienne Rich
I. Textbook Exercise Solutions & Important Questions — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
Where are the trees in the poem? Why is the forest empty? What will happen by morning?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The trees are unnaturally imprisoned inside the domestic space of human houses—in verandas and living rooms as decorative houseplants. The natural forest outside has been completely empty because human deforestation and commercial exploitation stripped it bare, leaving no trees for birds to perch, insects to hide, or sunlight to cast shadows. By morning, the rebellious trees will break free from human houses and repopulate the forest.

Q2
What physical struggle do the roots, leaves, twigs, and boughs undergo to break free?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The trees wage a fierce, silent midnight rebellion:

  • Roots: Work tirelessly all night to disengage themselves from cracks in the veranda floor.
  • Leaves: Strain urgently towards the window glass seeking light and freedom.
  • Twigs: Grow stiff and taut with desperate exertion.
  • Boughs: Shuffled under the roof like half-dazed, newly discharged hospital patients stumbling toward clinic doors.

Q3
What happens to the house and the moon when the trees break into the open night?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The glass panes shatter as the trees march triumphantly outside into the fresh night winds. The sky opens up, and the moon, which appeared whole in the unobstructed sky, now looks broken into fractured pieces like a shattered mirror, crowned in the canopy of the tallest oak tree.

Q4
How does Adrienne Rich use 'The Trees' as a double allegory for both ecological reclamation and feminist emancipation?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Ecological Dimension:
On a literal level, the poem portrays the unnatural imprisonment of nature by humans who reduce living wilderness to decorative indoor pots while razing natural forests. Nature reclaims its sovereign majesty by breaking domestic glass and returning to the wild.

2. Eco-Feminist Allegory:
As a passionate feminist poet, Adrienne Rich uses 'trees' as an extended metaphor for women who have historically been confined to domestic interiors—verandas, kitchens, and living rooms—repressed by patriarchal norms. The roots uncoupling from the floor represent women shattering societal shackles. The fractured moon crowned in the oak symbolizes female empowerment, reclaiming their rightful space in the public arena.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 16CBSE 2026-27
Poetic Simile & Personification
Analysis: Why does the poet compare the boughs to 'newly discharged patients'? Explain the psychological aptness of this simile.

Newly discharged hospital patients are weak, unsteady on their feet, dazed by bright sunlight after long confinement, yet ecstatic to leave medical captivity. Similarly, the tree branches have been bent and stunted under the low domestic ceilings of human houses. As they emerge into open air, they are half-dazed and disoriented by sudden vastness, yet driven by an unstoppable urge to regain natural health in the forest.
