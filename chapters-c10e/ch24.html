Q1
How did the invisible man first become visible in London? What had he done before that?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. First Becoming Visible: Two young boys in London were astonished to see fresh, muddy footprints appearing out of nowhere on the stone steps of a house. Griffin had stepped in mud, and as he walked, his muddy feet imprinted clear tracks in the snow before drying out and vanishing.

2. Prior Action: Before this, Griffin had set fire to his landlord's house in revenge because the landlord disliked him and had tried to evict him. To escape unseen without arrest, Griffin swallowed his experimental drugs, stripped off all his clothes, and became an invisible, homeless wanderer in the freezing winter.

Q2
What did Griffin do inside the large London department store? What happened the next morning?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

To escape the bitter January cold, Griffin slipped into a large London store before closing. Once inside, he broke open boxes and fitted himself out with warm clothes, an overcoat, and shoes. He fed himself with cold meat and coffee from the restaurant, stole sweets and wine from the grocery, and slept comfortably on a pile of quilts. The next morning, when the store assistants arrived, he panicked, ran, and was forced to strip off his newly acquired clothes one by one to become invisible again, escaping empty-handed into the freezing air.

Q3
What bizarre burglaries and supernatural events took place in the village of Iping?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Griffin arrived at Mrs Hall's inn in Iping wrapped in bandages with dark spectacles, a false nose, and bushy whiskers. Soon, two strange events occurred:

  • Clergyman's Burglary: The clergyman and his wife heard chinking money in their study at dawn. Looking inside with a poker, they found the room completely empty, yet their housekeeping money had vanished from the desk.
  • Haunted Furniture: When Mr. and Mrs. Hall inspected Griffin's empty room, the bedroom chair suddenly sprang to life, charged at Mrs Hall, and pushed them both out of the room, slamming and locking the door behind them.

Q4
Describe the dramatic confrontation between Griffin, Mrs Hall, and Constable Jaffers.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

When Mrs Hall demanded an explanation for his empty-room antics, Griffin lost his temper and shouted: 'You don't understand who or what I am? Very well—I'll show you!' He threw off his whiskers, spectacles, nose, and bandages, revealing a headless body to the horrified crowd. When Constable Jaffers arrived with a warrant to arrest him, Jaffers bravely attempted to arrest a man without a head. Griffin stripped off his shirt, socks, and pants, delivering invisible, savage blows to Jaffers and the villagers until the constable was knocked unconscious.

Q5
'Griffin was a brilliant scientist, but a lawless, antisocial monster.' Discuss how H.G. Wells warns against scientific discovery divorced from human morality.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Extraordinary Scientific Genius:
Griffin was undoubtedly a brilliant physiologist who synthesized a miraculous chemical compound capable of making the human body transparent as a sheet of glass while remaining solid to the touch. This was a monumental scientific triumph.

2. Moral Degradation and Lawlessness:
Instead of utilizing his discovery for the betterment of medical science or mankind, Griffin's antisocial hubris turned him into a petty criminal, arsonist, thief, and murderer:

  • He burned his landlord's property in petty revenge.
  • He stole food, clothes, and theater props in London, viciously attacking a shopkeeper from behind.
  • In Iping, he terrorized an entire village, robbed a priest, assaulted women, and battered a police officer.

Conclusion: Wells demonstrates that intellect without conscience is monstrous; scientific power divorced from empathy and ethics leads inevitably to isolation, paranoia, and self-destruction.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 24CBSE 2026-27
Ethics & Science
Analysis: How does the character of Constable Jaffers serve as a moral foil to Griffin?

Constable Jaffers represents unwavering duty, public service, and lawfulness. Even when confronted with the terrifying, unnatural spectacle of a headless man, Jaffers steadfastly maintains that if the magistrate issued an arrest warrant, the man must be arrested, head or no head. In contrast to Griffin's lawless egoism, Jaffers exhibits selfless bravery in defending his community.
