FIRST FLIGHT POETRY

Poem 8: Fog

Carl Sandburg — Extended Metaphor, Urban Vignette & Haiku-like Minimalism — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

The fog comes on little cat feet. It sits looking over harbour and city on silent haunches and then moves on.
— Carl Sandburg
I. Textbook Exercise Solutions & Important Questions — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
What does Sandburg compare the fog to? Mention three points of similarity between the fog and a cat.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Carl Sandburg compares the fog to a cat. The three striking points of similarity are:

  1. Silent Arrival: Just as a cat prowls stealthily without making a sound on soft padded paws, the fog rolls in quietly over the city unnoticed.
  2. Silent Sitting & Watching: Just as a cat sits curled up on its haunches calmly observing its surroundings, the fog settles over the harbor and city, silently overlooking everything.
  3. Mysterious Departure: Just as a cat quietly gets up and vanishes without warning, the fog lifts and dissipates silently.
CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Comparison identified: Fog compared to a cat1 Mark
Three parallels: Silent arrival on little feet, sitting on haunches, quiet departure2 Marks
Q2
What is the significance of the phrase 'on silent haunches'?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

'Haunches' refers to the hips or thighs of an animal. When a cat sits on its haunches with knees bent, it is motionless, alert, and ready to spring at any second. Similarly, the fog temporarily blankets the harbor and city in complete stillness, pausing briefly before evaporating into the atmosphere.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Literal posture: Sitting on folded hind legs in calm alertness1 Mark
Poetic resonance: Capturing the temporary, poised stillness of the blanketed fog1 Mark
Q3
In just six lines and twenty-one words, Carl Sandburg creates an immortal impressionist masterpiece. Analyze the poetic minimalism of 'Fog'.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Extended Metaphor (Conceit):
The entire poem is built upon a single unbroken metaphor without using 'like' or 'as'. Instead of writing an elaborate description of meteorological moisture, Sandburg personifies the fog as a living, feline creature.

2. Haiku Influence and Free Verse:
Influenced by Japanese Haiku and Imagism, the poem has no strict meter or end-rhyme. The brevity forces the reader to visualize the image intensely: the soft footfalls, the blanketed skyline, the quiet crouch over the docks, and the sudden vanishing.

3. Contrast between Nature and Urban Industrialism:
By enveloping a bustling industrial landscape ('harbour and city' with ships, cranes, and skyscrapers) in the soft, mysterious stillness of a cat, Sandburg demonstrates how nature effortlessly softens human machinery.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Extended metaphor without similes (pure metaphor / personification)2 Marks
Influence of imagism and haiku brevity (zero wasted syllables)1½ Marks
Contrast between quiet natural element and noisy urban industrial city1½ Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 17CBSE 2026-27
Extract & Style Analysis
Analysis: Does the poem contain any rhyme scheme? How does the free-verse form contribute to the poem's atmosphere?

No, the poem is composed in free verse with no set rhyme or uniform meter. The unrhymed, irregular lines flow naturally like drifting mist. The absence of rigid rhyme matches the shapeless, fluid, unpredictable movement of fog creeping over the city.
