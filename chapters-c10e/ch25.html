Q1
How did the children's book 'The Travels of Monarch X' become a turning point in Richard Ebright's life?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

By second grade, Ebright had collected all twenty-five species of butterflies found around his hometown of Reading, Pennsylvania, and was growing bored. His mother bought him 'The Travels of Monarch X' by Dr. Fredrick A. Urquhart of the University of Toronto. The book described the migration of monarch butterflies to Central America and invited readers to tag butterflies for research. This opened the boundless universe of global scientific research to young Ebright, permanently sparking his scientific career.

Q2
What lesson did Ebright learn when he lost at his first County Science Fair in seventh grade?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In seventh grade, Ebright entered the County Science Fair with slides of frog tissues shown under a microscope, but won nothing while others took home awards. He realized that science is not merely about displaying pretty slides; real science requires designing and executing genuine, controlled experiments to test hypotheses. This epiphany ignited his determination to conduct authentic experimental biology.

Q3
What breakthrough discovery did Ebright make regarding the twelve gold spots on a monarch pupa?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Everyone previously assumed that the twelve tiny gold spots on a monarch pupa were purely ornamental decorations. Ebright and a fellow student built an electronic device that proved the spots were not decorative at all; they secreted a vital juvenile hormone necessary for the pupa's full metamorphosis into an adult butterfly. This discovery won him first prize at the County Science Fair and a seat in the International Science Fair.

Q4
How did Ebright formulate his revolutionary theory about how cells read their DNA?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

While examining X-ray photos of the chemical structure of the hormone during his junior year at Harvard, Ebright had an epiphany: the hormone's molecular structure answered a fundamental riddle of biology. He realized that the cell can literally 'read' the blueprint of its DNA. Along with his roommate James R. Wong, he worked all night drawing diagrams, constructing plastic molecular models, and wrote a paper explaining that DNA directs heredity and cellular function, publishing it in a prestigious national science journal.

Q5
What are the three essential ingredients in the making of a true scientist? What role did Ebright's mother and teacher play in his success?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Three Ingredients of a Scientist:
According to the author, the recipe for a true scientist requires: (i) a first-rate mind, (ii) boundless curiosity, and (iii) the will to win for the right reasons—not for vanity or trophies, but to discover truth and excel at one's craft.

2. Role of Mentors:

  • His Mother: Was his constant companion after his father's death. She encouraged his curiosity, took him on trips, bought him telescopes, microscopes, cameras, and the fateful book on Monarchs, keeping him intellectually engaged every evening.
  • Mr. Richard A. Weiherer (Social Studies Teacher): Channeled Ebright's drive into public speaking and model United Nations debates, admiring Ebright's integrity—that he was competitive not for greed, but because he demanded the best from himself in every field.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 25CBSE 2026-27
Competency-Based Evaluation
Analysis: How does Ebright's journey demonstrate that scientific genius is not a genetic accident, but a product of sustained discipline, multi-disciplinary interests, and resilience?

Ebright was not merely an isolated lab biologist; he was a straight-A student, an accomplished debater, an outdoor canoeist, and an expert photographer. His multidisciplinary skills fed into his scientific vision—his ability to debate sharpened his scientific logic, while his photography aided his cellular imaging. When his initial experiments failed, he did not surrender; he refined his methodology. Genius is the cultivation of curiosity through relentless hard work.
