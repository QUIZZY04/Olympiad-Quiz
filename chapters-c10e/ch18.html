Poem 9: The Tale of Custard the Dragon

Ogden Nash — Parody, Mock-Heroic Ballad & True Bravery vs Boasting — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

Belinda lived in a little white house, With a little black kitten and a little grey mouse, And a little yellow dog and a little red wagon, And a realio, trulio, little pet dragon. Now the name of the little black kitten was Ink, And the little grey mouse, she called him Blink, And the little yellow dog was sharp as Mustard, But the dragon was a coward, and she called him Custard. Custard the dragon had big sharp teeth, And spikes on top of him and scales underneath, Mouth like a fireplace, chimney for a nose, And realio, trulio daggers on his toes.
— Ogden Nash

Q5
How is 'The Tale of Custard the Dragon' a mock-heroic ballad that reveals the difference between empty boasting and authentic courage?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Mock-Heroic Ballad Parody:
A ballad traditionally narrates heroic deeds in solemn verse. Ogden Nash parodies this genre by featuring whimsical pets, comic rhyming words ('realio, trulio'), and an inversion of roles: the fierce dragon is a gentle pacifist while the mouse and kitten claim lion-taming courage.

2. Authentic Courage vs Vain Boasting:
When real existential danger strikes, all the loud braggarts flee in panic. The supposedly 'cowardly' Custard instantly risks his life to protect his family without boasting. After the crisis passes, the other pets resume making excuses (Mustard saying he would have been twice as brave if not flustered), and gentle Custard humbly agrees that everyone is braver than him, demonstrating true nobility.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 18CBSE 2026-27
Value-Based Reflection
Analysis: What moral does the poem teach about judging people based on self-promotion versus concrete actions?

The poem illustrates that those who brag the loudest about their bravery and accomplishments are usually the first to collapse when real adversity arrives. True courage is quiet, unassuming, and reveals itself through deeds rather than empty rhetoric. Custard's genuine heroism paired with modesty teaches that humility and readiness to act define real strength.
