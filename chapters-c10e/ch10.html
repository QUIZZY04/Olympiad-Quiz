FIRST FLIGHT POETRY

Poem 1: Dust of Snow

Robert Frost — Nature, Mood Transformation & Healing — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

The way a crow Shook down on me The dust of snow From a hemlock tree Has given my heart A change of mood And saved some part Of a day I had rued.
— Robert Frost
I. Textbook Exercise Solutions & Important Questions — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
What is a 'dust of snow'? What does the poet say has changed his mood?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

A 'dust of snow' refers to the fine, powdery particles or flakes of snow. The sudden, gentle shower of these snow particles shaken down upon the poet by a crow perched on a hemlock tree changed the poet's gloomy, depressed mood into one of sudden joy and freshness.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Definition: Fine powdery snowflakes or particles1 Mark
Cause of change: Shower of snow shaken down by crow from hemlock branch1 Mark
Q2
How does Frost present nature in this poem? What is unusual about his choice of a crow and a hemlock tree?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Unlike traditional Romantic poets who celebrate melodious birds like nightingales or stately trees like oaks and maples, Frost intentionally selects two negative, ill-omened symbols: an ugly, harsh-voiced crow and a poisonous hemlock tree with white flowers. By using these dark omens to bring about an uplifting, joyful mental rejuvenation, Frost subverts poetic conventions and shows that nature in all its forms has the power to heal.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Unusual symbols: Ill-omened crow and poisonous hemlock tree1½ Marks
Inversion of convention: Dark symbols become agents of joyful healing1½ Marks
Q3
What does the poet mean by 'saved some part of a day I had rued'?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The word 'rued' means held in regret or sorrow. The poet had spent the earlier part of the day in a somber, despondent frame of mind, feeling that the day was ruined. The unexpected touch of snow broke his depressive reverie, allowing him to appreciate the remaining day with gratitude, thus saving it from being wasted.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Meaning of 'rued': Regretted, spent in melancholy or sorrow1 Mark
Saved part: Reclaimed the rest of the day with positivity and cheer1 Mark
Q4
How does a seemingly trivial moment in nature have a profound therapeutic impact on the human mind? Analyze the poetic craft of 'Dust of Snow'.
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Therapeutic Power of Nature:
In 'Dust of Snow', Robert Frost illustrates that human beings often trap themselves in self-created melancholia, sorrow, and trivial anxieties. Nature, through an insignificant micro-event—a crow shaking loose a dusting of snow—startles the poet out of his dark rumination. The cold, crisp sensation reconnects him with the physical universe, shattering his depressive paralysis.

2. Poetic Craft & Structure:

  • Conciseness: Just two stanzas, eight short lines, and twenty-four words capture an entire psychological transformation.
  • Rhyme Scheme: Strict abab cdcd rhyme scheme providing musical harmony.
  • Enjambment: A single continuous sentence running without end-line punctuation until the final full stop, mimicking the swift, uninterrupted flow of emotional renewal.
CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Therapeutic impact: Breaking depressive paralysis through natural touch2 Marks
Subversion of dark omens (crow, hemlock) into agents of light1½ Marks
Poetic devices: Rhyme scheme abab cdcd, enjambment, brevity1½ Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 10CBSE 2026-27
Extract Analysis
Analysis: Read the line: 'Has given my heart / A change of mood'. What figure of speech is used in 'Has given my heart', and what does it reveal about the seat of emotions?

The figure of speech is Synecdoche (where a part represents the whole—the 'heart' represents the poet's entire emotional and mental psyche), along with Alliteration in 'Has / Heart'. It reveals that the transformation was not merely superficial or intellectual, but reached the deepest emotional core of his being.
