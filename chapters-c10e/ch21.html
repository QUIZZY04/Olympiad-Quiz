Q1
Who is the narrator (Hari Singh)? How does he win Anil's trust and enter his home?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Hari Singh is a seasoned fifteen-year-old thief and confidence trickster who changes his name every month to evade the police and his previous employers. He approaches twenty-five-year-old Anil at a wrestling match, flattering him with easy compliments. When Anil admits he has no money to hire him, Hari lies that he knows how to cook. Though his first meal is so atrocious that Anil gives it to stray dogs, Hari's disarming, appealing smile wins Anil over, and Anil agrees to teach him.

Q2
What does Anil do for a living? How does he treat Hari Singh?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Anil is a struggling freelance writer who earns by fits and starts, writing articles for magazines. When he gets a cheque, he celebrates lavishly with friends, and when broke, he borrows and worries until the next payment. He treats Hari with exceptional kindness and patience: he teaches him to cook, write his name, write complete sentences, and add numbers, trusting him with the door key and grocery purchases.

Q3
How does Hari Singh steal Anil's money? Why did he miss the 10:30 Express to Lucknow?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. The Theft: Anil brought home a bundle of six hundred rupees in fifty-rupee notes from selling a book to a publisher and tucked it under his mattress. At midnight, Hari crept silently into the room, slid his hand under the mattress, and slipped the money out without waking Anil.

2. Missing the Train: Hari reached the railway station just as the 10:30 Express to Lucknow was chugging out. He could easily have jumped into one of the carriages, but an unexplainable hesitation and a stirring conscience held him back on the platform until the train vanished into the night.

Q4
Why does Hari Singh decide to return to Anil and put the stolen money back?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Sitting drenched in the rain in the maidan, Hari analyzed the reactions of different men upon being robbed: the greedy man shows fear, the rich man shows anger, and the poor man shows acceptance. But Anil, he knew, would show only a touch of sadness—not for the loss of money, but for the loss of trust. Furthermore, Hari realized that stealing might give him six hundred rupees to live like an oil-rich Arab for a week, but learning to read and write sentences would make him a truly respected, clever, and great man. Driven by this desire for education and self-respect, he crept back and slipped the damp notes under Anil's mattress.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Anil's sadness would be for betrayal of trust, not loss of cash1½ Marks
Realization that literacy and education offer true greatness, not theft1½ Marks
Q5
'A thief can be reformed not by punishment or prison, but by unconditional trust, forgiveness, and education.' Elucidate with reference to Anil and Hari Singh.
6 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Limitations of Punitive Law:
Standard judicial punishment rarely reforms criminals; prison often hardens resentment and refines criminal methods. Hari Singh had spent years cheating masters and dodging police, entirely unbothered by laws or reprimands.

2. Anil's Method of Reformation:
Anil's silent benevolence worked where police batons had failed:

  • He offered absolute trust, giving a street boy the key to his house.
  • He offered education—the supreme tool of social transformation. By teaching Hari to write sentences, Anil opened the door to legitimate professional dignity.
  • The ultimate test arrived the morning after the theft. When Anil handed Hari a damp fifty-rupee note, promising to pay him regularly and announcing: 'Today we'll start writing sentences', Hari realized Anil knew about the nocturnal robbery and the rain-soaked cash. Yet, Anil spoke not a single word of blame, nor did he report him to the police.

Anil's silent forgiveness and steadfast commitment to Hari's education permanently conquered the criminal in Hari, proving that unconditional empathy is the most potent moral catalyst for human redemption.

Character & Ethical Analysis
Analysis: Why did Anil decide not to hand Hari Singh over to the police even after discovering the wet notes?

Anil possessed profound human wisdom. He understood that handing Hari to the police would brand him as a habitual criminal and push him deeper into the underworld. Anil recognized that Hari had overcome his own thieving instinct and walked back through the rainy night solely out of loyalty and a yearning for education. Anil rewarded this moral triumph with silent grace, choosing to save a soul rather than penalize a lapse.
