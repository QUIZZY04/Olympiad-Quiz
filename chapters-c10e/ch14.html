FIRST FLIGHT POETRY

Poem 5: The Ball Poem

John Berryman — Epistemology of Loss, Maturity & Material Transience — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

What is the boy now, who has lost his ball, What, what is he to do? I saw it go Merrily bouncing, down the street, and then Merrily over — there it is in the water! No use to say ‘O there are other balls’: An ultimate shaking grief fixes the boy As he stands rigid, trembling, staring down All his young days into the harbour where His ball went. I would not intrude on him; A dime, another ball, is worthless. Now He senses first responsibility In a world of possessions. People will take Balls, balls will be lost always, little boy. And no one buys a ball back. Money is external. He is learning, well behind his desperate eyes, The epistemology of loss, how to stand up Knowing what every man must one day know And most know many days, how to stand up.
— John Berryman
I. Textbook Exercise Solutions & Important Questions — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
Why does the poet say: 'I would not intrude on him; a dime, another ball, is worthless'?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The poet refuses to intervene or buy the boy a replacement ball because the lost ball is irreplaceable in its emotional value. The ball was not merely a ten-cent rubber toy; it was steeped in personal memories of the boy's childhood years. Offering him money or another ball would trivialized his genuine grief and rob him of an essential rite of passage—learning how to confront personal loss independently.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Emotional value: Ball symbolized memories of his young childhood days1½ Marks
Pedagogical restraint: Intervening with money robs him of learning resilience1½ Marks
Q2
What does the poet mean by 'in a world of possessions'? What is 'first responsibility'?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

A 'world of possessions' refers to a materialistic society where people covet, accumulate, and identify with physical property. In such a world, the boy senses his 'first responsibility'—the sober realization that one must protect what one cherishes, and that material things can be permanently lost or destroyed without replacement.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
World of possessions: Materialistic society focused on ownership1 Mark
First responsibility: Owning consequences and accepting transience1 Mark
Q3
What does 'epistemology of loss' mean in the poem?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

'Epistemology' is the philosophical study of the nature of knowledge. The 'epistemology of loss' means understanding the fundamental reality, nature, and truth of loss—learning what it truly feels like to lose something precious, and acquiring the mental strength to survive grief and stand up again.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Definition: Understanding the philosophical nature and truth of loss1 Mark
Application: Developing emotional resilience to stand up after heartbreak1 Mark
Q4
'Money is external. And no one buys a ball back.' How does John Berryman elevate the loss of a simple ball to a universal meditation on the human condition?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Symbolism of the Ball:
The bouncing ball is a poignant metaphor for childhood innocence, pure joy, and loved ones. When the ball rolls into the dark harbor water, the boy sees 'all his young days' swallowed up by the abyss, marking the end of carefree innocence.

2. Limitations of Material Wealth:
By asserting that 'Money is external', Berryman emphasizes that currency can only purchase physical, commodified substitutes. Money cannot repurchase lost time, departed loved ones, shattered relationships, or lost innocence. 'No one buys a ball back' means that certain life experiences are absolute and irreversible.

3. Universal Human Maturity:
Every human being must inevitably face bereavement, disillusionment, and loss. True adulthood begins when one learns to stand upright in the wake of tragedy, accepting loss not as an anomaly, but as the foundational law of human existence.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Ball as metaphor for childhood innocence and irreplaceable attachments2 Marks
Critique of wealth: Money is external and cannot buy back lost moments1½ Marks
Universal philosophy: Maturing by learning 'how to stand up' after loss1½ Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 14CBSE 2026-27
Psychological Insight
Analysis: How does Berryman's stance of non-interference align with modern emotional counseling for children experiencing disappointment?

Modern developmental psychology stresses that overprotecting children from grief or instantly replacing lost belongings fosters emotional fragility and entitlement. By letting the child stand rigid, tremble, and process his own grief, the poet respects the boy's psychological dignity. Experiencing and processing sadness naturally builds coping mechanisms, enabling the child to develop authentic emotional resilience.
