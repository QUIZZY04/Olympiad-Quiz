✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Q8
Who does Lencho think has taken the rest of the money? What is the supreme irony of the ending in 'A Letter to God'?
6 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Lencho's Suspicion:
Lencho firmly believed that the post office employees had embezzled the missing thirty pesos. In his second letter to God, he pleaded: 'Send me the rest, since I need it very much. But don't send it to me through the mail, because the post office employees are a bunch of crooks.'

2. The Supreme Situational Irony:
Irony is a literary device where the actual outcome is humorously or tragically opposite to what is expected. In this story:

  • The postmaster and postal staff had exhibited extraordinary selflessness, empathy, and sacrifice by parting with their own hard-earned money to preserve a stranger's spiritual faith.
  • Instead of receiving gratitude or praise, these very benefactors were branded as a 'bunch of crooks' and thieves by the recipient.
  • Lencho's blind, naive faith in God made him suspect human goodness, creating a poignant and humorous paradox where noble charity was rewarded with bitter condemnation.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 1CBSE 2026-27
Case Study / Thematic Analysis
Analysis: Lencho's faith in God was unquestionable, but his faith in humanity was non-existent. How does this story highlight the conflict between faith and practical human understanding?

Lencho possesses a childlike, dogmatic faith in the metaphysical supreme being, believing God actively reads posted mail and sends currency notes. However, he is completely cynical about his fellow humans, immediately assuming the postal workers are dishonest thieves. The story illustrates that while faith gives people the mental fortitude to face disasters, faith without practical discernment can turn into blind delusion and ingratitude. True godliness was actually manifested in the flesh-and-blood postmaster's quiet generosity, which Lencho failed to recognize.

Extract-Based Question
Analysis: Read the extract and answer: 'What faith! I wish I had the faith of the man who wrote this letter. Starting up a correspondence with God!' (a) Who spoke these words? (b) What action did the speaker take to uphold this faith?

(a) The postmaster spoke these words to himself upon reading Lencho's first letter addressed to God.
(b) To preserve the writer's remarkable faith, the postmaster decided to answer the letter and mobilized his staff, friends, and personal funds to collect 70 pesos to send to Lencho.
