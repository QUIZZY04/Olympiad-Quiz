FIRST FLIGHT POETRY

Poem 3: A Tiger in the Zoo

Leslie Norris — Freedom vs Captivity & The Plight of Caged Wildlife — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

He stalks in his vivid stripes The few steps of his cage, On pads of velvet quiet, In his quiet rage. He should be lurking in shadow, Sliding through long grass Near the water hole Where plump deer pass. He should be snarling around houses At the jungle’s edge, Baring his white fangs, his claws, Terrorising the village! But he’s locked in a concrete cell, His strength behind bars, Stalking the length of his cage, Ignoring visitors. He hears the last voice at night, The patrolling cars, And stares with his brilliant eyes At the brilliant stars.
— Leslie Norris
I. Textbook Exercise Solutions & Important Questions — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
How does the poet contrast the tiger in the zoo with the tiger in its natural habitat?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. In the Zoo: The tiger is trapped inside a cramped concrete cell. His magnificent strength is locked behind steel bars. He paces the few steps of his cage on velvet pads, helpless, sullen, and ignoring human visitors in silent rage.

2. In the Wild Jungle: He should be lurking in the shadows of tall trees, sliding silently through long grass near the water hole to ambush plump deer, and snarling at the forest edge, terrifying villagers with his glistening white fangs and sharp claws.

Q2
What does the poet mean by 'quiet rage'? What literary device is used here?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The phrase 'quiet rage' is an Oxymoron (combining contradictory words). The tiger is seething with immense, ferocious fury at having his majestic freedom stripped away, yet his rage must remain 'quiet' and suppressed because he is entirely powerless against the concrete walls and iron bars of his cage.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Literary device: Oxymoron (quiet + rage)1 Mark
Meaning: Suppressed, impotent fury due to helplessness behind iron bars1 Mark
Q3
What does the tiger do at night in the zoo? What do his brilliant eyes look at?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

At night, when human visitors depart, the tiger listens to the distant, mechanical sounds of the patrolling guard cars. He stares longing into the night sky with his brilliant, luminous eyes at the brilliant, sparkling stars, silently yearning for the boundless freedom of the cosmic universe.

Q4
How does 'A Tiger in the Zoo' present a powerful ethical critique of human entertainment at the cost of animal subjugation?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Deprivation of Sovereign Dignity:
Leslie Norris strips away the romantic glamour of zoos to expose the cruel tragedy of wildlife incarceration. The tiger, nature's apex predator and lord of the forest, is reduced to a pathetic museum artifact for the casual entertainment of uncaring tourists.

2. Emotional and Psychological Torment:
The poem underscores the psychological devastation of captivity: pacing repetitive small steps, suppressing primal hunting instincts, and enduring mechanical noises instead of nocturnal forest sounds.

3. Poetic Juxtaposition:
The repetition of 'brilliant'—his brilliant eyes gazing at the brilliant stars—creates a poignant climax. Both eyes and stars share cosmic magnificence, yet the stars are eternally free in the boundless sky while the tiger remains chained in an artificial cage, pleading for animal rights.

Competency-Based Evaluation
Analysis: Why does the tiger ignore the visitors who come to see him at the zoo?

The tiger ignores visitors because he holds them in silent, aristocratic contempt. He recognizes that they are merely curious voyeurs who gawk at his confinement without offering empathy or freedom. Aware of his current impotence behind iron bars, he refuses to acknowledge them, maintaining his quiet pride by turning his back on their gaze.
