Chapter 7: The Necklace

Guy de Maupassant — Vanity, Borrowed Glory & The Bitter Price of Pride — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I. Textbook Exercise Solutions & Important Questions — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
Why was Matilda Loisel perpetually unhappy and discontented with her life?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Born into a family of clerks with no dowry or social prospects, Matilda felt born for all the delicacies and luxuries of aristocratic life. Instead, she married an ordinary clerk in the Ministry of Public Instruction. She suffered incessantly from the shabby walls, worn chairs, and modest food of her home, tormented by vivid daydreams of silent antechambers hung with Oriental tapestries, bronze torchieres, exquisite banquets on gleaming silver, and whispered flirtations in aristocratic salons.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Discontented because she believed she was born for aristocratic luxury1½ Marks
Tormented by modest apartment and daydreams of wealth and high society1½ Marks
Q2
How did Matilda react to the invitation to the Minister's ball? How did her husband arrange her dress and jewelry?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Her Spiteful Reaction: Instead of being delighted, Matilda threw the invitation spitefully across the table, weeping that she had nothing appropriate to wear to such an elite gathering.

2. Arrangement: Her devoted husband sacrificed 400 francs he had saved to purchase a hunting gun to buy her a gown. To satisfy her craving for jewelry, she visited her wealthy school friend, Madame Forestier, who lent her a dazzling diamond necklace in a black satin box.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Angry and weeping because she lacked formal gown and jewels1½ Marks
Husband gave 400 francs (hunting savings); borrowed diamond necklace from friend1½ Marks
Q3
How was Matilda's experience at the ball? When and how was the necklace lost?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Triumph at Ball: Matilda was an absolute sensation—the prettiest woman in the ballroom, elegant, gracious, smiling, and intoxicated with delight. All the cabinet attaches and the Minister noticed her, dancing until 4 a.m.

2. The Loss: Returning home, she rushed to the mirror to admire herself in her final glory and uttered a shriek of horror: the diamond necklace was missing from her neck. It had slipped off unnoticed either in the carriage or on the streets.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Ball triumph: Prettiest woman, intoxicated with admiration and joy1½ Marks
Loss: Discovered missing before mirror at home; lost in carriage/street1½ Marks
Q4
How did the Loisels replace the lost necklace? What financial ruin did they plunge into?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Unable to find the necklace, they located an identical diamond necklace at the Palais-Royal priced at 36,000 francs. Loisel used his 18,000 francs inheritance from his father and borrowed the remaining 18,000 francs from usurers and loan sharks at ruinous interest rates. To repay this crushing debt, they dismissed their maid, rented a cheap garret, and spent ten brutal years in grinding poverty.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Replacement cost: 36,000 francs (used 18,000 inheritance + 18,000 usurious debt)1½ Marks
Consequences: Dismissed maid, moved to attic, 10 years of grueling labor1½ Marks
Q5
How did ten years of grueling poverty transform Matilda physically and emotionally?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The ten years of harsh physical labor aged Matilda terribly. She became the crude, loud, muscular woman of an impoverished working-class household, with untidy hair, red coarse hands, and skirt askew, washing greasy pots and scrubbing floors. Yet she bore her burden with heroic resignation, having paid off her debt with her own sweat and dignity.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Physical transformation: Aged, coarse red hands, loud voice, untidy skirts1½ Marks
Moral shift: Hard working-class stoicism; paid debts with heroic labor1½ Marks
Q6
What shocking truth does Madame Forestier reveal at the end of the story? What is the supreme irony of Matilda Loisel's life?
6 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. The Shocking Climax:
Meeting Madame Forestier on the Champs-Elysees ten years later, Matilda proudly reveals that her lost beauty was due to replacing the lost diamond necklace at a cost of 36,000 francs. Deeply touched, Madame Forestier clasps Matilda's worn hands and exclaims with pity: 'Oh! My poor Matilda! Mine were false. They were not worth over five hundred francs!'

2. Supreme Irony of Fate:
The revelation exposes the devastating, cruel irony of Matilda's entire adult life:

  • Matilda lived a borrowed fantasy of wealth for just one night, only to ruin thirty years of her life in genuine, crushing poverty.
  • She threw away thirty-six thousand francs, her youth, beauty, and domestic happiness to replace a worthless piece of paste imitation jewelry worth a mere five hundred francs.
  • Her petty vanity and fear of confessing the loss to her friend turned a minor embarrassing incident into lifelong slavery, demonstrating how a single moment of false pride can destroy an entire life.
CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Revelation: Original necklace was imitation glass/paste worth 500 francs2 Marks
Cruel situational irony: 10 years of brutal sacrifice wasted on worthless paste2 Marks
Moral critique of vanity, lack of honesty, and false bourgeois pride2 Marks
Q7
What desperate efforts did Monsieur Loisel make on the night of the ball and during the subsequent week to search for and recover the lost necklace?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Though exhausted and due at his office at ten, Monsieur Loisel immediately redressed and retraced their steps on foot in the freezing dawn, walking all the streets where they had strolled. He went to police headquarters, cab company offices to trace the cab number, and advertised a reward in the morning newspapers. After a week of fruitless searching that brought no clue, Loisel was forced to concede defeat and accept that the necklace was irrevocably lost, setting off ten years of debt.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Immediate night search: Retracing steps on foot in freezing cold1½ Marks
Institutional search: Police, cab companies, newspaper reward notice1½ Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 26CBSE 2026-27
Value-Based Application
Analysis: How would the tragedy of Matilda's life have been averted if she had practiced honesty and frankness with Madame Forestier immediately after the ball?

Had Matilda gathered moral courage to tell Madame Forestier the truth immediately, the friend would have instantly revealed that the necklace was a cheap five-hundred-franc imitation. The Loisels could have easily compensated her with a few days' salary without borrowing a single franc. False pride and the paralyzing fear of admitting loss ruined their lives, proving that honesty is not just a moral virtue, but a practical safeguard against catastrophe.
