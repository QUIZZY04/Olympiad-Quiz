FIRST FLIGHT POETRY

Poem 2: Fire and Ice

Robert Frost — Human Passions, Destructive Vices & Apocalyptic Warning — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

Some say the world will end in fire, Some say in ice. From what I've tasted of desire I hold with those who favor fire. But if it had to perish twice, I think I know enough of hate To say that for destruction ice Is also great And would suffice.
— Robert Frost
I. Textbook Exercise Solutions & Important Questions — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
What do 'fire' and 'ice' symbolize in the poem? How do they cause the end of the world?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. 'Fire' Symbolism: Fire represents unbridled human passions—fiery desire, greed, lust, anger, ambition, and avarice. Like wild fire, unchecked desires spread rapidly, consuming relationships, societies, and resources until the world burns itself out.

2. 'Ice' Symbolism: Ice represents cold hatred, indifference, bigotry, rigidity, and cruelty. Unlike the fury of fire, ice destroys quietly by numbing human empathy, freezing warmth and brotherhood until humanity perishes in cold isolation.

Q2
Why does the poet say he holds with those who favor fire, yet acknowledges ice would also suffice?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The poet aligns himself initially with fire based on his personal life experiences with human desires and burning passions. However, reflecting on human nature, he observes that cold hatred and quiet malice are equally widespread and destructive, concluding that ice is equally potent and sufficient to end civilization.

The rhyme scheme of the nine-line poem is aba abc bcb. This compact, interlocking rhyme scheme knits the contrasting concepts of fire and ice together, creating a rhythmic inevitability that mirrors the apocalyptic certainty of the world's destruction.

Q4
How does Robert Frost use cosmological theories to deliver a biting moral critique of human civilization?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Cosmological vs Psychological Destruction:
Scientific debates during Frost's era centered on whether the universe would perish through heat death (scorching solar incineration) or cold death (freezing ice age). Frost brilliantly transposes these cosmological theories into psychological metaphors for human moral failings.

2. Warning Against Self-Destruction:
Frost warns that humanity does not require an asteroid or cosmic catastrophe to destroy itself; our own untamed vices are more than adequate. Fiery geopolitical greed causes devastating wars, while cold communal bigotry and indifference rot society from within. The understated, chilling conclusion ('would suffice') serves as a profound wake-up call to master our passions.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 11CBSE 2026-27
Value-Based Application
Analysis: In today's world of climate change and geopolitical conflicts, how is Robert Frost's 'Fire and Ice' exceptionally prophetic?

Frost's poem speaks directly to contemporary crises: environmental destruction and global warming mirror the burning 'fire' of human consumerist greed, while wars, xenophobia, and indifference to suffering mirror the 'ice' of emotional numbness. If humanity fails to temper fiery desires with sustainability and icy prejudices with empathy, our civilization risks fulfilling Frost's apocalyptic prophecy.
