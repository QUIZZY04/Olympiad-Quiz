FIRST FLIGHT POETRY

Poem 6: Amanda!

Robin Klein — Adolescent Autonomy, Parental Nagging & Escapist Reverie — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

Don’t bite your nails, Amanda! Don’t hunch your shoulders, Amanda! Stop that slouching and sit up straight, Amanda! (There is a languid, emerald sea, where the sole inhabitant is me— a mermaid, drifting blissfully.) Did you finish your homework, Amanda? Did you tidy your room, Amanda? I thought I told you to clean your shoes, Amanda! (I am an orphan, roaming the street. I pattern soft dust with my hushed, bare feet. The silence is golden, the freedom is sweet.)
— Robin Klein
I. Textbook Exercise Solutions & Important Questions — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
How old do you think Amanda is? What nagging instructions are constantly hurled at her?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Amanda is a young adolescent schoolgirl, likely around ten to twelve years old, as indicated by her homework, acne breakouts, and untidy room. Her parent perpetually pesters her with negative behavioral commands: not to bite her nails, not to hunch her shoulders, to sit up straight, to finish her homework, to clean her room and shoes, not to eat chocolates, and not to sulk or look moody.

Q2
Why are stanzas 2, 4, and 6 placed in parentheses? Who speaks in them?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The parenthetical stanzas represent Amanda's internal mental reveries and subconscious escapism. While the odd stanzas represent the harsh, authoritative external voice of her nagging parent, the bracketed stanzas give voice to Amanda's silent soul retreating into fantasy worlds. The parentheses physically shelter her thoughts from the loud intrusions of the adult world.

Q3
What three personas does Amanda fantasize about adopting? What does each fantasy reveal?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Mermaid: In a languid, emerald sea, drifting blissfully as the sole inhabitant, revealing her craving for peace, beauty, and solitude.

2. Orphan: Roaming barefoot on silent streets making patterns in the dust, where 'silence is golden, freedom is sweet', revealing that she would rather be parentless than endure constant scolding.

3. Rapunzel: Living in a high, secluded tower with tranquil, rare life, though she resolves never to let down her bright hair so that no rescuer can ever disturb her solitude.

Q4
How does 'Amanda!' capture the universal tug-of-war between well-intentioned parental conditioning and an adolescent's yearning for space?
5 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Smothering Parental Hovering:
Amanda's parent is not abusive, but suffers from obsessive micro-management. In attempting to mold Amanda into an impeccable, disciplined lady fit for polite society, the parent bombards her with relentless criticism, destroying her self-esteem and natural joy.

2. Defense Mechanism of Reverie:
Unable to argue openly, Amanda builds psychological fortresses through imaginative withdrawal. Her daydream of being an orphan is particularly poignant and shocking—it demonstrates that excessive parental badgering can make a child feel that orphanhood is preferable to life under authoritarian surveillance.

3. Final Irony:
The poem ends with the parent reprimanding her: 'Stop that sulking at once, Amanda!... Anyone would think that I nagged at you, Amanda!' The parent is completely blind to her own incessant nagging, worrying only about public perception.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 15CBSE 2026-27
Parent-Child Communication
Analysis: How can parents balance instilling good manners and hygiene without driving their children into complete emotional detachment like Amanda?

Effective parenting requires empathy, active listening, and respecting personal boundaries rather than issuing endless negative imperatives ('Don't do this, don't do that'). If behavioral guidance is offered through dialogue, positive reinforcement, and collaborative discussions rather than public scolding and micro-management, children feel valued rather than smothered, eliminating the psychological urge to escape into isolated fantasies.
