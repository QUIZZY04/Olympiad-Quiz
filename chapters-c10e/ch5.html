Chapter 5: Glimpses of India

I. A Baker from Goa | II. Coorg | III. Tea from Assam — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

I. Textbook Exercise Solutions & Important Questions — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27
Q1
Part I: What are the elders in Goa nostalgic about? What was the traditional baker called, and what did he wear?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Nostalgia: The Goan elders fondly reminisce about the good old Portuguese days and their famous, crusty loaves of bread. While the original Portuguese bakers may have vanished, the mixers, molders, and traditional fiery furnaces still endure.

2. The Pader & Kabai: The village baker was known as the 'pader'. In the old days, bakers wore a peculiar single-piece long dress reaching down to their knees called the 'kabai'. Later in the author's childhood, they wore shirts and trousers shorter than full-length and longer than half-pants.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Nostalgia: Portuguese loaves of bread and traditional wood furnaces1½ Marks
Baker name: 'Pader'; traditional knee-length attire: 'Kabai'1½ Marks
Q2
How were traditional Goan marriages, engagements, and festivals incomplete without bread?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Bread was indispensable to every social occasion in Goan village life:

  • Marriage Gifts: Marriage gifts were meaningless without the sweet bread known as 'bol'.
  • Engagements: A daughter's engagement required the mother to prepare special sandwiches.
  • Festivals & Christmas: Cakes and 'bolinhas' were an absolute must for Christmas and all major church festivals. A village without a baker's furnace was simply unthinkable.
CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Sweet bread 'bol' essential for marriage gifts1 Mark
Sandwiches for daughter's engagement1 Mark
Cakes and 'bolinhas' mandatory for Christmas & church celebrations1 Mark
Q3
Part II: Where is Coorg situated? What is the story regarding the Greek or Arabic descent of the Kodavus?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Location: Coorg (Kodagu), Karnataka's smallest district, is nestled midway between coastal Mangalore and Mysore—a piece of heaven of evergreen rainforests, spices, and coffee plantations.

2. Origin Theories:

  • Greek Theory: A part of Alexander the Great's army moved south along the coast and settled there when return became impractical, marrying local women.
  • Arabic Theory: Kodavus wear a traditional long black coat with an embroidered waist-belt known as kuppia, which closely resembles the kuffia worn by Arabs and Kurds.
CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Location: Midway between Mysore and Mangalore (Karnataka)1 Mark
Greek descent: Remnants of Alexander's army settled and married locals1 Mark
Arabic descent: Traditional coat 'kuppia' resembles Arab 'kuffia'1 Mark
Q4
What is the Coorg Regiment famous for? Who was General Cariappa?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The Coorg Regiment is one of the most decorated and valiant regiments in the Indian Army. The very first Chief of the Indian Army, General Cariappa, was a proud Coorgi (Kodavu). Furthermore, Kodavus are the only community in India permitted to carry firearms without a license, testifying to their legendary bravery and martial heritage.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Coorg Regiment: One of the most decorated regiments in the Indian Army1 Mark
General Cariappa: First Chief of the Indian Army was a Coorgi1 Mark
Unique privilege: Permitted to carry firearms without license1 Mark
Q5
Part III: What are the two legends regarding the discovery of tea described by Rajvir?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Chinese Legend: An ancient Chinese emperor always boiled water before drinking it. One day, a few twigs of the burning firewood fell into the boiling water, imparting a delightful, invigorating flavor. They were discovered to be tea leaves.

2. Indian Buddhist Legend: Bodhidharma, an ancient Buddhist ascetic, cut off his eyelids because he felt drowsy during meditation. Ten tea plants sprouted out of his eyelids. The leaves of these plants, when put into hot water and drunk, banished sleep.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Chinese legend: Twigs accidentally dropped into emperor's boiling water1½ Marks
Indian Buddhist legend: Bodhidharma's cut eyelids sprouted tea plants1½ Marks
Q6
How does 'Glimpses of India' celebrate the distinct cultural flavors, traditions, and geography of Goa, Coorg, and Assam?
6 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The three travelogues in 'Glimpses of India' paint a rich, vibrant mosaic of India's cultural and natural heritage:

  • Goa (Cultural Continuity): Highlights the lingering Portuguese influence through the village baker ('pader'). Baking is not merely a commercial trade but a social institution; sweet bread ('bol') and bolinhas are bound to culinary rituals, demonstrating how colonial history blends harmoniously into coastal domestic life.
  • Coorg (Martial Honor & Wilderness): Explores the rugged hills of Kodagu, teeming with coffee aromas, wild elephants, and Mahseer fish in the Kaveri. It celebrates the Kodavus' fierce martial pride, distinct Greek/Arabic ancestry, hospitable traditions, and exceptional bravery.
  • Assam (Industrial & Natural Opulence): Showcases the endless sea of tea bushes across the Brahmaputra valley. It blends historical myths (Bodhidharma and Chinese emperors) with modern botanical knowledge of tea plucking and flushes.

Together, the chapter fosters deep pride in the kaleidoscope of India's unity in diversity.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Goa: Portuguese baking tradition, pader, social rituals, bol2 Marks
Coorg: Martial pride, Kodavus, lush geography, General Cariappa2 Marks
Assam: Tea gardens, legends, industrial and economic prominence2 Marks
Q7
Part I (A Baker from Goa): What was the peculiar traditional dress worn by Goan bakers called 'kabai', and how had it changed by the author's childhood?
2 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In traditional Portuguese times, the baker or bread-seller wore a peculiar single-piece long frock reaching down to the knees, known as the kabai. By the author's childhood, this had changed to a shirt and trousers that were shorter than full-length trousers but longer than half-pants. Even today, anyone in Goa wearing half-pants reaching below the knees invites the comment that he is dressed like a pader (baker).

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Kabai: Single-piece long knee-length frock in Portuguese days1 Mark
Childhood alteration: Shirts and intermediate three-quarter pants1 Mark
Q8
Part II (Coorg): Describe the diverse wildlife, river Kaveri, and adventure sports that make Coorg an ecological paradise for travelers.
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Coorg is an ecological haven nestled in the Western Ghats. The sacred river Kaveri obtains its crystal waters from Coorg's hills and forests, teeming with Mahaseer—a large freshwater fish. Kingfishers dive for catch, while squirrels, langurs, and macaque monkeys drop partially eaten fruit into clear ripples. Wild elephants enjoy being scrubbed in the river by mahouts. The rugged terrain offers high-energy adventure sports like river rafting, canoeing, rappelling, rock climbing, mountain biking, and numerous trekking trails through Brahmagiri hills.

CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Fauna: Mahaseer fish, kingfishers, langurs, elephants bathed in Kaveri1½ Marks
Adventure sports: River rafting, canoeing, rappelling, rock climbing1½ Marks
🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 5CBSE 2026-27
Competency-Based Evaluation
Analysis: Pranjol's father commended Rajvir: 'You seem to have done your homework before coming.' Why is prior research and intellectual curiosity essential when traveling?

Rajvir transformed a routine train journey into an enriching educational experience because he read extensively about tea legends, flushes, and botanical facts before visiting Dhekiabari. While Pranjol took his native tea surroundings for granted, Rajvir observed the pruning, plucking, and seasonal yield with passionate appreciation. Prior research enriches travel, transforming tourists from passive spectators into culturally enlightened observers.
