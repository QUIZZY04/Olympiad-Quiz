Q1
Why was Sulekha nicknamed 'Bholi' (the simpleton)? What physical and neurological misfortunes did she suffer in childhood?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

When she was ten months old, Sulekha fell off a cot on her head, which damaged some part of her brain, leaving her mentally backward; hence everyone called her 'Bholi' (the simpleton). At two years old, an attack of smallpox left her entire body pockmarked, sparing only her eyes. Furthermore, she could not speak until she was five, and when she finally learned, she stammered heavily, prompting other children to mimic and mock her.

Q2
Why did Ramlal's wife agree to send Bholi to school, while refusing to send her other daughters?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

When the Tehsildar inaugurated the village primary school for girls, he ordered Numberdar Ramlal to set an example by sending his daughters. Ramlal's wife objected to sending Radha, Mangla, or Champa, fearing that nobody would marry educated girls. However, she readily agreed to send Bholi, arguing that with her ugly pockmarked face and lack of sense, she had zero chance of getting married anyway, so the school teachers could worry about her.

Q3
How did Bholi's school teacher transform her from a terrified, stammering child into a confident student?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

While Bholi wept in a corner in terror, the teacher approached her with gentle, loving warmth—a tone Bholi had never heard in her family. When Bholi stammered 'Bh-Bh-Bholi', the teacher did not laugh, but affectionately encouraged her to complete her name. She gifted her a book with colorful pictures of birds and animals, reassuring Bholi that through education she would become more learned and respected than anyone in the village, and no one would ever dare laugh at her again.

Q4
Who was Bishamber Nath? Why did Bholi's parents agree to marry her to him?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Bishamber Nath was a prosperous, middle-aged grocer from another village with a big shop, his own house, and several thousand rupees in the bank. He was around fifty years old—the same age as Bholi's father—limped when he walked, and had grown-up children from a first marriage. Bholi's parents accepted his proposal because he did not ask for any dowry initially and lived far away, ignorant of Bholi's pockmarks and mental history.

Q5
What caused the crisis at the wedding ceremony? How did Bishamber blackmail Ramlal?
3 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

At the garland ceremony, a woman slipped back Bholi's veil, revealing her face. Seeing her pockmarks, Bishamber stopped abruptly and refused to marry her unless Ramlal paid him a cash dowry of five thousand rupees on the spot. Desperate to save his family's honor, Ramlal wept and placed his turban at Bishamber's feet, finally handing over a bundle of five thousand rupees.

Q6
'Education is the supreme catalyst for female emancipation.' How did schooling transform Bholi into Sulekha, a woman of unshakeable courage and self-respect?
6 Marks
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. From 'Mute Cow' to Independent Voice:
Throughout childhood, Bholi was treated as a submissive, dispensable burden—a 'dumb cow' who stammered in fear. However, years of dedicated schooling ignited a quiet revolution inside her soul, nurturing intellect, self-worth, and moral discernment.

2. The Climactic Act of Defiance:
When Bishamber greedily raised the garland after extorting five thousand rupees, Bholi's hand struck out like lightning. She flung the wedding garland into the sacred fire, threw off her veil, and spoke in a clear, loud, steady voice with zero stammer. She firmly declared: 'Father! Take back your money. I am not going to marry this man!'

3. Triumph of Sulekha:
When old women mocked her as shameless, Bholi silenced them with dignified fire. She announced that to honor her father's age, she had agreed to marry a limping, elderly widower, but she would never accept a greedy, cowardly wretch as her husband. She promised to serve her parents in their old age and teach in the very school where she found her voice. Watching quietly from a corner, her smiling teacher witnessed the masterpiece she had sculpted—proving that education transforms voiceless victims into courageous sovereign women.

🎯 Competency-Based & Value-Based Questions — Unit 27CBSE 2026-27
Social Critique & Gender Empowerment
Analysis: How does 'Bholi' expose the deeply rooted evils of dowry, patriarchy, and superficial beauty standards in Indian society?

K.A. Abbas delivers a trenchant critique of patriarchal social conditioning. Ramlal's household valued daughters solely as marriage commodities: fair and pretty daughters were married off easily, while Bholi was neglected due to skin scars. Bishamber viewed marriage as a commercial transaction, shameless in extorting money from a humiliated father. Bholi's rebellion shatters this feudal mindset, demonstrating that a woman's true beauty lies in self-respect, moral courage, and financial self-reliance.
