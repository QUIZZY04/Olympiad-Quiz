AH2

Bringing Up Kari

An Alien Hand • Author: Dhan Gopal Mukerji — The thrilling upbringing, mischievous antics, and heroism of a baby elephant

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
Kari was a five-month-old baby elephant given to the nine-year-old narrator to raise. Kari lived in a sturdy pavilion under a thatched roof resting on thick tree trunks. He consumed forty pounds of twigs daily, relishing tender banyan shoots, and enjoyed a morning sand-rub and bath in the river. Kari was sensitive and mischievous like a baby: he once developed a taste for ripe bananas and used his long black trunk like a stealthy python to snatch them through dining room windows! When caught, he felt so humiliated by scolding that he never stole again. Kari proved his supreme courage and empathy when he pushed the narrator into the deep river to rescue a drowning boy. Finally, the narrator explains the rigorous training required for an elephant, especially 'the Master Call'—a strange whistling hiss that enables a lost traveler in the jungle to command their elephant to tear down trees and create a safe passage home.
🎯 Central Theme: The profound bond of mutual affection, intelligence, and empathy between human beings and noble wild animals.
📖 Intext Comprehension Checks
Comprehension Check & Text Q1
The enclosure in which Kari lived had a thatched roof that rested on thick tree trunks. Why?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
The thatched roof rested on thick tree trunks so that:
  1. It would not collapse if Kari bumped against the heavy wooden poles as he moved or romped around inside the pavilion.
  2. Elephants possess enormous physical strength even as babies; only deeply anchored tree trunks could withstand Kari's weight and playful jostling.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Preventing collapse when Kari playfully bumped against the poles 1 Mark
Anchorage capable of withstanding the elephant's mounting weight 1 Mark

2 Marks
Detailed Solution
Yes, Kari thoroughly enjoyed his morning bath in the river.
Evidence from text:

  • After that, he would plunge into the water and stay submerged for a long time.

Comprehension Check & Text Q3
Finding good twigs for Kari took a long time. Why?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
Gathering twigs for Kari was a laborious task because:
  1. Elephants are extremely fastidious eaters—if a twig was mutilated, bruised, or damaged, Kari would refuse to touch it.
  2. The narrator had to climb hazardous, tall trees to cut only the most succulent, tender young saplings and banyan shoots.
  3. It required a sharp hatchet (taking half an hour to sharpen) so that the cuts were clean and smooth.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Kari's fastidious refusal of mutilated or broken twigs 1 Mark
Need to climb tall trees and sharpen hatchet for clean succulent cuts 1 Mark
Comprehension Check & Text Q4
Why did Kari push his friend (the narrator) into the stream?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
Kari pushed the narrator into the rushing stream because a boy was drowning at the bottom of the river.
Kari had spotted the drowning boy lying flat on the riverbed, but being heavy and unable to lift the boy single-handedly without sinking into the swift current, Kari forcefully shoved the narrator into the water to prompt him into saving the drowning victim. Kari then rushed in to assist both boys when the current proved too strong.

He slowly snaked his long, dark, flexible trunk through the open window, reached the dining table, curled his trunk around the large fruit dish, and scooped up every ripe banana, pulling them outside silently. The family originally suspected the servants and then the narrator, before the truth came to light.

Comprehension Check & Text Q7
Kari learnt the commands to sit down and to walk. What were the instructions for each command?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
  • To Sit Down: The command word was 'Dhat', accompanied by pulling his ear gently. Upon hearing 'Dhat', Kari would bend his knees and sit down.
  • To Walk: The command word was 'Mali', accompanied by pulling his trunk forward. Upon hearing 'Mali', Kari would start marching forward.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
'Dhat' + ear pull for sitting down 1 Mark
'Mali' + trunk forward pull for walking 1 Mark
Comprehension Check & Text Q8
What is 'the master call'? Why is it the most important signal for an elephant to learn?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. What is the Master Call: It is a peculiar, eerie sound—a strange mixture of a hissing snake and a snarling tiger—made directly into the elephant's ear.
2. Why it is the most important signal:
  • It takes nearly five long years for an elephant to master it.
  • If a master gets lost in a dense, pitch-black jungle at night where no path is visible, giving the master call commands the elephant to tear down trees in front of him with its trunk.
  • This crashes branches, frightens away prowling tigers, leopards, and wild monkeys, and cuts a safe, illuminated corridor directly through the impenetrable jungle all the way home.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Description: strange hiss-and-snarl sound hissed into ear 1 Mark
Significance: lifesaving emergency command to clear a jungle path 2 Marks

Competency-Based Case Study: Wildlife Ethics
What does Kari's reaction to being scolded for stealing fruit tell us about the emotional intelligence and self-respect of elephants?

Elephants possess remarkable emotional intelligence, moral comprehension, and dignity:

  • When the narrator led Kari to the fruit and firmly scolded him, Kari did not retaliate with violence. He bowed his head in palpable shame and understood that he had violated trust.
  • He never repeated the offense. The author remarks that an elephant accepts justified punishment with dignity, but if punished unjustly, will remember the grievance and avenge it. This underscores the necessity of treating intelligent animals with justice and empathy.
