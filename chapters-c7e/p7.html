P7

Meadow Surprises

Honeycomb (Poem) • Poet: Lois Brandt Phillips — A keen observer's journey through the hidden marvels of lush grassland

📜 Complete Poem Text & Stanzas

Meadows have surprises, You can find them if you look; Walk softly through the velvet grass, And listen by the brook. You may see a butterfly Rest upon a buttercup And unfold its drinking straws To sip the nectar up. You may scare a rabbit Who is sitting very still; Though at first you may not see him, When he hops you will. A dandelion whose fuzzy head Was golden days ago Has turned to airy parachutes That flutter when you blow. Explore the meadow houses, The burrows in the ground, A nest beneath tall grasses, The ant’s amazing mound. Oh! Meadows have surprises And many things to tell; You may discover these yourself, If you look and listen well.
Key Poetic Devices:
  • Metaphor: 'velvet grass' compares the lush softness of grass to luxurious velvet. 'Drinking straws' compares the butterfly's proboscis to drinking tubes. 'Airy parachutes' compares dandelion seed filaments to miniature parachutes.
  • Alliteration: 'butterfly / buttercup', 'softly / surprises'.
  • Sensory Imagery: Tactile ('velvet grass'), auditory ('listen by the brook'), and visual ('golden days ago', 'airy parachutes', 'ant's amazing mound').
Chapter Summary & Core Theme
Lois Brandt Phillips invites readers into the gentle wonderland of meadows. A meadow is packed with secret surprises, but they unveil themselves only to those who walk softly and listen attentively. In this green world, one can watch a delicate butterfly unroll its proboscis (drinking straw) to sip nectar from a buttercup, startle a camouflaged rabbit into bounding leaps, blow away the fluffy parachute seeds of a mature dandelion, discover subterranean animal burrows, spot bird nests nestled in tall grass, and marvel at the architectural genius of an anthill.
🎯 Central Theme: The breathtaking microscopic and wildlife wonders of nature revealed to those who walk gently and observe with mindfulness.

Working with the Poem — Q1
Which line in every stanza (or the opening/closing lines) suggests that you have to be observant to see the meadow surprises?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
The lines that emphasize keen observation are:
  • Stanza 1: "You can find them if you look; / Walk softly through the velvet grass, / And listen by the brook."
  • Stanza 6: "You may discover these yourself, / If you look and listen well."
Both lines remind us that nature's secrets remain hidden from hurried, noisy passersby; they reward only patient watchers who look closely and listen quietly.

Working with the Poem — Q2
What are the surprises mentioned in the poem that a meadow has to offer?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
The meadow offers an astonishing variety of natural surprises:
  1. The Velvet Grass & Brook: Soft grassy carpets and the gentle babbling music of a running stream.
  2. The Butterfly: Sipping sweet nectar from a yellow buttercup using its slender proboscis ('drinking straws').
  3. The Camouflaged Rabbit: Sitting motionlessly in hiding, suddenly revealed when it bounds away in rapid hops.
  4. The Metamorphic Dandelion: Once a bright golden bloom, now transformed into airy white parachutes that disperse on the breeze.
  5. Meadow Houses: Animal burrows underground, delicate bird nests sheltered beneath tall grass, and towering anthills.

Working with the Poem — Q3
Describe the transformation of a dandelion flower as described in the poem.
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
A few days prior, the dandelion was a vibrant, golden-yellow blossom soaking in the sunlight. Over a few days, its petals withered, and the flower transformed into a spherical white seed-head with delicate, fuzzy filaments. These filaments act as 'airy parachutes'—when a child blows on them or the wind whispers through the field, they detach effortlessly and drift gracefully through the air to colonize new ground.

Working with the Poem — Q4
What are 'meadow houses'? Mention the specific animal habitats named in the poem.
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
'Meadow houses' refers to the ingenious natural dwellings constructed by diverse small creatures within the grassland ecosystem.
The specific habitats named in the poem are:
  1. Burrows in the ground (dug by rabbits, moles, or field mice for subterranean shelter).
  2. Nests beneath tall grasses (woven discreetly by ground-nesting birds).
  3. The ant's amazing mound (intricate underground cities with raised soil domes built by ants).

Competency-Based Environmental Awareness
What scientific lesson about biodiversity and habitat preservation is embedded in the poem 'Meadow Surprises'?

The poem illustrates that an ordinary grassland is not empty vacant land, but a thriving, interdependent ecological community:

  • Pollination: Butterflies sipping nectar sustain flora propagation.
  • Seed Dispersal: Dandelion parachutes demonstrate airborne reproductive adaptation.
  • Micro-habitats: Underground burrows, nests, and ant mounds prove that grassland soil provides critical shelter for countless species. Destroying meadows for concrete development wipes out vital biodiversity.
