AH3

Golu Grows a Nose

An Alien Hand • Author: Rudyard Kipling — A delightful origin fable of how the curious baby elephant acquired his versatile trunk

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
Long ago, elephants had no long trunks—only a small, bulgy nose the size of a boot that could wiggle but could not pick things up. In Africa, there lived a baby elephant named Golu who was full of insatiable curiosity. He continually asked awkward questions to his relatives: asking his tall aunt ostrich why she didn't fly, his tall uncle giraffe why his skin was spotty, his huge uncle hippopotamus why his eyes were red, and his hairy uncle baboon why melons tasted like melons. None could answer him. Finally, Golu asked the kolokolo bird what the crocodile ate for dinner. The bird advised him to journey to the banks of the great, grey-green, greasy Limpopo River. There Golu met the python and then encountered the crocodile. The deceitful crocodile lured Golu close and clamped its jaws onto Golu's nose, intending to drag him into the deep water for feast. A fierce tug-of-war ensued, with the python wrapping itself around Golu's legs. Golu's nose stretched out five feet long before the crocodile let go! While Golu waited for it to shrink, he discovered the magnificent advantages of a long trunk: swatting flies, plucking grass, scooping mud, and spraying cool water.
🎯 Central Theme: The perils and rewards of insatiable curiosity, courage, and how evolutionary adversity yields great blessings.

Comprehension Check & Text Q7
Who helped Golu on the river bank? How?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. Who helped: The wise and helpful python helped Golu on the riverbank.
2. How the python helped:
  • When the crocodile clamped its jaws on Golu's nose and dragged him toward the deep water, the python rushed forward and coiled itself twice around Golu's stomach.
  • Digging its muscular body into the bank, the python pulled backward with all its might while Golu pulled too.
  • With combined effort, they won the tug-of-war, forcing the crocodile to release Golu's nose.

Comprehension Check & Text Q8
Name two things the elephant can do with his trunk, and two things he cannot do.
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
Two things an elephant CAN do with his trunk:
  1. Pluck fresh bundles of tall grass and delicate tree leaves and stuff them directly into its mouth.
  2. Scoop up cool mud and spray jets of cold water over its head to keep cool on hot days (and swat away biting flies).
Two things an elephant CANNOT do with his trunk:
  1. Walk, run, or bear its body weight (the trunk is not a weight-bearing limb).
  2. Digest food directly (food must be transferred to the mouth and stomach).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Two correct abilities (feeding, spraying water/mud, swatting) 1 Mark
Two correct limitations (locomotion, actual digestion) 1 Mark

Fable Analysis / HOTS
What moral insight does Kipling offer regarding curiosity, danger, and turning physical change into an asset in 'Golu Grows a Nose'?

Kipling's fable illustrates three timeless life lessons:

  • The Double Edge of Curiosity: Pure inquisitiveness drives exploration, but unchecked naivety without caution can lead one directly into the jaws of danger.
  • The Value of True Mentors: The python did not mock Golu's stretched nose; instead, it guided him to recognize its hidden benefits.
  • Turning Adversity into Strength: What initially seemed like a painful injury (a nose pulled five feet long) transformed into the elephant's most versatile, multi-functional tool for survival.
