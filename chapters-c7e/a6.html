A Tiger in the House

An Alien Hand • Author: Ruskin Bond — The poignant journey of Timothy the tiger cub from domestic pet to Lucknow zoo

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
During an elaborate hunting expedition in the Terai jungles near Dehra Dun, Ruskin Bond's Grandfather discovered an orphaned four-month-old tiger cub hiding among the roots of a banyan tree. Grandfather brought him home, and Grandmother named him Timothy. Timothy was nurtured on bottled milk and raw mutton, sharing the house with Toto the monkey and a mongrel puppy. Timothy was playful, clean, and affectionate, sleeping on the cook Mahmoud's bed. However, when Timothy grew to full six-month maturity, predatory instincts emerged: he stalked cats, killed poultry, and looked menacingly at Mahmoud. Grandfather prudently donated Timothy to the Lucknow Zoo. Six months later, Grandfather visited the zoo to see Timothy. Finding a magnificent tiger in a cage, Grandfather stroked its forehead, tickled its ears, and smacked its mouth affectionately while the tiger purred and licked his hands. As Grandfather bid farewell, a trembling keeper revealed the horrifying truth: Timothy had died of pneumonia two months prior; this caged tiger was a ferocious wild beast trapped only last month, notorious for its bad temper! Grandfather quietly whispered, 'Goodnight, Timothy,' and walked away with a heavy heart.
🎯 Central Theme: The instinctive nature of wild predators, the tragedy of caging majestic animals, and the mysterious power of fearless affection.

Exercises — Q1
Why did the wild tiger allow Grandfather to stroke its forehead and tickle its ears without attacking?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
The ferocious wild tiger did not attack Grandfather because:
  • Absence of Fear: Animals instinctively sense adrenaline, terror, and aggression in humans. Grandfather approached the cage with calm, pure, fearless love and authority.
  • Hypnotic Affection: The tiger had experienced only harsh prods and hostility from zoo visitors and guards. Grandfather's gentle stroking, ear-scratching, and affectionate petting soothed the beast, stunning its predatory rage into calm submission.

Wildlife Conservation & Ethics / HOTS
What message does Ruskin Bond convey about keeping wild predators as domestic house pets?

Ruskin Bond presents a cautionary and empathetic perspective on wild animals and domesticity:

  • While wild animals can display genuine affection and bonding during infancy, their genetic evolutionary instincts cannot be erased by warm milk or pampered sofas.
  • Keeping large predators in domestic settings inevitably endangers human life and leads to the tragedy of caging them in artificial zoo enclosures. True love for wildlife demands preserving their natural jungle habitats rather than domesticating or caging them.
