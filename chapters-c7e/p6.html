Mystery of the Talking Fan

Honeycomb (Poem) • Poet: Maude Rubin — The mechanical chatter of an unlubricated ceiling fan silenced by oil

📜 Complete Poem Text & Stanzas

Once there was a talking fan — Electrical his chatter. I couldn’t quite hear what he said And I hope it doesn’t matter Because one day somebody oiled His little whirling motor And all the mystery was spoiled — He ran as still as water.
Key Poetic Devices:
  • Personification: The ceiling fan is personified as a talkative individual ('talking fan', 'electrical his chatter', 'what he said', 'his little whirling motor').
  • Simile: 'He ran as still as water' compares the silent, effortless rotation of the lubricated fan to calm, smooth-flowing water.
  • Rhyme Scheme: abcbdefe (fan / chatter / said / matter; oiled / motor / spoiled / water).
Chapter Summary & Core Theme
In 'Mystery of the Talking Fan', Maude Rubin weaves a playful tale around an old, squeaky electric ceiling fan. The child-speaker imagines that the fan's metallic, rhythmic screeching is actually an urgent language—an 'electrical chatter'. The child wonders what secrets or complaints the fan is trying to communicate. However, before the child can decipher this whimsical mystery, a practical mechanic or family member oils the motor. Lubrication instantly eliminates the friction, and the mystery dissolves as the fan hums along smoothly and silently 'as still as water'.
🎯 Central Theme: Childhood imagination personifying inanimate household appliances before scientific reasoning and maintenance intervene.
✍️ Working with the Poem
Working with the Poem — Q1
Fans don't talk, but it is possible to imagine that they do. What is it, then, that sounds like the fan's talk?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
What sounds like the fan's 'talk' is the loud, metallic, rhythmic friction created by the dry, unoiled bearings and motor parts inside the ceiling fan as it rotates. As metal rubs dryly against metal at high speed, it produces a continuous, high-pitched whirring or squeaking noise, which the imaginative child perceives as animated 'talking'.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Frictional squeaking / rattling of dry unoiled bearings and rotating motor parts 1 Mark
Imaginative perception of mechanical sound as speech 1 Mark
Working with the Poem — Q2 (i & ii)
Complete the following sentences:
(i) The chatter was electrical because...
(ii) It is mysterious because...
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
(i) The chatter was electrical because the fan runs on electric power, and the sound was generated by an electric motor rotating its metallic parts.
(ii) It is mysterious because the speaker could not comprehend or decipher what the fan was trying to communicate through its bizarre mechanical sounds.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
(i) Powered by electricity / motor operating on electric current 1 Mark
(ii) Inability to understand the imagined message / hidden meaning 1 Mark
Working with the Poem — Q3
What do you think the talking fan was demanding?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
The talking fan was desperately demanding oil and lubrication for its parched, friction-ridden whirling motor! Its noisy mechanical groans were a plea to be relieved of frictional wear and tear so that it could rotate smoothly and silently.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Demanding oil / lubrication for dry motor parts 1 Mark
Relief from friction and mechanical strain 1 Mark
Working with the Poem — Q4
How does an electric fan manage to throw so much cool air when it is turned on?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
When an electric fan is turned on: 1. The electric current energizes the electric motor, causing the shaft and attached fan blades to spin at high velocity. 2. The angled (curved) blades slice through the air, drawing air from behind and pushing it forcefully downwards and outwards. 3. This circulating airflow accelerates the evaporation of perspiration from our skin, producing a refreshing cooling effect.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Electric motor spinning angled blades at high velocity 1 Mark
Displacing air and enhancing evaporative cooling 1 Mark
💡 Competency-Based & HOTS Questions (CBSE 2026-27 Pattern) Analytical Thinking
Extract-Based Critical Thinking
Read the lines:
"And all the mystery was spoiled — He ran as still as water."
Why does the poet say the mystery was 'spoiled'? Explain the simile 'as still as water'.

Why the mystery was spoiled: The child derived poetic enchantment from imagining the fan as a sentient conversationalist. When practical lubricating oil eradicated the squeak, it reduced the wondrous 'living companion' back to a dull, silent, ordinary machine.

Explanation of the simile: 'As still as water' compares the fan's restored motion to calm, unruffled water—it now spins with such seamless quietness and balance that it produces no vibration or disturbance whatsoever.
