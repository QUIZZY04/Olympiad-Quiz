H4

The Ashes That Made Trees Bloom

Honeycomb (Prose) • Author: William Elliot Griffis — A Japanese folktale of fidelity, virtue, and magical blossoming cherry trees

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
In 19th-century Japan, an honest, kind-hearted old couple kept a pet dog named Muko, treating him like their own child. One day, Muko guided the kind old man to a spot in the garden and scratched the earth, unearthing a pile of glittering gold coins. The old couple gave generously to the poor and lived comfortably. Their envious, wicked neighbors dragged Muko into their garden, expecting gold, but found only a dead, foul-smelling kitten. In rage, the wicked couple beat Muko to death. Muko's spirit appeared to the good old man in a dream, instructing him to cut down a pine tree over his grave to make a wooden mortar and mill. When used to prepare New Year's rice pastries and bean sauce, the dough magically turned into showers of pure gold coins. The greedy neighbors borrowed the mortar, but their dough turned into a squirming mass of foul worms, so they chopped it into firewood. Muko's spirit appeared again, telling the good man to sprinkle the ashes of the mill onto withered trees. When the grand Daimio (feudal lord) paraded by, the good old man climbed a bare cherry tree, scattered a pinch of ash, and the tree burst into glorious pink blossoms. The delighted Daimio showered him with silk robes, cakes, and wealth. When the envious neighbor attempted the same, the wind blew the dry soot into the eyes and noses of the Daimio and his wife; the royal guards dragged the greedy man down and beat him soundly, leaving him to perish in the mud.
🎯 Central Theme: Virtue, kindness to animals, and benevolence bring prosperity, while greed, malice, and cruelty lead to destruction.
📖 Intext Comprehension Checks
Comprehension Check — Page 60 (Q1)
Why did the neighbors kill the dog?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution

The wicked, envious neighbors killed Muko because they dragged him into their garden and forced him to find buried treasure for them. When Muko sniffed and scratched at a spot, the greedy man eagerly dug up the soil, only to find a rotting, stinking dead kitten. Enraged at the disappointment and foul odor, the wicked old man beat Muko brutally to death with a hoe.

✍️ Working with the Text
Working with the Text — Q1
The old farmer is a kind person. What actions of his show his kindness?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
The old farmer's extraordinary kindness is evident from several actions: 1. Treated Muko as a Child: Having no children of their own, the couple made Muko a cushion of blue crepe, fed him boiled rice with their own chopsticks, and offered him delicate strips of fish. 2. Compassion for Wildlife: While tilling his rice fields, the old farmer deliberately turned up sods of earth to provide grubs and worms for hungry white herons. 3. Charity to the Poor: When Muko led him to a pile of buried gold, the old couple bought land, hosted a feast for their friends, and distributed plentiful alms to their impoverished neighbors.

Working with the Text — Q2
What did the dog do to lead the farmer to the hidden gold?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
Muko ran towards the farmer, putting his paws against the farmer's legs and gesturing with his head towards a spot behind him. When the farmer followed him to see what the matter was, Muko began scratching the ground eagerly with his paws. Thinking there might be a bone or bit of fish, the farmer struck his hoe into the earth, unearthing a gleaming mound of gold coins.

Working with the Text — Q3
How did the spirit of the dog help the farmer first? How did it help him next?
4 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. First Help (Mortar & Mill): The spirit appeared in a dream and told the farmer to cut down the pine tree over his grave to carve a mortar for rice pastry and a hand-mill for bean sauce. When the farmer ground the rice and beans, the dough magically transformed into a sparkling fountain of gold coins.
2. Second Help (Magic Ashes): When the jealous neighbors burned the wooden mill, Muko's spirit instructed the farmer to collect the ashes of the mill and sprinkle them on withered, dead trees. The ashes miraculously caused withered cherry trees to burst into glorious, fragrant pink blossoms, earning the farmer immense royal honor from the Daimio.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
First instance: Wooden mortar/mill yielding gold coins 2 Marks

Working with the Text — Q4
Why did the Daimio reward the farmer but punish his neighbor for the same act?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. The Good Farmer's Act: The kind farmer was gentle, reverent, and gifted with magical purity. When he scattered the ash, a withered cherry tree instantly burst into breathtaking pink blossoms, delighting the Daimio and filling the procession with sweet fragrance.
2. The Wicked Neighbor's Act: The greedy neighbor acted out of jealousy and malice. He climbed the tree greedily without bowing. When he threw the ash, not a single bud blossomed; instead, a sudden gust of wind blew the fine, choking soot directly into the eyes, noses, and royal palanquin of the Daimio and his wife, spoiling the dignity of the procession. The royal guards thrashed the wicked man and left him in the ditch.

Competency-Based Extract Question
How does the Japanese tale illustrate the universal moral law of Karma?

The tale is an allegory of karmic justice. Selfless love, kindness to voiceless animals, and humility are rewarded with prosperity and peace that outlives physical death. In contrast, selfishness, cruelty, and parasitic envy result in self-inflicted ruin and public disgrace.
