The Tiny Teacher

An Alien Hand • Author: Adapted from Natural History — The extraordinary social discipline, industry, and wisdom of the common ant

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
Though the ant is the smallest, commonest creature we see every day, it is also the wisest. Naturalists who have studied ants closely in artificial 'ant-farms' reveal an astonished world of complex architecture and social harmony. In an anthill (which contains thousands of rooms and corridors), each ant performs an assigned role with clockwork precision without ever quarrelling. The queen ant lays eggs, nurse ants care for the grubs, worker ants clean and gather food, soldiers guard the nurseries, and sweepers keep the premises clean. Ants even keep other insects like beetles, lesser breeds of ants, and the greenfly (whom they 'milk' for honeydew like a cow). From this 'tiny teacher', human beings can learn invaluable lessons of hard work, discipline, hygiene, unwavering loyalty to duty, and peaceful cooperation.
🎯 Central Theme: Humility, tireless dedication, peaceful coexistence, and selfless division of labor within the animal kingdom.

Comprehension Check — Page 3 (Q3)
In what ways is an ant’s life peaceful?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
An ant’s life is remarkably peaceful for the following reasons:
  1. Flawless Division of Labor: Every ant performs its designated duty (queen, worker, soldier, cleaner, nurse) with total dedication and diligence.
  2. Zero Interference: No ant ever interferes with or encroaches upon the work of another group (workers never forage in soldiers' territory, and soldiers never fetch food).
  3. Absence of Internal Conflict: No ant ever fights with other members of its nest. They communicate politely through their antennae (feelers), greet each other respectfully, and share resources amicably.

Comprehension Check — Page 5 (Q1)
How long does a grub take to become a cocoon, and what happens after that to become a complete ant?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. A grub takes about two to three weeks of feeding, nursing, and cleaning to spin itself into a cocoon.
2. The cocoon then lies motionless without food or activity for another three weeks.
3. Finally, the cocoon breaks open, and a perfect, fully-formed ant emerges, ready for training.

Comprehension Check — Page 5 (Q2)
Why do worker ants carry the grubs about?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
Worker ants carry the grubs (infant ants) outside their dark subterranean chambers every single day for:
  • Air: Ensuring proper aeration and fresh ventilation.
  • Exercise: Stimulating healthy physiological growth and circulation.
  • Sunshine: Absorbing sunlight and warmth, which is vital for their development into healthy pupae.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Daily routine for fresh air and ventilation 1 Mark
Exercise and warm sunshine essential for healthy development 1 Mark
Comprehension Check — Page 5 (Q3)
What jobs are new ants trained for? Name the roles.
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
New ants are trained by experienced veteran ants to perform specialized duties, including:
  • Workers: Foraging for food, maintaining tunnels, and cleaning rooms.
  • Soldiers / Guards: Patrolling entrances and defending the nest against intruders.
  • Builders: Excavating new galleries and repairing anthill chambers.
  • Cleaners / Sweepers: Disposing of refuse and maintaining hygiene in the colony.

Comprehension Check — Page 5 (Q4)
Name some other creatures that live in anthills. Why do ants want these alien creatures to live in their nests?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. Other creatures living in anthills: Beetles, lesser breeds of ants, and the greenfly (aphid).
2. Why ants keep them:
  • Pleasant Smells: Some creatures produce natural scents that are pleasant to ant senses.
  • Sweet Juices: Others secrete sweet nectar and juices that ants relish.
  • Pets & Playthings: Some serve as harmless pets, just as humans keep dogs and cats.
  • Honeydew (Milk): The greenfly is the ant's 'cow'. Ants train and stroke it gently with their antennae, coaxing it to secrete a drop of delicious sweet honeydew, which ants drink with relish.

✍️ Chapter-End Exercises
Exercises — Q1
Discuss the reasons why the ant is called 'The Tiny Teacher'. What three lessons can we learn from the tiny teacher?
5 Marks
Detailed Solution
Why called 'The Tiny Teacher':
Although the ant is one of the smallest and most fragile insects, its conduct exhibits supreme intelligence, discipline, and communal harmony. It silently teaches humanity how to live peacefully, ethically, and productively without greed, war, or laziness.

Three Invaluable Lessons We Can Learn:
  1. Tireless Hard Work & Devotion to Duty: Every ant performs its assigned work from dawn to dusk without complaining or procrastinating. Humans can learn diligence and professional commitment from them.
  2. Strict Sense of Discipline & Cooperation: In an anthill of thousands, not a single ant ever fights with another or shirks responsibility. They cooperate seamlessly for the collective welfare of the colony.
  3. Loyalty to the Homeland & Collective Care: Ants put the welfare of the colony, the safety of their young (grubs), and the cleanliness of their shared home above individual selfishness.

💡 Competency-Based & HOTS Questions (CBSE 2026-27 Pattern) Analytical Thinking
Competency-Based Comparison: Ant Colony vs Human Society
Despite possessing advanced technology and vast intelligence, human societies frequently suffer from wars, crime, and social inequality. Contrast this with the governance of an anthill.

An ant colony operates on pure meritocracy, absolute selflessness, and mutual respect:

  • No Class Warfare: Queen ants do not oppress workers; soldier ants do not exploit nurses. Every role is respected as vital to survival.
  • Zero Crime or Corruption: Ants do not hoard food for private greed; every grain collected is stored in public granaries for collective welfare.
  • Communication without Malice: By communicating purely for facts and mutual protection, ants eliminate misunderstandings, deceit, and jealousy. Human beings would eradicate wars if they adopted the ant's selfless social contract.
