Garden Snake

Honeycomb (Poem) • Poet: Muriel L. Sonne — Overcoming unfounded phobias and recognizing ecological harmony

📜 Complete Poem Text & Stanzas

I saw a snake and ran away... Some snakes are dangerous, they say; But mother says that kind is good, And eats up insects for his food. So when he wiggles in the grass I’ll stand aside and watch him pass, And tell myself, “There’s no mistake, It’s just a harmless garden snake!”
Key Poetic Devices:
  • Rhyme Scheme: aabbccdd (away/say, good/food, grass/pass, mistake/snake) with neat rhyming couplets.
  • Alliteration: 'wiggles in the grass', 'snake / stand'.
  • Tone Shift: Shifts from initial childish panic ('I saw a snake and ran away') to enlightened, scientific composure ('It's just a harmless garden snake!').
Chapter Summary & Core Theme
Muriel L. Sonne's poem 'Garden Snake' captures a child's sudden encounter with a reptile in the yard. Initially, conditioned by scary adult warnings that snakes are deadly, the child panics and flees. However, the child's mother dispels this myth with sound biological facts: common garden snakes are entirely non-venomous and beneficial because they feed on garden insects that destroy crops. Armed with this knowledge, the child replaces terror with calm curiosity, resolving to stand aside politely and watch the harmless creature wiggle past in peace.
🎯 Central Theme: Differentiating harmless wildlife from dangerous stereotypes, and appreciating ecological balance.

Working with the Poem — Q4
A snake has no legs or feet, but it moves very fast. Can you explain how snakes move?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
Although snakes have no limbs, they move with remarkable speed through serpentine locomotion:
  1. A snake's body contains hundreds of flexible vertebrae and ribs coupled with powerful muscle bands.
  2. It curves its body into lateral S-shaped waves, pushing backward and outward against irregularities in the ground, rocks, and grass stems.
  3. Broad, overlapping scales (scutes) on its belly grip the rough ground, preventing backward sliding and propelling the snake forward smoothly.

HOTS / Scientific Literacy
How does the mother's guidance in 'Garden Snake' debunk popular superstitions and promote humane wildlife coexistence?

In popular culture and folklore, snakes are universally demonized as evil, deadly monsters that must be beaten to death on sight. The mother counters this prejudice with scientific literacy and ecological wisdom:

  • She teaches that out of thousands of snake species, the vast majority are harmless and docile.
  • She identifies their role as a farmer's and gardener's ally—acting as natural biocontrol agents that devour disease-carrying rodents and crop-destroying insects without using chemical poisons.
  • By teaching respect instead of blind hysteria, she fosters peaceful, informed coexistence with nature.
