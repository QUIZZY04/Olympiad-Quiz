H8

A Homage to Our Brave Soldiers

Honeycomb (Prose) • Author: CBSE / National Curriculum — Young students explore the National War Memorial and honor the supreme sacrifices of our armed forces

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
Through a warm exchange of letters between two school friends, Anand and Soumya, this inspiring chapter narrates their educational trip to the National War Memorial near India Gate in New Delhi. Spread across 40 acres, the memorial honors post-independence soldiers who made the supreme sacrifice in wars (1947, 1962, 1965, 1971, Kargil 1999) and counter-terrorism operations. Soumya describes the architecture consisting of four concentric circular ramparts: Amar Chakra (Circle of Immortality with the eternal flame and obelisk), Veerta Chakra (Circle of Bravery depicting 6 bronze relief murals of joint combat operations), Tyag Chakra (Circle of Sacrifice with granite brick tablets bearing the names of over 26,000 fallen martyrs), and Raksha Chakra (Circle of Protection planted with over 600 trees). The students reflect on the supreme gallantry of Param Vir Chakra recipients like Major Somnath Sharma and Captain Vikram Batra, concluding that true patriotism lies in living with dedication, discipline, and unity for the nation.
🎯 Central Theme: Patriotism, selfless courage, and reverent national memory honoring India's fallen heroes.

Comprehension Check — Page 115 (Q1)
Where is the National War Memorial located, and what is its primary national significance?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
The National War Memorial is located in New Delhi, adjacent to India Gate in the C-Hexagon complex.
Significance: It stands as a sacred national monument dedicated to commemorating the supreme courage, valor, and sacrifice of armed forces personnel who laid down their lives defending the sovereignty and integrity of independent India since 1947.

Working with the Text — Q1
Name the four concentric circles that comprise the architectural layout of the National War Memorial.
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
The National War Memorial is designed as four concentric circles embodying the spiritual journey of a soldier:
  1. Amar Chakra (Circle of Immortality): The central ring featuring the 15.5-meter obelisk and the eternal flame.
  2. Veerta Chakra (Circle of Bravery): A covered gallery featuring six bronze murals depicting joint combat operations.
  3. Tyag Chakra (Circle of Sacrifice): Concentric granite brick walls inscribed with the names of over 26,000 fallen martyrs in golden letters.
  4. Raksha Chakra (Circle of Protection): The outermost ring consisting of over 600 dense trees symbolizing soldiers guarding the borders.

Working with the Text — Q2
What is the Param Vir Chakra? Name any two heroic recipients mentioned in the chapter.
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
The Param Vir Chakra (PVC) is India's highest military decoration, awarded for the most conspicuous bravery, supreme gallantry, or pre-eminent act of self-sacrifice in the face of the enemy.
Two heroic recipients:
  • Major Somnath Sharma: First recipient of the PVC (Posthumous), Battle of Badgam, 1947.
  • Captain Vikram Batra: Kargil War, 1999 (Famous war-cry: "Yeh Dil Maange More!").

Working with the Text — Q3
How does the author describe the atmosphere and feeling of standing at the Amar Chakra?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
Standing before the Amar Chakra evokes deep reverence, solemn humility, and patriotic pride. The gentle flicker of the eternal flame beneath the majestic granite obelisk symbolizes that though soldiers may fall in battle, their spirit and service are immortal. Observing the solemn silence, the synchronized ceremonial guard mount, and reading the touching verse — "When you go home, tell them of us and say, for your tomorrow, we gave our today" — leaves every visitor with moist eyes and a renewed pledge of loyalty to the nation.

Competency-Based Value Question: Civic Responsibility
What does it mean for young students today to live up to the sacrifices made by our armed forces?

Living up to the martyrs' sacrifice does not only mean fighting on battlefields; it means practicing everyday active citizenship: upholding communal harmony, obeying laws, pursuing academic excellence, protecting public property, and serving our communities with honesty, discipline, and unity.
