The Rebel

Honeycomb (Poem) • Poet: D. J. Enright — A humorous exploration of non-conformity, individualism, and social contrast

📜 Complete Poem Text & Stanzas

When everybody has short hair, The rebel lets his hair grow long. When everybody has long hair, The rebel cuts his hair short. When everybody talks during the lesson, The rebel doesn't say a word. When nobody talks during the lesson, The rebel creates a disturbance. When everybody wears a uniform, The rebel dresses in fantastic clothes. When everybody wears fantastic clothes, The rebel dresses soberly. In the company of dog lovers, The rebel expresses a preference for cats. In the company of cat lovers, The rebel puts in a good word for dogs. When everybody is greeting the rain, The rebel regrets the absence of sun. When everybody is greeting the sun, The rebel remarks on the need for rain. When everybody goes to the meeting, The rebel stays at home and reads a book. When everybody stays at home and reads a book, The rebel goes to the meeting. When everybody says, Yes please, The rebel says, No thank you. When everybody says, No thank you, The rebel says, Yes please. It is very good that we have rebels. You may not find it very good to be one.
Key Poetic Devices:
  • Antithesis / Contrast: Every pair of couplets presents stark opposite behaviors (short hair vs long hair, silence vs disturbance, uniform vs fantastic clothes, dogs vs cats, rain vs sun).
  • Irony: The rebel's entire non-conformity is ironically completely dependent upon what everyone else does—he has no independent opinion except to do the exact opposite of the crowd.
  • Repetition: Repetitive rhythmic structure ('When everybody... The rebel...').
Chapter Summary & Core Theme
D. J. Enright's poem 'The Rebel' portrays an individual who instinctively goes against the tide in every social setting. If the crowd has short hair, he grows his long; if everyone speaks in class, he stays mute, but if everyone is attentive, he creates a disturbance. He prefers dogs among cat enthusiasts and cats among dog lovers. He craves rain on sunny days and sun on rainy days. The poet concludes with a profound psychological paradox: society needs rebels because they break monotony and think differently, but being a rebel yourself is extremely difficult because society ostracizes and isolates you.
🎯 Central Theme: The nature of the individualist/rebel whose actions consistently contradict majority norms, provoking thought on tolerance versus discipline.

Working with the Poem — Q1 (i to vi)
Answer the following questions:
(i) If someone doesn't wear a uniform to school, what do you think the teacher will say?
(ii) When everyone wants a clear sky, what does the rebel want most?
(iii) If the rebel has a dog for a pet, what is everyone else likely to have?
(iv) Why is it good to have rebels?
(v) Why is it not good to be a rebel oneself?
(vi) Would you like to be a rebel? If yes, why? If not, why not?
5 Marks
Detailed Solution
(i) The teacher will likely scold the student, question their lack of school discipline, and may issue a reprimand, fine, or ask them to stand out for breaking the school dress code.
(ii) When everyone hopes for a clear, sunny sky, the rebel wants rain and clouds the most.
(iii) If the rebel chooses a dog as a pet, everyone else in the group is most likely to be fond of cats.
(iv) It is good to have rebels because they challenge outdated traditions, offer fresh and unconventional perspectives, encourage critical thinking, and prevent society from degenerating into blind, thoughtless conformity.
(v) It is not good to be a rebel oneself because an habitual rebel is often viewed with annoyance, disliked by authority, misunderstood by peers, and frequently suffers loneliness, social isolation, and exclusion.
(vi) Reflective Answer: I would not like to be a total rebel like the one in the poem because contrarianism purely for the sake of being opposite is childish and disrespectful. However, I would certainly like to have an independent mind—having the courage to stand up against injustice or unfair conventions when my conscience demands it.

💡 Competency-Based & HOTS Questions (CBSE 2026-27 Pattern) Analytical Thinking
Critical Analysis / HOTS
Analyze the concluding couplet: 'It is very good that we have rebels. / You may not find it very good to be one.' Why does the poet draw this sharp distinction?

The poet draws a profound distinction between societal benefit and personal cost:

  • From Society's perspective: Rebels are valuable catalysts. They prevent groupthink, mock absurd conformist rituals, and spark innovation and social reform. Without non-conformists, societies turn stagnant and totalitarian.
  • From the Individual's perspective: Living as an outcast requires immense emotional stamina. A rebel faces constant friction, parental disapproval, social alienation, and reprimands from institutions. Thus, while society enjoys having them, being one carries heavy psychological and social burdens.
Extract-Based Question
Read the lines:
"When everybody wears a uniform, The rebel dresses in fantastic clothes. When everybody wears fantastic clothes, The rebel dresses soberly."
(a) What does 'fantastic clothes' mean here?
(b) What does 'dresses soberly' mean?
(c) What fundamental motive drives the rebel's dress choices?

(a) 'Fantastic clothes' refers to flashy, colourful, flamboyant, or informal party wear.

(b) 'Dresses soberly' means dressing in simple, plain, formal, modest, and subdued clothing.

(c) His sole driving motive is contrast and defiance—he does not dress for fashion or comfort, but purely to oppose whatever standard attire the collective majority has adopted.
