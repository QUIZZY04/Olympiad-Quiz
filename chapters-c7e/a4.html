AH4

Chandni

An Alien Hand • Author: Zakir Husain — A heroic goat chooses a brief hour of perilous freedom over a lifetime in captivity

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
Dr. Zakir Husain's celebrated allegory tells the story of Abbu Khan, an old man living in Almora who loved pet goats. Despite his loving care, delicious green grass, and individual names, his goats repeatedly snapped their ropes and dashed up into the scenic pine-clad hills. In the hills lived an old, monstrous wolf who hunted and devoured them all. Heartbroken, Abbu Khan vowed never to keep goats again, but lonely in old age, he bought a pretty young white goat named Chandni. He thought Chandni would love him and never leave. However, the intoxicating beauty of the open hills and sweet breeze captivated Chandni. She stopped eating grass and grew pale. When Abbu Khan locked her in a small hut, she jumped out of an open window and bolted to freedom. In the hills, Chandni rejoiced in pure, unhindered liberty. At dusk, the monstrous wolf emerged with glowing eyes. Instead of surrendering or fleeing cowardly, Chandni stood firm like a brave soldier, fighting fiercely with her small horns through the night. At dawn, she fell dead, drenched in blood. While a flock of birds debated who won, a wise old bird proclaimed with absolute conviction: 'Chandni is the winner.'
🎯 Central Theme: The sacred, non-negotiable value of freedom and dignity, which is infinitely superior to safe slavery.

Comprehension Check — Page 37 (Q1)
Why did Abbu Khan's goats want to run away? What happened to them in the hills?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. Why they wanted to run away: Goats in hilly regions possess an innate, unquenchable passion for freedom. They hated being tethered to stakes by ropes and confined inside small compounds. They craved running free on the vast mountain slopes.
2. What happened to them in the hills: A ferocious, bloodthirsty old wolf lived in the hills. As soon as the defenseless runaway goats wandered into the forest, the wolf ambushed, killed, and devoured them.

Comprehension Check — Page 37 (Q2)
Abbu Khan said, "No more goats in my house ever again." Then why did he buy a young goat?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
Abbu Khan was an elderly, childless bachelor. Without his beloved goats, he felt terribly lonely, depressed, and isolated in his cottage. Overwhelmed by his acute loneliness, he could not live without a pet companion, so he purchased a charming young goat, believing that a young goat brought up with affectionate love would never leave him.

✍️ Chapter-End Exercises
Exercises — Q1
Why did the wise old bird declare Chandni the winner?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
The wise old bird declared Chandni the winner because:
  • While the wolf won physical possession of her meat, Chandni won the moral and spiritual victory.
  • Knowing fully well that she was physically outmatched by the predator, she did not flee like a coward, whine, or beg for mercy.
  • She stood bravely on her firm legs, lowered her small horns, and fought relentlessly throughout the night until her last breath.
  • She died as a free, dignified soul rather than living in the shameful security of captivity. Her courage elevated her to ultimate victory.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Moral and spiritual victory over physical defeat 1.5 Marks
Fighting to the last breath with dignity and uncompromising love for freedom 1.5 Marks
Exercises — Q2
"Death in an open field is better than life in a small hut." Discuss this statement in the context of Chandni.
4 Marks
Detailed Solution
This statement forms the philosophical core of Dr. Zakir Husain's story:
  • Life in the Small Hut: In Abbu Khan's hut, Chandni had safety, fresh grass, and affection. But this safety was conditional upon permanent subjugation—she was tied to a stake, enclosed by walls, and deprived of her natural instinct to roam the mountains. For a spirited soul, such a life is spiritual death.
  • Death in the Open Field: The open mountain slope represented genuine freedom, self-determination, and dignity. Chandni tasted pure ecstasy running wild for a single day. When the wolf arrived, she chose to face death with courage.
  • Conclusion: A short life lived in freedom and dignity is infinitely superior to a long life spent in luxurious bondage. Chandni's sacrifice is a timeless tribute to the human and animal spirit that refuses to bow to slavery.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Contrasting the safe slavery of the hut with spiritual decay 1.5 Marks
The open field as the symbol of dignity, courage, and self-determination 1.5 Marks
Coherent concluding philosophical judgment 1 Mark

Allegorical Competency / HOTS
How can the story of Chandni be interpreted as an allegory for freedom struggles against imperial or oppressive rule?

Written by Dr. Zakir Husain (who served as the President of India and was an eminent educator and freedom fighter), the story serves as a poignant political and moral allegory:

  • Abbu Khan's Enclosure: Represents colonial or paternalistic rulers who claim they provide infrastructure and security ('good grass and care') to justify keeping nations enslaved.
  • The Wolf: Represents the ruthless tyranny and brutality of oppressive forces.
  • Chandni's Heroic Stand: Represents freedom fighters who refuse to barter their soul for safety. Even when facing overwhelming force, they choose martyrdom over subjugation, proving that the love of liberty is invincible.
