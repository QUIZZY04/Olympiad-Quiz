P1

The Squirrel

Honeycomb (Poem) • Poet: Mildred Bowers Armstrong — A playful observation of the lively, nimble creature in the garden

📜 Complete Poem Text & Stanzas

He wore a question mark for tail, An overcoat of gray, He sat up straight to eat a nut. He liked to tease and play, And if we ran around his tree, He went the other way.
Key Poetic Devices:
  • Metaphor: 'He wore a question mark for tail' — The curled shape of the squirrel's bushy tail is directly compared to the punctuation mark '?'. Also 'overcoat of gray' compares its thick grey fur to a woollen winter coat.
  • Personification: The poet addresses the squirrel as 'He' and attributes conscious human emotions like teasing and playing: 'He liked to tease and play'.
  • Rhyme Scheme: abcbdb (gray / play / way rhyme together in lines 2, 4, and 6).
  • Imagery: Vivid visual imagery of a squirrel sitting upright on its hind legs nibbling a nut.
Chapter Summary & Core Theme
In this charming six-line poem, Mildred Bowers Armstrong captures the mischievous spirit and agility of a common garden squirrel. The poet describes the squirrel's tail curving elegantly upwards like a punctuation question mark, and its loose gray fur resembling an overcoat. Perched upright on its hind legs eating a nut, the squirrel displays an inquisitive, frolicsome nature. It enjoys teasing human observers, darting instantly around to the opposite side of the tree trunk whenever anyone approaches to play or get a closer look.
🎯 Central Theme: Delight in nature's small, energetic creatures and their playful, elusive interactions with humans.
✍️ Working with the Poem
Working with the Poem — Q1
Why does the poet say the squirrel "wore a question mark for tail"? Draw a squirrel, or find a picture of a squirrel sitting on the ground. How would you describe its tail?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. Why the poet says so: When a squirrel sits upright on its hind legs to nibble a nut, its long, bushy tail arches upward and curls gracefully at the tip, bearing an uncanny physical resemblance to the punctuation symbol of a question mark (?).
2. Description of the tail: The squirrel's tail is exceptionally bushy, soft, feather-light, and curved. It arches gracefully over its back like an ornamental plume or a fluffy curved feather duster, balancing the creature as it scampers across slender branches.

Working with the Poem — Q2
Do we usually say that an animal 'wears' a coat? What do we say? (Think: Does an animal wear a coat? Consult a dictionary if you like, and find out how 'wear' is used in different ways.)
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
No, we do not usually say that an animal 'wears' a coat.
  • In standard English, we say that an animal has a natural coat of fur, hair, or feathers, or is covered with fur.
  • Poetic license and usage: The poet uses the verb 'wore' figuratively (poetic license) to personify the squirrel, portraying its loose, wrinkled gray skin and dense fur as though it had deliberately put on an oversized human overcoat.
  • Other usages of 'wear':
    • To have on one's body (clothes, glasses, a smile: She wore a pleasant smile).
    • To damage or erode through friction or time (The steps were worn away by centuries of footsteps).
    • To hold one's hair in a particular style (He wears his hair long).

Working with the Poem — Q3
"He liked to tease and play." Who is teasing whom? How?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. Who is teasing whom: The squirrel is playfully teasing the poet and the children (or vice versa, in a mutual game of tag between the human observers and the wild creature).
2. How the teasing happens: Whenever the human observers try to run around the tree to approach the squirrel and watch it closely, the clever creature instantly scampers around the tree trunk to the opposite side, disappearing from their line of sight. It refuses to let anyone catch it, turning every human step into a delightful game of hide-and-seek.

Extract-Based Comprehension (CBSE Pattern)
Read the following extract and answer the questions that follow:
"He wore a question mark for tail, An overcoat of gray, He sat up straight to eat a nut."
(a) Identify the figure of speech in the first line.
(b) What was the squirrel doing while sitting straight?
(c) What does the 'overcoat of gray' suggest about the squirrel's appearance?

(a) The figure of speech is a Metaphor (the shape of the tail is directly equated to a question mark without using 'like' or 'as').

(b) The squirrel was sitting upright on its haunches holding and cracking a nut with its front paws to eat it.

(c) It suggests that the squirrel's natural grey fur was so dense, loose, and protective that it looked like a cozy tailored woollen overcoat.

HOTS / Analytical Question
What qualities of nature does the poet highlight through the brief depiction of a squirrel?

Mildred Bowers Armstrong highlights that joy in nature does not require grand mountains or ferocious predators; immense delight resides in small, ordinary garden moments. Nature is playful, innocent, non-threatening, and elusive. The squirrel's quick reflexes also showcase nature's innate instinct for self-preservation combined with harmless mischief.
