P5

Trees

Honeycomb (Poem) • Poet: Shirley Bauer — A heartfelt catalog of the manifold blessings that trees bestow upon living creatures

📜 Complete Poem Text & Stanzas

Trees are for birds. Trees are for children. Trees are to make tree houses in. Trees are to swing swings on. Trees are for the wind to blow through. Trees are to hide behind in ‘Hide and Seek.’ Trees are to have tea parties under. Trees are for kites to get caught in. Trees are to make cool shade in summer. Trees are to make no shade in winter. Trees are for apples to grow on, and pears; Trees are to chop down and call, “TIMBER-R-R!” Trees make mothers say, “What a lovely picture to paint!” Trees make fathers say, “What a lot of leaves to rake this fall!”
Key Poetic Devices:
  • Anaphora / Catalog Verse: Repeated phrasing 'Trees are for...' / 'Trees are to...' listing the diverse roles of trees in simple, rhythmic cadence.
  • Contrast: Summer shade vs winter sunlight; mothers' artistic admiration ('lovely picture to paint') vs fathers' pragmatic domestic labor ('leaves to rake this fall').
  • Onomatopoeia: The lumberjack's shout 'TIMBER-R-R!' evoking the crash of felled wood.
Chapter Summary & Core Theme
Shirley Bauer's poem 'Trees' presents an affectionate catalog of how trees enrich the lives of everyone in the ecosystem. For wildlife, trees provide nesting sanctuaries for birds and channels for refreshing breezes. For children, trees are playgrounds for building treehouses, hanging rope swings, playing hide-and-seek, and accidentally catching kites. For adults, they offer idyllic picnic shade in scorching summers, bare branches allowing warm winter sun, sweet fruits like apples and pears, timber for building homes, inspiration for artists, and a yearly carpet of autumn leaves to rake.
🎯 Central Theme: The ecological, domestic, and recreational importance of trees to birds, children, and humans across seasons.
✍️ Working with the Poem
Working with the Poem — Q1
What are the games or human activities which use trees, or in which trees also participate?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
The poem highlights several lively games and human activities involving trees:
  • For Children:
    • Building cozy wooden treehouses among branches.
    • Tying rope swings on sturdy branches to swing back and forth.
    • Playing the game of 'Hide and Seek' by ducking behind broad tree trunks.
    • Flying kites (where trees often humorously 'catch' them).
  • For Adults & Families:
    • Hosting outdoor afternoon tea parties under the cool canopy.
    • Resting in the refreshing shade during hot summer afternoons.
    • Harvesting delicious seasonal fruits like apples and pears.
    • Chopping timber for building and heating.
    • Painting scenic landscapes (mothers) and raking dry fallen leaves in autumn (fathers).

Children's games: swings, hide-and-seek, treehouses, kite flying 1.5 Marks
Adult activities: tea parties, summer shade, harvest, painting, raking 1.5 Marks
Working with the Poem — Q2 (i & ii)
(i) "Trees are to make no shade in winter." What does this mean? (Can you see why trees are helpful even in winter?)
(ii) "Trees are for apples to grow on, or pears." Do you agree that one purpose of a tree is to have fruit on it? Or do you think this line is humorous?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
(i) Meaning of "make no shade in winter":
During harsh, chilly winters, deciduous trees shed all their leaves. Because the branches are bare, they do not block the sun's gentle rays. Sunlight filters freely through the leafless branches, allowing people to bask outdoors in the comforting warmth of the winter sun. Thus, by shedding leaves and casting no deep shade, trees bestow warmth when it is needed most.

(ii) Purpose of fruit / Humorous angle:
Biologically, fruit trees produce fruit to encase seeds and ensure natural reproduction. However, from a child's human-centric view, the poet humorously and delightfully asserts that nature exists specifically for human delight—trees grow sweet, juicy apples and pears as if presenting tasty treats ready for children to pick and enjoy.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
(i) Deciduous leaf-fall allowing warm winter sunlight to filter through 1.5 Marks
(ii) Dual perspective: biological seed dispersal vs poetic human delight 1.5 Marks
Working with the Poem — Q3
Compare the contrasting reactions of mothers and fathers towards trees as presented in the final two lines of the poem.
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
The final two lines juxtapose two delightful, contrasting adult perspectives:
  • The Mother's Aesthetic Perspective: The mother views the tree through an artistic, sentimental lens, admiring its majestic foliage, colors, and serene beauty: "What a lovely picture to paint!"
  • The Father's Pragmatic / Labor Perspective: The father views the tree through practical household chores, groaning at the heavy yard work awaiting him as autumn winds scatter thousands of dead leaves: "What a lot of leaves to rake this fall!"
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Mother's artistic and aesthetic appreciation 1 Mark
Father's practical realization of autumn maintenance work 1 Mark
💡 Competency-Based & HOTS Questions (CBSE 2026-27 Pattern) Analytical Thinking
Environmental Competency / HOTS
In an era of rapid deforestation and climate change, why is Shirley Bauer's poem more relevant today than ever?

Bauer's poem reminds us that trees are not merely economic timber ('Trees are to chop down and call TIMBER!'); they are living sanctuaries underpinning all terrestrial life. They regulate local climate (cooling summer heat), sustain avian biodiversity, enrich soil, foster human social connection (family tea parties), and provide irreplaceable psychological peace. Preserving trees is essential for both environmental survival and child wellbeing.
