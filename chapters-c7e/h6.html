Expert Detectives

Honeycomb (Prose) • Author: Sharada Dwivedi — Ten-year-old Maya and seven-year-old Nishad investigate an enigmatic recluse

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
Ten-year-old Maya and her seven-year-old brother Nishad (nicknamed 'Seven' because his name corresponds to the seventh musical note 'Ni') are intrigued by Mr. Nath, a reclusive tenant living in their mother's clinic building. Maya's vivid imagination, fueled by detective fiction, convinces her that Mr. Nath is a dangerous criminal on the run who hides stolen millions in his room trunk and has burn scars from a police shootout. However, gentle Nishad observes that Mr. Nath is thin, gaunt, lonely, and visibly malnourished. When their marble rolls into Mr. Nath's room on a rainy monsoon holiday, Nishad visits him, offers him a bar of chocolate, and questions Ramesh, the restaurant boy who delivers his daily meals. While Maya compiles a dramatic 'Fact Sheet' detailing her criminal theories, Nishad firmly refuses to judge Mr. Nath unfairly, declaring that he will befriend the lonely man regardless of Maya's theories.
🎯 Central Theme: Prejudice and imagination versus empathy and open-minded observation in human judgment.
📖 Intext Comprehension Checks
Comprehension Check — Page 89 (Q1)
What did Nishad give Mr. Nath? Why?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution

Nishad gave Mr. Nath a bar of chocolate. When Nishad peered into Mr. Nath's room to retrieve his marble, he was deeply moved by the man's gaunt, starved, and sickly appearance. Believing that Mr. Nath was weak from hunger, Nishad offered him chocolate as a gesture of childlike warmth and sympathy.

Working with the Text — Q1
What does Nishad find out about Mr. Nath from Ramesh? List the facts.
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
Nishad learned the following facts from Ramesh, who brings food from the restaurant:
  • Regular Meals: Mr. Nath takes two simple, identical meals every day (morning and evening): two chapatis, dal, and a vegetable.
  • Tea Habit: He drinks two cups of tea daily: one in the morning and one in the afternoon.
  • Cash Payments & Tips: He always pays promptly in cash and tips Ramesh generously.
  • Sunday Visitor: Every Sunday afternoon, a tall, fair, stout man wearing spectacles visits him, and the visitor talks incessantly while Mr. Nath listens quietly.

Working with the Text — Q2
Why does Maya think Mr. Nath is a crook? Who does she say the Sunday visitor is?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. Why Maya thinks he is a crook: Maya points out that Mr. Nath does not work, stays cooped up in his room all day, has no friends, receives no letters, and everyone in the building is scared of him. She believes the scars on his face are burn marks from a police shootout, and that he keeps stolen loot locked in his trunk.
2. Identity of the Sunday Visitor: Maya theorizes that the Sunday visitor is Mr. Nath's accomplice in crime, who holds the rest of the stolen treasure and visits periodically to give Mr. Nath his share of the loot.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Maya's suspicions: reclusive lifestyle, no work, facial scars as police shootout marks 1.5 Marks
Sunday visitor identified as criminal accomplice delivering loot 1.5 Marks
Working with the Text — Q3
Does Nishad agree with Maya about Mr. Nath? How does he feel about him?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
No, Nishad completely disagrees with Maya.
He feels deep empathy and sorrow for Mr. Nath. He argues that even if Mr. Nath is poor or reclusive, it does not prove he is a criminal. Nishad believes Mr. Nath is simply a lonely, ailing human being who lacks friends, and he firmly resolves to visit him and become his friend.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Disagreement with Maya's criminal theories 1 Mark
Empathy for his loneliness and desire to offer friendship 1 Mark
💡 Competency-Based & HOTS Questions (CBSE 2026-27 Pattern) Analytical Thinking
Competency-Based Character Contrast
Contrast the analytical approaches of Maya and Nishad. What lesson does this teach us about prejudice?

Maya represents confirmation bias: she has formed a preconceived verdict and twists every neutral observation (reclusiveness, tipping, Sunday visitor) to fit her sensational detective fantasy. In contrast, Nishad approaches reality with an open heart and emotional intelligence, prioritizing basic human compassion over unfounded criminal labels. The story warns that jumping to conclusions without evidence breeds cruel prejudice.
