The Bear Story

An Alien Hand • Author: Axel Munthe — A delightful misunderstanding in the forest between a pet bear and a wild bear

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
A wealthy lady lived in a manor house on the border of a vast forest. Years ago, her foresters found a starving, helpless bear cub and brought it home. The lady raised it on bottled milk, bread, porridge, potatoes, cabbages, and turnips. The cub grew into a gigantic, powerful bear, but he was completely vegetarian, gentle, and amiable. He lived amicably with three dogs, children rode on his back, and he never harmed any creature. His only vice was a passion for apples and sweet beehives. Because he once tried to rob beehives and knocked down orchard trees, he was chained up when naughty and on Sunday afternoons when the lady crossed the dense forest to visit her married sister. One Sunday, halfway through the forest, the lady was startled to hear twigs snapping and saw a bear charging toward her! Outraged that her pet had disobeyed and broken his chain, the lady sternly scolded him and hit him on the nose with her umbrella until the parasol broke in two. The bear turned around and ambled back. When the lady reached home, she began furiously scolding her pet bear chained in his kennel. The old cook indignantly defended him, revealing that the pet bear had remained chained like an angel all afternoon—the bear she had scolded and beaten in the woods was a fierce, wild forest bear!
🎯 Central Theme: The power of affectionate training over brute animal instinct, and the comical errors caused by mistaken identity.
📖 Intext Comprehension Checks
Comprehension Check & Text Q1
Where did the lady find the bear cub? How did she bring it up?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
1. Where found: The bear cub was found by the lady's foresters in the deep woods, half-dead with hunger and crying helplessly beside its dead mother.
2. How brought up: The kind lady brought it up like a human infant, feeding it bottled milk initially. Later, the bear was fed a wholesome vegetarian diet of bread, porridge, boiled potatoes, cabbages, and turnips.

Comprehension Check & Text Q2
The bear grew up, but he was a most amiable bear. Give three examples of his friendly nature.
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
Three clear examples demonstrating the bear's friendly and amiable nature:
  1. Friendship with Animals: He lived in perfect peace with the three dogs of the house, allowing them to pull his ears, tease him, and sleep comfortably between his paws.
  2. Affection toward Children: Children from the neighboring village rode safely on his broad back and romped around him without fear.
  3. Gentleness with Farm Livestock: He watched the cattle, ponies, and poultry in the estate grazing peacefully without ever showing any predatory aggression or desire to harm them.

Peaceful relationship with house dogs 1 Mark
Allowing village children to ride his back safely 1 Mark

Comprehension Check & Text Q3
What did the bear eat? What was he forbidden to do?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
  • What he ate: Bread, porridge, potatoes, cabbages, turnips, and fresh fruits (especially sweet apples). He was never given meat.
  • What he was forbidden to do: He was strictly forbidden from climbing apple trees in the orchard to shake down ripe fruit, and forbidden from tampering with the beehives in the garden.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Vegetarian diet: porridge, bread, vegetables, apples 1 Mark
Forbidden to climb apple trees and tamper with beehives 1 Mark
Comprehension Check & Text Q4
When was the bear tied up with a chain? Why?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
The bear was chained up:
  1. At night, as is customary with large watchdogs.
  2. Whenever he committed mischief (such as getting stung by bees after meddling with beehives).
  3. On Sunday afternoons when the lady walked across the deep forest to visit her married sister, because it was dangerous for a large bear to wander through forest paths where other people might panic.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
At night and as punishment for mischief (beehives) 1 Mark
Sunday afternoons during lady's walk to her sister's house 1 Mark
Comprehension Check & Text Q5
What happened one Sunday when the lady was going to her sister's house?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
Halfway through the dense forest, the lady heard twigs crackling behind her. Looking back, she was shocked to see a bear trotting toward her at full speed. Assuming it was her pet who had broken his chain, she was furious at his disobedience. When the bear reached her, she ordered him home, but seeing him sniff her dress and ignore her commands, she struck him hard on the nose with her heavy umbrella until it snapped. The bewildered bear stopped, turned around, and walked back.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Encountering a charging bear in the dense forest 1 Mark
Assuming it was her disobedient pet bear 1 Mark
Hitting it on the nose with an umbrella, forcing it to retreat 1 Mark
Comprehension Check & Text Q6
What did the cook say to the lady when she came back home?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
When the lady returned home and began furiously scolding her chained bear, the cook ran out indignantly and defended the pet. The cook revealed that the bear had behaved like an angel all afternoon: he had not moved an inch from his kennel, had remained securely chained the entire time, and had been sitting patiently watching the gate, eagerly awaiting his mistress's return.

Irony & Humor Analysis / HOTS
What makes the climax of 'The Bear Story' extraordinarily humorous and ironic?

The humor and irony arise from the supreme contrast between human indignation and wild reality:

  • The lady believed she was disciplining a naughty pet for sneaking out, treating a dangerous apex predator like an errand boy and smacking it with an everyday parasol.
  • The wild forest bear was so utterly confounded and stunned by an angry human woman shouting commands and assaulting its sensitive nose with a broken umbrella that it retreated in polite bewilderment.
  • The realization that she had struck a ferocious wild bear in the middle of a desolate forest—and survived purely due to her audacity—transforms an everyday scolding into hilarious dramatic irony.
