An Alien Hand

An Alien Hand • Author: Jayant Narlikar — A Martian civilization's underground survival and the arrival of NASA's Viking lander

Chapter Summary & Core Theme
Jayant Narlikar's gripping science-fiction story is set on Mars. Centuries ago, Mars enjoyed a thriving surface ecosystem, but gradual changes in the Sun's radiation caused planetary climate catastrophe: rivers froze, air thinned, and flora and fauna perished. The Martian civilization survived by moving underground, relying on advanced solar-powered life-support machinery. Young Tilloo lived in these underground colonies and was forbidden from accessing the surface without a special spacesuit. One day, Tilloo stole his father's security card, slipped through the secret passage, but was caught by security sensors. His father gently explained why surface survival was lethal. Meanwhile, Martian space scanners detected two alien spacecraft orbiting their planet and touching down on the surface. The Martian underground council met in tense conference. While President and Defense Chief Number 1 urged restraint, Number 2 (a scientist) recommended passive non-interference so the aliens would not discover Martian underground existence. On the surface, a mechanical arm ('an alien hand') emerged from the Viking lander and began digging soil samples. Watching the live monitor in the control room, fascinated young Tilloo saw a bright red button on the control console and pressed it! A loud siren shrieked: the mechanical hand ceased working, its electric motor jammed. Back on Earth at NASA, scientists broadcast a live conference announcing the sudden malfunction, subsequently beaming radio signals to repair the mechanical arm, while concluding that the Martian soil held no signs of life.
🎯 Central Theme: Scientific curiosity, planetary survival, vigilance, and the mutual mystery of extraterrestrial exploration.

Comprehension Check — Page 73 (Q1)
What did Number 1 and Number 2 suggest when the alien spacecraft landed on Mars?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
  • Number 1 (In charge of Defense): Suggested waiting until the spacecraft landed. Although they had the military capability to blast the spacecraft out of orbit, destroying it would be foolish because it would reveal Martian existence to the universe. He recommended letting it touch down safely.
  • Number 2 (Scientist): Strongly recommended passive non-interference and silent observation. He pointed out that the spacecraft was unmanned (carrying only mechanical instruments). Damaging it would alert the alien planet of life on Mars; letting it collect soil in silence was the safest strategy.

1. What the mechanical hand did: The metallic arm emerged slowly from the spacecraft, touched the surface of Mars, and began scooping up red Martian soil samples into a specimen container for biological analysis.
2. Why it stopped functioning: While watching the live monitor in the control room, young Tilloo was overpowered by an irresistible urge to press the prominent red button on the control console. His father pulled him away, but the button had been activated. The jamming command neutralised the electric motor inside the mechanical hand, freezing it instantly.

Exercises — Q1
If you had to choose between the views of Number 1 and Number 2 regarding dealing with alien spacecraft, whose advice would you support and why?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
I would support the advice of Number 2 (the scientist) for the following reasons:
  1. Preventing Interplanetary Conflict: Blasting or destroying an exploratory spacecraft could be interpreted as an act of interplanetary warfare, provoking retaliatory strikes from an advanced alien civilization.
  2. Strategic Invisibility: Since the craft was unmanned, doing nothing maintained the Martian civilization's supreme advantage—their subterranean existence remained completely undetectable.
  3. Scientific Prudence: Passive surveillance yields valuable data about the aliens' technology without exposing one's own vulnerabilities.

Science & Space Exploration Competency / HOTS
What is the historical NASA context behind Jayant Narlikar's story 'An Alien Hand', and why did the story reverse the conventional perspective of alien invasion?

Jayant Narlikar's brilliant story provides two profound insights:

  • Historical Context: The story was inspired by NASA's historic Viking 1 and Viking 2 missions (1976), which landed robotic probes on Mars to test Martian soil for organic compounds and microbes.
  • Reversing the Perspective: In standard science fiction, 'aliens' are terrifying invaders attacking Earth. Narlikar reverses the camera angle: to the Martians, we humans are the strange 'aliens' invading their peaceful world with robotic arms! This cultivates empathy, cosmic humility, and critical scientific introspection.
