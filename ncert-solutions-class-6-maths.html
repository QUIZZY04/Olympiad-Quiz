Chapter 1: Patterns in Mathematics

NCERT Ganita Prakash (Class 6) — Number Sequences, Triangular & Square Numbers, Matchstick Patterns, Palindromes & Rule Formulation | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Mathematical Concepts & Formulas
  • Patterns: A sequence of numbers, geometric shapes, or designs that follow a definite, repeatable rule.
  • Triangular Numbers: Numbers that can be arranged in the shape of an equilateral triangle: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28, 36, ... The n-th triangular number is given by $T_n = \frac{n(n + 1)}{2}$.
  • Square Numbers: Numbers formed by multiplying an integer by itself: $1^2=1, 2^2=4, 3^2=9, 4^2=16, 5^2=25, ...$ ($S_n = n^2$). Sum of two consecutive triangular numbers gives a square number: $T_{n-1} + T_n = n^2$.
  • Sum of First n Odd Numbers: The sum of the first $n$ odd consecutive numbers equals $n^2$: $1 + 3 + 5 + \dots + (2n - 1) = n^2$.
  • Matchstick Patterns & Algebraic Rules: If each additional unit requires a fixed number of sticks $d$, the rule for $n$ shapes is given by $d \cdot n + c$. For example, a row of $n$ squares requires $3n + 1$ matchsticks; a row of $n$ triangles requires $2n + 1$ matchsticks.

Each step adds the next natural number (+2, +3, +4, +5).

- Next term: 21 + 7 = 28
- Next term: 28 + 8 = 36
The next three numbers are 21, 28, and 36.
(ii) Mathematical Name:
These numbers are called Triangular Numbers because dots representing them can be arranged into an equilateral triangle.
(iii) Sum of 3ʳᵈ and 4ᵗʰ Triangular Numbers:
3ʳᵈ triangular number = 6; 4ᵗʰ triangular number = 10.
Sum = 6 + 10 = 16 = 4².
Since 16 = 4 × 4, the sum is indeed a perfect square.

In a newly constructed school auditorium, the chairs in the rows are arranged in a triangular stage pattern. Row 1 has 3 chairs, Row 2 has 5 chairs, Row 3 has 7 chairs, and so on.

NCERT Ganita Prakash (Class 6) — Points, Lines, Rays, Angle Classifications, Clock Revolutions, Protractor Measurement, Linear Pairs & Intersecting Lines | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Geometric Concepts & Angle Classifications
  • Point, Line, Ray & Line Segment: A point marks a fixed position with zero dimensions. A line extends infinitely in both directions ($overleftrightarrow{AB}$). A line segment has two fixed endpoints ($overline{AB}$). A ray has one initial point and extends indefinitely in one direction ($\vec{AB}$).
  • Angle Classifications:
    • Acute Angle: Greater than 0° and less than 90°.
    • Right Angle: Exactly equal to 90° ($\frac{1}{4}$ of a full revolution).
    • Obtuse Angle: Greater than 90° and less than 180°.
    • Straight Angle: Exactly equal to 180° ($\frac{1}{2}$ of a full revolution).
    • Reflex Angle: Greater than 180° and less than 360°.
    • Complete Angle: Exactly equal to 360° (1 full revolution).
  • Complementary & Supplementary Angles: Two angles are complementary if their sum is 90°. Two angles are supplementary if their sum is 180°.
  • Linear Pair & Vertically Opposite Angles: When two lines intersect, adjacent angles forming a straight line add to 180° (linear pair), and vertically opposite angles are equal.

Q8
Two angles form a linear pair. If one angle is 36° more than the other angle, find the measure of both angles.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Standard Step-by-Step Solution
Let the smaller angle be $x$.
Then the larger angle is $(x + 36^\circ)$.
Since they form a linear pair, their sum is $180^\circ$:
$x + (x + 36^\circ) = 180^\circ$
$2x + 36^\circ = 180^\circ \implies 2x = 180^\circ - 36^\circ = 144^\circ$

📌 Key Number Concepts & Place Value Systems
  • Indian System of Numeration: Commas after Hundreds (3 digits), then after every 2 digits: Crores, Ten Lakhs, Lakhs, Ten Thousands, Thousands, Hundreds, Tens, Ones. (e.g., $7,34,56,819$).

  • Roman Numerals: $I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100, D = 500, M = 1000$. Symbol cannot be repeated more than 3 times; $V, L, D$ are never repeated or subtracted.
  • Magic Squares: A square grid where numbers in each row, column, and both diagonals sum to the exact same constant (Magic Constant). For a $3 \times 3$ grid using numbers $1$ to $9$, Magic Sum $= 15$.

📌 Key Number Theory & Divisibility Rules
  • Factors & Multiples: A factor of a number divides it exactly without a remainder. A multiple is obtained by multiplying the number by integers.
  • Prime & Composite: A prime number has exactly two distinct factors: 1 and itself (e.g., $2, 3, 5, 7, 11$). 2 is the only even prime. 1 is neither prime nor composite.
  • Co-prime Numbers: Two numbers having only 1 as a common factor are called co-primes (e.g., 4 and 9).
  • Divisibility Tests:
    • By 2, 5, 10: Last digit is even (for 2); 0 or 5 (for 5); 0 (for 10).
    • By 3 & 9: Sum of digits is divisible by 3 (or 9).
    • By 4 & 8: Last 2 digits divisible by 4; last 3 digits divisible by 8.
    • By 6: Divisible by both 2 and 3.
    • By 11: Difference between the sum of digits at odd places and sum of digits at even places is 0 or divisible by 11.
  • HCF & LCM: Highest Common Factor (HCF) is the greatest common divisor. Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) is the smallest common positive multiple. For two numbers $a$ and $b$: $\text{HCF}(a, b) \times \text{LCM}(a, b) = a \times b$.

📌 Key Mensuration Formulas & Units
  • Perimeter: The distance covered along the boundary forming a closed figure when you go around the figure once. Units: cm, m, km.

  • Area: The amount of surface enclosed by a closed figure. Units: sq cm ($\text{cm}^2$), sq m ($\text{m}^2$).
  • Area Formulas:
    • Rectangle: $\text{Area} = \text{Length} \times \text{Breadth} = l \times b$.
    • Square: $\text{Area} = \text{Side} \times \text{Side} = s^2$.
  • Estimating Area on Squared Paper: Count fully covered squares as 1, more than half as 1, exactly half as $\frac{1}{2}$, and ignore less than half.

📌 Key Fraction Concepts & Operation Rules
  • Fraction: A number representing a part of a whole. In $\frac{a}{b}$, $a$ is numerator and $b$ is denominator ($b \neq 0$).
  • Types of Fractions:
    • Proper Fraction: Numerator < Denominator (value < 1), e.g., $\frac{3}{7}$.
    • Improper Fraction: Numerator $\ge$ Denominator (value $\ge$ 1), e.g., $\frac{11}{4}$.
    • Mixed Fraction: Combination of a whole number and a proper fraction, e.g., $2\frac{3}{4} = \frac{2 \times 4 + 3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$.
  • Equivalent Fractions: Fractions having identical values, obtained by multiplying or dividing numerator and denominator by the same non-zero integer.
  • Simplest Form: A fraction $\frac{a}{b}$ is in simplest form if $\text{HCF}(a, b) = 1$.
  • Addition & Subtraction: Convert unlike fractions to like fractions using the LCM of their denominators before adding or subtracting.

📌 Key Symmetry Concepts & Properties

📌 Key Integer Concepts & Sign Rules
  • Integers ($mathbb{Z}$): The collection of positive natural numbers, zero, and negative natural numbers: $dots, -4, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, dots$
  • Ordering on the Number Line: Numbers to the right are always greater than numbers to the left. For any positive integer $a$, $-a < 0 < a$. As negative numbers move further left, their value decreases (e.g., $-10 < -2$).
  • Absolute Value: The numerical value of an integer regardless of its sign, denoted by $|x|$. $|-5| = 5$, $|5| = 5$.
  • Rules for Addition & Subtraction:
    • Same sign: Add absolute values and assign the common sign: $(+5) + (+3) = +8$; $(-5) + (-3) = -8$.
    • Different signs: Subtract smaller absolute value from larger and assign the sign of the larger absolute value: $(+7) + (-4) = +3$; $(-9) + (+2) = -7$.
    • Subtraction: To subtract an integer, add its additive inverse: $a - b = a + (-b)$.

NCERT Ganita Prakash (Class 6) — Simplest Form of Ratios, Proportions, Extreme & Mean Terms & The Unitary Method | CBSE 2026-27

📌 Key Ratio, Proportion & Unitary Rules
  • Ratio: Comparing two quantities of the same kind and with the same unit by division. Denoted by $a : b$ (read as "$a$ is to $b$"). Ratio has no units.
  • Simplest Form of Ratio: Divide both terms by their HCF: $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{a \div \text{HCF}}{b \div \text{HCF}}$.
  • Proportion: If two ratios are equal, they are in proportion: $a : b :: c : d$ (or $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$).
    • $a$ and $d$ are called extreme terms (extremes).
    • $b$ and $c$ are called middle terms (means).
    • Rule: $\text{Product of Extremes} = \text{Product of Means} \implies a \times d = b \times c$.
  • Unitary Method: The method in which first we find the value of one unit by division, and then the value of the required number of units by multiplication.

📌 Key Concepts & Formulas
