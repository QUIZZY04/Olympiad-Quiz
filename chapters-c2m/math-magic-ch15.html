Chapter 15: How Many Ponytails?

Counting Hairstyles, Shoe Colors, Drinking Water & Fruit Charts | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Collecting, organizing, and recording survey data from classmates.
  • Categorizing hairstyles (1 ponytail, 2 ponytails, short hair).
  • Reading tally tables and pictographs for favorite fruits and shoe colors.
  • Comparing data categories: identifying most, least, and finding differences.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (16 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Textbook Exercise • Hairstyle Count
Look at the children in a classroom. Count and record:
• Number of children with two ponytails = 8
• Number of children with one ponytail = 6
• Number of children with short hair = 14
Which hairstyle is the most common?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Answer:
• Two ponytails = 8
• One ponytail = 6
• Short hair = 14
• Since 14 is the highest number, short hair is the most common hairstyle.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Short hair identified 1 M
Comparison of 14 > 8 > 6 explained 1 M
Q2 Textbook Exercise • Shoe Colors Survey
Look at the shoes worn by children in Class 2:
• Black shoes = 16 children
• Brown shoes = 9 children
• White sports shoes = 7 children
(a) Which colour shoe is worn by the most children?
(b) How many more children wear black shoes than brown shoes?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) Black shoes are worn by the most children (16).
(b) Difference = 16 - 9 = 7 more children.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
(a) Black shoes 1 M
(b) Difference of 7 calculated 1 M
Q3 Textbook Exercise • Water Drinking Chart
A survey asked children how many glasses of water they drink every day:
• 1 glass: 3 children
• 2 glasses: 6 children
• 3 glasses: 12 children
• 4 or more glasses: 9 children
(a) How many children drink 3 glasses of water?
(b) How many children drink less than 3 glasses of water?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) Number of children drinking 3 glasses = 12 children.
(b) Less than 3 glasses = 1 glass (3) + 2 glasses (6) = 3 + 6 = 9 children.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
(a) 12 children 1 M
(b) 9 children calculated 1 M
Q4 Textbook Table • Transportation Survey
Children come to school by different means:
• By School Bus: 18
• By Bicycle: 7
• On Foot (Walking): 10
• By Auto-rickshaw: 5
(a) Which is the most popular way of coming to school?
(b) What is the total number of children surveyed?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) The most popular way is School Bus (18 children).
(b) Total children = 18 + 7 + 10 + 5 = 25 + 15 = 40 children.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
(a) School Bus identified 1 M
(b) Total 40 children calculated 1 M
Q5 Pictograph Reading
In a fruit chart, each smiley face 🙂 represents 1 child. If Banana has 4 smileys and Mango has 7 smileys, how many children like Banana and how many like Mango?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Calculation:
• Banana = 4 smileys × 1 = 4 children.
• Mango = 7 smileys × 1 = 7 children.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Banana: 4 children 1 M
Mango: 7 children 1 M
Q6 Comparative Deduction
In a pet survey: 15 children have dogs, 9 have cats, and 4 have birds. How many more children have dogs than cats?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
• Dogs = 15
• Cats = 9
• Difference = 15 - 9 = 6 more children.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Subtraction 15 - 9 shown 1 M
6 more children correct 1 M
Q7 Tally Marks Introduction
What is the purpose of drawing tally marks or tick marks when counting items in a survey?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Explanation:
• Tally marks allow us to record data one by one as people answer, without needing to erase or rewrite numbers.
• They group counts into batches of 5, making final addition simple and error-free.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Quick recording without erasing explained 1 M
Batching of counts explained 1 M

✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
• January = 4
• February = 2
• July = 5

CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
November identified 1 M
Count 6 verified as highest 1 M
Q9 Ice-Cream Flavor Comparison
A survey of 25 children found: 10 like Vanilla, 8 like Chocolate, and the rest like Strawberry. How many children like Strawberry?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
• Vanilla + Chocolate = 10 + 8 = 18 children.
• Total children = 25.
• Strawberry lovers = 25 - 18 = 7 children.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
10 + 8 = 18 calculated 1 M
25 - 18 = 7 calculated 1 M
Q10 Table Data Interpretation
State one true fact and one question you can ask by looking at the Hairstyle data table (8 two ponytails, 6 one ponytail, 14 short hair).
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Answer:
• True Fact: More children have short hair than all ponytail styles combined (14 short hair vs 8 + 6 = 14 ponytail styles, making them equal).
• Question: How many girls in the class have two ponytails?
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Valid true fact from data 1 M
Valid analytical question 1 M
Q11 True or False
State True or False:
(a) A data table helps us see and compare information quickly.
(b) In a survey of 20 children, the total of all categories can add up to 50.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answers:
(a) True. Organizing data into tables makes comparison simple.
(b) False. If only 20 children are surveyed and each answers once, the sum of categories cannot exceed 20.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
(a) True 1 M
(b) False with survey logic explanation 1 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Class 2 Favorite Fruit Survey

The class teacher conducted a survey on children's favorite fruits: 12 children like Mango, 8 like Banana, 5 like Apple, and 7 like Orange.

Analytical Task: Which fruit is liked the most? Which fruit is liked the least? How many more children like Mango than Banana? How many children participated in the survey?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Most liked fruit: Mango (12 children).
2. Least liked fruit: Apple (5 children).
3. Difference (Mango vs Banana): 12 - 8 = 4 more children.
4. Total children: 12 + 8 + 5 + 7 = 32 children.
Rubric: 1 M for most & least liked + 1 M for difference (4) + 1 M for total participants (32)
