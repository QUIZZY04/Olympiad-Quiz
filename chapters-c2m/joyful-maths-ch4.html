Chapter 4: Shadow Story

2D Outlines, Footprints & Tracing Shadows | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Tracing everyday objects (leaves, coins, bottle caps) to discover 2D outlines.
  • Observing shadows cast by the sun at morning, noon, and evening.
  • Associating shapes with animal footprints in nature.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (2 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Tracing Activity • Object Outlines
What 2D shape will you get if you trace around the base of:
(a) A ₹5 coin?
(b) An eraser?
(c) A matchbox?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
• (a) ₹5 Coin: A Circle.
• (b) Eraser: A Rectangle.
• (c) Matchbox: A Rectangle.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Circle, Rectangle, Rectangle identified 3 M
Q2 Animal Footprints • Matching
Match the animal with its footprint: Duck, Elephant, Dog. Who has webbed feet and who has large round footprints?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answer:
• Duck: Webbed feet with three connected toes for swimming.
• Elephant: Large, heavy, round footprint.
• Dog: Small paw print with claw marks and pads.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate animal-footprint description 3 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Sundial Tree Experiment

Tanvi measures the shadow of a small school tree at 8:00 AM, 12:00 PM, and 4:30 PM.

Analytical Task: At which time was the tree's shadow the shortest? Why does shadow length change during the day?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. The shadow was shortest at 12:00 PM (Noon).
2. Because at noon the sun is directly overhead, causing shadows to be short. In early morning and late afternoon, the sun is lower in the sky, creating longer shadows.
Rubric: 1.5 M for 12:00 PM + 1.5 M for sun position explanation
