Chapter 6: Foods We Eat

Diversity of Indian Cuisines: Regional Staples (Rice, Wheat, Tapioca, Mustard Oil), Age-based Diets | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Staple foods depend on geography and climate: Rice in Kerala, West Bengal; Wheat and Bajra in Punjab, Rajasthan.
  • Dietary needs change with age: Soft liquids (milk, dal water) for infants; light, soft food for the elderly; energetic meals for growing children.
  • Unique regional delicacies: Tapioca and fish in Kerala, Sarson ka Saag and Makki ki Roti in Punjab, Momos in Sikkim.
  • Family food sharing: Eating meals together and showing empathy when food is scarce.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (17 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Age-Specific Diets
Why can a 6-month-old baby and a 75-year-old grandmother not eat the same crispy fried corn cob (bhutta) as a 10-year-old child?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
• 6-Month-Old Baby: Has no teeth and an underdeveloped digestive system, surviving primarily on mother's milk and liquid porridge.
• 75-Year-Old Grandmother: Often has missing or weak teeth and slower digestion, needing soft foods like dal-khichdi, mashed rotis, and stewed vegetables rather than hard corn.
• 10-Year-Old Child: Has strong, healthy teeth and high energy needs, easily chewing and digesting crunchy roasted corn.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Contrast of infant dental and digestive stage 1 Mark
Contrast of elderly dental and metabolic needs 1 Mark
Growing child's chewing capacity 1 Mark
Q2 Regional Cuisines
Why do people living in Kerala eat a lot of rice and coconut, while people in Rajasthan eat bajra and lentils?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Dietary habits depend on local agricultural harvests and climate:
• Kerala: High coastal rainfall and fertile soils allow abundant paddy and coconut cultivation; hence, coconut curries and rice are staple foods.
• Rajasthan: A dry, arid desert climate with scarce rainfall allows drought-resistant millets like Bajra (pearl millet) and hardy pulses to thrive; hence, Bajre ki Roti and Dal-Baati are staples.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Connection between climate/soil and crop growth in Kerala 1.5 Marks
Connection between desert climate and drought-resistant crops in Rajasthan 1.5 Marks
Q3 Food Empathy
What should you do if you notice that a classmate has not brought a lunch box to school?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
We should gently and politely invite our classmate to sit with us and share our meal, making sure they feel respected and cared for without feeling embarrassed.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Sharing food with dignity and empathy 2 Marks
Q4 Nutrition Across Ages • Foods We Eat
In Vipul's family, why could his Dadi (grandmother) not eat hard bhutta (roasted corn cob)? What food was prepared for her instead?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Diet & Age:
• Dadi is very old and has lost most of her natural teeth, so she cannot chew hard, chewy roasted corn.
• For Dadi, her mother prepared soft dal with crushed chapatis soaked in it (dal-baati or dal-roti) with a little sugar, making it soft and easy to digest.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Absence of teeth/weak gums explained 1.5 M
Soft soaked chapati in dal identified 1.5 M

Q6 Regional Foods • Geographical Differences
Why do people in coastal Kerala eat tapioca and coconut curry, while people in Punjab eat wheat and mustard greens?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Geographical Food Habits:
• People eat what grows most abundantly and easily in their local climate and soil. Kerala's humid tropical coast is ideal for coconut trees and tapioca, while Punjab's fertile alluvial plains produce rich harvests of wheat and mustard.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Climate and soil availability as key food determinant 1.5 M
Specific Kerala vs Punjab crop linkages explained 1.5 M
Q7 Food Etiquette • Hunger vs Boredom
How do you know that you are genuinely hungry? Describe the feeling.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Physical Hunger:
• When genuinely hungry, our stomach feels empty and makes rumbling sounds, our body energy drops, and any simple healthy food (like plain roti and dal) looks delicious and satisfying.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Physical hunger sensations accurately described 2.0 M
Q8 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'Foods We Eat', describe how students can observe this environmental phenomenon in their own neighborhood. What three things should they note down in their field notebook?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Neighborhood Field Observation:
1. Direct Observation: Students can observe the living ecosystem and community activities related to Foods We Eat during a morning nature walk.
2. Three Field Log Entries:
• (a) Physical appearance, color, and behavior of the species or object.
• (b) Interactions with surroundings (e.g., sources of water, food, shade, or shelter).
• (c) Human impact or community care practices observed.
3. Conclusion: Recording firsthand observations builds sharp environmental awareness and respect for biodiversity.

Observation methodology clearly described 1.5 Marks
Three concrete notebook parameters listed 1.5 Marks
Q9 Scientific Classification • Sorting Activity
Based on the concepts in 'Foods We Eat', classify the following into two distinct categories and explain the reason for your classification:
(Item A, Item B, Item C, Item D).
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Reasoning:
• Category 1 (Natural / Sustainable): Elements directly provided by nature that replenish naturally and cause zero pollution.
• Category 2 (Man-Made / Processed): Products manufactured by human tools that require energy and produce waste.
• Reason: Understanding the difference helps us reduce our ecological footprint and practice green habits.

Q10 Discussion & Value Reflection • Care & Compassion
How can young children practice kindness and empathy towards living beings or community helpers, as taught in 'Foods We Eat'? Give two practical daily examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Practicing Everyday Kindness:
1. Daily Habit 1: Keep a shallow earthen bowl of clean fresh water on the balcony or under a tree for thirsty birds and small animals during warm afternoons.
2. Daily Habit 2: Speak respectfully to community sanitation workers and delivery persons, offering them clean drinking water and never littering on roads.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Two practical, student-led empathy habits 2.0 Marks
Alignment with chapter moral values 1.0 Mark
Q11 Environmental Riddle • Who Am I?
Environmental Riddle from 'Foods We Eat': I have no color of my own, yet I make the world green. You drink me when thirsty, and clouds carry me across mountains. Who am I? Mention two ways we can save me from wastage.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solving the Nature Riddle:
• Answer: WATER (जल / पानी).
• Two Ways to Save Water:
1. Turn off the running tap while brushing teeth or soaping hands (saves up to 6 liters per minute).
2. Reuse kitchen vegetable-wash water to water garden pots and indoor plants.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct identity (Water) 1.0 Mark
Two practical conservation methods 2.0 Marks
Q12 Hands-on Project & Craft • Eco-Friendly Living
Describe a simple hands-on craft or reuse activity related to 'Foods We Eat' that can be done using discarded household materials. What are the steps?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Eco-Craft Project: Upcycled Planter / Feeder:
• Materials Needed: Discarded clean coconut shell or plastic bottle, jute string, potting soil, vegetable seeds.
• Steps:
1. With adult help, make 2 small holes on opposite sides for hanging.
2. Fill with garden soil mixed with dry compost leaves.
3. Plant fenugreek (methi) or coriander seeds and sprinkle water gently.
• Learning Outcome: Demonstrates the 'Reuse' principle of waste management while greening home spaces.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear list of recycled materials 1.0 Mark
Step-by-step procedure explained 1.5 Marks
Environmental learning objective stated 0.5 Mark

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Nutritional Diversity: Millets as Climate-Resilient Superfoods

Under the National Millet Mission, schools are introducing traditional millets (Ragi, Jowar, Bajra) into the weekly school menu alongside wheat and rice.

Analytical Task: Why are millets nutritionally superior to polished white rice, and why are they environmentally friendly crops to grow?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Nutritional Advantages: Millets are rich in calcium, iron, and dietary fiber with a low glycemic index, preventing childhood anemia, bone weakness, and blood sugar spikes.
Environmental Benefits: Unlike water-guzzling crops like paddy (which require thousands of liters of flooded water per kilogram), millets are hardy, drought-tolerant grains that grow in dry, low-fertility soils with minimal rainwater and zero chemical fertilizers.
Rubric: Nutritional superiority (calcium/iron/fiber) and climate resilience (low water/drought resistance) (4 Marks).
