Chapter 10: This World of Things

Exploring Everyday Materials: Wood, Clay, Metal, Glass, Fabric, and Their Distinct Properties | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Classification of materials based on origin: Natural (wood, cotton, stone) and human-made (plastic, glass, nylon).
  • Properties of materials: Hard vs soft, rough vs smooth, opaque vs transparent, waterproof vs absorbent.
  • Matching properties to practical uses: Why umbrellas use waterproof fabric and cooking pots use metals.
  • The lifecycle of materials: Mining, crafting, usage, wear and tear, and disposal.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (17 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Material Properties & Suitability
Why are cooking frying pans made of metal (like iron or aluminum) while their handles are made of wood or Bakelite plastic?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
This design relies on heat conduction properties:
1. Metal Body (Pan): Metals are excellent conductors of heat. They absorb heat rapidly from the stove flame and distribute it evenly across the base to cook food quickly.
2. Wood / Bakelite Handle: Wood and Bakelite are bad conductors of heat (thermal insulators). They remain cool even when the pan is piping hot, allowing us to hold the pan safely without burning our hands.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Explanation of metal as a good thermal conductor 1.5 Marks
Explanation of wood/Bakelite as an insulator for burn safety 1.5 Marks
Q2 Transparency & Light Properties
Distinguish between transparent, translucent, and opaque materials with two practical household examples of each.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
• Transparent: Materials that allow light to pass through completely, enabling us to see clearly across them (e.g., Clear window glass, pure drinking water).
• Translucent: Materials that allow only partial light to pass through, creating blurred or hazy vision (e.g., Butter paper, frosted bathroom glass).
• Opaque: Materials that block light completely, casting a dark shadow and preventing vision across them (e.g., Wooden door, brick wall, metal plate).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear physical definitions of transparent, translucent, and opaque 1.5 Marks
Accurate household examples for each category 1.5 Marks
Q3 Absorbency & Waterproofing
Why do we wear cotton clothes in hot summers and synthetic raincoats in monsoons?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Cotton is a natural porous fiber that absorbs body sweat easily and allows air circulation to keep our skin cool through evaporation. Synthetic raincoat fabrics (like rubberized nylon or PVC) are completely waterproof and non-porous, preventing rain droplets from soaking through to our clothes.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Cotton's sweat absorption and breathability 1 Mark
Raincoat fabric's waterproof barrier property 1 Mark

Q5 Float or Sink Experiment • Density
In an experiment testing buoyancy in a bucket of water:
(a) Why does an iron needle sink, but a hollow iron bowl float?
(b) Why does a dry sponge float initially but sink after some time?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Buoyancy Physics:
(a) Iron Needle vs Bowl:
• An iron needle is solid and dense, displacing very little water, so it sinks.
• A hollow iron bowl contains trapped air inside its curved shape, which makes its overall weight lighter than the large volume of water it pushes aside, allowing it to float.

(b) Sponge Behavior:
• A dry sponge has tiny open pores filled with air, making it very light so it floats.
• As water fills all the pores, displacing the air, the sponge becomes heavy and eventually sinks.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Water displacement and air volume explained for bowl 1.5 M
Pore air displacement explained for wet sponge 1.5 M

Q7 Natural vs Synthetic Materials
Differentiate between natural materials and synthetic (man-made) materials with one example of each.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Definitions & Examples:
• Natural Material: Obtained directly from nature (plants, animals, earth) without chemical modification (e.g., Wood, Cotton, Wool, Clay).
• Synthetic Material: Created artificially by humans in factories through chemical processing (e.g., Plastic, Nylon, Polythene).

Q13 Health & Hygiene • Good Habits
Why must we wash our hands with soap before eating and after using the toilet? Describe the correct 6-step method.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Importance & Method:
• Washing with soap removes invisible disease-causing germs and bacteria.
• Steps: Wet hands, apply soap, rub palms together, wash backs of hands, clean between fingers and under nails, rinse thoroughly under running water, and dry with a clean towel.

Q17 Review Questions • True or False
State True or False:
(a) Plants take in carbon dioxide and give out oxygen during daytime.
(b) Rainwater harvesting means collecting and storing rainwater for future use.
(c) Burning dry leaves and plastic in open air keeps the environment healthy.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Answers:
• (a) True: Photosynthesis produces oxygen.
• (b) True: Rainwater harvesting replenishes groundwater.
• (c) False: Burning releases toxic smoke and causes severe air pollution. Dry leaves should be composted into organic manure.

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Sustainable Material Substitution: Moving Beyond Single-Use Plastics

A town council notices that drainage pipes are repeatedly clogged with thin single-use plastic grocery bags, plastic straws, and thermocol plates, causing artificial floods during monsoon rain showers.

Analytical Task: Apply your scientific understanding of material properties to recommend biodegradable, eco-friendly substitutes for plastic bags, cups, and plates.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Plastic is synthetic, waterproof, and non-biodegradable; it chokes drains and persists in nature for hundreds of years. We can substitute plastic with materials that match functional needs while being completely compostable: (1) Jute and Cotton: Strong, reusable woven fibers for shopping bags. (2) Dried Areca Nut Palm Leaf (Pattal): Naturally pressed sturdy, leak-proof plates for community feasts that biodegrade into rich soil compost within weeks. (3) Terracotta Kulhads: Porous earthenware cups that impart a pleasant earthy aroma and break down safely back into natural clay soil. (4) Paper or Bamboo Straws: Organic plant fibers replacing plastic drinking tubes.
Rubric: Critique of non-biodegradability, specific sustainable replacements (jute, areca palm leaves, clay kulhads), soil biodegradability (4 Marks).
