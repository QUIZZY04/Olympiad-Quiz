Chapter 4: Getting to Know Plants

Observing Plant Anatomy, Root Systems, Leaf Venation, Photosynthesis, and Habitats | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Classification of plants based on stem structure: Herbs, shrubs, trees, climbers, and creepers.
  • Root functions: Anchoring the plant firmly in soil and absorbing water and minerals.
  • Leaves as food factories: Chlorophyll, sunlight, air, and water combining in photosynthesis.
  • Leaf patterns: Reticulate venation (net-like) and parallel venation with distinct leaf margins.
Reticulate: Net-like (Peepal, Mango) • Parallel: Straight lines (Grass, Banana) Chlorophyll traps sunlight for photosynthesis

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (18 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Plant Classification
How do climbers and creepers differ from trees? Give two common examples of each.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Plants differ based on the strength and thickness of their stems:
1. Trees: Have thick, hard, woody brown trunks that stand erect independently (e.g., Banyan tree, Neem tree).
2. Climbers: Have weak, green stems that cannot stand upright alone. They produce tendrils to climb up walls, fences, or other trees (e.g., Money plant, Pea plant, Grapevine).
3. Creepers: Have soft, fragile stems and heavy fruits that creep along the horizontal surface of the ground (e.g., Pumpkin, Watermelon).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Contrast of stem characteristics (woody vs weak) 1.5 Marks
Accurate botanical examples for trees, climbers, and creepers 1.5 Marks
Q2 Leaf Structure & Function
Why are green leaves known as the 'food factories' or 'kitchen' of a plant?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Leaves are called the food factories because they prepare nourishment for the entire plant through the process of photosynthesis:
• Leaves contain a green pigment called chlorophyll that traps light energy from the sun.
• They absorb carbon dioxide gas from surrounding air through tiny pores called stomata.
• Roots absorb water and minerals from soil and send them up the stem into the leaves.
• Using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide, the leaf manufactures sugar (glucose) and releases life-giving oxygen into the air.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Explanation of photosynthesis and chlorophyll 1.5 Marks
Role of stomata, water, and oxygen release 1.5 Marks
Q3 Botanical Observation
Explain the difference between reticulate venation and parallel venation with leaf rubbings.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
• Reticulate Venation: The veins branch out to create a web-like or criss-cross network across the entire leaf blade (e.g., Rose, Peepal, China Rose).
• Parallel Venation: The veins run side-by-side in parallel straight lines from the base to the leaf tip (e.g., Grass, Wheat, Maize, Banana).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear visual description of both vein patterns 1.5 Marks
Accurate leaf examples 0.5 Marks
Q4 Botanical Diversity • Herbs, Shrubs, and Trees
Classify plants into Herbs, Shrubs, and Trees based on their stems:
(a) What type of stem does a Herb have? Give an example.
(b) What type of stem does a Shrub have? Give an example.
(c) What type of stem does a Tree have? Give an example.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Plant Classification:
• (a) Herb: Has a green, soft, delicate stem with no wood (e.g., Mint, Coriander, Wheat).
• (b) Shrub: Medium-sized plant with a hard, woody stem branching out from near the base, resembling a bush (e.g., Rose, Hibiscus, Lemon).
• (c) Tree: Tall plant with a single, thick, hard, dark woody stem called the trunk (e.g., Neem, Banyan, Mango).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct stem trait and example for Herb 1.0 M
Correct stem trait and example for Shrub 1.0 M
Correct stem trait and example for Tree 1.0 M
Q5 Leaf Art Activity • Texture Rubbing
Explain how to take a leaf rubbing using a wax crayon as described in the textbook activity.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Leaf Rubbing:
1. Place a fresh leaf with prominent veins on a flat wooden table with its rough underside facing upward.
2. Lay a thin sheet of white paper flat over the leaf.
3. Take a wax crayon on its side and gently rub it back and forth over the paper.
4. The raised pattern of the leaf's central midrib and side veins will magically appear on the paper.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Positioning rough underside facing up 1.0 M
Technique of rubbing crayon gently on side 1.0 M
Accurate result of leaf veins pattern emerging 1.0 M

Q7 Photosynthesis Basics • Plant Nutrition
What four essential ingredients do green plants need to prepare their own food?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Four Essentials for Plants:
1. Sunlight (from the Sun)
2. Water (absorbed by roots from soil)
3. Carbon Dioxide (absorbed from air through leaf pores/stomata)
4. Chlorophyll (the green pigment present in leaves).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
All four essentials accurately named 2.0 M

Q11 Environmental Riddle • Who Am I?
Environmental Riddle from 'Getting to Know Plants': I have no color of my own, yet I make the world green. You drink me when thirsty, and clouds carry me across mountains. Who am I? Mention two ways we can save me from wastage.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solving the Nature Riddle:
• Answer: WATER (जल / पानी).
• Two Ways to Save Water:
1. Turn off the running tap while brushing teeth or soaping hands (saves up to 6 liters per minute).
2. Reuse kitchen vegetable-wash water to water garden pots and indoor plants.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct identity (Water) 1.0 Mark
Two practical conservation methods 2.0 Marks
Q12 Hands-on Project & Craft • Eco-Friendly Living
Describe a simple hands-on craft or reuse activity related to 'Getting to Know Plants' that can be done using discarded household materials. What are the steps?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Eco-Craft Project: Upcycled Planter / Feeder:
• Materials Needed: Discarded clean coconut shell or plastic bottle, jute string, potting soil, vegetable seeds.
• Steps:
1. With adult help, make 2 small holes on opposite sides for hanging.
2. Fill with garden soil mixed with dry compost leaves.
3. Plant fenugreek (methi) or coriander seeds and sprinkle water gently.
• Learning Outcome: Demonstrates the 'Reuse' principle of waste management while greening home spaces.

Clear list of recycled materials 1.0 Mark
Step-by-step procedure explained 1.5 Marks
Environmental learning objective stated 0.5 Mark

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Investigative Botanical Inquiry: Sunlight and Plant Growth

Simran placed two identical potted mint plants on her balcony. She watered both with 100 ml of water daily, but placed an opaque cardboard box over Plant A while Plant B was exposed to normal sunlight. After 10 days, Plant A's leaves turned pale yellow and wilted, whereas Plant B was thriving and deep green.

Analytical Task: What scientific conclusion can Simran draw from this controlled observation, and how does chlorophyll depend on light?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Simran can conclude that sunlight is an indispensable requirement for photosynthesis and healthy plant life. Plant A could not produce chlorophyll in total darkness; without chlorophyll, it could not synthesize food from water and air, resulting in energy starvation, loss of green pigmentation (etiolation), and eventual wilting. Plant B, having regular sunlight, synthesized food continuously and grew vibrant leaves.
Rubric: Hypothesis testing, role of sunlight in chlorophyll formation, controlled experiment interpretation (4 Marks).
