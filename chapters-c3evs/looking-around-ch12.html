Chapter 12: Work We Do

Community Helpers and Occupations: Doctors, Sweepers, Postmen, Traffic Police, and Child Rights | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Community helpers and essential public occupations: Doctors, nurses, teachers, sweepers, postmen, shopkeepers, and traffic police.
  • Interdependence: No community can function without the collective contributions of all workers.
  • The importance of education: Every child has the fundamental right to go to school instead of being forced into child labor (tea stalls/brick kilns).
  • Treating community sanitary workers with respect and dignity.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (18 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Community Roles
Describe the crucial role played by sanitation workers (safai karamcharis) in keeping towns healthy.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Sanitation workers wake up before dawn to sweep dusty public streets, clear choked municipal drains, and collect household garbage. If they stopped working for even two days, streets would overflow with rotting waste, flies and mosquitoes would multiply uncontrollably, and dangerous disease epidemics like dengue, malaria, and cholera would spread.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Specific duties (sweeping, drain cleaning, garbage clearing) 1.5 Marks
Public health consequence if work stops (epidemics/rot) 1.5 Marks
Q2 Child Rights and Child Labor
Why is child labor illegal, and what should be done if you see a young child washing dishes at a tea stall instead of being in school?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Child labor is illegal because every child has the fundamental constitutional right to free education, recreation, and healthy physical development. Working in dangerous, exhausting conditions ruins children's futures. We should inform school authorities, local child welfare committees, or call the Childline Helpline (1098) so the child can be rescued and enrolled in a school.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Fundamental right to education and hazards of child labor 1.5 Marks
Actionable reporting via Childline 1098 or authorities 1.5 Marks
Q3 Dignity of Labor
What does the phrase 'Dignity of Labor' mean in our daily society?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
It means that every honest type of work—whether done by a surgeon, an engineer, a farmer, or a street sweeper—is equally respectable and worthy of honor. No honest work is low or inferior.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Equal respect for all honest occupations without discrimination 2 Marks
Q4 Daily Labor • Deepali's Routine
In 'Work We Do', Deepali cannot go to school. Why? Describe her daily chores.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Deepali's Routine & Responsibilities:
• Deepali is the eldest sister. Her father sells vegetables and her mother washes utensils in homes. Deepali has to stay home to cook food for the family, clean the house, and look after her three younger brothers and sisters.
• Even though she loves learning and books, her heavy family responsibilities forced her to leave school.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Eldest child carrying sibling care and chores 1.5 M
Parental poverty causing drop out of school 1.5 M
Q5 Bustling Neighborhood Scene • Community Workers
Look at the picture of the bustling city square in the chapter. Name four community workers working there and their occupations.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Neighborhood Workers:
1. Postwoman / Postman: Sorting and delivering mail.
2. Traffic Police Officer: Directing vehicles and pedestrians at the intersection.
3. Doctor and Nurse: Treating patients at the neighborhood dispensary.
4. Cobbler (Mochi): Sitting under a roadside tree polishing and stitching worn shoes.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Four distinct community workers with accurate roles 4.0 M
Q6 Right to Education • Child Labor vs School
Why should every child below 14 years be in school rather than working for money?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Child Rights & Education:
• Childhood is the time for growth, play, and learning. Under the Indian Constitution (Right to Education Act), every child has the fundamental right to free, quality education. Child labor deprives children of their future and harms their physical and mental health.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Right to Education Act cited 1.5 M
Physical, emotional, and future empowerment rationale 1.5 M
Q7 Clock Routine • Daily Time Management
At what time do you usually wake up, leave for school, and go to sleep?

✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Personal Time Schedule:
• Wake up: 6:15 AM
• Leave for school: 7:30 AM
• Go to sleep: 9:30 PM.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Realistic, healthy daily timetable values 2.0 M
Q8 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'Work We Do', describe how students can observe this environmental phenomenon in their own neighborhood. What three things should they note down in their field notebook?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Neighborhood Field Observation:
1. Direct Observation: Students can observe the living ecosystem and community activities related to Work We Do during a morning nature walk.
2. Three Field Log Entries:
• (a) Physical appearance, color, and behavior of the species or object.
• (b) Interactions with surroundings (e.g., sources of water, food, shade, or shelter).
• (c) Human impact or community care practices observed.
3. Conclusion: Recording firsthand observations builds sharp environmental awareness and respect for biodiversity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Observation methodology clearly described 1.5 Marks
Three concrete notebook parameters listed 1.5 Marks
Q9 Scientific Classification • Sorting Activity
Based on the concepts in 'Work We Do', classify the following into two distinct categories and explain the reason for your classification:
(Item A, Item B, Item C, Item D).
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification & Reasoning:
• Category 1 (Natural / Sustainable): Elements directly provided by nature that replenish naturally and cause zero pollution.
• Category 2 (Man-Made / Processed): Products manufactured by human tools that require energy and produce waste.
• Reason: Understanding the difference helps us reduce our ecological footprint and practice green habits.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct grouping into two categories 1.5 Marks
Scientific explanation for classification 1.5 Marks
Q10 Discussion & Value Reflection • Care & Compassion
How can young children practice kindness and empathy towards living beings or community helpers, as taught in 'Work We Do'? Give two practical daily examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Practicing Everyday Kindness:
1. Daily Habit 1: Keep a shallow earthen bowl of clean fresh water on the balcony or under a tree for thirsty birds and small animals during warm afternoons.
2. Daily Habit 2: Speak respectfully to community sanitation workers and delivery persons, offering them clean drinking water and never littering on roads.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Two practical, student-led empathy habits 2.0 Marks
Alignment with chapter moral values 1.0 Mark
Q11 Environmental Riddle • Who Am I?
Environmental Riddle from 'Work We Do': I have no color of my own, yet I make the world green. You drink me when thirsty, and clouds carry me across mountains. Who am I? Mention two ways we can save me from wastage.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solving the Nature Riddle:
• Answer: WATER (जल / पानी).
• Two Ways to Save Water:
1. Turn off the running tap while brushing teeth or soaping hands (saves up to 6 liters per minute).
2. Reuse kitchen vegetable-wash water to water garden pots and indoor plants.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct identity (Water) 1.0 Mark
Two practical conservation methods 2.0 Marks
Q12 Hands-on Project & Craft • Eco-Friendly Living
Describe a simple hands-on craft or reuse activity related to 'Work We Do' that can be done using discarded household materials. What are the steps?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Eco-Craft Project: Upcycled Planter / Feeder:
• Materials Needed: Discarded clean coconut shell or plastic bottle, jute string, potting soil, vegetable seeds.
• Steps:
1. With adult help, make 2 small holes on opposite sides for hanging.
2. Fill with garden soil mixed with dry compost leaves.
3. Plant fenugreek (methi) or coriander seeds and sprinkle water gently.
• Learning Outcome: Demonstrates the 'Reuse' principle of waste management while greening home spaces.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear list of recycled materials 1.0 Mark
Step-by-step procedure explained 1.5 Marks
Environmental learning objective stated 0.5 Mark

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Community Interdependence: When Public Services Halt

During a torrential rainstorm, municipal sanitation workers, electricity linemen, and water pipeline mechanics work knee-deep in water all night to restore power and unblock sewage drains.

Analytical Task: How does this event illustrate that our daily comforts depend entirely on unseen, hardworking community helpers?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Modern life is an interconnected web where no individual is self-sufficient. While we rest safely indoors, linemen risk electrical shocks to restore power and municipal workers brave filthy drains to prevent urban flooding. Recognizing this interdependence shatters social prejudices and reminds us to treat every community worker with profound gratitude, courtesy, and fair living wages.
Rubric: Appreciation of community interdependence, frontline workers' sacrifice, moral dignity of labor (4 Marks).
