Chapter 3: Celebrating Festivals

Cultural Diversity, Traditional Delicacies, Festive Music, and Unified Community Celebrations | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Festivals of India: Harvest festivals (Bihu, Pongal, Baisakhi), religious festivals (Diwali, Eid, Christmas, Gurpurab).
  • Special festive foods and sweets prepared with seasonal agricultural harvests.
  • Eco-friendly celebrations: Rangoli with flower petals, earthen diyas, and avoiding firecrackers.
  • The underlying spirit of festivals: Brotherhood, generosity, forgiveness, and community sharing.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (17 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Festival Diversity & Significance
What are harvest festivals? Name two harvest festivals celebrated in different parts of India and their special dishes.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Harvest festivals are joyous community celebrations held when standing agricultural crops ripen and farmers gather their hard-earned yield.

Examples:
1. Pongal (Tamil Nadu): Celebrated to honor the Sun God and cattle after harvesting paddy and sugarcane. The special dish is 'Sweet Pongal' made of newly harvested rice, jaggery, milk, and cashew nuts boiled in an earthen pot.
2. Bihu / Magh Bihu (Assam): Marks the end of the winter harvesting season. People construct thatched bamboo huts (Bhelaghar) and prepare traditional rice cakes called 'Pitha' and 'Laru' (sesame/coconut sweets).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Precise definition of harvest festivals 1 Mark
Identification of two regional harvest festivals with authentic festive foods 2 Marks
Q2 Eco-Friendly Celebrations
Why should we avoid bursting chemical firecrackers during festivals? List three harmful effects.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Bursting chemical firecrackers causes severe harm to our living environment:
1. Dangerous Air Pollution: Firecrackers release toxic fumes (sulfur dioxide, lead, barium) that trigger asthma, coughs, and breathing distress in infants and elderly individuals.
2. Noise Pollution: The loud explosions terrify street animals, pet dogs, and nesting birds, causing severe trauma and hearing loss.
3. Toxic Chemical Litter: Burnt cracker residues leave hazardous chemical dust on streets that pollutes groundwater when washed by rain.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Air pollution and human health impact 1 Mark
Noise impact on birds and domestic animals 1 Mark
Hazardous solid waste generation 1 Mark
Q3 Community Harmony
What is the noble message behind community feasts such as the 'Langar' organized on Gurpurab?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The message behind 'Langar' is absolute human equality, brotherhood, and selfless service. People of all castes, religions, rich and poor, sit together on the floor in a straight row (pangat) and partake of the same freshly cooked vegetarian food, teaching us that everyone is equal in the eyes of God.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Concept of equality and selfless community service 1.5 Marks
Inclusivity without discrimination 0.5 Marks

Q5 Harvest Festivals • Agricultural Connection
(a) What is a 'Harvest Festival'?
(b) Name two harvest festivals celebrated in different parts of India.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Harvest Festivals Explained:
(a) Definition:
• A harvest festival is a joyous celebration held by farmers and communities when agricultural crops (like wheat, rice, or sugarcane) are ripe and harvested. It is a thanksgiving to nature, sun, and rain.

(b) Two Indian Harvest Festivals:
• 1. Baisakhi (celebrated in Punjab for wheat harvest).
• 2. Pongal / Onam (celebrated in Tamil Nadu and Kerala for rice harvest).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear agricultural definition of harvest celebration 1.5 M
Two authentic Indian harvest festivals named with states 1.5 M
Q6 Eco-Friendly Celebrations • Environmental Protection
Why should we avoid bursting noisy, smoky firecrackers during festivals? Give two environmental reasons.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Eco-Friendly Festive Reasons:
1. Air Pollution & Health: Firecracker smoke releases toxic gases and heavy chemicals that cause severe breathing problems, asthma attacks, and eye irritation for young children and elderly citizens.
2. Noise Pollution & Animals: The deafening blasts terrify street animals, pet dogs, and birds, who have far more sensitive hearing than humans.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Air pollution/health impacts explained 1.0 M
Noise pollution and animal distress explained 1.0 M

Q8 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'Celebrating Festivals', describe how students can observe this environmental phenomenon in their own neighborhood. What three things should they note down in their field notebook?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Neighborhood Field Observation:

Q10 Discussion & Value Reflection • Care & Compassion
How can young children practice kindness and empathy towards living beings or community helpers, as taught in 'Celebrating Festivals'? Give two practical daily examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Practicing Everyday Kindness:
1. Daily Habit 1: Keep a shallow earthen bowl of clean fresh water on the balcony or under a tree for thirsty birds and small animals during warm afternoons.
2. Daily Habit 2: Speak respectfully to community sanitation workers and delivery persons, offering them clean drinking water and never littering on roads.

Q11 Environmental Riddle • Who Am I?
Environmental Riddle from 'Celebrating Festivals': I have no color of my own, yet I make the world green. You drink me when thirsty, and clouds carry me across mountains. Who am I? Mention two ways we can save me from wastage.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solving the Nature Riddle:
• Answer: WATER (जल / पानी).
• Two Ways to Save Water:
1. Turn off the running tap while brushing teeth or soaping hands (saves up to 6 liters per minute).
2. Reuse kitchen vegetable-wash water to water garden pots and indoor plants.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct identity (Water) 1.0 Mark
Two practical conservation methods 2.0 Marks
Q12 Hands-on Project & Craft • Eco-Friendly Living
Describe a simple hands-on craft or reuse activity related to 'Celebrating Festivals' that can be done using discarded household materials. What are the steps?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Eco-Craft Project: Upcycled Planter / Feeder:
• Materials Needed: Discarded clean coconut shell or plastic bottle, jute string, potting soil, vegetable seeds.
• Steps:
1. With adult help, make 2 small holes on opposite sides for hanging.
2. Fill with garden soil mixed with dry compost leaves.
3. Plant fenugreek (methi) or coriander seeds and sprinkle water gently.
• Learning Outcome: Demonstrates the 'Reuse' principle of waste management while greening home spaces.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear list of recycled materials 1.0 Mark
Step-by-step procedure explained 1.5 Marks
Environmental learning objective stated 0.5 Mark

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Environmental Citizenship During Festive Celebrations

During Diwali and Ganesh Utsav, vast numbers of plaster-of-paris idols painted with chemical dyes and plastic decorations are submerged into local rivers and lakes, poisoning aquatic life and rendering water unfit for drinking.

Analytical Task: As responsible young environmental ambassadors, how can students encourage their neighborhoods to adopt 100% green and eco-friendly festive celebrations?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Students can launch school awareness campaigns to inspire green celebrations: (1) Advocate for idols made exclusively of unbaked local river clay (shadu mati) embedded with seeds, which dissolve harmlessly in home garden pots to sprout plants. (2) Replace synthetic chemical colors with natural flower petals (marigold, rose), turmeric, and spinach paste for rangolis. (3) Light traditional clay diyas filled with mustard or sesame oil instead of single-use plastic electric strings. (4) Celebrate with music, storytelling, and sharing sweets with underprivileged children rather than spending money on crackers.
Rubric: Clay seed-idols, natural bio-rangolis, water protection awareness, and empathetic community sharing (4 Marks).
