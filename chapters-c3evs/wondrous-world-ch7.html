Chapter 7: Water — A Precious Gift

Water Cycle, Sources of Fresh Water, Scarcity, Conservation Techniques, and Rainwater Harvesting | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Natural freshwater sources: Rain, rivers, lakes, ponds, springs, and underground wells.
  • States of water: Solid (ice), liquid (water), and gas (water vapor) in the continuous water cycle.
  • Everyday water wastage vs water conservation: Turning off running taps, bucket baths.
  • Traditional and modern rainwater harvesting: Rooftop collection, baolis (stepwells), and percolation pits.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (17 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Water Science & The Water Cycle
Describe the three key stages of the water cycle: Evaporation, Condensation, and Precipitation.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The continuous movement of water on Earth is governed by three primary stages:
1. Evaporation: Solar heat warms up surface water in oceans, lakes, and rivers, converting liquid water into invisible water vapor that rises into the atmosphere.
2. Condensation: As water vapor climbs higher into cooler atmospheric regions, it cools down and condenses into microscopic water droplets that cluster together to form fluffy clouds.
3. Precipitation: When clouds grow heavy and cool, the accumulated water droplets fall back to the ground as rain, hail, or snow, replenishing water bodies.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear physical explanation of evaporation and condensation 2 Marks
Description of precipitation completing the hydrological cycle 1 Mark
Q2 Water Conservation Habits
Suggest four practical habits that can save liters of clean water in a school every day.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Fix Leaking Taps: Report dripping taps to school maintenance immediately; a slow drip wastes over 20 liters of water daily.
2. Turn Off Taps While Soap-Washing: Shut the tap while lathering hands with soap or brushing teeth instead of letting water run continuously.
3. Repurpose Leftover Drinking Water: Empty unused water from student water bottles into flowerbeds or potted plants rather than dumping it down the sink drain.
4. Install Aerators / Push-Taps: Use push-button taps that automatically stop flow after 5 seconds in restrooms.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Four distinct, actionable water-saving habits 2 Marks
Practical applicability to school routines 1 Mark
Q3 Rainwater Harvesting
What is rooftop rainwater harvesting and why is it essential in cities?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Rooftop rainwater harvesting is the technique of capturing clean rainwater that falls on building roofs, passing it through sand-gravel filters, and diverting it into underground storage tanks or percolation recharge pits.
It is essential because it recharges declining groundwater levels, prevents city waterlogging during heavy monsoon downpours, and provides fresh water during drought seasons.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Definition of rooftop rainwater collection and filtration 1 Mark
Benefits of groundwater recharge and flood prevention 1 Mark

Q5 Rainwater Harvesting • Conservation Technique
What is Rainwater Harvesting, and how does it help a community facing water shortage?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Rainwater Harvesting Explained:
• Rainwater Harvesting is the method of collecting and storing pure rainwater that falls on rooftops and open grounds instead of letting it run off into dirty drains.
• Benefits: The collected water is filtered and stored in underground cisterns (tankas) for drinking, or channeled into recharge pits to raise the groundwater level for dry summer months.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear operational definition of collecting roof runoff 1.5 M
Groundwater recharge and summer storage benefits 1.5 M
Q6 Home Water Audit • Preventing Wastage
Suggest three practical ways in which a Class 3 student can save water at home every day.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Water-Saving Habits:
1. Turn off the tap while brushing teeth and use a mug of water for rinsing instead of running water.
2. Bathe using a bucket and mug instead of taking long overhead showers.
3. Reuse the wastewater from the kitchen water purifier (RO filter) to mop floors and water garden plants.

Three actionable, realistic daily water conservation habits 3.0 M

Q12 Hands-on Project & Craft • Eco-Friendly Living
Describe a simple hands-on craft or reuse activity related to 'Water — A Precious Gift' that can be done using discarded household materials. What are the steps?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Eco-Craft Project: Upcycled Planter / Feeder:
• Materials Needed: Discarded clean coconut shell or plastic bottle, jute string, potting soil, vegetable seeds.
• Steps:
1. With adult help, make 2 small holes on opposite sides for hanging.
2. Fill with garden soil mixed with dry compost leaves.
3. Plant fenugreek (methi) or coriander seeds and sprinkle water gently.
• Learning Outcome: Demonstrates the 'Reuse' principle of waste management while greening home spaces.

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Community Drought Mitigation: Reviving a Village Stepwell

A drought-prone village faced acute drinking water shortages during summers because tube wells had dried up. The village panchayat cleaned and restored an ancient 200-year-old stone stepwell (baoli) that had been abandoned and filled with plastic rubbish.

Analytical Task: Analyze why traditional water structures like stepwells (baolis) are scientifically superior to deep borewells in drought-prone areas.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Traditional stepwells (baolis) are architectural masterpieces of natural ecological engineering. Unlike deep borewells that recklessly extract fossil groundwater until aquifers collapse, stepwells rely on seasonal rainwater runoff and shallow seepage. The wide porous stone walls allow rainwater to infiltrate naturally, filter sediment through surrounding sandy soil layers, and maintain a perennial subterranean water pool without electricity or motors. Reviving baolis recharges local aquifers and provides community-controlled water resilience.
Rubric: Traditional water wisdom vs deep borehole depletion, ecological percolation, sustainable community water management (4 Marks).
