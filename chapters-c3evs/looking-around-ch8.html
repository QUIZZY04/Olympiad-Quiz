Chapter 8: Flying High

Avian Diversity: Beak Shapes, Feathers, Flying Habits, Claws, and Ecological Roles | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Diverse bird beaks adapted to food: Hooked beaks for meat (Eagle), curved beaks for cracking nuts (Parrot), needle beaks for nectar (Sunbird), flat beaks for filtering mud (Duck).
  • Feather functions: Flight feathers on wings, down feathers keeping the body warm, and tail feathers steering like a rudder.
  • Unique bird traits: The Owl can rotate its head almost fully backward; the Myna jerks its neck back and forth.
  • The role of scavenger birds (Vultures, Crows) as 'nature's sanitation squad' cleaning carcasses.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (17 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Beak Adaptations
Why do different birds have different shapes of beaks? Explain with three examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Different birds have different beak shapes because their beaks are specialized biological tools adapted to their specific diet:
1. Eagle / Vulture: Strong, sharp, hooked beaks to tear through tough meat and flesh.
2. Parrot: Hard, hooked curved beak to crack open tough nutshells and bite into hard green fruits.
3. Woodpecker: Long, straight, chisel-like beak to peck deep holes into tree bark to pull out hidden wood-boring insects.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Explanation of dietary adaptation principle 1.5 Marks
Three accurate bird-beak functional pairings 1.5 Marks
Q2 Feather Functions
What are the two main purposes of feathers on a bird's body?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Flight & Steering: Strong, broad flight feathers on wings and tail give aerodynamic lift and help steer direction in the air.
2. Thermal Insulation & Weatherproofing: Soft, fluffy down feathers close to the skin trap warm air to protect the bird from freezing cold and shed water droplets during rain.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Flight aerodynamic role 1.5 Marks
Thermal insulation and waterproofing role 1.5 Marks
Q3 Unique Avian Facts
Which bird can turn its head around backwards to see behind itself, and which bird is known for copying human speech?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The Owl can rotate its head around almost completely (up to 270 degrees) to observe behind itself, and the Parrot is famous for imitating and repeating human speech.

Q5 Bird Beaks • Adaptation to Diet
Match each bird's beak shape to the food it eats:
• Eagle/Vulture: [Short strong beak for cracking seeds, Hooked sharp beak for tearing meat, Long slender beak for sucking nectar, Flat broad beak for scooping mud]
• Sparrow/Pigeon: [Short strong beak for cracking seeds, Hooked sharp beak for tearing meat, Long slender beak for sucking nectar, Flat broad beak for scooping mud]

Q6 Bird Feathers • Flight and Warmth
What two essential functions do feathers perform for a bird?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Functions of Feathers:
1. Flight: Long, strong wing and tail feathers provide lift and balance, enabling birds to fly and steer.
2. Body Warmth & Waterproofing: Soft down feathers trap a layer of warm air against the body, keeping the bird warm during cold nights and shedding rainwater.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Flight mechanism explained 1.0 M
Thermal insulation/waterproofing explained 1.0 M
Q7 Owl's Unique Feature • Neck Rotation
What special ability does an owl have regarding its eyes and neck?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Owl's Anatomy:
• An owl cannot move its eyes inside its sockets, so nature has gifted it a flexible neck that can rotate up to 270 degrees backward to see all around silently in the dark.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Fixed eyes and 270-degree neck rotation explained 2.0 M
Q8 Observation Time • Animals and Habits
In Chapter 8 ('Flying High'), what animals did the characters observe, and how do they move from place to place?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Observations:
Animals move in diverse ways adapted to their bodies:

• Reptiles (Lizard, Snake): Crawl on walls or slither on the ground.

Q10 Discuss & Share • Our Senses
How do our five sense organs help us understand and navigate our daily environment?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Five Senses:
1. Eyes: Help us see shapes, colours, and identify hazards.
2. Ears: Help us hear traffic horns, spoken words, and alarm sounds.
3. Nose: Helps us smell food, flowers, and detect smoke or gas leaks.
4. Tongue: Helps us taste sweet, sour, salty, and bitter food.
5. Skin: Helps us feel touch, heat, cold, roughness, and pain.

Q11 Water Cycle & Weather • Rain and Clouds
Where does rainwater come from, and why do plants and trees look fresher and greener after a rain shower?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Rain and Plant Health:
• Rain comes from water evaporating from rivers, ponds, and seas, forming clouds in the sky that cool and condense into rain droplets.
• Rain washes off thick dust accumulated on leaves, unblocks stomata for breathing, and deeply soaks the roots with fresh mineral-rich moisture.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Water cycle / cloud formation 1.5 M
Why plants look greener after rain 1.5 M

Q14 Shelter & Houses • Diversity in Homes
Why are houses in heavy-rainfall regions like Assam built on bamboo stilts with sloping roofs?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Stilt Houses:
• Bamboo Stilts: Keep the house 10 to 12 feet above ground level to prevent floodwaters and wild animals from entering.
• Sloping Roofs: Allow torrential rainwater to slide off quickly without accumulating or causing leaks.

Q16 Web of Life • Food Chain
What is the 'Web of Life'? Draw a simple food chain showing how grass, deer, and lion are connected.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Web of Life & Food Chain:
• The Web of Life shows that every living organism is interconnected and depends on others for survival.
• Food Chain: Sunlight → Grass (Producer) → Deer (Herbivore) → Lion (Carnivore).
If grass disappears, deer starve; if deer disappear, lions cannot survive.

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Avian Conservation: The Vanishing House Sparrow

Old grandparents recall that when they were children, dozens of chirping house sparrows nested inside ceiling ventilators and ate dropped rice grains in courtyards. Today, house sparrows have largely disappeared from modern glass-fronted concrete cities.

Analytical Task: Analyze the environmental causes behind the decline of urban sparrows and recommend two citizen actions to bring them back.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Causes of Sparrow Decline: (1) Modern matchbox-style concrete houses lack open ventilators and eaves, leaving no safe nesting crevices. (2) Heavy chemical gardening pesticides kill small green caterpillars that mother sparrows feed to newly hatched chicks. (3) Excessive concrete paving eliminates open soil patches where birds dust-bathe and forage for grass seeds.

Citizen Actions: Install artificial wooden nest boxes under balcony roofs, keep a regular terracotta water and millet feeder, and plant native green shrubs in gardens.
Rubric: Urban architecture impact, pesticide elimination of insect food, practical nesting/feeding mitigation (4 Marks).
