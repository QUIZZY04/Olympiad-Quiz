Chapter 2: Going to the Mela

Exploring Local Fairs, Community Markets, Crafts, Traditional Rides, and Cultural Heritage | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • The cultural significance of village and town melas (fairs) as hubs of celebration and trade.
  • Traditional artisans and occupations: Potters, weavers, bangle makers, and toy artisans.
  • Safety rules in crowded public places: Holding an adult's hand, remembering emergency contacts.
  • Using local coins and currency to purchase handmade goods and traditional snacks.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (15 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Community Culture & Crafts
What kinds of artisan stalls and local goods do people find in a community mela?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
A community mela displays rich local crafts and livelihoods:
1. Clay Pottery & Toys: Earthen pots (matkas), kulhads, clay whistles, and painted figurines made by local potters.
2. Handmade Textiles & Bangles: Colorful lac and glass bangles, embroidered dupattas, and handloom kurtas.
3. Handcrafted Wooden & Bamboo Items: Wooden spinning tops (lattu), bamboo flutes, and hand-woven wicker baskets.
4. Traditional Sweets: Jalebi, imarti, cotton candy, roasted groundnuts, and sweet batashas.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Detailed listing of 4 distinct artisanal goods/stalls 2 Marks
Appreciation of traditional livelihoods 1 Mark
Q2 Safety Protocols in Public Spaces
What important safety precautions should every child observe when visiting a crowded fair?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Children must follow these essential safety rules in crowded places:
1. Stay With Adults: Always hold a parent's or guardian's hand and never wander off toward toy stalls alone.
2. Emergency Information Card: Keep a pocket slip containing parents' names, phone numbers, and home address.
3. Identify Safe Help Points: If separated, never leave the venue with strangers; immediately approach a police officer or the official Mela Information & Public Announcement Booth.
4. Establish a Meeting Spot: Agree upon a noticeable landmark (e.g., the clock tower or entry gate) where family members can reunite if separated.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear safety actions (holding hands, ID slip, safe personnel) 2 Marks
Reunion protocol in case of getting lost 1 Mark
Q3 Hygiene & Environmental Care
How can visitors help keep the mela grounds clean and free from plastic garbage?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Visitors can protect the environment by:
• Carrying cloth bags for purchases to avoid single-use polythene bags.
• Disposing of used leaf plates (pattals), sugarcane peels, and paper cups strictly into designated color-coded dustbins.
• Avoiding food wastage by taking only what they can finish.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Anti-littering and waste segregation practices 1.5 Marks
Use of eco-friendly alternatives 0.5 Marks
Q4 Local Crafts • Artisans at the Mela
At a village or neighborhood mela, you see artisans making handicrafts.
(a) Name two handmade toys commonly sold by artisans at a traditional fair.
(b) Why should we support traditional local artisans instead of buying factory-made plastic toys?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Handicrafts & Fair Economy:
(a) Traditional Handmade Toys:
• 1. Clay figurines / whistles (mitti ke khilone).
• 2. Wooden spinning tops (lattu) / wooden rocking horses.

(b) Supporting Artisans:
• Traditional artisans preserve ancient cultural heritage and use eco-friendly natural materials (clay, wood). Buying from them provides livelihood to rural craftsmen and keeps our country's folk arts alive.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Two authentic handmade toys named 1.0 M
Cultural preservation and ecological benefits explained 2.0 M
Q5 Community Helpers • Safety at the Mela
Which community helpers work hard at a crowded mela to keep visitors safe and comfortable?
List three helpers and their responsibilities.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Community Helpers at the Fair:
1. Police Officers: Maintain law and order, control crowds, and run the 'Lost and Found' booth for lost children.
2. Sanitation Workers: Continuously empty waste bins and sweep paper plates to keep the fairgrounds clean and hygienic.
3. First-Aid Medics / Doctors: Provide immediate medical care, bandages, and oral hydration to anyone injured or dizzy.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Three distinct helpers with accurate fairground duties 3.0 अंक
Q6 Clay Modelling Activity • Making Diya / Toy
Describe the four simple steps to make a small clay diya or clay bowl using natural potters' clay.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Clay Craft:
1. Kneading: Knead damp clay thoroughly with fingers until it is soft, smooth, and free of air bubbles.
2. Ball Making: Roll a piece of clay between palms into a smooth sphere.
3. Shaping: Press your thumb into the center to make a hollow bowl, pinching the rim outward to form the groove for the wick.
4. Drying: Place the diya in the shade to dry, then sun-bake it so it turns firm and sturdy.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Four procedural steps of clay modelling described 3.0 M
Q7 Mela Map Navigation • Basic Directions
If the Giant Wheel is to your North and the Food Stalls are to your East, in which direction must you turn from the Giant Wheel to reach the Food Stalls?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Directional Logic:
• Facing North towards the Giant Wheel, East is directly to your Right.
• Therefore, to go from the Giant Wheel towards the Food Stalls, you must travel in the South-East direction (or turn right towards the East).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct directional logic and turn identified 2.0 M
Q8 Observational Field Study • Nature Journal
In 'Going to the Mela', describe how students can observe this environmental phenomenon in their own neighborhood. What three things should they note down in their field notebook?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Neighborhood Field Observation:
1. Direct Observation: Students can observe the living ecosystem and community activities related to Going to the Mela during a morning nature walk.
2. Three Field Log Entries:
• (a) Physical appearance, color, and behavior of the species or object.
• (b) Interactions with surroundings (e.g., sources of water, food, shade, or shelter).
• (c) Human impact or community care practices observed.
3. Conclusion: Recording firsthand observations builds sharp environmental awareness and respect for biodiversity.

Q10 Discussion & Value Reflection • Care & Compassion
How can young children practice kindness and empathy towards living beings or community helpers, as taught in 'Going to the Mela'? Give two practical daily examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Practicing Everyday Kindness:
1. Daily Habit 1: Keep a shallow earthen bowl of clean fresh water on the balcony or under a tree for thirsty birds and small animals during warm afternoons.
2. Daily Habit 2: Speak respectfully to community sanitation workers and delivery persons, offering them clean drinking water and never littering on roads.

Q11 Environmental Riddle • Who Am I?
Environmental Riddle from 'Going to the Mela': I have no color of my own, yet I make the world green. You drink me when thirsty, and clouds carry me across mountains. Who am I? Mention two ways we can save me from wastage.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solving the Nature Riddle:
• Answer: WATER (जल / पानी).
• Two Ways to Save Water:
1. Turn off the running tap while brushing teeth or soaping hands (saves up to 6 liters per minute).
2. Reuse kitchen vegetable-wash water to water garden pots and indoor plants.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct identity (Water) 1.0 Mark
Two practical conservation methods 2.0 Marks
Q12 Hands-on Project & Craft • Eco-Friendly Living
Describe a simple hands-on craft or reuse activity related to 'Going to the Mela' that can be done using discarded household materials. What are the steps?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Eco-Craft Project: Upcycled Planter / Feeder:
• Materials Needed: Discarded clean coconut shell or plastic bottle, jute string, potting soil, vegetable seeds.
• Steps:
1. With adult help, make 2 small holes on opposite sides for hanging.
2. Fill with garden soil mixed with dry compost leaves.
3. Plant fenugreek (methi) or coriander seeds and sprinkle water gently.
• Learning Outcome: Demonstrates the 'Reuse' principle of waste management while greening home spaces.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear list of recycled materials 1.0 Mark
Step-by-step procedure explained 1.5 Marks
Environmental learning objective stated 0.5 Mark

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Supporting Local Artisans and Economic Sustainability

At a district fair, modern imported plastic battery toys are sold right next to a traditional potter selling hand-moulded clay bird whistles. Many children rush to the plastic toys, while the elderly potter sits with few buyers.

Analytical Task: Why is it important to support traditional local artisans at fairs, and what can school communities do to promote eco-friendly indigenous crafts?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Supporting traditional artisans is vital because their crafts represent centuries of cultural heritage, utilize biodegradable natural materials (clay, wood, natural dyes), and provide honest livelihoods to rural families. Plastic toys generate non-recyclable electronic and microplastic waste. Schools can organize 'Heritage Craft Days', invite local potters to conduct workshops, and encourage children to purchase eco-friendly festive gifts directly from local craftspeople.
Rubric: Appreciation of indigenous crafts, environmental comparison (clay vs plastic), and actionable cultural promotion ideas (4 Marks).
