Chapter 8: Food We Eat

Nutritional Food Groups, Balanced Diet, Seasonal Regional Foods, and Avoiding Food Waste | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • The three primary food groups: Energy-giving foods, body-building foods, and protective foods.
  • Components of a balanced thali: Cereals (rotis/rice), pulses (dal), seasonal vegetables, curds, and salad.
  • Regional and cultural food varieties across India based on local climate and agriculture.
  • Food hygiene: Washing vegetables before peeling, eating fresh cooked meals, and avoiding street junk food.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (16 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Nutritional Classification
Classify our food into three major groups and explain the health benefits of each group.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Our food is divided into three essential functional groups:
1. Energy-Giving Foods (Carbohydrates & Fats): Rice, wheat, potatoes, bananas, butter, and nuts provide steady stamina for running, studying, and physical play.
2. Body-Building Foods (Proteins): Pulses, milk, paneer, curd, chickpeas, and eggs supply essential building blocks to repair cells, build muscles, and help young children grow taller.
3. Protective Foods (Vitamins & Minerals): Fresh green vegetables (spinach, carrots) and citrus fruits (oranges, amla) build robust immune defenses to shield our bodies from infections, coughs, and illnesses.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear identification of three nutritional food groups 1.5 Marks
Specific health benefits and daily food examples for each 1.5 Marks
Q2 Healthy Eating Habits vs Junk Food
Why should we avoid eating packaged junk foods (chips, cold drinks, instant noodles) on a daily basis?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Packaged junk foods contain excessive quantities of refined white flour (maida), unhealthy trans-fats, artificial preservatives, chemical colors, and refined sugar, while lacking dietary fiber, vitamins, and minerals. Regular consumption leads to dental cavities, childhood obesity, sluggish digestion, and weakened immunity.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of harmful components (maida, excess salt/sugar/trans-fats) 1.5 Marks
Impact on physical vitality, teeth, and immunity 1.5 Marks
Q3 Food Preservation & Anti-Waste
What can families do with leftover cooked food to prevent it from spoiling or being thrown in the garbage?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
1. Safe Refrigeration: Store leftover curries and rice in clean airtight containers inside the refrigerator and consume them within 24 hours.
2. Creative Culinary Repurposing: Transform leftover rotis into delicious vegetable rolls or bread upma, and use leftover boiled rice for lemon rice or vegetable fried rice.
3. Sharing with Those in Need: Pack excess untouched food hygienically and share it with community helpers or food distribution drives.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Practical storage and hygienic handling 1 Mark
Creative culinary repurposing or community donation 1 Mark
Q4 Food Origins • Plant vs Animal Sources
Classify the following food items into 'Obtained from Plants' and 'Obtained from Animals':
Milk, Rice, Honey, Apple, Egg, Spinach, Ghee, Mustard oil.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Food Origin Classification:
• Obtained from Plants: Rice (cereal), Apple (fruit), Spinach (leafy vegetable), Mustard oil (seeds).
• Obtained from Animals: Milk (cows/buffaloes), Honey (bees), Egg (hens), Ghee (clarified butter from milk).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Four correct plant-origin foods 2.0 M
Four correct animal-origin foods 2.0 M

Q6 Fireless Cooking Activity • Sprouted Salad
Describe how to prepare a healthy sprouted moong salad without using fire or a gas stove.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Fireless Sprouted Moong Salad:
1. Take a cup of sprouted green moong beans in a mixing bowl.
2. Add finely chopped cucumber, tomato, and fresh coriander leaves.
3. Squeeze half a fresh lemon for tangy Vitamin C.
4. Sprinkle a pinch of salt and roasted cumin powder (chaat masala), mix well with a spoon, and serve fresh.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Sprouts and chopped vegetables mentioned 1.5 M
Lemon and spices seasoning without fire 1.5 M
Q7 Food Preservation • Traditional Methods
Why do our grandmothers add a thick layer of mustard oil and salt when making mango pickles?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Science of Pickling:
• Salt draws out moisture and kills bacteria, while mustard oil forms an airtight barrier over the raw mangoes that blocks airborne microbes and fungi, preserving the pickle fresh for over a year.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Role of salt (drying) and oil (barrier) in food preservation 2.0 M

Q10 Discussion & Value Reflection • Care & Compassion
How can young children practice kindness and empathy towards living beings or community helpers, as taught in 'Food We Eat'? Give two practical daily examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Practicing Everyday Kindness:

Q11 Environmental Riddle • Who Am I?
Environmental Riddle from 'Food We Eat': I have no color of my own, yet I make the world green. You drink me when thirsty, and clouds carry me across mountains. Who am I? Mention two ways we can save me from wastage.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solving the Nature Riddle:
• Answer: WATER (जल / पानी).
• Two Ways to Save Water:
1. Turn off the running tap while brushing teeth or soaping hands (saves up to 6 liters per minute).
2. Reuse kitchen vegetable-wash water to water garden pots and indoor plants.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct identity (Water) 1.0 Mark
Two practical conservation methods 2.0 Marks
Q12 Hands-on Project & Craft • Eco-Friendly Living
Describe a simple hands-on craft or reuse activity related to 'Food We Eat' that can be done using discarded household materials. What are the steps?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Eco-Craft Project: Upcycled Planter / Feeder:
• Materials Needed: Discarded clean coconut shell or plastic bottle, jute string, potting soil, vegetable seeds.
• Steps:
1. With adult help, make 2 small holes on opposite sides for hanging.
2. Fill with garden soil mixed with dry compost leaves.
3. Plant fenugreek (methi) or coriander seeds and sprinkle water gently.
• Learning Outcome: Demonstrates the 'Reuse' principle of waste management while greening home spaces.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear list of recycled materials 1.0 Mark
Step-by-step procedure explained 1.5 Marks
Environmental learning objective stated 0.5 Mark

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

School Mid-Day Meal Quality and Nutritional Balanced Plates

In a primary school, many students routinely leave green leafy vegetables and lentils on their plates, eating only plain rice and potato chips. The school health nurse notices frequent iron-deficiency fatigue and poor night vision among several students.

Analytical Task: Design an attractive, child-friendly nutrition strategy to encourage Class 3 students to enjoy iron-rich greens, carrots, and lentils daily.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
To make nutritious foods exciting for young children: (1) Colorful Rainbow Plates: Challenge students to create a 'Rainbow Lunch' where their plate must feature at least three bright colors (orange carrots/papaya for Vitamin A, deep green spinach/fenugreek for iron, and yellow lentils for protein). (2) Interactive School Kitchen Garden: Allow children to sow, water, and harvest spinach and coriander leaves themselves; children take immense pride in eating food they grew. (3) Creative Cooking: Blend iron-rich spinach into colorful green rotis ('Hulk Parathas') or incorporate lentils into crunchy vegetable cutlets.
Rubric: Understanding micronutrient deficiencies (Vitamin A and iron), creative food presentation, school gardens, behavioral nudges (4 Marks).
