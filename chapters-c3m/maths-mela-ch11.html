Chapter 11: Filling and Lifting

Capacity (Litres & Millilitres), Weight (Kilograms & Grams) & Comparison | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Understanding capacity: Litre (L) for large liquids and Millilitre (mL) for small amounts.
  • 1 Litre = 1000 Millilitres (1 L = 1000 mL).
  • Understanding weight: Kilogram (kg) for heavy items and Gram (g) for light items.
  • 1 Kilogram = 1000 Grams (1 kg = 1000 g).
  • Using non-standard and standard balance scales to compare weights.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Capacity Unit Selection • Real-World Items
State whether you would measure the capacity of the following in 'Litres (L)' or 'Millilitres (mL)': (a) A medicine dropper, (b) Water in a swimming pool, (c) A cup of tea, (d) Petrol in a car tank.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Units:
• (a) Medicine dropper: Millilitres (mL) (very small liquid volume)
• (b) Water in swimming pool: Litres (L) (huge liquid volume)
• (c) A cup of tea: Millilitres (mL) (approx 150-200 mL)
• (d) Petrol in a car tank: Litres (L) (approx 35-50 L).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct unit for dropper and tea (mL) 1.0 M
Correct unit for pool and car petrol (L) 1.0 M
Q2 Capacity Conversion & Arithmetic
A bucket holds 10 litres of water. (a) How many 500 mL mugs of water are needed to fill this bucket completely? (b) How many 1-litre bottles can be filled from this bucket?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solution:
(a) Mugs of 500 mL:
• We know that 1 Litre = 2 mugs of 500 mL (500 mL + 500 mL = 1000 mL = 1 L).
• For 10 Litres = 10 × 2 = 20 mugs.

(b) 1-Litre bottles:
• 10 Litres ÷ 1 Litre = 10 bottles.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear conversion statement (1 L = 2 × 500 mL) 1.5 M
Correct answer for mugs (20) and bottles (10) 1.5 M
Q3 Weight Units & Balancing
A vegetable seller has standard weights of 1 kg, 500 g, 200 g, and 100 g. How can she weigh: (a) 1 kg 700 g of potatoes? (b) 800 g of tomatoes using the least number of weights?

500 g + 200 g + 100 g = 800 g (3 weights).

Q5 Balance Scale • Weights in Grams and Kilograms
A grocer wants to weigh 1 kg of sugar. He has standard weights of 500 g, 200 g, and 100 g.
(a) How many 500 g weights balance 1 kg?
(b) How many 200 g weights balance 1 kg?
(c) Give a combination using all three weights (500 g, 200 g, 100 g) that together equal 1 kg.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Weights:
• 1 kg = 1000 grams.
(a) 500 g Weights:
• 1000 ÷ 500 = 2 weights of 500 g.

(b) 200 g Weights:
• 1000 ÷ 200 = 5 weights of 200 g.

(c) Combined Weights:
• One 500 g weight = 500 g
• Two 200 g weights = 400 g
• One 100 g weight = 100 g
• Total = 500 g + 400 g + 100 g = 1000 g = 1 kg.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct answers for (a): 2 weights, and (b): 5 weights 1.5 M
Correct mixed combination summing to 1000 g 1.5 M
Q6 Weight Addition & Subtraction • Grocery Sack
A family bought a 25 kg sack of wheat flour. In the first week, they used 8 kg 500 g. In the second week, they used 7 kg 250 g.
(a) What was the total weight of flour used in the two weeks?
(b) How much flour remains in the sack?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) Total Flour Used:
• Week 1: 8 kg 500 g
• Week 2: 7 kg 250 g
• Sum: (8 + 7) kg + (500 + 250) g = 15 kg 750 g.

(b) Remaining Flour:
• Total in sack = 25 kg 000 g
• Used = 15 kg 750 g
• Subtraction: 24 kg 1000 g - 15 kg 750 g = 9 kg 250 g.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct addition of compound weights (15 kg 750 g) 1.5 M
Correct subtraction from 25 kg (9 kg 250 g) 1.5 M
Q7 Measuring Jug Puzzle • Liquid Volume
A bucket holds 12 litres of water. You only have a 2-litre jug and a 500 mL mug.
(a) How many full 2-litre jugs are needed to fill the bucket completely?
(b) How many 500 mL mugs are needed to equal one 2-litre jug?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) Jugs to fill bucket:
• Total bucket capacity = 12 litres.
• Jug capacity = 2 litres.
• Number of jugs = 12 ÷ 2 = 6 jugs.

(b) Mugs per jug:
• 1 litre = 1000 mL = two 500 mL mugs.
• 2 litres = 2 × 2 = 4 mugs of 500 mL.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct calculation for jugs (6 jugs) 1.5 M
Correct calculation for mugs (4 mugs) 1.5 M

Q12 HOTS & Puzzle • Logical Reasoning
Mathematical Riddle from Filling and Lifting: I am a number. Double me, then add 10, and you will get 170. What number am I? Verify your answer.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Algebraic Verification:
1. Let the unknown number be N.
2. Condition: (2 × N) + 10 = 170.
3. Subtract 10 from both sides: 2 × N = 160.
4. Divide by 2: N = 80.
5. Verification: Double of 80 is 160, plus 10 gives 170. Correct!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Formulating statement: 2N + 10 = 170 1.5 M
Final number N = 80 with verification 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Community Kitchen Lemonade Preparation

To make lemonade for 50 people at a sports day, students use 8 litres of water, 1 litre of lemon juice, and 500 mL of sugar syrup.

Analytical Task: What is the total volume of lemonade prepared in millilitres? If each glass holds 200 mL, can they serve 45 glasses?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Convert all volumes to millilitres (mL):
• Water = 8 L = 8,000 mL
• Lemon juice = 1 L = 1,000 mL
• Sugar syrup = 500 mL
• Total volume = 8,000 + 1,000 + 500 = 9,500 mL.
2. Volume needed for 45 glasses of 200 mL each = 45 × 200 mL = 9,000 mL.
3. Since 9,500 mL > 9,000 mL, yes, they can easily serve 45 glasses (with 500 mL left over).
Rubric: 1.5M litre to millilitre conversion and summation + 1.5M capacity comparison.
