Chapter 10: Play with Patterns

Repeating Patterns, Growing Patterns, Number Sequences & Secret Coded Messages | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Identifying rules of repeating shape patterns (AB, AAB, ABC).
  • Growing patterns in shapes and numbers (e.g., triangular numbers, square sequences).
  • Even and odd number patterns: ending digits (0, 2, 4, 6, 8 vs 1, 3, 5, 7, 9).
  • Secret messages: decoding messages with letter-to-number replacement rules.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (28 Questions)

Q1 Growing Patterns • Number Rules
Look at the pattern: 1, 3, 6, 10, ___, ___. (a) What are the next two numbers? (b) State the mathematical rule for how the pattern grows.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Analysis:
• From 1 to 3: +2
• From 3 to 6: +3
• From 6 to 10: +4
• Next step: +5 → 10 + 5 = 15.
• Next step: +6 → 15 + 6 = 21.
• (a) Next two numbers: 15, 21 (Triangular numbers).
• (b) Rule: Each step adds 1 more than was added in the previous step (+2, +3, +4, +5, +6...).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct calculation of 15 and 21 1.5 M
Clear rule formulation explaining growing increments 1.5 M
Q2 Even and Odd Patterns • Properties
Classify the following as Even or Odd: 246, 381, 500, 789. What happens when you add two odd numbers together? Give an example.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification:
• 246: Ends in 6 → EVEN
• 381: Ends in 1 → ODD
• 500: Ends in 0 → EVEN
• 789: Ends in 9 → ODD.

Rule: The sum of any two odd numbers is always an EVEN number.
• Example: 3 (Odd) + 5 (Odd) = 8 (Even); 7 + 9 = 16 (Even).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct even/odd classification for all 4 numbers 1.5 M
Correct rule and supporting example (Odd + Odd = Even) 1.5 M
Q3 Secret Coded Message • Decoding
If each letter is replaced by its alphabetical position (A=1, B=2, C=3... Z=26): (a) Decode the message: '7 - 15 - 15 - 4'. (b) Encode the word 'BOOK' in numbers.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solutions:
• (a) Decoding '7 - 15 - 15 - 4':
7 = G, 15 = O, 15 = O, 4 = D → GOOD.
• (b) Encoding 'BOOK':
B = 2, O = 15, O = 15, K = 11 → 2 - 15 - 15 - 11.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct decoding to GOOD 1.0 M
Correct encoding for BOOK (2-15-15-11) 1.0 M
Q4 Number Towers • Pyramid Patterns
In a number tower, the number in each block is the sum of the two blocks directly below it.
The bottom row has blocks: [10], [20], [30].
(a) Find the numbers in the two blocks of the second row.
(b) Find the number in the top block of the tower.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Number Tower:
• Bottom row: [10], [20], [30].
(a) Second Row:
• First block = 10 + 20 = 30.
• Second block = 20 + 30 = 50.

(b) Top Block:
• Top block = 30 + 50 = 80.

Completed Tower:
[ 80 ]
[ 30 ] [ 50 ]
[ 10 ] [ 20 ] [ 30 ]
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct second row blocks (30 and 50) 1.5 M
Correct top block (80) 1.5 M

Q7 Even and Odd Patterns • Digit Endings
(a) Write all even numbers between 41 and 51.
(b) What digits do all even numbers end with?
(c) Is the sum of two even numbers always even or odd? Give an example.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Even & Odd Rules:
• (a) Even Numbers: 42, 44, 46, 48, 50.
• (b) Even Endings: All even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8 in the ones place.
• (c) Sum Rule: The sum of two even numbers is always Even.
- Example: 4 + 6 = 10 (Even), 12 + 14 = 26 (Even).

Q12 HOTS & Puzzle • Logical Reasoning
Mathematical Riddle from Play with Patterns: I am a number. Double me, then add 10, and you will get 160. What number am I? Verify your answer.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Algebraic Verification:
1. Let the unknown number be N.
2. Condition: (2 × N) + 10 = 160.
3. Subtract 10 from both sides: 2 × N = 150.
4. Divide by 2: N = 75.
5. Verification: Double of 75 is 150, plus 10 gives 160. Correct!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Formulating statement: 2N + 10 = 160 1.5 M
Final number N = 75 with verification 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Tile Border Geometric Pattern

A walkway is paved with a repeating 4-tile sequence: [Circle, Square, Triangle, Hexagon] that repeats continuously along a path of 50 tiles.

Analytical Task: Which shape will be on the 20th tile? Which shape will be on the 47th tile?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Sequence length = 4 shapes.
• 1 = Circle, 2 = Square, 3 = Triangle, 4 = Hexagon.
2. 20th tile: 20 ÷ 4 = 5 with remainder 0 (4th shape) → Hexagon.
3. 47th tile: 47 ÷ 4 = 11 with remainder 3 (3rd shape) → Triangle.
Rubric: 1.5M modulo calculation for 20th tile + 1.5M modulo calculation for 47th tile.
