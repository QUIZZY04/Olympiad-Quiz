Chapter 5: Shapes and Designs

2D Geometrical Shapes, Corners, Edges, Floor Patterns, Weaving & Tangrams | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Comparing edges: straight edges vs curved edges.
  • Corners (vertices) in 2D shapes: triangles (3), quadrilaterals (4), circles (0).
  • Floor tiling patterns: tessellations that tile without gaps.
  • Traditional Indian textile weaving patterns and borders.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (17 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Corner & Edge Classification
Look at these objects: (1) A sheet of paper, (2) A wheel, (3) A blackboard, (4) An egg. State whether each object has straight edges or curved edges, and how many corners it has.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Classification:
• 1. Sheet of paper: Straight edges (4) → 4 corners.
• 2. Wheel: Curved edge (1 continuous circular boundary) → 0 corners.
• 3. Blackboard: Straight edges (4) → 4 corners.
• 4. Egg: Curved surface → 0 corners.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct straight/curved edge identification 1.0 M
Correct corner counts for all 4 items 1.0 M
Q2 Tangram Dissection • 7-Piece Tangram
In a standard 7-piece Tangram puzzle: (a) How many triangles are there? (b) Are all triangles of the same size? (c) Name the two other shapes in the puzzle.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Tangram Composition:
• (a) Triangles: There are 5 triangles.
• (b) Sizes: No, they are of different sizes: 2 large triangles, 1 medium triangle, and 2 small triangles.
• (c) Other shapes: 1 Square and 1 Parallelogram.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct count of triangles (5) 1.0 M
Distinction between large, medium, small triangles 1.0 M
Naming square and parallelogram 1.0 M
Q3 Tessellation & Floor Patterns
Explain what a tessellation is. Draw or describe how a combination of triangles can tile a floor completely without leaving any gaps.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Explanation:
• A tessellation is a pattern made of repeating geometric shapes that covers a flat surface completely without any gaps or overlaps.
• Triangle Tiling: Two identical triangles can be placed together along their diagonal to form a rectangle. These rectangles can then be arranged in neat rows and columns to cover any floor seamlessly.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Definition of tessellation (no gaps, no overlaps) 1.0 M
Explanation of triangle pairing to form rectangles 1.0 M
Q4 Counting Shapes • Star and Geometrical Figures
Look at a standard 6-pointed star figure made of overlapping triangles:
(a) How many small triangles can you count?
(b) How many corners (vertices) does the 6-pointed star have?
(c) Does a circle have any corners?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Shape Analysis:
• (a) Small Triangles: There are 6 outer pointed triangles, plus 2 large central overlapping triangles = 8 triangles in total.
• (b) Corners: The 6 outward points form 6 corners (or 12 vertices counting inner corners).
• (c) Circle Corners: No, a circle is a continuous smooth curve with 0 corners.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct triangle count with explanation 1.0 M
Correct corner identification 1.0 M
Correct circle property (0 corners) 1.0 M

Q6 Clapper Activity • Paper Craft Geometry
In the 'Paper Clapper' activity described in the textbook:
(a) What shape of paper is used to begin with?
(b) How many times is the paper folded along its edges?
(c) What causes the loud 'pop' or clapping sound when swung down sharply?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Paper Clapper Mechanics:
• (a) Paper Shape: A standard rectangular sheet of paper (like newspaper or scrap notebook paper).
• (b) Folds: It is folded lengthwise in half, then folded down several times into a thick band, and finally folded in half across the middle.
• (c) The Clapping Sound: When swung sharply downward, the sudden rush of air opens the inner folded pocket violently, snapping the paper open to produce the loud clapping sound.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct initial paper shape (rectangular) 1.0 M
Clear description of folding sequence 1.0 M
Physical explanation of air pressure opening paper pocket 1.0 M

Q8 Textbook Exercise • Real-Life Math
In Chapter 5 ('Shapes and Designs'), a student solves an NCERT word problem: If 1 kg of apples costs ₹60 and 1 kg of oranges costs ₹40, how much do 2 kg of apples and 3 kg of oranges cost altogether?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Cost of 2 kg apples = 2 × ₹60 = ₹120.
2. Cost of 3 kg oranges = 3 × ₹40 = ₹120.
3. Total cost = ₹120 + ₹120 = ₹240.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Apples = ₹120 1 M
Oranges = ₹120 1 M
Total = ₹240 1 M
Q9 In-Text Activity • Hands-on Investigation
In 'Shapes and Designs', how do you measure and compare quantities accurately? Explain with an example using standard units.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Measurement Explanation:
• Standard units provide consistent and reliable measurements across the world.
• For length, we use metres (m) and centimetres (cm).
• For weight, we use kilograms (kg) and grams (g).
• For liquids, we use litres (L) and millilitres (mL).
• Unlike non-standard units (such as footsteps or handspans), standard units give the exact same measurement no matter who measures.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Standard units defined 1.5 M
Examples for length, weight, liquid 1.5 M
Q10 Practice Time • Two-Step Word Problem
Suresh bought a school bag for ₹175 and a pencil box for ₹45. He gave a ₹500 note to the shopkeeper. How much change did he receive?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Total money spent = ₹175 + ₹45 = ₹220.
2. Amount paid = ₹500.
3. Change returned = ₹500 - ₹220 = ₹280.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Total spent = ₹220 1.5 M
Change = ₹280 1.5 M

Q14 Time & Calendar • Date Calculation
Rohan's summer vacation starts on 15th May and ends on 30th June. How many total days of vacation does Rohan get? (Remember: May has 31 days).
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Days Count:
• Days in May: From 15th to 31st = (31 - 15) + 1 = 17 days.
• Days in June: Full 30 days = 30 days.
• Total vacation days = 17 + 30 = 47 days.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
May days = 17 1.5 M
June days = 30; Total = 47 days 1.5 M
Q15 Equal Sharing • Division Problem
Mother bakes 36 cookies and wants to distribute them equally among 4 children. How many cookies does each child receive? Write the division sentence.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Equal Sharing:
• Total cookies = 36.
• Number of children = 4.
• Cookies per child = 36 ÷ 4 = 9 cookies.
• Division sentence: 36 ÷ 4 = 9.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
36 ÷ 4 = 9 cookies 2 M

Q17 Money • Making a Change
A toy train costs ₹85. You pay with a ₹100 note. The shopkeeper gives you change in 5-rupee coins only. How many 5-rupee coins do you receive?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Calculations:
• Change due = ₹100 - ₹85 = ₹15.
• Number of 5-rupee coins = ₹15 ÷ ₹5 = 3 coins.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Change = ₹15 1 M
3 coins of ₹5 1 M
🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Handloom Saree Border Design

A master weaver from Varanasi designs a border pattern using alternating red squares and yellow triangles. Every 10 cm repeat of the pattern uses 2 squares and 4 triangles.

Analytical Task: How many squares and how many triangles will be woven along a 1-metre (100 cm) saree border?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Total border length = 1 metre = 100 cm.
2. Number of 10 cm pattern repeats = 100 ÷ 10 = 10 repeats.
3. Squares needed = 10 × 2 = 20 squares.
4. Triangles needed = 10 × 4 = 40 triangles.
Rubric: 1M meter to cm conversion + 1M pattern repeats + 1M shape counts.
