Chapter 8: Fair Share

Concept of Equal Sharing, Grouping, Introduction to Division & Fractions | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Equal sharing: dividing a collection of items equally among a number of people.
  • Equal grouping: finding how many groups of a given size can be formed.
  • Division as repeated subtraction.
  • Understanding halves (1/2) and quarters (1/4) of a whole collection.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Equal Sharing • Division Basics
Mother has 20 biscuits. She shares them equally among her 4 children. (a) How many biscuits does each child get? (b) Write the division sentence and repeated subtraction steps.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solution:
• Total biscuits = 20
• Number of children = 4
(a) Each child gets = 20 ÷ 4 = 5 biscuits.

(b) Repeated Subtraction:
1. 20 - 4 = 16 (1st biscuit each)
2. 16 - 4 = 12 (2nd biscuit each)
3. 12 - 4 = 8 (3rd biscuit each)
4. 8 - 4 = 4 (4th biscuit each)
5. 4 - 4 = 0 (5th biscuit each)
• Division sentence: 20 ÷ 4 = 5.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct calculation of each child's share (5) 1.5 M
Correct repeated subtraction sequence and division statement 1.5 M
Q2 Equal Grouping • Practical Word Problem
There are 24 pencils in a box. If teacher puts 6 pencils in each pencil pouch, how many pouches can be filled? Are any pencils left over?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solution:
• Total pencils = 24
• Pencils per pouch = 6
• Number of pouches = 24 ÷ 6 = 4 pouches.
• Remainder = 0, so no pencils are left over.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct division calculation (4 pouches) 1.0 M
Correct statement regarding zero remainder 1.0 M
Q3 Fractions of a Collection • Halves & Quarters
In a box of 16 apples: (a) Find half (1/2) of the apples. (b) Find one-fourth (1/4) of the apples. Explain what happens if you add half and one-fourth together.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Calculations:
• (a) Half (1/2): 16 ÷ 2 = 8 apples.
• (b) One-fourth (1/4): 16 ÷ 4 = 4 apples.
• Combined: 8 + 4 = 12 apples (which represents 3/4 or three-quarters of the collection, leaving 4 apples).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct calculation of 1/2 of 16 (8) 1.0 M
Correct calculation of 1/4 of 16 (4) 1.0 M
Accurate combination explanation (12 apples) 1.0 M
Q4 Fraction of a Collection • Word Problem
A teacher brought 20 apples to class to share among students.
(a) Find 1/2 (half) of the 20 apples.
(b) Find 1/4 (one-fourth) of the 20 apples.
(c) Find 3/4 (three-fourths) of the 20 apples.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Calculations:
(a) Half (1/2):
• 20 ÷ 2 = 10 apples.

(b) One-Fourth (1/4):
• 20 ÷ 4 = 5 apples.

(c) Three-Fourths (3/4):
• 3 × (20 ÷ 4) = 3 × 5 = 15 apples.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct half calculation (10 apples) 1.0 M
Correct quarter calculation (5 apples) 1.0 M
Correct three-fourths calculation (15 apples) 1.0 M
Q5 Equal Sharing with Remainder • Pencils Problem
A box has 15 pencils. Four friends (Aman, Bhuvan, Chitra, and Deepa) want to share them equally.
(a) How many pencils will each friend get?
(b) How many pencils will be left over in the box?
(c) Write the division equation with quotient and remainder.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) & (b) Sharing and Remainder:
• Total pencils = 15
• Number of friends = 4
• 4 × 3 = 12 pencils distributed.
• Each friend gets 3 pencils.
• Left over = 15 - 12 = 3 pencils left over.

(c) Division Equation:
• 15 ÷ 4 = Quotient = 3, Remainder = 3.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct share per child (3 pencils) 1.0 M
Correct remainder (3 pencils) 1.0 M
Correct division format with quotient and remainder 1.0 M
Q6 Visual Fractions • Shading Geometric Figures
A circle is divided into 4 equal quarters.
(a) If you shade 2 quarters, what fraction of the circle is shaded? Write it in simplest form.
(b) How many quarters must be shaded to show 1 whole circle?
(c) Which is larger: 1/2 of a chapati or 1/4 of the same chapati?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solution:
(a) Shading 2 Quarters:
• Fraction = 2/4 = 1/2 (Half).

(b) One Whole Circle:
• All 4 quarters must be shaded (4/4 = 1).

(c) Comparison:
• 1/2 is larger than 1/4. Half a chapati gives two quarters, which is twice as much as one quarter.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct fraction simplification: 2/4 = 1/2 1.0 M
Correct quarters for whole (4 quarters) 1.0 M
Correct comparison with justification (1/2 > 1/4) 1.0 M
Q7 Team Formation • Equal Division
In Class 3, there are 28 students. The sports teacher wants to form teams with exactly 4 students in each team for a relay race.
(a) How many complete teams can be formed?
(b) If the teacher instead forms teams of 5 students each, how many teams will be formed, and how many students will be substitutes?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) Teams of 4:
• 28 ÷ 4 = 7 teams.
• 0 students left over.

(b) Teams of 5:
• 5 × 5 = 25 students.
• 5 complete teams can be formed.
• Substitutes left over = 28 - 25 = 3 students.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct calculation for teams of 4 (7 teams) 1.5 M
Correct calculation for teams of 5 (5 teams and 3 substitutes) 1.5 M

Q12 HOTS & Puzzle • Logical Reasoning
Mathematical Riddle from Fair Share: I am a number. Double me, then add 10, and you will get 140. What number am I? Verify your answer.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Algebraic Verification:
1. Let the unknown number be N.
2. Condition: (2 × N) + 10 = 140.
3. Subtract 10 from both sides: 2 × N = 130.
4. Divide by 2: N = 65.
5. Verification: Double of 65 is 130, plus 10 gives 140. Correct!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Formulating statement: 2N + 10 = 140 1.5 M
Final number N = 65 with verification 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Picnic Fruit Distribution

During a picnic, 30 bananas and 18 oranges are to be packed into 6 picnic baskets so that every basket gets an equal number of each fruit.

Analytical Task: How many bananas and how many oranges will go into each basket? How many total fruits are in each basket?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Bananas per basket = 30 ÷ 6 = 5 bananas.
2. Oranges per basket = 18 ÷ 6 = 3 oranges.
3. Total fruits per basket = 5 + 3 = 8 fruits.
Rubric: 1M bananas division + 1M oranges division + 1M total per basket.
