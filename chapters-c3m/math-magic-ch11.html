Chapter 11: Jugs and Mugs

Liquid Capacity, Litres (L) & Millilitres (mL), Kheer Party & Medicine Doses | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Estimating and measuring capacity using non-standard vs standard units.
  • 1 Litre = 1000 Millilitres (1 L = 1000 mL).
  • Equivalent fractions: 500 mL = 1/2 Litre; 250 mL = 1/4 Litre.
  • Calculating daily water intake and beverage preparation for guests.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (24 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Capacity Combinations • Making 1 Litre
How many of each container will you need to make exactly 1 Litre (1,000 mL)? (a) 200 mL glasses, (b) 100 mL medicine cups, (c) 500 mL bottles.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Calculations for 1,000 mL:
• (a) 200 mL glasses: 1,000 ÷ 200 = 5 glasses.
• (b) 100 mL medicine cups: 1,000 ÷ 100 = 10 cups.
• (c) 500 mL bottles: 1,000 ÷ 500 = 2 bottles.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct count for 200 mL (5) 1.0 M
Correct count for 100 mL (10) 1.0 M
Correct count for 500 mL (2) 1.0 M
Q2 Medicine Dosage Calculation • Practical Math
A doctor prescribes 5 mL of cough syrup to be taken 3 times a day for 5 days. (a) How many mL of syrup does the patient take in one day? (b) How many total mL are needed for the full 5-day course?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Solution:
• Dose per time = 5 mL
• Times per day = 3
(a) Daily consumption: 5 mL × 3 = 15 mL per day.

(b) 5-Day course total: 15 mL × 5 days = 75 mL in total.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct daily dosage calculation (15 mL) 1.5 M
Correct 5-day course calculation (75 mL) 1.5 M
Q3 Kheer Party • Real-World Sharing
At the wedding party of the squirrels, 10 litres of sweet kheer was cooked. If each bowl holds 100 mL, how many guests can be served with kheer?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-step Solution:
1. Total kheer = 10 litres.
2. Convert litres to millilitres: 10 × 1000 mL = 10,000 mL.
3. Capacity of 1 bowl = 100 mL.
4. Number of guests served = 10,000 ÷ 100 = 100 guests.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct conversion of 10 L to 10,000 mL 1.5 M
Correct division to find 100 guests 1.5 M
Q4 Bunny and Banno's Wedding Feast • Kheer Consumption
At Bunny and Banno's wedding feast, delicious kheer was served:
• The donkey drank 10 glasses of 100 mL each.
• The monkey drank 5 glasses of 200 mL each.
• The elephant drank 10 litres.
(a) How many millilitres of kheer did the donkey drink? Convert to litres.
(b) How many millilitres did the monkey drink? Convert to litres.
(c) Who drank more kheer: the donkey or the monkey?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Capacity Math:
(a) Donkey's Kheer:
• 10 glasses × 100 mL = 1000 mL = 1 Litre.

(b) Monkey's Kheer:
• 5 glasses × 200 mL = 1000 mL = 1 Litre.

(c) Comparison:
• Both the donkey and the monkey drank exactly the same amount (1 Litre each).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct calculation for donkey (1000 mL = 1 L) 1.0 M
Correct calculation for monkey (1000 mL = 1 L) 1.0 M
Correct equality conclusion 1.0 M
Q5 Clever Crow Story • Water Displacement
A thirsty crow found a pitcher with water deep at the bottom. He dropped 10 small pebbles into the pitcher.
(a) Why did the water level rise when pebbles were dropped?
(b) If each pebble displaced 50 mL of water, by how many mL did the water level rise in total?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Water Displacement Science & Math:
(a) Why Water Rises:
• Pebbles take up space (volume) at the bottom of the pitcher. Because two objects cannot occupy the same space at the same time, the solid pebbles push the water upward.

(b) Total Water Rise:
• 10 pebbles × 50 mL = 500 mL (or half a litre).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear physical explanation of volume displacement 1.5 M
Correct multiplication (10 × 50 mL = 500 mL) 1.5 M
Q6 Measuring 1 Litre • Fractional Bottle Volumes
How many of each container are required to fill a 1-litre (1000 mL) bottle completely?
(a) 500 mL bottles
(b) 250 mL glasses
(c) 200 mL cups
(d) 100 mL ladles
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Conversions into 1000 mL:
• (a) 500 mL: 1000 ÷ 500 = 2 bottles.
• (b) 250 mL: 1000 ÷ 250 = 4 glasses.
• (c) 200 mL: 1000 ÷ 200 = 5 cups.
• (d) 100 mL: 1000 ÷ 100 = 10 ladles.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct values for (a): 2 and (b): 4 2.0 M
Correct values for (c): 5 and (d): 10 2.0 M
Q7 Household Water Usage • Daily Estimation
An average family uses:
• Cooking & drinking: 25 Litres
• Bathing: 80 Litres
• Washing clothes & utensils: 65 Litres
(a) What is the total water used by the family in one day?
(b) If their overhead water tank holds 500 Litres, how many litres of water remain in the tank at the end of the day?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Usage:
(a) Total Daily Usage:
• 25 L + 80 L + 65 L = 170 Litres.

(b) Remaining Water:
• Tank capacity = 500 Litres.
• Used = 170 Litres.
• Remaining = 500 - 170 = 330 Litres.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct total usage sum (170 L) 1.5 M
Correct tank remainder (330 L) 1.5 M

Q12 HOTS & Puzzle • Logical Reasoning
Mathematical Riddle from Jugs and Mugs: I am a number. Double me, then add 10, and you will get 170. What number am I? Verify your answer.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Algebraic Verification:
1. Let the unknown number be N.
2. Condition: (2 × N) + 10 = 170.
3. Subtract 10 from both sides: 2 × N = 160.
4. Divide by 2: N = 80.
5. Verification: Double of 80 is 160, plus 10 gives 170. Correct!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Formulating statement: 2N + 10 = 170 1.5 M
Final number N = 80 with verification 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

School Drinking Water Conservation

A leaking tap in the school corridor drips 100 mL of water every hour. The plumber was able to fix it after 12 hours.

Analytical Task: How much water was wasted in millilitres? How much is that in litres and millilitres?
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Water wasted in 12 hours = 12 × 100 mL = 1,200 mL.
2. In litres and millilitres: 1,200 mL = 1,000 mL + 200 mL = 1 Litre 200 Millilitres (1 L 200 mL).
Rubric: 1.5M multiplication calculation + 1.5M conversion to litres and millilitres.
