Chapter 5: Fun with Shapes

Edges, Corners, Flat & Curved Surfaces, 2D Patterns & Tangram Creations | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Identifying 2D shapes: rectangles, squares, triangles, and circles.
  • Properties of shapes: counting straight sides, curved sides, and corners (vertices).
  • Special properties: opposite sides equal in rectangles, all 4 sides equal in squares.
  • Creating pictures with basic shapes and exploring tiling patterns.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (18 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Textbook Exercise • Shape Attributes
Look at the following objects: A postcard, a round plate, a triangular birthday cap, and a chessboard. State the shape, number of edges, and number of corners for each.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Shape Breakdown:
• Postcard: Rectangle → 4 straight edges, 4 corners.
• Round Plate: Circle → 1 curved edge, 0 corners.
• Birthday Cap face: Triangle → 3 straight edges, 3 corners.
• Chessboard: Square → 4 equal straight edges, 4 corners.

Q2 Tangram Investigation • Problem Solving
How many pieces are in a 5-piece tangram? Which pieces are triangles, and can you combine two small triangles to make a square? Show the working.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Explanation:
1. A 5-piece tangram has 5 pieces: 3 triangles, 1 square, and 1 parallelogram.
2. Making a square: Yes, by placing the longest sides (hypotenuses) of two identical small right-angled triangles together, their right angles become two opposite corners of a new square.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct composition of 5-piece tangram 1.0 M
Accurate geometric explanation of forming a square 1.0 M
Q3 Practical Application • Tiling & Tessellation
Why can square and rectangular floor tiles cover a room completely without any gaps, but circular coins leave gaps when placed next to each other?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Geometric Reason:
• Squares and rectangles have straight edges and 90° corner angles. When placed side by side, their edges align flush against each other, filling all 360° around every point with zero empty space.
• Circles have curved boundaries. When round items touch, circular arcs curve away from each other, leaving open gaps between them. Hence, circles cannot tessellate.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Explanation of straight edges and corners fitting flush 1.5 M
Explanation of curved arc gaps in circles 1.5 M

Q5 7-Piece Tangram • Shape Assembly
In a standard 7-piece tangram puzzle:
(a) How many triangles are there in total?
(b) How many 4-sided shapes are there, and what are their names?
(c) Which two triangles are exactly identical in size?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) Triangles in 7-piece Tangram:
• There are 5 triangles in total (2 large triangles, 1 medium triangle, and 2 small triangles).

(b) 4-Sided Shapes:
• There are 2 four-sided shapes: 1 Square and 1 Parallelogram.

(c) Identical Triangles:
• The 2 large triangles are exactly identical to each other.

Q6 Tiling & Tessellation • Floor Patterns
Which of the following geometric shapes can cover a flat floor completely without leaving any gaps or overlaps?
(a) Squares
(b) Equilateral triangles
(c) Circles
Explain why circles cannot tile a flat surface.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Explanation:
• (a) Squares: Yes, squares tile a floor seamlessly (like bathroom tiles).
• (b) Equilateral Triangles: Yes, equilateral triangles fit together perfectly around a point without gaps.
• (c) Circles: No, circles cannot tile a plane.
Why Circles Cannot Tile: When identical circles are placed touching each other, curved spaces (interstitial gaps) are always left between them. Curved boundaries cannot fill straight plane vertices completely.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct identification of squares and triangles as tiling shapes 1.5 M
Clear mathematical reason why circles leave gaps 1.5 M
Q7 Paper Folding • Corners Exploration
Take a square sheet of paper. It has 4 corners.
(a) If you fold one corner diagonally, how many corners does the paper have now?
(b) Can you fold a piece of paper so that it has exactly 3 corners? What shape does it become?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Hands-on Geometry:
(a) Folding One Corner:
• By folding down one corner, a new straight edge is formed across that corner, creating 2 new corners in place of the 1 folded corner.
• Total corners = 4 - 1 + 2 = 5 corners (it becomes a pentagon).

(b) Folding to 3 Corners:
• Yes. If you fold a square sheet in half along its diagonal from corner to opposite corner, the folded paper forms a Triangle, which has exactly 3 corners.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct deduction of 5 corners after folding one corner 1.5 M
Correct identification of diagonal fold creating a triangle with 3 corners 1.5 M
Q8 Textbook Exercise • Practical Problem Solving
In Chapter 5 ('Fun with Shapes'), a student solves an exercise problem: If 1 packet contains 12 items, how many items are in 4 such packets? Explain using repeated addition and multiplication.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Repeated addition: 12 + 12 + 12 + 12 = 48 items.
2. Multiplication fact: 4 × 12 = 48 items.
3. Both methods yield the exact same answer. Multiplication is efficient repeated addition.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Repeated addition 12+12+12+12 = 48 1 M
Multiplication 4 × 12 = 48 1 M
Q9 In-Text Activity • Concept Exploration
In 'Fun with Shapes', how do you verify your mathematical calculations? Give an example using two numbers that add up to 100.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Verification Method:
• Take two numbers: 65 and 35. Sum = 65 + 35 = 100.
• Verification by inverse operation (subtraction): 100 - 35 = 65, and 100 - 65 = 35.
• Since subtraction brings back the original numbers, the addition is confirmed correct.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Addition 65 + 35 = 100 1 M
Inverse check 100 - 35 = 65 1 M

Q11 Word Problem • Real Life Application
A school library received 3 boxes of mathematics activity books. Each box contained 25 books. During the week, 38 books were distributed to Grade 3 children. How many books remain in the library?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Total books received = 3 × 25 = 75 books.
2. Books distributed = 38 books.
3. Books remaining = 75 - 38 = 37 books.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme

Remainder 75 - 38 = 37 1.5 M

Q15 Let us Do • Shapes and Symmetry
Draw a line of symmetry through each shape:
(a) A rectangle
(b) An equilateral triangle
(c) A circle.
How many lines of symmetry does a circle have?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Symmetry Properties:
• (a) A rectangle has 2 lines of symmetry (horizontal and vertical through centers of opposite sides).
• (b) An equilateral triangle has 3 lines of symmetry (from each vertex to opposite midpoint).
• (c) A circle has infinite lines of symmetry (any diameter passing through the center).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Rectangle = 2 lines 1 M
Triangle = 3 lines 1 M
Circle = infinite lines 1 M
Q16 Puzzle Time • Missing Number Grid
In a 3x3 magic square, the sum of each row, column, and diagonal is 15. The center number is 5. What numbers go in the opposite corners if one corner is 2?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Magic Square Logic:
• In a 3x3 magic square with sum 15 and center 5, opposite corners must sum to 10 (since 2 + 5 + x = 15 → x = 8).
• If one corner is 2, the diagonally opposite corner must be 8.
• Other corner pairs: 4 and 6.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Opposite corner = 8 with reasoning 2 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Classroom Floor Tiling Project

The school is laying square tiles of side 1 metre in an activity hall that is 6 metres long and 4 metres wide.

Analytical Task: How many 1-metre square tiles are needed to pave the entire floor? If each tile costs ₹50, find the total cost of tiles.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
1. Area of floor = Length × Width = 6 m × 4 m = 24 square metres.
2. Area of 1 tile = 1 m × 1 m = 1 square metre.
3. Number of tiles needed = 24 ÷ 1 = 24 tiles.
4. Total cost = 24 × ₹50 = ₹1,200.
Rubric: 1.5M tile count calculation + 1.5M total cost multiplication.
