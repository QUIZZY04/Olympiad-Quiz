Working of Institutions

Democratic Politics – I | Chapter 4 — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

NCERT Textbook Exercises — 100% Question Coverage
Question 1
Why is the Lok Sabha more powerful than the Rajya Sabha, even though the Rajya Sabha is called the 'Upper Chamber'?
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Although the Rajya Sabha is constitutionally called the 'Upper Chamber', the Lok Sabha (House of the People) exercises decisive supremacy in our democratic parliamentary framework in three critical ways:

  1. Deadlocks in Ordinary Bills: In case of disagreement over an ordinary bill, a joint sitting of both Houses is convened. Because the Lok Sabha has more than double the membership (543) of the Rajya Sabha (245), the view of the Lok Sabha prevails.
  2. Absolute Supremacy over Money Bills: Money bills (the Union Budget) can originate only in the Lok Sabha. Once passed by the Lok Sabha, the Rajya Sabha cannot reject it; it can only delay it by a maximum of 14 days or suggest amendments, which the Lok Sabha may accept or reject.
  3. Control over the Council of Ministers: The Prime Minister and Council of Ministers are accountable solely to the Lok Sabha. If a majority of Lok Sabha members pass a No-Confidence Motion, the Prime Minister and all ministers must immediately resign. The Rajya Sabha has no power to pass a No-Confidence Motion.

Question 2
Distinguish between the Political Executive and the Permanent Executive. Why does the Political Executive have the final say?
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The executive branch of government is split into two distinct tiers:

  • Political Executive: Ministers elected by the people for a specific tenure of 5 years (Prime Minister, Cabinet Ministers). They make major policy decisions and are directly accountable to Parliament and the public.
  • Permanent Executive (Civil Services): Civil servants (IAS, IPS, IRS) appointed on a long-term basis through competitive merit-based examinations until retirement. They provide administrative continuity, technical expertise, and execute the policies formulated by the political executive.

Why the Political Executive has the Final Say:
In a democracy, the will of the people is sovereign. The minister is elected by the people and is directly answerable to the electorate for the consequences of policy decisions. While civil servants possess expert technical knowledge, the minister understands the broader socio-political aspirations of the public. Hence, the minister decides the overarching goals, and the permanent bureaucracy works under their direction.

Question 3
Why is an independent and integrated judiciary considered the cornerstone of Indian democracy?
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

An independent and integrated judiciary (Supreme Court at the apex, High Courts at state levels, and Subordinate Courts) is essential to preserve constitutional governance in India:

  1. Independence from Political Pressure: Judges are appointed through the Collegium system. Once appointed, it is virtually impossible to remove a judge; removal requires an impeachment motion passed by a two-thirds majority in both Houses of Parliament. Salaries and allowances cannot be reduced during their tenure.
  2. Guardian of the Constitution & Judicial Review: The Supreme Court and High Courts have the power of Judicial Review. They can declare any law passed by Parliament or executive order invalid if it violates the provisions of the Constitution.
  3. Protector of Fundamental Rights: Under Articles 32 and 226, citizens can directly approach the Supreme Court or High Courts if their rights are violated. The court issues prerogative writs (Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, etc.) and allows Public Interest Litigation (PIL) to defend the rights of the poor.

Question 4
Describe the powers, functions, and constitutional position of the Prime Minister of India.
5 Marks (100-120 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The Prime Minister is the most important political institution in India, serving as the Head of Government:

  1. Leader of the Majority Party and Appointment: The President appoints the leader of the majority party or coalition in the Lok Sabha as Prime Minister. The PM remains in office as long as he/she commands the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
  2. Formation of Council of Ministers: The President appoints all other ministers on the advice of the Prime Minister. The PM is free to choose ministers, allocate portfolios, and dismiss any minister.
  3. Chairs Cabinet Meetings: The PM presides over Cabinet meetings, coordinates the work of different ministries, settles disputes between departments, and exercises general supervision over all ministries.
  4. Link Between Cabinet and President: The PM communicates all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to the administration of the affairs of the Union and proposals for legislation to the President.
  5. Voice of the Nation: The Prime Minister represents the country at international summits, leads policy announcements in Parliament, and shapes national foreign and economic policies.

Institutional Policy-Making Case Study
Explain how the Mandal Commission recommendations (27% OBC reservation) became law through the interaction of different state institutions.

The implementation of the Mandal Report illustrates the synchronized functioning of constitutional institutions: (1) The Executive Initiative: The Mandal Commission submitted its report in 1980. In 1989, the National Front government under V.P. Singh included its implementation in their election manifesto; (2) President and Prime Minister: The President mentioned it in his address; the Prime Minister informed Parliament; (3) Cabinet Decision & Office Memorandum: The Union Cabinet formally decided to implement 27% quota, and the Department of Personnel and Training issued the official Office Memorandum on 13 August 1990; (4) Judicial Resolution: Aggrieved citizens challenged it in the Supreme Court (Indira Sawhney and Others vs Union of India case, 1992). The 11-judge bench upheld the reservation while ordering the exclusion of the 'creamy layer', demonstrating how the judiciary peacefully resolves institutional deadlocks.

Executive Power & Coalition Dynamics
How has the era of coalition governments since 1989 constrained the powers of the Indian Prime Minister?

In a single-party majority government (e.g., Jawaharlal Nehru or Indira Gandhi eras), the Prime Minister wields near-absolute authority over the cabinet. However, in a coalition government, the Prime Minister's power is constrained: (1) The Prime Minister cannot take decisions arbitrarily; they must consult and accommodate diverse coalition partners and regional allies; (2) Key ministerial portfolios must be conceded to coalition partners; (3) The government's survival depends on coalition consensus; if an ally withdraws support, the government collapses. Thus, coalition dynamics replace prime ministerial command with constant negotiation and compromise.
