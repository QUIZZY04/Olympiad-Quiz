Geography: Contemporary India – I

Natural Vegetation and Wildlife

Contemporary India – I | Chapter 5 — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

NCERT Textbook Exercises — 100% Question Coverage
Question 1
Answer the following questions briefly:
(i) What is meant by virgin vegetation? Distinguish between endemic and exotic flora.
(ii) Name the factors responsible for the distribution of plants and animals in India.
(iii) What is a biosphere reserve? Give two examples.
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

(i) Virgin Vegetation & Endemic vs Exotic Flora:

  • Virgin Vegetation: A plant community which has grown naturally without human aid and has remained undisturbed by humans for a long time.
  • Endemic Species: Virgin vegetation species that are purely native and indigenous to India (e.g., Sal, Sandalwood).
  • Exotic Species: Plants that have been brought from outside India (e.g., Water Hyacinth, Eucalyptus).

(ii) Factors Determining Distribution:

  1. Relief: Land (fertile plains support agriculture, rough terrain supports forests) and Soil (alluvial, black, desert, and mangrove soils support specific vegetation).
  2. Climate: Temperature & Humidity, Photoperiod (duration of sunlight), and Annual Precipitation.

(iii) Biosphere Reserve: Multipurpose protected areas preserving genetic diversity in representative ecosystems of terrestrial and coastal/marine environments, where humans and nature co-exist sustainably. Examples: Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve and Sundarbans Biosphere Reserve.

Question 2
Distinguish between Tropical Evergreen and Tropical Deciduous Forests.
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

India’s two primary forest ecosystems differ substantially in precipitation, foliage, and commercial species:

Feature Tropical Evergreen (Rainforests) Tropical Deciduous (Monsoon Forests)
Annual Rainfall Heavy rainfall exceeding 200 cm with a very short dry season. Rainfall between 70 cm and 200 cm. Most widespread forest in India.
Leaf Shedding No definite time for shedding leaves; forests appear green all year round. Multilayered canopy exceeding 60m height. Shed their leaves for about 6 to 8 weeks in dry summer to conserve moisture.
Key Species & Distribution Ebony, Mahogany, Rosewood, Rubber, Cinchona. Found in Western Ghats, Andaman & Nicobar, Assam hills. Teak, Sal, Shisham, Sandalwood, Khair, Kusum, Bamboo. Found in eastern slopes of Western Ghats, Odisha, UP, Bihar.

Question 3
Describe the vegetation of high altitudes (Montane Forests).
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In mountainous regions, the decrease in temperature with increasing altitude leads to a corresponding, systematic zonation of natural vegetation belts:

  1. Wet Temperate Forests (1,000–2,000 metres): Characterized by evergreen broad-leaf trees such as oaks and chestnuts.
  2. Temperate Coniferous Forests (1,500–3,000 metres): Dominated by coniferous trees like pine, deodar, silver fir, spruce, and cedar. These forests cover mostly the southern slopes of the Himalayas.
  3. Temperate Grasslands (Above 3,000 metres): Common at high altitudes; used extensively for seasonal transhumance grazing by nomadic tribes like the Gujjars and Bakarwals.
  4. Alpine Vegetation (Above 3,600 metres): Silver fir, junipers, pines, and birches dominate, but trees become progressively stunted as they approach the snowline, transitioning into alpine grasslands, mosses, and lichens (Tundra vegetation).

Question 4
Quite a few species of plants and animals are endangered in India. Why? What steps has the government taken to protect them?
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Causes of Species Endangerment:
Approximately 1,300 plant species and several wildlife species are endangered in India due to: (1) Greed of commercial poachers for ivory, rhino horns, tiger bones, and pelt; (2) Habitat loss from forest clearing for agriculture and urbanization; (3) Pollution from chemical wastes; (4) Introduction of alien invasive species.

Government Protection Measures:

  1. 18 Biosphere Reserves have been set up in the country to protect flora and fauna (12 included in world network of biosphere reserves).
  2. Legislative Protection: Enactment of the comprehensive Wildlife Protection Act of 1972 banning hunting and trade in endangered species.
  3. Specialized Conservation Projects: Targeted projects like Project Tiger (1973), Project Elephant, and Project Great Indian Bustard.
  4. Protected Area Network: Establishment of 106 National Parks, 573 Wildlife Sanctuaries, and Zoological Gardens across the country.

Question 5
What are the major factors that influence the distribution and diversity of flora and fauna in India?
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The immense biodiversity of plant and animal life in India is determined by two broad sets of factors: Relief and Climate:

  1. Relief Factors:
    • Land: The nature of land directly and indirectly affects vegetation. Fertile level land is devoted to agriculture, whereas undulating and rough terrains support woodlands, grasslands, and wildlife shelter.
    • Soil: Different types of soils support different vegetation: sandy desert soils support cactus and thorny bushes; wet, marshy, deltaic soils support mangroves; hill slopes with deep soil support coniferous trees.
  2. Climatic Factors:
    • Temperature and Humidity: Character and extent of vegetation are determined by temperature along with humidity in the air. On the slopes of the Himalayas above 915 m, fall in temperature changes vegetation from tropical to subtropical, temperate, and alpine.
    • Photoperiod (Sunlight): Variation in duration of sunlight due to latitude, altitude, and season causes trees to grow faster in summer.
    • Precipitation: Areas of heavy rainfall (Western Ghats, Northeast) have dense evergreen forests compared to regions with scanty rainfall.

Mangrove Ecosystem & Coastal Defense
Describe the unique characteristics of Mangrove (Tidal) Forests and their vital ecological significance.

Mangrove tidal forests are found along coastal areas influenced by tides, where mud and silt accumulate. Dense mangroves feature tangled root systems that remain completely submerged underwater during high tides. The Sundari tree in the Ganga-Brahmaputra delta yields durable, hard timber, giving the region its name—Sundarbans. It is also the home of the Royal Bengal Tiger, turtles, crocodiles, and gharials.

Ecologically, mangroves act as vital bio-shields against coastal cyclones and tsunamis, prevent shoreline soil erosion, and serve as nursery grounds for marine fisheries.

Medicinal Flora & Traditional Knowledge
India has a rich tradition of utilizing medicinal plants. Name any four medicinal plants and their therapeutic uses.

India’s traditional Ayurveda utilizes over 2,000 medicinal plant species: (1) Sarpagandha: Used to treat blood pressure; found exclusively in India; (2) Jamun: Juice from ripe fruit prepared into vinegar; seed powder controls diabetes; (3) Neem: High antibiotic and antibacterial properties used in pharmaceuticals; (4) Tulsi: Cures cough, cold, and respiratory disorders.
