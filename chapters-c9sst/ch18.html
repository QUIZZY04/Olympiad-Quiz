Economics: Understanding Economic Development – I

People as Resource

Economics | Chapter 2 — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

NCERT Textbook Exercises — 100% Question Coverage
Question 1
What do you understand by 'people as resource'? How is human resource superior to other resources like land and physical capital?
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

'People as Resource' is a way of referring to a country’s working population in terms of their existing productive skills and abilities. When investments are made in education, training, and healthcare, the population transforms into Human Capital, capable of generating national wealth.

Why Human Resource is Superior:

  • Land and physical capital (machinery, raw materials) are inert, passive resources. They cannot become useful or productive on their own.
  • Only human resource possesses intelligence, initiative, and skills to harness land and operate physical capital to create value. A country like Japan has virtually no natural mineral resources, yet it is rich and technologically developed because it invested heavily in human capital (education and healthcare for its people).

Question 2
What is the role of education and health in human capital formation? Explain the difference between a virtuous cycle and a vicious cycle of human development.
5 Marks (100-120 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Education and healthcare are the two foundational pillars of human capital formation:

  1. Role of Education: Education enhances intellectual horizons, expands skills, broadens cognitive capability, increases labor productivity, and opens doors to higher-paying employment. It contributes towards the growth of society by enhancing national income, cultural richness, and administrative efficiency.
  2. Role of Health: Health is an indispensable basis for realizing one’s potential. An unhealthy worker suffers from absenteeism and lower productivity, becoming a liability for an employer, whereas a healthy person has the vitality and endurance to maximize overall earning potential.

Virtuous vs Vicious Cycle:

  • Virtuous Cycle: Educated and health-conscious parents invest heavily in their children's schooling, nutrition, and hygiene because they understand the value of human capital. This creates a rising cycle of prosperity and social advancement across generations.
  • Vicious Cycle: Disadvantaged, uneducated, and impoverished parents, who lack awareness, fail to invest in their children's education and health, perpetuating a trap of low-skilled, low-wage poverty across generations.

Question 3
What is the difference between Disguised Unemployment and Seasonal Unemployment? Why is educated unemployment a peculiar problem of India?
5 Marks (100-120 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Unemployment exists when people who are willing to work at going wages cannot find jobs. Two rural forms of unemployment are:

ParameterDisguised UnemploymentSeasonal Unemployment
NaturePeople appear to be employed, but their marginal productivity is zero.People cannot find work during certain months of the year.
OccurrenceCommon on small family agricultural plots where 8 people work on a plot requiring only 5. Removing 3 makes zero difference to total output.Common in rain-fed agriculture between sowing and harvest seasons.

Why Educated Unemployment is a Peculiar Indian Problem:
In urban India, youth with matriculation, graduation, and post-graduation degrees cannot find jobs. Paradoxically, there is a surplus of educated manpower in certain general theoretical disciplines (arts, general degrees) alongside an acute shortage of technical manpower with vocational skills needed by modern industry. Education creates high career expectations, but the formal manufacturing and corporate sectors have not generated adequate skilled employment opportunities.

Question 4
Distinguish between economic activities and non-economic activities. How are market activities different from non-market activities?
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

1. Economic vs Non-Economic Activities:

  • Economic Activities: Activities performed with the objective of earning money or livelihood that add value to the national income (e.g., a teacher teaching in school, farmer selling crops, doctor treating patients for a fee).
  • Non-Economic Activities: Activities performed out of love, care, duty, or social obligation without any financial remuneration (e.g., a mother cooking food for her family, volunteering in community service).

2. Market Activities vs Non-Market Activities:

  • Market Activities: Involve remuneration to anyone who performs the activity for pay or profit. This includes production of goods and services, including government service.
  • Non-Market Activities: Production of goods and services for self-consumption. This includes processing of primary products and own-account production of fixed assets (e.g., subsistence farming where crops are consumed by farmer's family).

Policy Intervention & Government Schemes
Evaluate the role of Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan and the Mid-Day Meal Scheme in enhancing human capital formation in India.

The Government of India launched decisive initiatives: (1) Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (now Samagra Shiksha): Guarantees universal elementary education for all children aged 6 to 14, bridging gender and social category gaps through school infrastructure and bridge courses; (2) Mid-Day Meal Scheme (PM POSHAN): Provides hot, nutritious cooked meals to primary school children. This scheme achieved a dual objective: it dramatically boosted daily school attendance and enrollment while combating widespread childhood malnutrition, ensuring that children are healthy enough to learn.

Gender Disparity & Economic Value
Why are women in the unorganised sector paid lower wages than men, and why is domestic household work not counted in National Income?

Women in the unorganised sector (agriculture, domestic help, brick kilns) receive lower wages because they historically have lower access to formal education, technical training, and skill specialization. Furthermore, the unorganised sector lacks job security, maternity benefits, and legal protection against wage discrimination.

Domestic work performed by women inside their own homes (cooking, cleaning, child-rearing) is considered a non-market activity performed out of affection and duty; because no monetary transaction takes place, it is excluded from National Income calculations, despite its immense economic value.
