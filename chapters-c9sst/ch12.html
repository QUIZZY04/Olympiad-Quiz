Democratic Politics – I (Civics)

What is Democracy? Why Democracy?

Democratic Politics – I | Chapter 1 — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

NCERT Textbook Exercises — 100% Question Coverage
Question 1
What is democracy? State the four essential features that distinguish a democracy from a non-democratic government.
5 Marks (100-120 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Democracy is a form of government in which the rulers are elected by the people. However, a minimal definition is insufficient; a true constitutional democracy must exhibit four essential structural features:

  1. Major Decisions by Elected Leaders: Final decision-making power must rest strictly with those elected by the citizens. In Pakistan under General Pervez Musharraf (1999–2002), elected representatives existed, but real power rested with military officers and the National Security Council; hence, it was not a true democracy.
  2. Free and Fair Electoral Competition: Elections must offer a real political choice between candidates. In China, elections occur regularly, but candidates must be approved by the Chinese Communist Party (CCP), giving no genuine choice. In Mexico until 2000, the PRI won every election through fraud and state co-option. A democracy requires that those currently in power have a fair chance of losing.
  3. One Person, One Vote, One Value: Each adult citizen must have one vote, and each vote must carry equal weight. In Saudi Arabia (until recently, women could not vote), Estonia (citizenship rules made it hard for Russian minorities to vote), and Fiji (indigenous Fijian votes counted more than Indo-Fijian votes), this principle was violated.
  4. Rule of Law and Respect for Rights: A democratic government must rule within limits set by constitutional law and citizens' rights. In Zimbabwe, Robert Mugabe won elections, but amended the constitution to harass opposition, censor media, and ignore court orders, creating an electoral autocracy.

Question 2
Critically examine the arguments against and arguments in favour of democracy.
5 Marks (100-120 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Democracy is not a magical cure for all human problems, but it remains the best known form of governance:

Arguments Against Democracy (Demerits):

  • Instability: Frequent changes in political leadership lead to policy inconsistency.
  • Lack of Morality: Democracy is often reduced to ruthless political competition and power play, leaving little room for ethics.
  • Delays in Decision-Making: Consulting diverse groups, parliamentary committees, and public debates inevitably slows down policymaking.
  • Electoral Corruption: Excessive expenditure during election campaigns fosters political corruption.

Arguments In Favour of Democracy (Merits):

  1. More Accountable Government: Rulers must respond to citizens' needs. For example, during the 1958–1961 famine, China lost nearly 30 million people under one-party rule, whereas multi-party democratic India did not experience famine of such severity because the government was held accountable by an independent press and opposition.
  2. Improves the Quality of Decision-Making: Involves widespread consultation, deliberation, and discussion, reducing the likelihood of rash, autocratic errors.
  3. Provides a Peaceful Method to Resolve Conflicts: In diverse societies like India with distinct languages and religions, democracy enables peaceful coexistence without perpetual civil war.
  4. Enhances the Dignity of Citizens: Recognizes all citizens as equal rulers, irrespective of wealth or education.
  5. Allows Correction of its Own Mistakes: Flawed decisions can be openly debated, and ruling parties can be voted out.
CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Key arguments against democracy (Instability, delays, political corruption)2.0 Marks
Arguments for: Accountability with historical China-India famine evidence1.5 Marks
Arguments for: Conflict resolution, citizen dignity, error self-correction1.5 Marks
Question 3
What are the major merits of a democratic form of government? State five compelling arguments in favour of democracy.
5 Marks (100-120 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Democracy is considered the finest form of government because of several institutional advantages:

  1. Accountability to the People: A democratic government is a more accountable government. Rulers must respond to the needs and grievances of citizens, failing which they are voted out in elections.
  2. Improves the Quality of Decision-Making: Democracy is based on consultation, discussion, and debate in parliaments. When many minds sit together, potential mistakes and rash judgments are discovered and corrected.
  3. Provides a Method to Deal with Differences and Conflicts: In a diverse country like India with diverse languages, religions, and castes, conflicting interests are inevitable. Democracy provides peaceful, constitutional mechanisms where no group is a permanent winner or permanent loser.
  4. Enhances the Dignity of Citizens: Democracy is based on the principle of political equality—the poorest and least educated citizen possesses the exact same voting power and constitutional rights as the richest and most educated.
  5. Allows Room to Correct Mistakes: Democratic rulers cannot hide their blunders. Public debate ensures mistakes are exposed, and citizens can compel rulers to change policies or change the rulers themselves.

Accountability and responsive governance1.0 Mark
Quality of consultative decision making1.0 Mark
Peaceful conflict resolution in diverse societies1.0 Mark
Enhancing citizen dignity through political equality1.0 Mark

Question 4
Explain the broader meaning of democracy beyond a mere form of government.
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

In everyday discourse, we view democracy as a form of government where citizens elect representatives. However, in its broader philosophical and societal sense, democracy is a universal principle of decision-making:

  • Democratic Decision-Making: A democratic decision involves consultation with and consent of all those who are affected by that decision. Those who are not powerful have the same say in taking the decision as those who are powerful.
  • Application to Family & Classroom: A family is democratic when parents and children sit together and arrive at common decisions without authoritarian dictate. A classroom is democratic when teachers allow students to ask questions freely.
  • Democracy as an Ideal: No country in the world is a perfect democracy; rather, democracy provides an ideal standard that all nations must continually strive towards—ensuring no citizen goes to bed hungry, and every citizen enjoys equal voice.
CBSE Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Democracy as principle of consultation and consent1.5 Marks
Application in daily institutions (family, school, workplace)1.5 Marks
Democracy as an ongoing aspirational ideal1.0 Mark
🎯 Competency & Case-Based Questions — Unit 12CBSE 2026-27
Case Study & Critical Thinking
Why is regular holding of elections alone not a sufficient guarantee of democracy? Explain with reference to Mexico under the PRI.

Elections alone do not constitute a democracy if they lack genuine competition and fairness. In Mexico, elections were held every six years from 1930 to 2000, yet the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) won every single presidential election.

The PRI used illicit state patronage: government employees were forced to attend party meetings, teachers coerced parents to vote for the PRI, television and radio networks gave exclusively favorable coverage to the ruling party, and polling booths were shifted at the eleventh hour. Because the opposition could never realistically win, it was an electoral autocracy, proving that free and fair opportunity to defeat incumbents is essential.

Democratic Values & Broader Application
What is meant by the 'broader meaning of democracy'? How can democratic principles be applied to everyday life beyond government?

Beyond an institutional system of government, democracy is a fundamental decision-making philosophy based on consultation, equality, and consent. A democratic decision involves all those who are affected by that decision, where the voice of the powerless carries the exact same weight as the voice of the most powerful.

This principle can be applied universally: (1) In a family, where children and parents discuss decisions together rather than one elder dictating; (2) In a classroom, where students are encouraged to ask questions freely; (3) In workplace organizations, where cooperative consultation replaces autocratic managerial diktats.
