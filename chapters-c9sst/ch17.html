Question 1
What are the four requirements for the production of goods and services (Factors of Production)? Explain with reference to farming.
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The production of goods and services requires the combination of four essential factors of production:

  1. Land: The primary fixed natural resource, including water, forests, and minerals. In farming, land is the basic surface on which crops are grown.
  2. Labour: Human physical and mental effort. Farming requires heavy manual labour for plowing, sowing, weeding, and harvesting. Small farmers provide their own family labour, while medium/large farmers hire landless farm labourers.
  3. Physical Capital: The variety of inputs required at every stage of production:
    • Fixed Capital: Tools, machines, tractors, tube wells, and buildings that can be reused over many years.
    • Working Capital: Raw materials and money in hand (HYV seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, cash to pay daily wages) consumed during production.
  4. Human Capital: The knowledge, enterprise, and skills needed to combine land, labour, and physical capital to produce an output for the market or self-consumption.

Question 2
What are the different ways of increasing production on the same piece of land? Use examples to explain.
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Since land area under cultivation is practically fixed, agricultural output is maximized through two primary methods:

  1. Multiple Cropping: Growing more than one crop on the same piece of land during a single year. In Palampur, farmers with reliable electricity-driven tube-well irrigation grow three crops annually: Kharif (Jowar/Bajra for cattle fodder during monsoons), followed by Potato as an intermediate crop, and Rabi (Wheat during winter).
  2. Modern Farming Methods (The Green Revolution): Using scientific agricultural inputs introduced in the late 1960s:
    • High Yielding Varieties (HYV) of Seeds: Produce far greater amounts of grain on a single plant compared to traditional seeds.
    • Chemical Fertilizers and Pesticides: Provide immediate plant nutrients and protect against pests.
    • Assured Irrigation: Electric tube wells, canals, and pump sets ensuring uninterrupted water supply.
    • Farm Machinery: Tractors and threshers for faster plowing and harvesting.

Question 3
How do medium and large farmers obtain capital for farming? How is it different from the small farmers?
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The mechanism of securing agricultural capital differs sharply across economic classes in rural India:

  • Medium and Large Farmers: They possess large landholdings and generate substantial surplus farm production. After keeping grain for family consumption, they sell surplus wheat in the market, earn handsome profits, and save money in bank accounts. They use these accumulated savings to purchase working capital (HYV seeds, fertilizers) for the next season or invest in tractors and tube wells. They also lend money to small farmers at high interest rates.
  • Small Farmers: Small farmers (like Savita) cultivate tiny plots (less than 2 hectares). Their total yield is barely enough for family subsistence, leaving zero marketable surplus. To arrange capital for seeds and fertilizers, they are forced to borrow from local moneylenders or large farmers (like Tejpal Singh) at extortionate interest rates (24% to 36%), often agreeing to work as underpaid bonded labourers on their fields during peak harvest seasons, trapping them in chronic debt.

Environmental Sustainability & Green Revolution
Scientific reports indicate that modern farming methods have overused the natural resource base. Justify this statement with reference to land and groundwater.

While the Green Revolution dramatically boosted crop yields, it caused severe environmental degradation: (1) Depletion of Groundwater: Continuous pumping of water through electric tube wells for thirsty HYV crops has lowered the water table drastically across Punjab, Haryana, and Western UP, threatening future agricultural viability; (2) Loss of Soil Fertility: Excessive application of chemical fertilizers kills beneficial soil bacteria and microorganisms. Fertilizers dissolve quickly in water and leach into groundwater, polluting drinking wells, rivers, and lakes, and forcing farmers to use progressively more chemicals each year to sustain the same yield.

Rural Non-Farm Economy
Why is it essential to promote non-farm production activities (dairy, small-scale manufacturing, transport) in rural India?

In rural India, land is scarce, unequally distributed, and agriculture suffers from severe disguised unemployment. Farm employment cannot absorb the growing rural workforce. Non-farm activities (such as village dairy, jaggery crushing, shopkeeping, tailoring, auto-rickshaw transport) require very little land, can be started with modest working capital, and provide year-round livelihood stability, reducing rural poverty and curbing distress migration to overcrowded urban slums.
