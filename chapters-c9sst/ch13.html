Democratic Politics – I (Civics)

Constitutional Design

Democratic Politics – I | Chapter 2 — CBSE Marking Scheme 2026-27

NCERT Textbook Exercises — 100% Question Coverage
Question 1
What was the Apartheid system in South Africa? How was it dismantled to establish a multiracial democratic constitution?
5 Marks (100-120 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Apartheid was an institutionalised system of racial discrimination and segregation imposed by the white European minority government on South Africa between 1948 and 1994:

  • Features of Apartheid: The population was divided into Whites, Blacks (three-fourths of the population), and Coloureds. Non-whites were forbidden from living in white areas without work permits. Trains, buses, hotels, hospitals, schools, and beaches were strictly segregated (known as 'petty apartheid'). Non-whites were denied voting rights and the right to form political associations.
  • Dismantling of Apartheid: Under the leadership of the African National Congress (ANC) and leaders like Nelson Mandela (who was imprisoned on Robben Island for 28 years), mass protests, strikes, and international sanctions mounted.
  • Transition to Democracy: Realising they could no longer rule by force, President F.W. de Klerk repealed discriminatory laws, lifted the ban on the ANC, and released Mandela in 1990. On 26 April 1994, South Africa emerged as a multiracial democracy. Both whites and blacks negotiated an extraordinary constitution ensuring equal human rights, mutual forgiveness, and power-sharing.

Question 2
Why do we need a Constitution? State the four major functions performed by a Constitution in a democracy.
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

A Constitution is the supreme set of written rules accepted by all citizens living together in a sovereign nation. It performs four vital functions:

  1. Generates Mutual Trust: It creates the degree of trust and coordination essential for people belonging to different religious, linguistic, and cultural groups to live peacefully together.
  2. Specifies Institutional Framework: It defines how the government will be constituted, who will exercise executive powers, and how decisions will be taken.
  3. Lays Down Limits on Power: It sets clear, enforceable boundaries on governmental authority and protects the fundamental rights of citizens against state tyranny.
  4. Expresses Collective Aspirations: It encapsulates the hopes of the people to build a just, equitable, and progressive society.

Question 3
Explain the major guiding values of the Indian Constitution as reflected in the Preamble.
5 Marks (100-120 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The Preamble serves as the preface and guiding philosophy of the Constitution of India, embedding core democratic ideals:

  1. We, the People of India: The Constitution was drawn up and enacted by the people through their elected representatives, not handed down by a king or outside power (popular sovereignty).
  2. Sovereign: India has supreme power to make decisions on internal as well as external matters; no foreign government can dictate terms.
  3. Socialist: Wealth generated socially should be shared equitably by society; government regulates land and industry ownership to reduce socio-economic inequalities.
  4. Secular: Citizens have complete freedom to follow any religion, and there is no official state religion; all religions are treated with equal respect.
  5. Democratic: A form of government where people enjoy equal political rights, elect their rulers, and hold them accountable.
  6. Republic: The head of the state (President) is an elected person and not a hereditary monarch.
  7. Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity: Guarantees social, economic, and political justice; freedom of thought and belief; equality of status and opportunity; and fraternity assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity of the Nation.

Question 4
Explain the major factors that contributed to the making of the Indian Constitution.
5 Marks (100-120 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

The drafting of the Constitution of India was aided by several historical, political, and institutional factors:

  1. Consensus during the National Movement: Unlike many newly independent countries, India's freedom struggle was not merely an anti-colonial movement, but a struggle to transform and democratize society. Basic values like universal adult franchise, right to freedom and equality, and minority protection had already gained national consensus through the Motilal Nehru Draft (1928) and the Karachi Resolution of INC (1931).
  2. Familiarity with Colonial Institutions: The British rule introduced rudimentary legislative institutions under the Government of India Acts (especially 1935). Indian leaders had gained valuable parliamentary experience working in provincial legislatures.
  3. Selective Learning from World Constitutions: Our leaders drew inspiration from the best practices across the world without blind copying: Parliamentary democracy and Rule of Law from Britain, Bill of Rights from the US Constitution, Directive Principles of State Policy from Ireland, and Ideals of Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity from the French Revolution.
  4. Representative Character of the Constituent Assembly: Elected by provincial legislatures, the Constituent Assembly represented all regions, religions, languages, and social classes. In 299 members, statesmen like Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, Jawaharlal Nehru, and Sardar Patel deliberated for 114 days over three years.

Consensus from freedom struggle (Nehru Draft 1928, Karachi 1931)1.5 Marks
Colonial legislative experience (Govt of India Act 1935)1.0 Mark
Synthesis of global constitutional ideals (US, UK, France, Ireland)1.5 Marks
Constituent Assembly deliberations and leadership1.0 Mark

Historical Foundation & Drafting Assembly
What factors contributed to the widespread public legitimacy and enduring acceptance of the Indian Constitution for over seven decades?

The enduring success of the Indian Constitution is grounded in: (1) Broad Representation: The Constituent Assembly represented members from all provinces, religious groups, castes, classes, and linguistic communities; (2) Consensus through Deliberation: Every clause was debated for 114 days over nearly three years in an open, transparent, and rational manner (documented in the 12 volumes of Constituent Assembly Debates); (3) Pre-existing Democratic Values: Consensus on key values (universal adult franchise, liberty, minority rights) had already been forged during the freedom struggle (Nehru Report 1928, Karachi Session 1931); (4) Living Document: It provides a flexible amendment process (Article 368) to evolve with changing societal aspirations.

Comparative Constitutional Analysis
How did South Africa's transition from racial apartheid to a constitutional democracy inspire the world?

South Africa’s transition is widely regarded as a modern constitutional miracle. After decades of state-sponsored torture and brutal racial oppression, the black majority opted not for violent retribution, but for national reconciliation and power-sharing with their former white oppressors.

Their 1996 constitution established the most extensive charter of human, socio-economic, and environmental rights in the world, embodying the spirit that no historical injustice should be repeated and that all races must build a shared, democratic future together.
