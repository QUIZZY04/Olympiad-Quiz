Question 1
What makes an election democratic? Enumerate the essential conditions of a democratic election.
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

For elections to be genuinely democratic, five minimum conditions must be fulfilled:

  1. Universal Adult Franchise: Everyone must have one vote, and every vote must have equal value ('One Person, One Vote, One Value').
  2. Real Choice of Alternatives: Parties and candidates must be free to contest elections and offer real, distinct policy choices to the electorate.
  3. Regular Intervals: Elections must be held regularly every few years (e.g., every five years in India) so that citizens can hold leaders accountable.
  4. Candidate of Choice Wins: The candidate preferred by the majority of the people must actually be declared elected.
  5. Free and Fair Atmosphere: Elections must be conducted in a completely independent, transparent environment where citizens can vote without fear, coercion, or intimidation.

Question 2
What are reserved constituencies? Why do we have reserved constituencies in India?
4 Marks (60-80 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Reserved Constituencies: Electoral areas specifically set aside where only candidates belonging to certain disadvantaged social categories can contest elections.

In the Lok Sabha, seats are reserved for Scheduled Castes (SC: 84 seats) and Scheduled Tribes (ST: 47 seats) in proportion to their population share.

Why We Have Reserved Constituencies:

  • The makers of the Constitution were conscious that in an open competitive election, socially and educationally weaker sections might not possess the resources, education, and connections to win against dominant, influential classes.
  • If deprived communities were shut out of the legislature, Parliament would lack the authentic voice of a significant segment of our population, making our democracy less representative and less legitimate.
  • Reservations guarantee institutional political empowerment to historic victims of caste oppression.

Question 3
What makes elections in India democratic? Explain the role and powers of the Election Commission of India.
5 Marks (100-120 words)
✅ CBSE Standard Answer (Max Word Count)

Elections in India are recognized globally as free and fair due to the extraordinary autonomy of the Election Commission of India (ECI) under Article 324:

  1. Constitutional Independence: The Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) is appointed by the President, but once appointed, the CEC is not answerable to the government or the President. Removing a CEC requires the same near-impossible impeachment procedure as a Supreme Court judge.
  2. Comprehensive Electoral Control: The ECI takes decisions on every aspect of the conduct and management of elections, from the announcement of polling dates to the declaration of results.
  3. Enforcement of Model Code of Conduct (MCC): It strictly enforces the MCC; it can reprimand or disqualify candidates and parties who use bribery, hate speech, or appeal to caste/religion.
  4. Command over Government Machinery: During election periods, civil servants and police officers on election duty operate under the direct disciplinary command of the ECI, not the ruling state or central governments.
  5. Power to Order Repolls: If polling at any polling booth or constituency is compromised by booth-capturing or EVM rigging, the ECI orders a fresh poll.

Electoral Challenges & Reforms
Despite an independent Election Commission, what major challenges prevent Indian elections from being fully fair and equal?

Indian elections face several deeply rooted structural challenges: (1) Money Power: Wealthy candidates and major parties enjoy an unfair advantage over smaller parties and independents, using illicit cash to sway voters; (2) Criminalisation of Politics: Candidates with serious criminal records secure tickets from major parties by leveraging muscle power; (3) Dynastic Politics: Tickets are disproportionately allocated to relatives of established political families; (4) Lack of Genuine Policy Choice: Major political parties often adopt similar economic and social policies, leaving voters with little ideological variety.

Voter Turnout & Democratic Participation
Unlike Western democracies, voter turnout in India is higher among the poor, illiterate, and underprivileged. Explain this unique sociological phenomenon.

In established Western democracies (like the USA), voter turnout is highest among affluent, white, highly educated citizens. In India, voter participation is significantly higher among Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, OBCs, and rural poor. This occurs because:

(1) Underprivileged citizens perceive elections as their primary weapon to exert direct pressure on the ruling class for food security, pensions, housing, and social dignity; (2) They strongly believe that their vote directly matters in shaping government policies; (3) Voting day is experienced as a profound festival of democratic equality where the poorest laborer stands in the exact same queue as the wealthiest industrialist.
