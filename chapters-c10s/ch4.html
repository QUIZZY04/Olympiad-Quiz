In-Text Page 61 • Q1
What would be the electron dot structure of carbon dioxide which has the formula $\text{CO}_2$?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Electron-dot Structure of Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$):

  1. Electronic Configuration:
    • Carbon ($Z=6$): $2, 4$ (needs 4 electrons to complete octet).
    • Oxygen ($Z=8$): $2, 6$ (needs 2 electrons to complete octet).
  2. Bonding Mechanism: The central carbon atom shares two pairs of electrons with each of the two oxygen atoms, forming two double covalent bonds: $$\mathbf{:\ddot{\text{O}}::C::\ddot{\text{O}}:} \quad \text{or} \quad \text{O}=\text{C}=\text{O}$$
  3. Each oxygen atom attains a stable neon octet ($8e^-$) and the central carbon atom attains a stable neon octet ($8e^-$).

In-Text Page 61 • Q2
What would be the electron dot structure of a molecule of sulphur which is made up of eight atoms of sulphur? (Hint: The eight atoms of sulphur are joined together in the shape of a ring.)
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Electron-dot Structure of Octasulphur ($S_8$):

  1. Electronic Configuration: Sulphur ($Z=16$): $2, 8, 6$ (has 6 valence electrons, requires 2 more electrons to complete its octet).
  2. Ring Structure (Crown/Puckered Conformation): In an $S_8$ molecule, each of the eight sulphur atoms forms single covalent bonds with two adjacent sulphur atoms by sharing one electron pair each: $$-S-S-S-S-S-S-S-S- \quad \text{(closed ring)}$$
  3. Each sulphur atom retains two non-bonding lone pairs ($4e^-$) and shares two single bonding pairs ($4e^-$), completing its stable octet of 8 electrons.

In-Text Page 68 • Q1
How many structural isomers can you draw for pentane?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Three Structural Isomers of Pentane ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{12}$):

  1. 1. n-Pentane (Pentane): Straight unbranched five-carbon chain: $$\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_3$$
  2. 2. Isopentane (2-Methylbutane): Four-carbon chain with a methyl branch at C-2: $$\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)-\text{CH}_2-\text{CH}_3$$
  3. 3. Neopentane (2,2-Dimethylpropane): Three-carbon chain with two methyl branches at C-2: $$\text{CH}_3-\text{C}(\text{CH}_3)_2-\text{CH}_3$$

Therefore, exactly 3 structural isomers exist for pentane.

In-Text Page 68 • Q2
What are the two properties of carbon which lead to the huge number of carbon compounds we see around us?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Two Unique Characteristics of Carbon:

  1. 1. Catenation: Carbon has the unique ability to form exceptionally strong, stable covalent bonds with other carbon atoms, giving rise to long straight chains, branched chains, and closed rings of arbitrary length and complexity. The small size of the carbon atom makes the $C-C$ bond energy very high ($348\text{ kJ/mol}$).
  2. 2. Tetravalency: Having a valency of four, a carbon atom is capable of bonding with four other atoms of carbon or atoms of monovalent elements (H, Cl) or polyvalent elements (O, N, S). It can also form single, double, and triple bonds ($C-C, C=C, C\equiv C$).

In-Text Page 71 • Q1
Why is the conversion of ethanol to ethanoic acid an oxidation reaction?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Chemical Explanation:

The conversion of ethanol to ethanoic acid is an oxidation reaction because it involves the addition of oxygen and removal of hydrogen:

$$\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{Alkaline }\text{KMnO}_4 + \Delta} \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$$
  • Ethanol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{O}$) contains 1 oxygen atom and 6 hydrogen atoms.
  • Ethanoic acid ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_4\text{O}_2$) contains 2 oxygen atoms and 4 hydrogen atoms.
  • There is a net gain of one oxygen atom and a loss of two hydrogen atoms per molecule, mediated by strong oxidising agents like alkaline $\text{KMnO}_4$ or acidified $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$.

In-Text Page 71 • Q2
A mixture of oxygen and ethyne is burnt for welding. Can you tell why a mixture of ethyne and air is not used?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Combustion Chemistry:

  1. Ethyne (acetylene, $\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH}$) has a very high carbon-to-hydrogen ratio ($92.3\%$ carbon by mass).
  2. When burnt in air: Atmospheric air contains only about $21\%$ oxygen by volume. Due to the limited oxygen supply, incomplete combustion occurs, producing a luminous, sooty flame with black smoke (unburnt carbon particles) and a relatively low flame temperature (around $2200^\circ\text{C}$), which is inadequate for welding high-melting metals.
  3. When burnt in pure oxygen (Oxy-acetylene torch): Complete combustion occurs according to: $$2\text{C}_2\text{H}_2(g) + 5\text{O}_2(g) \longrightarrow 4\text{CO}_2(g) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(g) + \text{Heat}$$ This produces a non-sooty, clean blue flame reaching an extremely high temperature of approximately $3000^\circ\text{C} - 3300^\circ\text{C}$, which instantly melts metals to weld them.

NCERT Exercise • Q4
Explain the nature of the covalent bond using the bond formation in $\text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Bond Formation in Chloromethane ($\text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$):

  1. Valence Electron Count:
    • Carbon ($Z=6$): $2, 4$ (needs 4 electrons).
    • Hydrogen ($Z=1$): $1$ (needs 1 electron).
    • Chlorine ($Z=17$): $2, 8, 7$ (needs 1 electron).
  2. Sharing of Electron Pairs: Carbon shares one electron each with three hydrogen atoms to form three $C-H$ single covalent bonds. It also shares its fourth valence electron with one chlorine atom to form a $C-Cl$ single covalent bond.
  3. Covalent & Polar Nature: All four bonds are formed by mutual sharing of electrons (covalent). However, chlorine is substantially more electronegative than carbon, pulling the shared pair of electrons towards itself. This creates a partial negative charge on chlorine ($\delta^-$) and a partial positive charge on carbon ($\delta^+$), giving the $C-Cl$ bond a polar covalent nature.

NCERT Exercise • Q6
What is an homologous series? Explain with an example.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Definition:

A series of structurally related organic compounds in which the same functional group substitutes for hydrogen in a carbon chain, such that successive members differ by a $-\text{CH}_2-$ unit (methylene group) and by 14 atomic mass units (u), is called an homologous series.

Example: Homologous Series of Alkanes (General formula: $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$):

  1. Methane ($\text{CH}_4$, mass = $16\text{ u}$)
  2. Ethane ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_6$, mass = $30\text{ u}$, difference = $-\text{CH}_2-$ and $14\text{ u}$)
  3. Propane ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_8$, mass = $44\text{ u}$)
  4. Butane ($\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}$, mass = $58\text{ u}$)

All members exhibit identical chemical properties but show a gradual gradation in physical properties (boiling point, melting point, density).

NCERT Exercise • Q8
Why does micelle formation take place when soap is added to water? Will a micelle be formed in other solvents such as ethanol also?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Mechanism of Micelle Formation:

  1. Soap Molecule Structure: A soap molecule (e.g., sodium stearate $\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COO}^-\text{Na}^+$) has two distinct parts:
    • A long hydrocarbon tail which is hydrophobic (water-fearing) and lipophilic (oil-loving).
    • An ionic carboxylate head ($-\text{COO}^-\text{Na}^+$) which is hydrophilic (water-loving).
  2. Micelle Assembly in Water: When dissolved in water, the hydrophobic hydrocarbon tails cluster together inside the core to escape contact with water, while the hydrophilic ionic heads point outwards into the aqueous medium. This spherical aggregate is called a micelle.
  3. Will Micelles Form in Ethanol?
    No, micelles will not form in ethanol. Ethanol is an organic solvent that is non-polar/mildly polar. The hydrocarbon tails of soap are soluble in ethanol, so they dissolve uniformly throughout ethanol rather than aggregating into micelles.

NCERT Exercise • Q10
Explain the formation of scum when hard water is treated with soap.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Formation of Scum:

Hard water contains dissolved salts of calcium and magnesium, such as calcium hydrogen carbonate $\text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2$ or magnesium sulphate $\text{MgSO}_4$.

When soap (sodium stearate, $\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COONa}$) is added to hard water, the sodium ions in soap are replaced by calcium and magnesium ions. This produces calcium and magnesium stearates, which are insoluble in water and separate as a curdy, sticky white precipitate called scum:

$$2\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COONa} + \text{Ca}^{2+} \longrightarrow (\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COO})_2\text{Ca}\downarrow\text{ (Scum)} + 2\text{Na}^+$$

This scum wastes soap and sticks to fabrics, hindering lather formation until all $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions are precipitated.

NCERT Exercise • Q11
What change will you observe if you test soap with litmus paper (red and blue)?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Observation and Chemical Reason:

  1. Observation:
    • Red litmus paper turns blue.
    • Blue litmus paper remains blue.
  2. Explanation: Soap is a sodium or potassium salt of a weak long-chain carboxylic acid (fatty acid) and a strong base (sodium hydroxide, $\text{NaOH}$). When dissolved in water, soap undergoes anionic hydrolysis: $$\text{RCOO}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{RCOOH} + \text{OH}^-$$ The release of excess hydroxide ions ($\text{OH}^-$) makes the soap solution basic/alkaline in nature ($\text{pH} \approx 8.5 - 9.5$). Consequently, it turns red litmus blue.

NCERT Exercise • Q12
What is hydrogenation? What is its industrial application?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Definition and Reaction:

The addition of hydrogen to unsaturated hydrocarbons (alkenes or alkynes having double or triple bonds) in the presence of a finely divided catalyst (such as Nickel ($\text{Ni}$) or Palladium) to convert them into saturated hydrocarbons is called hydrogenation:

$$\text{R}_2\text{C}=\text{CR}_2 + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ni catalyst, } 200^\circ\text{C}} \text{R}_2\text{CH}-\text{CHR}_2$$

Industrial Application:

Hydrogenation is widely used in the food industry to convert liquid unsaturated vegetable oils (which contain healthy double bonds) into solid edible saturated fats known as Vanaspati ghee (margarine):

$$\text{Vegetable oil (liquid)} + \text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ni}} \text{Vanaspati Ghee (solid)}$$

NCERT Exercise • Q13
Which of the following hydrocarbons undergo addition reactions? $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6$, $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8$, $\text{C}_3\text{H}_6$, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$ and $\text{CH}_4$.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Analysis of Hydrocarbons:

Only unsaturated hydrocarbons (alkenes $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$ and alkynes $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n-2}$) possess carbon-carbon double or triple bonds and undergo addition reactions.

  1. $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6$ (Ethane, $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$): Saturated alkane → No addition reaction.
  2. $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8$ (Propane, $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$): Saturated alkane → No addition reaction.
  3. $\text{C}_3\text{H}_6$ (Propene, $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$): Unsaturated alkene → YES, undergoes addition reaction.
  4. $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$ (Ethyne, $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n-2}$): Unsaturated alkyne → YES, undergoes addition reaction.
  5. $\text{CH}_4$ (Methane, $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$): Saturated alkane → No addition reaction.

Answer: $\text{C}_3\text{H}_6$ (Propene) and $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$ (Ethyne).

NCERT Exercise • Q14
Give a test that can be used to differentiate chemically between butter and cooking oil.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Chemical Test: Bromine Water Test

  1. Procedure: Take about 2 mL of liquid butter and 2 mL of cooking oil in separate test tubes. Add a few drops of reddish-brown bromine water (or alkaline potassium permanganate) to each test tube and shake well.
  2. Cooking Oil (Unsaturated fat): Cooking oil contains long unsaturated carbon chains with double bonds. It undergoes an addition reaction with bromine, causing the reddish-brown colour of bromine water to decolourise and turn colourless.
  3. Butter (Saturated fat): Butter contains saturated fatty acid chains. It does not react with bromine water under normal conditions; the reddish-brown colour of bromine water persists unchanged.

NCERT Exercise • Q15
Explain the mechanism of the cleaning action of soaps.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Cleaning Mechanism of Soap:

  1. Structure of Soap: A soap molecule has an elongated structure with two contrasting ends:
    • A non-polar hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail that repels water but dissolves in oil and grease.
    • A polar hydrophilic ionic head ($-\text{COO}^-\text{Na}^+$) that dissolves in water.
  2. Micelle Formation Around Dirt: Dirt and grease on soiled clothes are oily and water-insoluble. When soap is added to water containing dirty clothes, the hydrophobic tails embed themselves directly into the oil droplets, while the hydrophilic ionic heads project outwards into the water. This forms spherical aggregates called micelles trapping the oil droplet in their center.
  3. Emulsification and Removal: The ionic heads at the surface of micelles carry identical negative charges, causing them to repel each other and prevent coagulation into larger droplets. When the water is agitated (by scrubbing or washing machine), the soap micelles containing trapped grease are pulled away from the fabric into the water, forming a stable emulsion that is easily rinsed away.

Case Study Question • Esterification and Saponification
An organic compound 'A' of molecular formula $\text{C}_2\text{H}_6\text{O}$ on heating with excess concentrated sulphuric acid at $443\text{ K}$ gives compound 'B'. Compound 'A' on oxidation with alkaline $\text{KMnO}_4$ forms compound 'C'. When 'A' and 'C' react together in the presence of an acid catalyst, a sweet-smelling compound 'D' is produced. (i) Identify compounds A, B, C and D. (ii) Write the chemical equation for the formation of compound D. (iii) What happens when compound D is heated with sodium hydroxide solution? Name this reaction.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Identification of Compounds:
    • Compound 'A' is Ethanol ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$).
    • Compound 'B' is Ethene ($\text{CH}_2=\text{CH}_2$) formed by dehydration of ethanol at $443\text{ K}$.
    • Compound 'C' is Ethanoic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$) formed by oxidation of ethanol.
    • Compound 'D' is Ethyl ethanoate (an ester, $\text{CH}_3\text{COOCH}_2\text{CH}_3$) with a pleasant fruity smell.
  2. (ii) Esterification Equation: $$\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. }\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4} \text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
  3. (iii) Reaction with $\text{NaOH}$ (Saponification): Heating ester with $\text{NaOH}$ hydrolyses it back into alcohol and sodium ethanoate (soap base): $$\text{CH}_3\text{COOC}_2\text{H}_5 + \text{NaOH} \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{COONa} + \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$$ This reaction is known as Saponification.
