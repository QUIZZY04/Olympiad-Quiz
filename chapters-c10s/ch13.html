In-Text Page 242 • Q1
What are trophic levels? Give an example of a food chain and state the different trophic levels in it.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Definition:

Each step, stage, or feeding level in a food chain where transfer of energy and organic matter takes place from one group of organisms to another is called a trophic level.

Example of a Terrestrial Grassland Food Chain:

  1. First Trophic Level ($T_1$): Producers (Green Grass) → captures solar energy via photosynthesis.
  2. Second Trophic Level ($T_2$): Primary Consumers / Herbivores (Grasshopper) → feeds on grass.
  3. Third Trophic Level ($T_3$): Secondary Consumers / Primary Carnivores (Frog) → feeds on grasshopper.
  4. Fourth Trophic Level ($T_4$): Tertiary Consumers / Top Carnivores (Snake / Hawk) → feeds on frog.

In-Text Page 242 • Q2
What is the role of decomposers in the ecosystem?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Crucial Roles of Decomposers (Bacteria and Fungi):

  1. Nutrient Recycling (Biogeochemical Cycling): Decomposers secrete extracellular digestive enzymes that break down complex organic remains of dead plants and animal wastes into simple inorganic substances (carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus). These nutrients are returned to the soil, water, and air, where they are reabsorbed by green plants.
  2. Natural Scavenging and Cleansing: They decompose organic carcasses and agricultural residues, preventing environmental fouling and the piling up of dead matter on Earth.
  3. Maintaining Ecological Equilibrium: Without decomposers, all essential mineral nutrients would remain locked inside dead biomass, leading to exhaustion of soil fertility and collapse of the ecosystem.

In-Text Page 245 • Q1
What is ozone and how does it affect any ecosystem?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Ozone ($\text{O}_3$):

  1. Structure: Ozone is an allotrope of oxygen consisting of three oxygen atoms bonded together ($\text{O}_3$). While it is a deadly poison at ground level, high up in the stratosphere it forms a protective ozone layer.
  2. Protective Ecological Role: The stratospheric ozone layer acts as a celestial shield, absorbing more than $99\%$ of harmful ultraviolet (UV-B) radiation emitted by the Sun.
  3. Consequences of UV Penetration: If the ozone layer is depleted, excessive UV radiation reaching the Earth's surface causes skin cancer, cataract blindness, immune suppression in humans, and damages photosynthetic phytoplankton, crippling marine aquatic food chains.

In-Text Page 245 • Q2
How can you help in reducing the problem of waste disposal? Give any two methods.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Methods to Reduce Waste Disposal Problems:

  1. Segregation and Composting of Biodegradable Waste: Separate domestic garbage into biodegradable (vegetable peels, leftover food, dry leaves) and non-biodegradable waste at the source. Biodegradable waste can be converted into rich organic compost or biogas in local pits, reducing landfill pressure.
  2. Adopting the 3R Principles (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle):
    • Recycle non-biodegradable materials such as plastic bottles, glass, and metal scrap.
    • Replace single-use polythene bags with reusable cloth or jute bags.

NCERT Exercise • Q4
What will happen if we kill all the organisms in one trophic level?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Ecological Consequences:

If all organisms at a specific trophic level are eliminated, it triggers a devastating ecological imbalance throughout the entire food web:

  1. Starvation of Higher Trophic Levels: The predators in the next higher trophic level will face an immediate food crisis, leading to mass starvation, population collapse, or migration.
  2. Overpopulation of Lower Trophic Levels: The prey organisms in the preceding lower trophic level, freed from predatory control, will multiply uncontrollably. For example, if all carnivores (lions) are removed, herbivorous deer will multiply excessively, overgrazing and destroying all grassland vegetation and causing desertification.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Higher trophic levels starve and decline due to absence of food source1 Mark
Lower trophic levels multiply uncontrollably causing overgrazing/destruction of producers1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q5
Will the impact of removing all the organisms in a trophic level be different for different trophic levels? Can the organisms of any trophic level be removed without causing any damage to the ecosystem?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Analysis:

  1. Impact at Different Levels: Yes, the impact will differ in nature depending on the level removed:
    • Removing producers ($T_1$) is catastrophic and immediately collapses the entire ecosystem, as all life directly or indirectly depends on primary photosynthetic energy.
    • Removing herbivores ($T_2$) causes producers to overgrow and carnivores to starve.
    • Removing decomposers leads to accumulation of dead biomass and arrest of nutrient cycling.
  2. Can any trophic level be removed without damage? No, organisms of no trophic level can be removed without causing significant damage. In an ecosystem, every organism is interlinked in complex food webs; removing any level destabilizes ecological balance.

Yes, impact varies (removing producers is most fatal to all life)1 Mark

NCERT Exercise • Q6
What is biological magnification? Will the levels of this magnification be different at different levels of the ecosystem?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Biological Magnification (Biomagnification):

  1. Definition: The progressive increase in the concentration of toxic, non-biodegradable chemical pollutants (such as DDT, mercury, cadmium) in the tissues of living organisms at each successive trophic level of a food chain is called biological magnification.
  2. Variation Across Trophic Levels: Yes, the levels of magnification are drastically different at different trophic levels. Because non-biodegradable chemicals cannot be metabolized or excreted, they accumulate in body fat. Each predator consumes many prey organisms, concentrating the toxin.
  3. Consequently, the concentration of the chemical increases exponentially with each higher trophic level, reaching the maximum concentration in top carnivores / humans.

Definition: Progressive accumulation and concentration of non-biodegradable toxins along a food chain1 Mark

NCERT Exercise • Q7
What are the problems caused by the non-biodegradable wastes that we generate?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Problems Caused by Non-biodegradable Wastes:

  1. Soil and Water Pollution: Synthetic plastics, electronic waste, and toxic chemicals persist in the environment for centuries without decomposing, leaching heavy metals into ground water and contaminating soils.
  2. Biological Magnification: Toxic chemicals enter food chains and accumulate in tissues of animals and humans, causing organ failure, neurological damage, and cancer.
  3. Blockage of Drainage & Stray Animal Death: Discarded plastic bags choke municipal sewage drains causing floods, and when ingested by stray cattle, cause fatal intestinal blockage.

NCERT Exercise • Q8
If all the waste we generate is biodegradable, will this have no impact on the environment?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Critical Evaluation:

No, even biodegradable waste will have serious negative impacts if generated in excessive amounts or handled improperly.

Reasons:

  1. Foul Odour and Air Pollution: Large mounds of decomposing organic waste emit noxious, foul-smelling gases like hydrogen sulphide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) and methane ($\text{CH}_4$), which is also a potent greenhouse gas driving global warming.
  2. Breeding Ground for Pathogens: Rotting organic matter serves as an ideal breeding ground for flies, mosquitoes, and rodents, spreading epidemic diseases like cholera, typhoid, and dengue.
  3. Eutrophication of Water Bodies: If excessive biodegradable matter enters rivers and lakes, decomposer microbes consume all dissolved oxygen (high Biological Oxygen Demand, BOD), causing aquatic hypoxia and mass fish mortality.

NCERT Exercise • Q9
Why is damage to the ozone layer a cause for concern? What steps are being taken to limit this damage?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Concerns and Mitigation Measures:

  1. Why It Is a Concern: The ozone layer shields Earth from harmful solar ultraviolet-B (UV-B) radiation. Depletion of this protective layer allows dangerous UV rays to penetrate the atmosphere, leading to:
    • High incidence of skin cancers and melanoma in humans.
    • Cataracts and damage to human eyes and immune systems.
    • Destruction of young marine phytoplankton and reduction in crop yields.
  2. Steps Taken: In 1987, the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) successfully forged an international agreement called the Montreal Protocol to freeze and phase out the production of Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) used in refrigerators, aerosols, and air-conditioners. Today, all manufacturing companies produce CFC-free appliances worldwide.

Board Benchmark • Q14
State the 10 percent law of energy transfer in a food chain. If 20,000 J of solar energy is available to plants, how much energy will be transferred to a snake in the food chain: Plants -> Mice -> Snake -> Peacock?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

10 Percent Law (Raymond Lindeman, 1942):

Only about $10\%$ of the energy entering a particular trophic level is incorporated into its biomass and made available for transfer to the next higher trophic level. The remaining $90\%$ is lost as metabolic heat, respiration, and excretion.

Energy Calculation:

  1. Plants capture only about $1\%$ of incident solar energy: $$E_{\text{Plants}} = 1\% \text{ of } 20,000\text{ J} = \mathbf{200\text{ J}}$$
  2. Energy transferred to Mice (Herbivore, $T_2$): $$E_{\text{Mice}} = 10\% \text{ of } 200\text{ J} = \mathbf{20\text{ J}}$$
  3. Energy transferred to Snake (Secondary Consumer, $T_3$): $$E_{\text{Snake}} = 10\% \text{ of } 20\text{ J} = \mathbf{2\text{ Joules}}$$

Therefore, exactly 2 Joules of energy is available to the snake.

10% law definition: only 10% energy moves to next trophic level1 Mark

Case Study Question • Kulhads vs Disposable Plastic Cups
At Indian railway stations, trains previously served tea in reusable plastic cups, then shifted briefly to earthen clay cups (kulhads), and now use disposable paper cups. (i) What was the environmental problem caused by mass usage of disposable plastic cups? (ii) Why was mass use of earthen kulhads abandoned? (iii) Why are paper cups considered the best eco-friendly alternative?

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Plastic Cups Issue: Disposable plastic cups are non-biodegradable, choke municipal drainage lines, litter railway tracks, and when incinerated, release highly toxic dioxin fumes.
  2. (ii) Kulhad Drawback: Producing billions of earthen kulhads on a nationwide scale required stripping millions of tons of fertile agricultural topsoil. Firing them in kilns also consumed large quantities of wood fuel, causing topsoil depletion and deforestation.
  3. (iii) Advantage of Paper Cups: Disposable paper cups are biodegradable, decompose naturally in compost without generating toxic residues, do not deplete topsoil, and can be recycled.
