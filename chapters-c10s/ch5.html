Question 1
What are the major events that occur during the process of photosynthesis? Write the balanced chemical equation.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Major Biochemical Events during Photosynthesis:

Photosynthesis is the autotrophic process by which green plants synthesize carbohydrates (glucose) from inorganic carbon dioxide and water in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll:

$$6\text{CO}_2(aq) + 12\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \xrightarrow[\text{Chlorophyll}]{\text{Sunlight}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6(aq) + 6\text{O}_2(g)\uparrow + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$

Three Sequential Events:

  1. 1. Absorption of Light Energy: Chlorophyll pigments located in the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts absorb solar radiant energy.
  2. 2. Conversion of Light Energy into Chemical Energy & Photolysis of Water:
    The absorbed light energy excites electrons, driving the photolysis (splitting) of water molecules into hydrogen ions, electrons, and molecular oxygen gas: $$2\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{Light, Chlorophyll}} 4\text{H}^+ + 4e^- + \text{O}_2\uparrow$$ Chemical energy is assimilated as ATP and $\text{NADPH}$.
  3. 3. Reduction of Carbon Dioxide:
    During the light-independent (dark) enzymatic reactions (Calvin cycle) occurring in the stroma, carbon dioxide is reduced to carbohydrates (glucose) using the assimilatory power of ATP and $\text{NADPH}$.

Desert Plant Adaptation: Desert plants (CAM plants) take up $\text{CO}_2$ at night and store it as an intermediate organic acid (malic acid) to prevent daytime transpirational water loss. During the day, they use absorbed sunlight to complete carbohydrate synthesis.

Question 2
How are fats digested in our bodies? Where does this process take place? Explain the role of bile juice and pancreatic lipase.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Enzymatic Digestion in the Human Small Intestine:

The small intestine is the site of complete digestion of carbohydrates, proteins, and fats. It receives secretions from two major glands:

  1. 1. Secretion from Liver — Bile Juice:
    • Neutralization: The food coming from the stomach is highly acidic ($\text{pH} \approx 1.5 - 2$). Bile juice is alkaline and neutralizes the acidic chyme, creating an alkaline medium ($\text{pH} \approx 7.8 - 8.4$) necessary for pancreatic enzymes to function.
    • Emulsification of Fats: Fats are present in the intestine as large insoluble globules, which are difficult for water-soluble enzymes to break down. Bile salts break these large fat globules down into fine droplets (micelles), enormously increasing their surface area for enzyme action.
  2. 2. Secretion from Pancreas — Pancreatic Juice:
    • Pancreatic Amylase: Hydrolyzes remaining starch into maltose.
    • Trypsin: Digests proteins, peptones, and proteoses into smaller peptides.
    • Pancreatic Lipase: Breaks down emulsified triglycerides into fatty acids and glycerol.
  3. 3. Intestinal Juice (Succus Entericus):
    Enzymes secreted by the intestinal mucosal cells complete the digestion:
    • Complex Carbohydrates $\longrightarrow$ Glucose
    • Proteins $\longrightarrow$ Amino acids
    • Fats $\longrightarrow$ Fatty acids and Glycerol

Question 3
Explain the three different pathways by which glucose is broken down to release energy in various organisms.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Three Pathways of Glucose Breakdown in Respiration:

The first step in all living organisms is Glycolysis, occurring in the Cytoplasm, where 1 molecule of 6-carbon Glucose is broken down into two 3-carbon molecules of Pyruvate with a net gain of 2 ATP.

  1. Pathway 1: In the Absence of Oxygen (Anaerobic Respiration / Fermentation in Yeast):
    $$\text{Pyruvate} \xrightarrow{\text{In Yeast (Fermentation)}} 2\text{ Ethanol} (\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}) + 2\text{CO}_2 + \text{Energy (2 ATP)}$$ Incomplete breakdown yielding ethanol and carbon dioxide.
  2. Pathway 2: In Lack of Oxygen (In Human Skeletal Muscle Cells during Vigorous Exercise):
    $$\text{Pyruvate} \xrightarrow{\text{Lack of O}_2 \text{ in muscles}} 2\text{ Lactic acid} (\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}_3) + \text{Energy (2 ATP)}$$ The accumulation of lactic acid crystals inside fatigued muscle fibers causes painful muscle cramps.
  3. Pathway 3: In the Presence of Oxygen (Aerobic Respiration in Mitochondria):
    $$\text{Pyruvate} + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Mitochondria (Krebs cycle)}} 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Energy (36 to 38 ATP)}$$ Complete aerobic oxidation yielding carbon dioxide, water, and an enormous amount of metabolic energy.

Question 4
Describe double circulation of blood in human beings. Why is it necessary? Differentiate between arteries and veins.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Double Circulation in the Human Body:

1. Definition:

In human beings and other birds and mammals, blood travels through the heart twice during one complete cardiac cycle. Hence, it is termed Double Circulation. It consists of two distinct circulatory pathways:

  1. (a) Pulmonary Circulation:
    Deoxygenated blood collected from body tissues enters the Right Atrium $\to$ passes to Right Ventricle $\to$ pumped via Pulmonary Artery to the Lungs for oxygenation. Oxygenated blood from the lungs returns via Pulmonary Veins into the Left Atrium.
  2. (b) Systemic Circulation:
    Oxygen-rich blood passes from Left Atrium $\to$ Left Ventricle $\to$ pumped under high pressure through the Aorta to all systemic body organs and tissues. Deoxygenated blood loaded with metabolic $\text{CO}_2$ is returned via Superior and Inferior Vena Cava into the Right Atrium.

2. Why Double Circulation is Necessary in Humans:

  • Complete Separation of Oxygenated and Deoxygenated Blood: The 4-chambered heart (two atria, two ventricles) completely prevents the intermixing of oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood.
  • High Oxygen Delivery Efficiency: Birds and mammals are warm-blooded (endothermic) animals. They require an enormous amount of cellular energy to constantly maintain their constant core body temperature ($37^\circ\text{C}$ in humans) regardless of external environmental temperature fluctuations. Double circulation ensures a rapid and highly pressurized supply of oxygen to all cells.

3. Structural Differences: Arteries vs Veins:

Feature Arteries Veins
Direction of Flow Carry blood away from heart to body organs. Carry blood towards heart from body organs.
Vessel Walls Thick, highly elastic and muscular walls (withstand high pressure). Thin, less muscular walls (blood under low pressure).
Internal Valves Valves are absent (blood flows under high ventricular pressure). Have semilunar valves to prevent backflow of blood.

Question 5
Describe the structure and functioning of a nephron. Explain the three steps involved in the formation of urine in human kidneys.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Structure and Functioning of the Nephron:

The Nephron is the fundamental microscopic structural and functional filtration unit of the human kidney. Each human kidney contains approximately 1 to 1.2 million nephrons.

1. Structural Components of a Nephron:

  1. Bowman's Capsule: A cup-shaped double-walled sac located in the renal cortex.
  2. Glomerulus: A dense tuft of microscopic blood capillaries enclosed within Bowman's capsule, supplied by an afferent renal arteriole and drained by an efferent arteriole. Bowman's capsule and Glomerulus together form the Malpighian corpuscle.
  3. Renal Tubule: A long, highly coiled tubule consisting of:
    • Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)
    • Loop of Henle (hairpin loop descending into renal medulla)
    • Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)
    • Collecting Duct, which drains into the renal pelvis and ureter.

2. Three Steps of Urine Formation:

  1. Ultrafiltration (Glomerular Filtration):
    The diameter of the afferent arteriole is wider than that of the efferent arteriole. This creates very high hydrostatic pressure inside the glomerulus. Blood is filtered across the capillary endothelial pores and podocyte filtration slits into Bowman's capsule space. The resulting initial filtrate (about 180 litres/day in a healthy adult) contains water, glucose, amino acids, mineral salts, urea, and uric acid, but is free of blood cells and plasma proteins.
  2. Selective Reabsorption:
    As the filtrate flows down the PCT and Loop of Henle, essential nutrients are selectively reabsorbed back into the peritubular capillaries:
    • $100\%$ of glucose and amino acids are actively reabsorbed.
    • Major fractions of $\text{Na}^+, \text{K}^+$, and mineral ions are reabsorbed.
    • Water is reabsorbed by osmosis under the control of Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH). Out of 180 L of filtrate, approximately $1.5 - 2\text{ litres}$ is excreted daily as concentrated urine.
  3. Tubular Secretion:
    Additional metabolic wastes, extra $\text{K}^+$ ions, $\text{H}^+$ ions, and drug residues are actively secreted from peritubular capillaries into the tubular fluid in DCT, maintaining electrolyte balance and physiological blood pH ($\approx 7.4$).

Case Study Question • Renal Failure & Dialysis
A patient suffering from long-term uncontrolled diabetes developed end-stage chronic kidney disease (CKD) with high blood urea levels. The nephrologist put the patient on hemodialysis. (i) What is an artificial kidney (hemodialyzer)? (ii) How does the dialysis fluid differ from blood plasma? (iii) What important step of natural urine formation is absent in hemodialysis?

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Artificial Kidney: A biomedical device used to filter nitrogenous metabolic waste products (urea, creatinine, uric acid) from the patient's blood when the biological kidneys fail.
  2. (ii) Dialysing Fluid Composition: The dialysing fluid has the same osmotic pressure and identical concentration of essential salts, glucose, and water as normal blood plasma, but it completely lacks nitrogenous wastes (urea). Therefore, urea diffuses down the concentration gradient from blood through semi-permeable cellophane tubes into the dialysing fluid.
  3. (iii) Missing Step: Selective reabsorption is completely absent in an artificial kidney because the dialysing system operates by passive diffusion without physiological cellular transporters.
