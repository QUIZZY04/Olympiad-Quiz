In-Text Page 6 • Q1
Why should a magnesium ribbon be cleaned before burning in air?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Scientific Reason:

Magnesium is an active metal. When exposed to atmospheric air, it slowly reacts with oxygen to form a thin, stable, and protective inert layer of basic magnesium oxide ($MgO$) and magnesium carbonate on its surface:

$$2\text{Mg}(s) + \text{O}_2(g) \longrightarrow 2\text{MgO}(s)$$

This protective layer acts as a barrier that prevents direct contact between underlying magnesium metal and oxygen, hindering combustion. Rubbing with sandpaper removes this inert oxide coating, exposing fresh reactive magnesium so that it ignites smoothly with a dazzling white flame.

In-Text Page 10 • Q2
Why is the amount of gas collected in one of the test tubes in Activity 1.7 (electrolysis of water) double of the amount collected in the other? Name this gas.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Electrolysis of Water Explanation:

Water molecule consists of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom in a $2:1$ ratio by volume ($H_2O$). During electrolysis of acidulated water:

$$2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \xrightarrow{\text{Electricity}} 2\text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$

By stoichiometric mole ratio and Avogadro's Law, 2 volumes of hydrogen gas are liberated at the cathode for every 1 volume of oxygen gas liberated at the anode. Hence, the gas collected in double the volume is Hydrogen gas ($\text{H}_2$).

In-Text Page 13 • Q1
Why does the colour of copper sulphate solution change when an iron nail is dipped in it?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Displacement Reaction Mechanism:

Iron ($\text{Fe}$) is more reactive than copper ($\text{Cu}$) in the reactivity series. When an iron nail is dipped in blue copper sulphate solution, iron displaces copper:

$$\text{Fe}(s) + \text{CuSO}_4(aq) \longrightarrow \text{FeSO}_4(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$$ $$\text{(Grey)} + \text{(Blue)} \longrightarrow \text{(Light Green)} + \text{(Reddish Brown)}$$

The blue colour fades and changes to pale green due to formation of $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ (ferrous sulphate) ions, and displaced copper deposits as a reddish-brown coating on the iron nail.

Exercise Q4
What is a balanced chemical equation? Why should chemical equations be balanced?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

1. Definition: A chemical equation in which the total number of atoms of each element is equal on both the reactant side and the product side is called a balanced chemical equation.

2. Reason for Balancing: Chemical equations must be balanced to satisfy the Law of Conservation of Mass (formulated by Antoine Lavoisier), which states that mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. Therefore, the total mass of reactants must equal the total mass of products.

Exercise Q9
What does one mean by exothermic and endothermic reactions? Give examples.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

1. Exothermic Reactions: Chemical reactions in which heat or energy is evolved along with the formation of products.

Examples:

  • Burning of natural gas: $\text{CH}_4(g) + 2\text{O}_2(g) \longrightarrow \text{CO}_2(g) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(g) + \text{Heat}$
  • Respiration: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Energy}$

2. Endothermic Reactions: Chemical reactions in which energy is absorbed from the surroundings in the form of heat, light, or electricity.

Example:

  • Thermal decomposition of limestone: $\text{CaCO}_3(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CaO}(s) + \text{CO}_2(g)$

Exercise Q10
Why is respiration considered an exothermic reaction? Explain.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Biological and Chemical Explanation:

During digestion, food is broken down into simple glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$). In the cells, glucose combines with inhaled oxygen and undergoes controlled breakdown to produce carbon dioxide, water, and liberates energy:

$$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6(aq) + 6\text{O}_2(g) \longrightarrow 6\text{CO}_2(g) + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{Energy (ATP)}$$

Since heat/chemical energy is continuously released to sustain bodily metabolic processes, cellular respiration is an exothermic process.

Exercise Q11
Why are decomposition reactions called the opposite of combination reactions? Write equations for these reactions.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Opposite Nature Explained:

  • In a combination reaction, two or more substances combine to form a single substance: $$2\text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \longrightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{Energy}$$
  • In a decomposition reaction, a single substance breaks down upon supply of energy to form two or more simpler substances: $$2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \xrightarrow{\text{Electricity}} 2\text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$

Because combination builds a single compound from multiple reactants (usually exothermic) while decomposition breaks a single compound into multiple products (endothermic), they are direct chemical opposites.

Exercise Q18
Why do we apply paint on iron articles?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Mechanism of Rust Prevention:

Iron rusts when exposed to both atmospheric oxygen and moisture:

$$4\text{Fe} + 3\text{O}_2 + 2x\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 2\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O} \quad \text{(Rust)}$$

Applying a coat of paint creates an impermeable protective barrier on the iron surface that cuts off direct contact with atmospheric oxygen and water vapor, thereby preventing rusting and prolonging durability.

Exercise Q19
Oil and fat containing food items are flushed with nitrogen. Why?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Prevention of Rancidity:

Oils and fats in food items undergo slow oxidation in air, producing foul-smelling and bad-tasting volatile substances (a process called rancidity). Nitrogen is an unreactive, chemically inert gas. Flushing food packets with nitrogen displaces oxygen and provides an inert atmosphere that prevents oxidation of fats, keeping snacks crisp and fresh.

Exercise Q20
Explain the following terms with one example each: (a) Corrosion, (b) Rancidity.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

(a) Corrosion: The slow process of destruction and deterioration of a metal surface by the chemical or electrochemical action of atmospheric gases, moisture, or acids.

Example: Rusting of iron (formation of reddish-brown hydrated ferric oxide $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$) and blackening of silver articles due to formation of silver sulphide ($\text{Ag}_2\text{S}$).

(b) Rancidity: The aerial oxidation of unsaturated fats and oils present in food items resulting in an unpleasant smell and rancid taste.

Example: Butter left at room temperature for several days develops a foul taste and sour smell due to release of butyric acid.

(iii) Name two antioxidant additives used in fatty food preservation.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Chemical Phenomenon: Rancidity (specifically oxidative rancidity). Fats and oils in open air undergo slow oxidation by atmospheric oxygen to form volatile carboxylic acids, aldehydes, and ketones with rancid odor.
  2. (ii) Gas Used: Nitrogen gas ($ ext{N}_2$). Nitrogen is an unreactive, inert gas at room temperature due to its strong triple covalent bond ($N equiv N$). Flushing with nitrogen expels atmospheric oxygen and provides an inert atmosphere that prevents oxidation of fats.
  3. (iii) Antioxidant Additives: BHA (Butylated Hydroxyanisole) and BHT (Butylated Hydroxytoluene).
