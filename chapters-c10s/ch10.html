Question 1
What is meant by power of accommodation of the eye? State the near point and far point of a normal human eye.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Definition:

The ability of the human eye lens to adjust its focal length using the ciliary muscles so as to focus clearly on both nearby and distant objects onto the retina is called the Power of Accommodation.

Physiological Mechanism:

  1. Viewing Distant Objects (Relaxed Eye):
    When looking at objects at infinity, the ciliary muscles are completely relaxed. The eye lens becomes thin, its radius of curvature increases, and its focal length increases to its maximum value ($\approx 2.5\text{ cm}$, equal to eye globe diameter). Light rays converge sharply onto the retina.
  2. Viewing Nearby Objects (Accommodated Eye):
    When viewing nearby reading material, the ciliary muscles contract. This relaxes tension on the suspensory ligaments, allowing the flexible crystalline lens to become thicker and more convex. Its focal length decreases, converging diverging rays from close objects onto the retina.
  3. Visual Limits:
    • Near Point of Normal Eye (Least distance of distinct vision, $D$): $25\text{ cm}$. (Objects closer than 25 cm cause ocular strain and appear blurred).
    • Far Point of Normal Eye: Infinity ($\infty$).

Question 2
Differentiate between Myopia and Hypermetropia with respect to: (i) Definition, (ii) Causes, (iii) Image position, (iv) Corrective lens. What is Presbyopia?
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Comparative Study of Eye Defects and Optical Corrections:

1. Myopia (Near-sightedness / Short-sightedness):

  • Description: A defective vision in which a person can see nearby objects clearly, but cannot see distant objects distinctly. The far point of the myopic eye is closer than infinity.
  • Optical Cause:
    (a) Excessive curvature of the eye lens (lens is too thick and converges rays too strongly).
    (b) Elongation of the eyeball from front to back.
  • Image Formation: Light rays from a distant object converge to form an image in front of the retina rather than on the retina.
  • Correction: Corrected using spectacles with a Concave lens (Diverging lens) of appropriate focal length. The concave lens diverges incoming parallel rays so that they appear to come from the person's defective far point, focusing the final image precisely on the retina.

2. Hypermetropia (Far-sightedness / Long-sightedness):

  • Description: A defective vision in which a person can see distant objects clearly, but cannot see nearby reading material distinctly. The near point of the hypermetropic eye recedes beyond $25\text{ cm}$.
  • Optical Cause:
    (a) Focal length of the eye lens is too long (lens is too thin).
    (b) Eyeball has become too short.
  • Image Formation: Light rays from a nearby object focus behind the retina.
  • Correction: Corrected using a Convex lens (Converging lens) of appropriate focal length. The convex lens provides extra converging power to focus rays from the standard near point ($25\text{ cm}$) sharply onto the retina.

3. Presbyopia:

  • Caused by aging due to gradual weakening of ciliary muscles and diminishing flexibility of the crystalline lens. Corrected using bifocal lenses (upper portion concave for distant vision, lower portion convex for reading).

Question 3
Why do stars twinkle, but planets do not? Why is the sun visible to us about 2 minutes before actual sunrise?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Atmospheric Refraction Phenomenon:

1. Why Stars Twinkle:

  1. Stars are point-sized sources of light located immense light-years away from Earth.
  2. Earth's atmosphere consists of air layers with constantly fluctuating temperatures, air currents, and densities. As height decreases, optical density of air increases, causing continuous refraction of starlight towards the normal.
  3. Due to physical atmospheric turbulence, the optical path and effective refractive index of air along the line of sight fluctuate continuously.
  4. Consequently, the apparent position of the star wavers slightly, and the quantity of starlight entering the pupil fluctuates from instant to instant — appearing bright at one moment and faint at the next. This causes the twinkling effect.

2. Why Planets Do NOT Twinkle:

  • Planets are situated much closer to Earth and act as extended sources of light (a collection of an infinite number of point-sized sources).
  • Although light from individual points fluctuates, the total variation in the amount of light entering our eye from all the individual point sources averages out to zero, nullifying the twinkling effect. Hence, planets shine with a steady light.

3. Advanced Sunrise and Delayed Sunset:

Due to atmospheric refraction, the sun is visible to us about 2 minutes before the actual sunrise (when the sun is still below the horizon) and about 2 minutes after the actual sunset. This extends daylight by approximately 4 minutes every day.

Question 4
What is the Tyndall effect? Why does the clear sky appear blue? Why are danger signal lights always red?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Scattering of Light and Natural Optical Phenomena:

1. Tyndall Effect:

The phenomenon of scattering of light by colloidal or fine suspended particles in its path, making the illuminated path of the beam visible, is called the Tyndall Effect (e.g. sunlight streaming through a canopy of a dense forest or dust-filled cinema hall).

Figure: Dispersion of White Light through a Triangular Glass Prism into VIBGYOR Spectrum

2. Why the Clear Sky Appears Blue:

  • Earth's atmosphere contains gas molecules ($\text{N}_2, \text{O}_2$) whose physical sizes are smaller than the wavelength of visible light.
  • According to Rayleigh's Law of Scattering, the intensity of scattered light is inversely proportional to the fourth power of its wavelength: $$I \propto \frac{1}{\lambda^4}$$
  • Blue light has a much shorter wavelength ($\approx 400\text{ nm}$) than red light ($\approx 700\text{ nm}$). Blue light is scattered about 16 times more strongly by atmospheric molecules than red light. The scattered blue light enters our eyes from all directions, making the sky appear blue.
  • In Space: Astronauts see the sky as pitch black because there is no atmosphere to scatter sunlight.

3. Why Danger Signals are Red:

Red light has the longest wavelength in the visible spectrum and is scattered least by smoke and fog particles ($I \propto 1/\lambda^4$). It can penetrate long atmospheric distances without attenuation, remaining distinctly visible from far away.

Case Study Question • Rainbow Formation & Dispersion
After a light rain shower on a sunny afternoon, a magnificent curved spectrum of seven colours (rainbow) is observed in the sky opposite to the sun. (i) Name the phenomenon responsible for splitting sunlight into its component colours. (ii) Which optical component acts as a natural triangular prism in the atmosphere? (iii) Name the three sequential optical processes that occur inside a raindrop to form a rainbow.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Phenomenon: Dispersion of light (splitting of white light into its spectrum of constituent wavelengths: VIBGYOR).
  2. (ii) Natural Prisms: Tiny suspended spherical raindrops present in the humid atmosphere act like miniature glass prisms.
  3. (iii) Three Sequential Optical Processes:
    • 1. Refraction and Dispersion: Sunlight enters the raindrop and splits into seven colours because different wavelengths travel at different speeds and bend at different angles (violet bends most, red least).
    • 2. Internal Reflection: The dispersed light strikes the back inner surface of the raindrop and undergoes internal reflection.
    • 3. Final Refraction: As light emerges out of the raindrop back into the air, it refracts again, reaching the observer's eye as a rainbow with red at the top and violet at the bottom.
