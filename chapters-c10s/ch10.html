Chapter 10: The Human Eye and the Colourful World

Structure of Human Eye, Power of Accommodation, Myopia & Hypermetropia Correction, Refraction through Prism, Atmospheric Refraction & Scattering — CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27

NCERT In-Text Questions (Page 176) & Chapter-End Exercises (Questions 1 to 13)
In-Text Page 176 • Q1
What is meant by power of accommodation of the eye?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Definition:

The ability of the human eye lens to adjust its focal length with the aid of the ciliary muscles so as to focus clear, sharp images of objects situated at varying distances (from 25 cm to infinity) onto the retina is called the power of accommodation of the eye.

  • When viewing distant objects, ciliary muscles relax, the eye lens becomes thin, and focal length increases to maximum.
  • When viewing nearby objects, ciliary muscles contract, the crystalline lens becomes thicker and more convex, decreasing its focal length.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Ability of eye lens to adjust focal length via ciliary muscles to focus objects at various distances1 Mark
Mechanism: Relaxation for distant objects vs contraction/thickening for near objects1 Mark
In-Text Page 176 • Q2
A person with a myopic eye cannot see objects beyond 1.2 m distinctly. What should be the type of the corrective lens used to restore proper vision?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Calculation and Prescription:

  1. Defect: The person is suffering from Myopia (Near-sightedness). The far point of the eye has shifted from infinity to $d = 1.2\text{ m}$.
  2. To see distant objects at infinity ($u = -\infty$), the corrective lens must form a virtual image of the distant object at the person's defective far point ($v = -1.2\text{ m}$): $$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{-1.2\text{ m}} - \frac{1}{-\infty} = -\frac{1}{1.2\text{ m}}$$ $$f = \mathbf{-1.2\text{ m}}$$ $$P = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{-1.2\text{ m}} = -0.83\text{ D}$$
  3. Lens Type: A Concave lens (Diverging lens) of focal length $1.2\text{ m}$ (Power $-0.83\text{ D}$) is required.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Lens type: Concave lens (diverging lens)1 Mark
Calculation: f = -1.2 m, Power P = -0.83 D1 Mark
In-Text Page 176 • Q3
What is the near point and far point of the human eye with normal vision?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Reference Points for Normal Vision:

  1. Near Point (Least Distance of Distinct Vision, $D$): The minimum distance at which an object can be seen most distinctly and comfortably without eye strain is $25\text{ cm}$ for a normal young adult eye.
  2. Far Point: The farthest point up to which the eye can see objects clearly is Infinity ($\infty$).
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Near point = 25 cm (least distance of distinct vision)1 Mark
Far point = Infinity (infinite distance)1 Mark
In-Text Page 176 • Q4
A student has difficulty reading the blackboard while sitting in the last row. What could be the defect the child is suffering from? How can it be corrected?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Defect Diagnosis & Remedy:

  1. Defect: The student is suffering from Myopia (Short-sightedness / Near-sightedness) because they can see near objects clearly but have difficulty seeing distant objects distinctly (the blackboard image is formed in front of the retina).
  2. Correction: It can be corrected by wearing spectacles fitted with a Concave lens (Diverging lens) of suitable focal length and power. The concave lens diverges incoming parallel light rays so that they appear to originate from the myopic far point, bringing the focused image exactly onto the retina.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Diagnosis: Myopia (short-sightedness)1 Mark
Remedy: Spectacles with concave lenses of appropriate focal length/power1 Mark

NCERT Exercise • Q5
A person needs a lens of power -5.5 dioptres for correcting his distant vision. For correcting his near vision he needs a lens of power +1.5 dioptre. What is the focal length of the lens required for correcting (i) distant vision, and (ii) near vision?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) For Distant Vision: $$P_1 = -5.5\text{ D}$$ $$f_1 = \frac{1}{P_1} = \frac{1}{-5.5}\text{ m} = -\frac{10}{55}\text{ m} = -\frac{2}{11}\text{ m} \approx \mathbf{-0.182\text{ m}} = \mathbf{-18.2\text{ cm}}$$ (Concave lens of focal length $-18.2\text{ cm}$).
  2. (ii) For Near Vision: $$P_2 = +1.5\text{ D}$$ $$f_2 = \frac{1}{P_2} = \frac{1}{+1.5}\text{ m} = +\frac{10}{15}\text{ m} = +\frac{2}{3}\text{ m} \approx \mathbf{+0.667\text{ m}} = \mathbf{+66.7\text{ cm}}$$ (Convex lens of focal length $+66.7\text{ cm}$).
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
(i) Distant vision: f1 = -0.182 m = -18.2 cm1 Mark
(ii) Near vision: f2 = +0.667 m = +66.7 cm1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q6
The far point of a myopic person is 80 cm in front of the eye. What is the nature and power of the lens required to correct the problem?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. For viewing distant objects at infinity ($u = -\infty$), the corrective lens must form a virtual image at the person's defective far point ($v = -80\text{ cm} = -0.8\text{ m}$).
  2. Lens Formula: $$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{-0.8\text{ m}} - \frac{1}{-\infty} = -\frac{1}{0.8\text{ m}}$$ $$f = \mathbf{-0.8\text{ m}} = \mathbf{-80\text{ cm}}$$
  3. Power of Lens: $$P = \frac{1}{f\text{ (m)}} = \frac{1}{-0.8\text{ m}} = \mathbf{-1.25\text{ D}}$$
  4. Nature: A Concave lens of power $-1.25\text{ Dioptre}$ is required.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Focal length calculation f = -80 cm (-0.8 m)1 Mark
Power calculation P = -1.25 D and Concave lens nature1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q7
Make a diagram to show how hypermetropia is corrected. The near point of a hypermetropic eye is 1 m. What is the power of the lens required to correct this defect? Assume that the near point of the normal eye is 25 cm.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. Optical Situation:
    • Standard near point (object position), $u = -25\text{ cm} = -0.25\text{ m}$.
    • Defective near point (image position), $v = -1\text{ m} = -100\text{ cm}$.
  2. Lens Formula: $$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{-100} - \left(-\frac{1}{25}\right) = -\frac{1}{100} + \frac{1}{25} = \frac{-1 + 4}{100} = \frac{3}{100}\text{ cm}^{-1}$$ $$f = +\frac{100}{3}\text{ cm} = +\frac{1}{3}\text{ m}$$
  3. Power of Corrective Lens: $$P = \frac{1}{f\text{ (m)}} = \frac{1}{+1/3\text{ m}} = \mathbf{+3.0\text{ D}}$$
  4. Remedy: A Convex lens (converging lens) of power $+3.0\text{ D}$ is required. It converges incoming rays from $25\text{ cm}$ so they appear to diverge from the near point at $1\text{ m}$.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Correct identification: u = -25 cm, v = -100 cm1 Mark
Calculating f = +1/3 m and Power P = +3.0 D1 Mark
Convex lens specification and correction diagram description1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q8
Why is a normal eye not able to see clearly the objects placed closer than 25 cm?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Reason:

  1. To see nearby objects distinctly, the ciliary muscles must contract to increase the curvature and thickness of the crystalline lens, shortening its focal length.
  2. However, the ciliary muscles have a physiological limit to which they can contract. The lens cannot be compressed or curved beyond a maximum limit.
  3. If an object is held closer than $25\text{ cm}$, the required sharp convergence cannot be achieved by the eye lens, causing the light rays to focus behind the retina, resulting in blurred vision and severe ocular muscle strain.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Ciliary muscles cannot contract beyond a physiological limit1 Mark
Focal length cannot decrease further; image falls behind retina causing blurred vision1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q9
What happens to the image distance in the eye when we increase the distance of an object from the eye?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Answer:

The image distance in the eye remains constant (it always equals the fixed distance between the eye lens and the retina, approximately $2.5\text{ cm}$).

Explanation: When the object distance ($u$) increases, the ciliary muscles relax, making the crystalline lens thinner. This increases the focal length of the eye lens by accommodation such that the real and inverted image is always formed precisely on the fixed retina.

CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Image distance remains unchanged/constant (distance from lens to retina)1 Mark
Accommodation adjusts lens focal length so image always focuses on the retina1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q10
Why do stars twinkle?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Mechanism of Atmospheric Refraction:

  1. Stars are located at vast astronomical distances from Earth and act as point-sized sources of light.
  2. The Earth's atmosphere consists of continuously churning layers of air with differing physical temperatures, densities, and refractive indices ($n$).
  3. As starlight traverses these dynamically turbulent atmospheric layers, it undergoes continuous and fluctuating atmospheric refraction.
  4. This causes the apparent position of the star and the quantity of starlight reaching the observer's pupil to fluctuate rapidly from moment to moment. Sometimes more light enters our eye (star appears bright), and sometimes less light enters (star appears faint), producing the optical illusion called twinkling.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Stars are point-sized distant sources subject to atmospheric refraction1 Mark
Turbulent air layers cause continuous fluctuations in apparent position and light intensity entering eye1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q11
Explain why the planets do not twinkle.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Reason Why Planets Do Not Twinkle:

  1. Planets are situated much closer to Earth than distant stars. Hence, they do not appear as point-sized sources, but rather as extended optical sources (a dense collection of millions of individual point sources of light).
  2. Although light rays originating from individual points on a planet undergo atmospheric refraction and intensity fluctuations, the bright and faint variations from different points average out across the entire disc: $$\sum \Delta I_{\text{fluctuations}} \approx 0$$
  3. The total amount of light entering our eye from all points combined remains constant over time. Therefore, planets shine with steady light without twinkling.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Planets are close extended sources comprising a large collection of point sources1 Mark
Fluctuations from different point sources cancel out on average, nullifying twinkling1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q12
Why does the Sun appear reddish early in the morning and at sunset?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Phenomenon of Rayleigh Scattering:

  1. Early in the morning (at sunrise) and late in the evening (at sunset), the Sun is situated near the horizon. Sunlight must travel through a much thicker layer of the atmosphere and a greater distance to reach the observer compared to noon.
  2. According to Rayleigh's scattering law, the intensity of scattered light is inversely proportional to the fourth power of wavelength: $$I_{\text{scattered}} \propto \frac{1}{\lambda^4}$$
  3. Shorter wavelengths (blue, violet) are scattered away strongly in all directions by atmospheric molecules.
  4. The least-scattered longer wavelengths (red and orange) pass straight through the atmosphere and reach our eyes, causing the Sun and surrounding horizon to appear reddish.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Sun near horizon means light travels longer atmospheric distance1 Mark
Rayleigh scattering scatters short blue wavelengths; long red wavelengths reach observer1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q13
Why does the sky appear dark instead of blue to an astronaut?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Reason:

  1. The blue appearance of the sky on Earth is caused by the Rayleigh scattering of shorter blue wavelengths of sunlight by tiny gas molecules ($N_2, O_2$) present in the atmosphere.
  2. In outer space, there is a total absence of an atmosphere (vacuum).
  3. In the absence of air molecules and particulate matter, no scattering of sunlight takes place. Sunlight travels in straight lines without illumination of surrounding space. Hence, to an astronaut or in space, the sky appears completely pitch dark/black.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
No atmosphere or air particles exist at high altitudes / outer space1 Mark
Absence of scattering of light leaves surrounding space dark/black1 Mark

Case Study Question • Atmospheric Phenomena & Rainbow Formation
A rainbow is a natural spectrum appearing in the sky after a rain shower. (i) In which direction with respect to the Sun does a rainbow always form? (ii) What acts as tiny prisms in the atmosphere? (iii) Name the three sequential optical phenomena that occur inside a water droplet during rainbow formation.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Direction: A rainbow is always formed in a direction opposite to that of the Sun (the observer's back must face the Sun).
  2. (ii) Tiny Prisms: Suspended spherical raindrops in the atmosphere act as miniature prisms.
  3. (iii) Three Optical Phenomena:
    • 1. Refraction and Dispersion: Incident sunlight enters the front surface of the raindrop, refracting and splitting into constituent spectral colours (VIBGYOR).
    • 2. Internal Reflection: The dispersed rays strike the back inner wall of the droplet and undergo internal reflection.
    • 3. Refraction: The reflected coloured rays refract again as they emerge out of the front surface of the droplet towards the observer's eye.
