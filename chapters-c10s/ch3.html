Question 1
Give an example of a metal which: (i) is a liquid at room temperature, (ii) can be easily cut with a knife, (iii) is the best conductor of heat, (iv) is a poor conductor of heat.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Physical Exceptions in Metals and Non-metals:

  1. (i) Liquid metal at room temperature: Mercury ($\text{Hg}$). All other metals are crystalline solids.
  2. (ii) Metal easily cut with a knife: Sodium ($\text{Na}$) and Potassium ($\text{K}$). They are soft alkali metals with low density and weak metallic bonding.
  3. (iii) Best conductor of heat: Silver ($\text{Ag}$), closely followed by Copper ($\text{Cu}$).
  4. (iv) Poorest conductor of heat: Lead ($\text{Pb}$) and Mercury ($\text{Hg}$).
  5. Additional Key Exceptions:
    • Lustrous non-metal: Iodine ($\text{I}_2$).
    • Liquid non-metal at room temperature: Bromine ($\text{Br}_2$).
    • Non-metal conducting electricity: Graphite (allotrope of carbon).

Question 2
What are amphoteric oxides? Give two examples of amphoteric oxides. Write chemical equations showing amphoteric nature of aluminium oxide.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Definition:

Metal oxides that show both acidic as well as basic behaviour by reacting with both acids and bases to produce salt and water are called Amphoteric Oxides.

Two Examples:

  1. Aluminium oxide ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$)
  2. Zinc oxide ($\text{ZnO}$)

Balanced Chemical Equations for Aluminium Oxide ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$):

  1. (a) Basic behaviour (Reaction with Acid $\text{HCl}$):
    $$\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3(s) + 6\text{HCl}(aq) \longrightarrow 2\text{AlCl}_3(aq) + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$ Here, aluminium oxide acts as a base and reacts with acid to yield aluminium chloride (salt) and water.
  2. (b) Acidic behaviour (Reaction with Strong Base $\text{NaOH}$):
    $$\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3(s) + 2\text{NaOH}(aq) \longrightarrow 2\text{NaAlO}_2(aq) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$ $$\text{Aluminium oxide} + \text{Sodium hydroxide} \longrightarrow \text{Sodium aluminate} + \text{Water}$$ Here, aluminium oxide acts as an acidic oxide, reacting with the base to form the soluble complex salt sodium aluminate.

Question 3
Why is hydrogen gas not evolved when a metal reacts with nitric acid? Name two metals which react with very dilute nitric acid to evolve hydrogen gas.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Chemical Explanation:

  1. When most metals react with dilute mineral acids, hydrogen gas is normally evolved. However, when a metal reacts with Nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$), hydrogen gas ($\text{H}_2$) is not evolved.
  2. Reason: Nitric acid is a strong oxidising agent. As soon as hydrogen gas ($\text{H}_2$) is liberated at the metal surface, $\text{HNO}_3$ rapidly oxidises the nascent hydrogen into water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) and itself gets reduced to any of the nitrogen oxides ($\text{NO}_2, \text{NO}, \text{or } \text{N}_2\text{O}$).
  3. Exceptions: Very dilute nitric acid (about $1\% \text{ HNO}_3$) reacts with Magnesium ($\text{Mg}$) and Manganese ($\text{Mn}$) to evolve hydrogen gas: $$\text{Mg}(s) + 2\text{HNO}_3(\text{very dil}) \longrightarrow \text{Mg(NO}_3)_2(aq) + \text{H}_2(g)\uparrow$$ $$\text{Mn}(s) + 2\text{HNO}_3(\text{very dil}) \longrightarrow \text{Mn(NO}_3)_2(aq) + \text{H}_2(g)\uparrow$$

Question 4
Show the formation of magnesium chloride ($\text{MgCl}_2$) by the transfer of electrons. Explain why ionic compounds have high melting points and conduct electricity in the molten state.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Electron Transfer and Ionic Bond Formation in $\text{MgCl}_2$:

1. Electronic Configurations:

  • Magnesium ($Z = 12$): Electronic configuration $= 2, 8, 2$. It has 2 valence electrons. To attain stable neon octet ($2, 8$), it readily loses 2 valence electrons: $$\text{Mg} \longrightarrow \text{Mg}^{2+} + 2e^-$$
  • Chlorine ($Z = 17$): Electronic configuration $= 2, 8, 7$. It has 7 valence electrons. To attain stable argon octet ($2, 8, 8$), each chlorine atom accepts 1 electron: $$\text{Cl} + e^- \longrightarrow \text{Cl}^-$$

2. Electron-Dot Representation:

One magnesium atom transfers one electron each to two separate chlorine atoms:

$$\text{Mg}^{\bullet\bullet} + 2 \times \cdot\underset{\bullet\bullet}{\overset{\bullet\bullet}{\text{Cl}}}: \longrightarrow [\text{Mg}]^{2+} \left[:\underset{\bullet\bullet}{\overset{\bullet\bullet}{\text{Cl}}}:^-\right]_2 \implies \text{MgCl}_2$$

3. Physical Properties of Ionic Compounds:

  1. High Melting and Boiling Points: Ionic solids consist of closely packed cations and anions held together by extremely strong electrostatic forces of attraction. A huge amount of thermal energy is required to overcome these strong inter-ionic bonds.
  2. Electrical Conductivity: In the solid state, ions are locked rigidly in fixed lattice positions and cannot move; hence solid ionic compounds are insulators. However, in molten state or aqueous solution, the crystal lattice breaks down and free mobile ions are available to conduct electricity.

Question 5
Differentiate between roasting and calcination with examples. Explain how zinc is extracted from its sulphide and carbonate ores with balanced equations.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

1. Comparison: Roasting vs Calcination

Feature Roasting Calcination
Definition Heating ore strongly in the excess presence of air below its melting point. Heating ore strongly in the absence or limited supply of air below its melting point.
Type of Ore Applicable primarily to Sulphide ores. Applicable primarily to Carbonate and Hydrated oxide ores.
Gas Evolved Sulphur dioxide gas ($\text{SO}_2$) is evolved. Carbon dioxide gas ($\text{CO}_2$) or moisture ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) is driven off.

2. Balanced Chemical Reactions for Zinc Ore:

  1. Roasting of Zinc Blende ($\text{ZnS}$):
    $$2\text{ZnS}(s) + 3\text{O}_2(g) \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{ZnO}(s) + 2\text{SO}_2(g)\uparrow$$
  2. Calcination of Calamine ($\text{ZnCO}_3$):
    $$\text{ZnCO}_3(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{ZnO}(s) + \text{CO}_2(g)\uparrow$$

3. Reduction of Zinc Oxide to Zinc Metal:

The zinc oxide formed is subsequently reduced to metallic zinc by heating with carbon (coke) as a reducing agent in a retort:

$$\text{ZnO}(s) + \text{C}(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Zn}(g) + \text{CO}(g)$$

Zinc vapours are distilled and condensed into solid pure zinc.

Question 6
Explain the process of electrolytic refining of copper with electrode reactions and state what is meant by anode mud.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Electrolytic Refining of Copper ($\text{Cu}$):

Impure blister copper is refined to $99.99\%$ purity using an electrolytic cell.

Cell Setup:

  • Anode (positive electrode): Thick block of impure copper.
  • Cathode (negative electrode): Thin strip of pure copper.
  • Electrolyte: Acidified aqueous solution of copper sulphate ($\text{CuSO}_4 + \text{dil. H}_2\text{SO}_4$).
Figure: Electrolytic Refining Tank for Copper with Anode Mud Formation

Electrode Reactions:

  1. At Anode (Oxidation): Copper atoms from the impure anode lose electrons and dissolve into the electrolyte as cupric ions: $$\text{Cu}(s) \longrightarrow \text{Cu}^{2+}(aq) + 2e^-$$
  2. At Cathode (Reduction): An equivalent amount of pure $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ions from the solution migrate to the cathode, gain electrons, and deposit as pure copper metal: $$\text{Cu}^{2+}(aq) + 2e^- \longrightarrow \text{Cu}(s)$$
  3. Anode Mud: Soluble impurities pass into the solution, whereas insoluble, less reactive noble metal impurities (such as Gold $\text{Au}$, Silver $\text{Ag}$, Platinum $\text{Pt}$) settle below the anode at the bottom of the tank as anode mud.

Case Study Question • Corrosion & Galvanisation
In railway tracks, joints of broken steel rails are welded on-site using the Thermit process. Furthermore, coastal bridges made of iron are galvanised with zinc. (i) Write the balanced chemical equation for the Thermit reaction and explain why it is useful for welding rails. (ii) How does zinc prevent rusting of iron even if the zinc layer gets scratched?

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Thermit Reaction:
    $$\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3(s) + 2\text{Al}(s) \longrightarrow 2\text{Fe}(l) + \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3(s) + \text{Enormous Heat}$$ Aluminium is more reactive than iron and displaces it from ferric oxide. The reaction is so highly exothermic that the iron is produced in the molten liquid state. This molten iron flows directly into the gap between broken rail joints, welding them solidly upon solidification.
  2. (ii) Galvanisation & Sacrificial Protection:
    Zinc is more electropositive (more reactive) than iron. When the surface layer is scratched, zinc acts as a sacrificial anode. Zinc corrodes preferentially by losing electrons ($\text{Zn} \longrightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^-$), supplying electrons to the iron and preventing iron from oxidising to rust.
