In-Text Page 46 • Q1
Why is sodium kept immersed in kerosene oil?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Chemical Reason:

Sodium is an extremely reactive alkali metal with a very low activation energy. When exposed to open air at room temperature, it reacts vigorously with atmospheric oxygen and moisture in a highly exothermic reaction, generating sufficient heat to ignite the liberated hydrogen gas and catch fire spontaneously:

$$4\text{Na}(s) + \text{O}_2(g) \longrightarrow 2\text{Na}_2\text{O}(s)$$ $$2\text{Na}(s) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \longrightarrow 2\text{NaOH}(aq) + \text{H}_2(g)\uparrow + \text{Heat}$$

Kerosene oil is an inert hydrocarbon liquid that does not react with sodium and cuts off direct contact with atmospheric oxygen and water vapor. Therefore, sodium is stored immersed in kerosene oil to prevent accidental fires and ensure safety.

In-Text Page 49 • Q2
Why do ionic compounds have high melting points?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Scientific Explanation:

Ionic compounds consist of rigid three-dimensional crystal lattices containing closely packed oppositely charged ions (cations and anions) bound together by extremely strong inter-ionic electrostatic forces of attraction.

To overcome and break these immense electrostatic forces and separate the ions into a mobile liquid state, an enormous amount of thermal energy is required. Consequently, ionic compounds (such as $\text{NaCl}$, m.p. $1074\text{ K}$, and $\text{MgO}$, m.p. $3125\text{ K}$) possess very high melting and boiling points.

In-Text Page 53 • Q3
What chemical process is used for obtaining a metal from its oxide?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Chemical Process: Reduction

The chemical process used is Reduction (removal of oxygen from the metal oxide to yield free metal). The choice of reducing agent depends on the position of the metal in the reactivity series:

  1. Reduction by Carbon (Coke/Carbon monoxide): Used for moderately reactive metals (Zn, Fe, Pb): $$\text{ZnO}(s) + \text{C}(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Zn}(s) + \text{CO}(g)$$
  2. Reduction by Highly Reactive Metals (Thermite process with Al): Used when oxides are strongly bonded: $$\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3(s) + 2\text{Al}(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{Fe}(l) + \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3(s) + \text{Heat}$$
  3. Electrolytic Reduction: Used for highly reactive metals (Na, Ca, Mg, Al) where carbon cannot reduce their stable oxides.

NCERT Exercise • Q5
You are given a hammer, a battery, a bulb, wires and a switch. (a) How could you use them to distinguish between samples of metals and non-metals? (b) Assess the usefulness of these tests in distinguishing between metals and non-metals.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Experimental Differentiation:

  1. (a) Testing Procedures:
    • Malleability Test (Hammer): Strike the given sample repeatedly with the hammer. If the sample flattens into a thin sheet without breaking, it is a metal (malleable). If it shatters into pieces or powder, it is a non-metal (brittle).
    • Electrical Conductivity Test (Circuit): Connect the battery, bulb, switch, and connecting wires with test terminals. Insert the sample into the gap and close the switch. If the bulb glows, the sample is a good conductor of electricity and is a metal. If the bulb fails to glow, it is an insulator and is a non-metal.
  2. (b) Assessment of Usefulness:
    • These physical tests are practical, rapid, and generally reliable, but have important exceptions:
    • Exceptions: Carbon in the form of graphite is a non-metal that conducts electricity and will make the bulb glow. Sodium and potassium are metals that are soft and can be cut with a knife rather than hammered into sheets. Mercury is a liquid metal. Therefore, physical tests should be supplemented by chemical properties for absolute confirmation.

NCERT Exercise • Q12
Give reasons: (a) Platinum, gold and silver are used to make jewellery. (b) Sodium, potassium and lithium are stored under oil. (c) Aluminium is a highly reactive metal, yet it is used to make utensils for cooking. (d) Carbonate and sulphide ores are usually converted into oxides during the process of extraction.

✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Scientific Explanations:

  1. (a) Platinum, gold, and silver for jewellery: They are noble metals that possess high metallic lustre, remarkable malleability and ductility, and are extremely unreactive. They do not corrode, tarnish, or react with air, water, and sweat, maintaining their brilliant shine indefinitely.
  2. (b) Storage under oil for alkali metals: Sodium, potassium, and lithium have very low ionization enthalpies. In open air, they react vigorously with atmospheric oxygen and moisture, releasing hydrogen gas that immediately catches fire. Storing them submerged under paraffin or kerosene oil shields them from oxygen and moisture.
  3. (c) Aluminium for cooking utensils: When aluminium metal is exposed to air, it naturally forms an ultra-thin, continuous, tough, and impermeable protective coating of aluminium oxide ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$) on its surface. This oxide film prevents any further reaction with food acids and oxygen. Moreover, aluminium is lightweight, inexpensive, and an excellent conductor of heat.
  4. (d) Conversion of carbonate and sulphide ores to oxides: It is chemically and thermodynamically far easier to reduce a metal oxide to free metal using common reducing agents like carbon (coke) or $\text{CO}$ than to reduce metal carbonates or sulphides directly: $$\text{Calcination: } \text{ZnCO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{ZnO} + \text{CO}_2$$ $$\text{Roasting: } 2\text{ZnS} + 3\text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{ZnO} + 2\text{SO}_2$$

NCERT Exercise • Q13
You must have seen tarnished copper vessels being cleaned with lemon or tamarind juice. Explain why these sour substances are effective in cleaning the vessels.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Mechanism of Cleaning Tarnished Copper:

When copper vessels are exposed to moist air containing carbon dioxide and oxygen, they slowly react to form a dull green tarnish consisting of basic copper carbonate $[\text{CuCO}_3 \cdot \text{Cu(OH)}_2]$:

$$2\text{Cu} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 + \text{O}_2 \longrightarrow \text{CuCO}_3 \cdot \text{Cu(OH)}_2\text{ (Green coating)}$$

Lemon juice contains citric acid, and tamarind contains tartaric acid. These organic acids react with the basic copper carbonate and basic copper oxide to form soluble copper citrate/tartrate salts and water. Rinsing with water washes away these dissolved salts, exposing the fresh, shiny reddish-brown metallic copper surface.

NCERT Exercise • Q15
A man went door to door posing as a goldsmith. He promised to bring back the glitter of old and dull gold ornaments. An unsuspecting lady gave a set of gold bangles to him which he dipped in a particular solution. The bangles sparkled like new but their weight was reduced drastically. The lady was upset but, after a futile argument, the man beat a hasty retreat. Can you play the detective to find out the nature of the solution he had used?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Detective Chemical Deduction:

  1. Identity of the Solution: The impostor used a freshly prepared mixture known as Aqua Regia (Latin for 'royal water').
  2. Composition: Aqua regia is a fuming mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid ($\text{HCl}$) and concentrated nitric acid ($\text{HNO}_3$) in a $3 : 1$ ratio by volume.
  3. Why the Bangles Lost Weight: Gold is a noble metal that does not dissolve in either concentrated $\text{HCl}$ or concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$ alone. However, in aqua regia, nascent chlorine is liberated which readily oxidises and dissolves outer layers of metallic gold into soluble auric chloride ($\text{HAuCl}_4$): $$\text{Au} + 3\text{HNO}_3 + 4\text{HCl} \longrightarrow \text{HAuCl}_4 + 3\text{NO}_2 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$$ Dissolving the tarnished outer layer restored sparkle but stripped away grams of valuable gold, causing the drastic weight loss.

NCERT Exercise • Q16
Give reasons why copper is used to make hot water tanks and not steel (an alloy of iron).
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Reasons:

  1. Reactivity with Water/Steam: Copper does not react with cold water, hot water, or steam at any temperature, because copper lies below hydrogen in the electrochemical reactivity series. In contrast, iron in steel reacts readily with steam at high temperatures to form iron oxide and hydrogen gas, causing severe internal corrosion and structural weakening: $$3\text{Fe} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}(g) \longrightarrow \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 + 4\text{H}_2$$
  2. Thermal Conductivity: Copper is a far superior conductor of heat compared to steel, ensuring rapid and energy-efficient water heating.

Case Study Question • Thermite Welding & Metallurgy
Railway tracks in remote sections are joined on-site using the Thermite process rather than conventional electric arc welding. (i) Write the balanced chemical equation for the Thermite reaction. (ii) Why is this reaction classified as a displacement reaction and an exothermic reaction? (iii) In what physical state is the reduced metal obtained and why is that beneficial for track welding?

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Thermite Reaction: $$\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3(s) + 2\text{Al}(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{Fe}(l) + \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3(s) + \text{Heat}$$
  2. (ii) Classification:
    • Displacement Reaction: Aluminium is more electropositive and reactive than iron; hence aluminium displaces iron from ferric oxide.
    • Exothermic Reaction: The heat of formation of aluminium oxide ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$) is exceptionally high, releasing vast amounts of thermal energy.
  3. (iii) Physical State: The reduced iron is produced in the molten (liquid) state because the reaction temperature exceeds $2500^\circ\text{C}$ (well above iron's melting point of $1538^\circ\text{C}$). This glowing molten iron flows directly into the fissure between broken railway tracks, fusing them seamlessly into a single continuous rail upon cooling.
