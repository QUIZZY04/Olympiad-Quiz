Why does a compass needle get deflected when brought near a bar magnet?
[1 Marks]

Draw magnetic field lines around a bar magnet. What are their properties?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Properties of Magnetic Field Lines:

  1. Continuous Closed Loops: Outside the magnet, field lines emerge from the North pole and enter the South pole. Inside the magnet, they travel from South pole to North pole, forming continuous unbroken closed loops.
  2. Crowding Indicates Strength: The degree of closeness (density) of field lines represents the strength of the magnetic field. They are crowded closest at the poles where the field is strongest.
  3. Never Intersect: Two magnetic field lines never cross or intersect each other.

In-Text Page 213 • Q3
Why don't two magnetic field lines intersect each other?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Scientific Reason:

The tangent drawn at any point on a magnetic field line indicates the unique direction of the net magnetic field at that point.

If two field lines were to cross or intersect at a point, it would mean that at the single point of intersection, the magnetic field has two different directions simultaneously. If a compass needle were placed at that intersection, it would have to point in two opposite directions at the same instant, which is physically impossible. Therefore, two magnetic field lines can never cross each other.

In-Text Page 220 • Q1
Which of the following property of a proton can change while it moves freely in a magnetic field? (There may be more than one correct answer.) (a) mass (b) speed (c) velocity (d) momentum.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Correct Options: (c) velocity and (d) momentum

Reason: When a charged proton moves perpendicular to a magnetic field, the magnetic Lorentz force ($F = q v B$) acts perpendicular to its direction of motion. Because the force is perpendicular to velocity, it does zero work on the proton; hence its kinetic energy and speed remain strictly constant. However, the force continuously deflects the direction of motion, changing its velocity vector. Since momentum is a vector ($\vec{p} = m \vec{v}$), the momentum changes continuously.

In-Text Page 222 • Q2
What is the principle of an electric motor? What is the role of the split ring in an electric motor?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

1. Principle of an Electric Motor:

An electric motor operates on the principle of the magnetic force on a current-carrying conductor. When a rectangular coil carrying electric current is placed in a magnetic field, opposite sides experience equal and opposite magnetic forces according to Fleming's left-hand rule, creating a torque that rotates the coil continuously.

2. Role of the Split Ring (Commutator):

The split ring acts as a commutator. It reverses the direction of current flowing through the two arms of the rotating coil every half rotation. Reversing current direction ensures that the torque continues to act in the same rotational direction, sustaining continuous unidirectional rotation.

NCERT Exercise • Q8
How does a solenoid behave like a magnet? Can you determine the north and south poles of a current–carrying solenoid with the help of a bar magnet? Explain.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Magnetic Behaviour of a Solenoid:

  1. A solenoid is a long cylindrical helical coil of many insulated copper turns. When an electric current flows through it, magnetic field lines emerge from one end and enter the opposite end, forming an identical magnetic field pattern to that of a bar magnet.
  2. Determining Polarity using a Bar Magnet:
    • Suspend a bar magnet with known North and South poles near one end of the active solenoid.
    • If the North pole of the bar magnet is repelled by that end of the solenoid, that end is the North pole (like poles repel).
    • If the North pole is attracted, that end is the South pole (unlike poles attract).
  3. Alternatively: By the Clock Face Rule, an end where current flows anti-clockwise is a North pole; clockwise is a South pole.

NCERT Exercise • Q9
When is the force experienced by a current–carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field largest?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Condition for Maximum Magnetic Force:

The magnetic force on a conductor of length $l$ carrying current $I$ in a magnetic field $B$ is given by:

$$F = I l B \sin\theta$$

Where $\theta$ is the angle between the direction of current and the magnetic field. The force is maximum when $\sin\theta$ has its maximum value of $1$ ($\theta = 90^\circ$).

Therefore, the force is largest when the direction of the current is perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to the direction of the magnetic field ($F_{\text{max}} = I l B$).

Electric Motor:

NCERT Exercise • Q13
A coil of insulated copper wire is connected to a galvanometer. What will happen if a bar magnet is (i) pushed into the coil, (ii) withdrawn from inside the coil, (iii) held stationary inside the coil?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Observations based on Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction:

  1. (i) Magnet pushed into the coil: The magnetic flux linked with the coil increases rapidly. An induced current is generated, causing the galvanometer needle to undergo a momentary deflection in one direction.

NCERT Exercise • Q16
Explain the underlying principle and working of an electric generator by drawing a labelled diagram. What is the function of brushes?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Electric Generator:

  1. Principle: Works on the principle of electromagnetic induction. When a closed armature coil is mechanically rotated in a magnetic field, the magnetic flux linking the coil continuously changes, inducing an alternating electric current.
  2. Working: As coil ABCD rotates in the magnetic field, arm AB moves upwards and arm CD moves downwards.   3. Function of Brushes: Flexible carbon brushes maintain constant sliding electrical contact with the rotating slip rings to draw out the induced current into the external load circuit without twisting the wires.

Causes of Short Circuit:

An electric short circuit occurs when the live wire and the neutral wire come into direct physical contact with each other without any intervening resistance or electrical load.

This typically happens due to:

  1. Damage or wear-and-tear of the protective plastic insulation coating on wires.
  2. A fault or internal loose connection inside an electrical appliance.

Board Benchmark • Q34
Explain the difference between Direct Current (DC) and Alternating Current (AC). Why is AC preferred for long distance power transmission?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

DC vs AC:

  1. Direct Current (DC): Electric current that flows strictly in one constant direction over time (e.g., from dry cells and batteries). Frequency is $0\text{ Hz}$.
  2. Alternating Current (AC): Electric current that periodically reverses its direction of flow at regular intervals of time. In India, AC reverses direction every $1/100\text{ second}$, having a frequency of $50\text{ Hz}$.
  3. Why AC is Preferred for Transmission: AC voltage can be stepped up to very high voltages ($132\text{ kV}, 400\text{ kV}$) using step-up transformers for long-distance transmission, which drastically reduces current ($I$) and minimizes Joule heat losses ($I^2 R$) over hundreds of kilometres. It is then safely stepped down to $220\text{ V}$ for domestic consumption.

(iii) What color insulation is standardly used for live, neutral, and earth wires?

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Potential Difference: The potential difference between the live wire ($220\text{ V}$) and the neutral wire ($0\text{ V}$) is $220\text{ Volts}$ at an AC frequency of $50\text{ Hz}$.
  2. (ii) Connection of Switch and Fuse: The switch and fuse are always connected in the Live wire. When the switch is turned off or the fuse blows, the appliance is completely isolated from the high voltage ($220\text{ V}$) supply. If connected in the neutral wire, the appliance would remain live even when turned off, posing a lethal electrocution risk to anyone touching it.
  3. (iii) Color Insulation Standards:
    • Live wire: Red (or Brown)
    • Neutral wire: Black (or Light Blue)
    • Earth wire: Green (or Green with yellow stripes)
