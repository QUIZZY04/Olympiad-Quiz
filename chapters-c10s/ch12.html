Question 1
List the important properties of magnetic field lines. Why don't two magnetic field lines intersect each other?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Properties of Magnetic Field Lines:

  1. Direction of Field Lines: Outside a bar magnet, magnetic field lines emerge from the North pole and enter the South pole. Inside the magnet, the direction is from the South pole to the North pole. Thus, magnetic field lines form continuous closed loops.
  2. Relative Strength (Density): The degree of closeness (crowding) of the field lines is a measure of the strength of the magnetic field. The field is strongest near the poles where lines are crowded.
  3. Non-Intersecting Nature: No two magnetic field lines ever intersect each other. If they did intersect at a point, a compass needle placed at that intersection point would have to point in two different directions simultaneously, which is physically impossible.

Question 2
State Maxwell's Right-Hand Thumb Rule. What is a solenoid? Describe the nature of the magnetic field inside a current-carrying solenoid.
[4 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Right-Hand Thumb Rule and Solenoid Magnetic Field:

1. Maxwell's Right-Hand Thumb Rule:

Imagine holding a straight current-carrying conductor in your right hand such that your outstretched thumb points in the direction of current. Then your curled fingers encircling the wire point in the direction of the concentric magnetic field lines.

2. Magnetic Field of a Solenoid:

  • A Solenoid is a long coil containing many circular turns of insulated copper wire wrapped closely in the shape of a cylinder.
  • When electric current passes through the solenoid, it produces a magnetic field that is identical to that of a bar magnet. One end of the solenoid behaves as a magnetic North pole and the other end as a South pole.
  • Inside the core of the solenoid, the magnetic field lines are parallel straight lines. This indicates that the magnetic field is completely uniform at all points inside the solenoid.
  • Electromagnet: When a soft iron rod is placed inside the solenoid core, the strong magnetic field magnetizes the iron, forming a powerful temporary electromagnet.

Question 3
State Fleming's Left-Hand Rule. When is the force experienced by a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field maximum?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Fleming's Left-Hand Rule:

Statement: Stretch the thumb, forefinger, and middle finger of your left hand mutually perpendicular to each other such that:

  1. The Forefinger points in the direction of the Magnetic Field ($\vec{B}$).
  2. The Middle finger points in the direction of the Electric Current ($\vec{I}$).
  3. Then the Thumb points in the direction of the Mechanical Force / Motion ($\vec{F}$) acting on the conductor.

Condition for Maximum Force:

The mechanical force experienced by a conductor of length $l$ carrying current $I$ in a magnetic field $B$ is given by $F = I l B \sin\theta$.

  • The force is maximum when the conductor is oriented perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to the magnetic field ($\sin 90^\circ = 1$).
  • The force is zero when the conductor is parallel ($0^\circ$) to the magnetic field ($\sin 0^\circ = 0$).

Question 4
Describe the three wires used in domestic electric circuits with their color codes and potential differences. Explain why two separate circuits (5 A and 15 A) are used in houses.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Domestic Electric Supply Circuits:

1. Three Types of Wires in Domestic Circuits:

  1. Live Wire (Phase wire, usually Red / Brown insulation): Carries current from the power station at a high electric potential of $220\text{ V}$.
  2. Neutral Wire (Negative wire, usually Black / Blue insulation): Completes the circuit back to the generator at zero potential ($0\text{ V}$). The potential difference between live and neutral wires in India is $220\text{ V} - 0\text{ V} = 220\text{ V}$ AC ($50\text{ Hz}$).
  3. Earth Wire (Grounding wire, usually Green / Yellow insulation): A safety wire connected to a copper plate buried deep underground; carries zero potential under normal operation.

2. Two Independent Circuits in Domestic Households:

  • 15 A Power Circuit: For high power-rating heating appliances (air conditioners, geysers, refrigerators, washing machines).
  • 5 A Lighting Circuit: For low power-rating appliances (fans, LED tube lights, television, laptops).

3. Electrical Safety Devices:

  • Electric Fuse / MCB (Miniature Circuit Breaker): Protects circuits and household appliances from damage caused by overloading and short circuits by automatically breaking current flow when threshold exceeds rated capacity.
  • Earthing: Connects metallic bodies of appliances to earth, preventing severe electric shocks.

Case Study Question • AC vs DC in Long-Distance Power Transmission
Electricity generated at hydroelectric or thermal power stations is transmitted over hundreds of kilometers to cities using Alternating Current (AC). (i) What is the fundamental difference between Alternating Current (AC) and Direct Current (DC)? (ii) What is the frequency of domestic AC supply in India? (iii) State one major advantage of AC over DC in power transmission.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) AC vs DC:
    • Direct Current (DC): Electric current flows in a single constant unidirectional path without reversing polarity (e.g. from dry cells/batteries).
    • Alternating Current (AC): Electric current reverses its direction of flow periodically at regular intervals of time.
  2. (ii) AC Frequency in India: $50\text{ Hz}$ (50 cycles per second). The current changes direction every $\frac{1}{100}\text{ second}$ (100 reversals per second).
  3. (iii) Major Advantage of AC: Alternating current can be stepped up to extraordinarily high voltages (e.g. $132\text{ kV}, 400\text{ kV}$) using step-up transformers. Because power loss in transmission lines is $P_{\text{loss}} = I^2 R$, transmitting at high voltage dramatically reduces current ($I$), enabling electric power to be transmitted over hundreds of kilometers with negligible Joule heating loss. DC cannot be transformed easily.
