Chapter 7: How do Organisms Reproduce?

Asexual Modes (Fission, Budding, Spores, Vegetative Propagation), Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants, Human Reproductive System & Contraception — CBSE Board Marking Scheme 2026-27

NCERT In-Text Questions (Pages 118, 123, 128) & Chapter-End Exercises (Questions 1 to 11)
In-Text Page 118 • Q1
What is the importance of DNA copying in reproduction?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Significance of DNA Copying:

  1. Transmission of Genetic Blueprint: DNA contains all biochemical instructions for cellular design, organ structure, and physiological functioning. Accurate DNA replication before cell division ensures that identical genetic blueprints are transmitted from parental cells to offspring.
  2. Facilitating Natural Variations: No biochemical replication mechanism is 100% error-free. Small subtle inaccuracies during replication introduce minor genetic variations in the progeny. These variations provide raw material for natural selection, enabling species to survive in changing ecological environments.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Ensures accurate inheritance of genetic traits and body design from parent to offspring1 Mark
Inaccuracies during replication create variations essential for evolution and survival1 Mark
In-Text Page 118 • Q2
Why is variation beneficial to the species but not necessarily for the individual?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Variation: Species vs Individual:

  1. For the Individual: A drastic mutation or sudden variation may be disadvantageous or lethal for an individual organism if it disrupts normal cellular metabolism or physiology in its current habitat.
  2. For the Species: If a catastrophic environmental change occurs (such as global warming, temperature rise, or drought), a uniform population without variation could be completely wiped out. However, if some variant individuals in the population possess heat-resistant or drought-resistant traits, they will survive the catastrophe and reproduce, preventing the species from becoming extinct.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Individual may suffer disadvantage or death from drastic unsuited mutations1 Mark
Species benefits because variant members survive sudden ecological crises, preventing extinction1 Mark
In-Text Page 123 • Q1
How does binary fission differ from multiple fission?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Comparison:

Feature Binary Fission Multiple Fission
Number of Daughters Parent cell divides into exactly two equal daughter cells. Parent cell divides simultaneously into many daughter individuals.
Conditions & Cyst Occurs during favorable conditions; no cyst is formed. Often occurs during unfavorable conditions; parent encloses in a protective cyst wall.
Examples Amoeba, Paramecium, Leishmania. Plasmodium (malarial parasite), encysted Amoeba.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Binary fission: Parent divides into 2 equal daughters in favorable conditions (Amoeba)1 Mark
Multiple fission: Parent divides into many daughters simultaneously inside cyst (Plasmodium)1 Mark
In-Text Page 123 • Q2
How will an organism be benefited if it reproduces through spores?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Benefits of Spore Formation:

  1. Protective Thick Wall: Spores (as in Rhizopus bread mould) are microscopic reproductive units enclosed by thick, durable protective coats that resist extreme desiccation, high temperatures, and lack of nutrients. They can remain dormant for months.
  2. Easy Aerial Dispersal: Spores are minute and lightweight, allowing them to be dispersed over vast geographic distances by wind and air currents.
  3. Rapid Proliferation: A single sporangium produces thousands of spores. Upon landing on a suitable moist substrate with organic food, each spore germinates into a new mycelium colony.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Thick protective coat withstands unfavorable harsh environmental conditions1 Mark
Lightweight allows wide aerial dispersal and rapid multiplication upon favorable contact1 Mark
In-Text Page 123 • Q3
Can you think of reasons why more complex organisms cannot give rise to new individuals through regeneration?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Reason:

  1. Simple organisms like Planaria and Hydra have relatively simple, non-differentiated body organization containing abundant uncommitted stem-like reserve cells capable of rapid mitosis and differentiation into any tissue.
  2. In complex multicellular organisms (like humans, birds, insects), cells are organized into specialized tissues, tissues into organs, and organs into integrated organ systems. Once cells differentiate into specialized nerve, muscle, or bone cells, they lose the totipotent ability to regenerate an entire organism from a severed body part.
  3. Therefore, complex organisms require specialized reproductive tissues and germ cells (gametes) rather than simple physical regeneration.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Complex organisms have high structural differentiation: cells -> tissues -> organs -> systems1 Mark
Specialised differentiated cells lose totipotency and cannot regenerate an entire body from a fragment1 Mark
In-Text Page 123 • Q4
Why is vegetative propagation practised for growing some types of plants?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Advantages of Vegetative Propagation:

  1. Seedless Plants: Plants that have lost the capacity to produce viable seeds (such as banana, seedless grapes, orange, rose, and jasmine) can be propagated only through vegetative parts (cuttings, runners, grafting).
  2. Genetic Uniformity: All offspring plants produced vegetatively are genetically identical clones of the parent, preserving desirable commercial characteristics (fruit flavor, disease resistance, flower size).
  3. Faster Maturity: Plants grown vegetatively bear flowers and fruits much earlier than those raised from seeds.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Propagates seedless plants (banana, rose, jasmine) that cannot reproduce by seeds1 Mark
Preserves exact desirable parental traits (genetic clones) and ensures faster flowering/fruiting1 Mark
In-Text Page 123 • Q5
Why is DNA copying an essential part of the process of reproduction?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Essential Nature of DNA Copying:

  1. Reproduction involves the creation of a new independent living individual resembling its parents in basic body structure and biochemical capabilities.
  2. DNA in chromosomes stores the master biochemical blueprint for building proteins and cell structures.
  3. Therefore, DNA copying is essential to create two identical copies of genetic material so that each daughter cell receives a complete set of genetic instructions along with sufficient cellular apparatus to sustain life.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
DNA contains all genetic information for structural and metabolic functioning1 Mark
Copying ensures both daughter cells receive complete parental genetic material1 Mark

In-Text Page 128 • Q2
What is the role of the seminal vesicles and the prostate gland?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Functions of Accessory Glands in Male Reproductive System:

The secretions of the seminal vesicles and prostate gland mix with sperm cells from the vas deferens to form semen:

  1. Fluid Medium for Transport: Their alkaline secretions provide a liquid medium that allows sperm cells to swim actively and smoothly through the male urethra and female reproductive tract.
  2. Nutrition: Seminal vesicle fluid contains fructose, citric acid, and enzymes that provide metabolic energy to power sperm motility.
  3. Neutralising Acidity: The alkaline secretions neutralize the acidic environment of both the male urethra and the female vagina, protecting sperm from death.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Provide fluid medium and nutrients (fructose) for sperm motility and viability1 Mark
Alkaline secretions neutralise acidity in urethra and vagina to protect sperms1 Mark
In-Text Page 128 • Q3
What are the changes seen in girls at the time of puberty?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Secondary Sexual Characteristics in Girls at Puberty (Ages 10–12):

  1. Development of Breasts: Enlargement of mammary glands with darkening of skin of the nipples.
  2. Pubic and Axillary Hair: Growth of fine hair in the pubic region and underarm armpits.
  3. Broadening of Hips: Widening of the pelvic girdle and fat deposition on hips and thighs.
  4. Initiation of Menstruation (Menarche): Commencing monthly menstrual bleeding cycle signaling ovarian ovulation.
  5. Skin Changes: Increased sebaceous gland secretion, sometimes leading to acne.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Breast development, pelvic widening, and pubic/axillary hair growth1 Mark
Commencement of menstruation (menarche) and ovulation1 Mark
In-Text Page 128 • Q4
How does the embryo get nourishment inside the mother's body?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Nourishment via Placenta:

  1. The developing embryo is connected to the maternal uterine wall by a specialized disc-like vascular tissue called the Placenta.
  2. Structure: The placenta contains millions of microscopic finger-like villi on the embryonic side, embedded into large maternal blood spaces on the mother's side.
  3. Function: This vascular interface provides a huge surface area through which glucose, amino acids, and oxygen diffuse directly from maternal blood into fetal capillaries, while metabolic wastes ($\text{CO}_2$, urea) produced by the embryo diffuse into maternal blood for excretion.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Specialised disc-shaped tissue called placenta connecting embryo to uterine wall1 Mark
Placental villi facilitate diffusion of glucose and oxygen into embryo and remove waste1 Mark
In-Text Page 128 • Q5
If a woman is using a copper-T, will it help in protecting her from sexually transmitted diseases?
[1 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Answer: No, a copper-T does not protect against Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STDs).

Reason: A copper-T is an Intrauterine Device (IUD) placed inside the uterus to prevent implantation of the embryo. It does not provide a physical barrier to prevent fluid contact between reproductive organs; hence viral and bacterial STD pathogens (like HIV, syphilis, gonorrhoea) can still be transmitted. Only barrier methods (condoms) protect against STDs.

CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
No; copper-T prevents pregnancy/implantation but does not block fluid contact transmitting STDs1 Mark

NCERT Exercise • Q4
What are the advantages of sexual reproduction over asexual reproduction?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Advantages of Sexual Reproduction:

  1. Genetic Diversity & Variation: Sexual reproduction involves the fusion of gametes from two genetically distinct individuals, combined with crossing-over during meiosis. This produces offspring with novel genetic combinations and high phenotypic diversity.
  2. Adaptation and Survival: Increased variation enhances a population's capacity to adapt to changing environments, resist emerging pathogens, and avoid extinction during natural catastrophes.
  3. Driver of Evolution: Variations generated by sexual reproduction serve as the fundamental raw material for natural selection and speciation.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Generates genetic diversity through combination of genes from two parents and meiosis1 Mark
Promotes better adaptability and survival against changing environmental conditions1 Mark
Provides essential variations fueling natural selection and biological evolution1 Mark

What are the functions performed by the testis in human beings?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Dual Functions of Testes:

  1. Spermatogenesis: Production of male gametes (sperms) within the seminiferous tubules.
  2. Hormone Secretion: Endocrine Leydig cells in the testes secrete the primary male sex hormone testosterone, which controls:
    • Development of secondary sexual characteristics in males (facial hair, deep voice, muscle development).
    • Maturation of sperm and male accessory reproductive glands.

NCERT Exercise • Q6
Why does menstruation occur?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Reason for Menstruation:

  1. Every month, one ovary releases a mature egg (ovulation). In anticipation of receiving a fertilized zygote, the inner lining of the uterus (endometrium) thickens, becomes soft, and develops an extensive blood capillary network to nourish a potential embryo.
  2. If the egg is not fertilised by a sperm, the unfertilised ovum disintegrates.
  3. Deprived of hormonal support (progesterone levels plummet), the thick, vascular uterine endometrial lining breaks down and sheds.
  4. The detached mucosal lining along with blood and unfertilised egg is discharged through the vagina as menstruation, lasting approximately 3 to 5 days.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Uterine lining thickens each month to prepare for receiving fertilized egg1 Mark
If fertilisation fails, vascular endometrial lining breaks down and discharges as blood/tissue1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q7
Draw a labelled diagram of the longitudinal section of a flower.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Four Whorls of a Typical Flower (Longitudinal Section):

  1. Calyx (Sepals): Outermost green leaf-like whorl protecting the flower in bud stage.
  2. Corolla (Petals): Brightly coloured whorl that attracts insect pollinators.
  3. Androecium (Male Reproductive Whorl - Stamens):
    • Filament: Slender supportive stalk.
    • Anther: Bilobed terminal head producing pollen grains.
  4. Gynoecium / Pistil (Female Reproductive Whorl - Carpel):
    • Stigma: Sticky terminal surface that traps pollen grains.
    • Style: Elongated tubular neck connecting stigma to ovary.
    • Ovary: Swollen basal chamber enclosing one or more ovules with female egg cells.

Labelled female carpel whorl: Stigma, style, ovary with ovules1.5 Marks

NCERT Exercise • Q8
What are the different methods of contraception?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Contraceptive Methods:

  1. 1. Barrier Methods: Physical barriers like condoms, diaphragms, and cervical caps prevent physical contact and entry of sperms into the female genital tract. (Condoms also provide protection against STDs).
  2. 2. Chemical / Hormonal Methods: Oral contraceptive pills contain synthetic estrogen and progesterone combinations that inhibit ovulation so that no egg is released from the ovary.
  3. 3. Intrauterine Contraceptive Devices (IUCDs): Devices such as Copper-T inserted into the uterus by healthcare providers prevent implantation of the blastocyst.
  4. 4. Surgical Methods (Sterilization):
    • Vasectomy: In males, a small section of each vas deferens is cut and tied to prevent sperm transfer into semen.
    • Tubectomy: In females, a portion of each fallopian tube is cut and tied to prevent eggs from reaching the site of fertilisation.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Barrier methods (condoms) and chemical/hormonal pills preventing ovulation1 Mark
IUCDs (Copper-T) preventing uterine implantation1 Mark
Surgical methods: Vasectomy (males) and Tubectomy (females) as permanent contraception1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q9
How are the modes for reproduction different in unicellular and multicellular organisms?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Comparison:

  1. Unicellular Organisms: The entire body consists of a single microscopic cell. Reproduction is predominantly asexual and occurs by simple direct cell division (binary fission in Amoeba, budding in yeast, multiple fission in Plasmodium) where the parent cell divides into two or more daughters without specialized germ cells.
  2. Multicellular Organisms: Possess complex bodies with structural differentiation into tissues and organs. They cannot reproduce by simple fission because cells are specialized. Reproduction predominantly occurs sexually through specialized reproductive organs producing haploid gametes (sperms and ova), ensuring genetic recombination.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Unicellular: Simple direct asexual cell division (binary fission/budding) without gametes1 Mark
Multicellular: Complex reproductive organs producing specialized haploid gametes via meiosis1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q10
How does reproduction help in providing stability to populations of species?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Role of Reproduction in Population Stability:

  1. Living organisms in a population inevitably die due to aging, predation, disease, and environmental hazards.
  2. Reproduction counterbalances mortality by continually adding new individuals of the same species to replace deceased members.
  3. DNA copying during reproduction transmits species-specific morphological and physiological traits that keep the population suited to its specific ecological niche, maintaining demographic and genetic stability.
CBSE Board Marking Scheme Breakdown 2026-27
Replaces individuals lost due to death, disease, and aging to maintain population size1 Mark
Transmits adaptive body design ensuring stable occupation of ecological niche1 Mark
NCERT Exercise • Q11
What could be the reasons for adopting contraceptive methods?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Reasons for Adopting Contraceptive Methods:

  1. Family Planning and Population Control: Prevents unwanted pregnancies, allowing parents to space births and support children with adequate nutrition, education, and resources.
  2. Protection of Maternal Health: Frequent, closely spaced pregnancies cause severe maternal anaemia, malnutrition, and reproductive health hazards.
  3. Prevention of STDs: Barrier methods (condoms) provide effective protection against sexually transmitted infections such as HIV/AIDS, syphilis, and gonorrhoea.

Prevention of unwanted pregnancies and family planning1 Mark
Protection of maternal health and prevention of STDs like HIV1 Mark

Case Study Question • Double Fertilisation in Angiosperms
In flowering plants, the pollen tube delivers two male gametes into the embryo sac inside the ovule. (i) What is syngamy and what does it produce? (ii) What is triple fusion and what does it produce? (iii) Why is this process called double fertilisation?

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Syngamy: The fusion of one haploid male gamete ($n$) with the haploid female egg cell ($n$) to form a diploid zygote ($2n$), which later develops into the embryo.
  2. (ii) Triple Fusion: The fusion of the second haploid male gamete ($n$) with the diploid secondary nucleus / two polar nuclei ($2n$) to form the triploid Primary Endosperm Nucleus (PEN, $3n$), which develops into nutritive endosperm tissue.
  3. (iii) Double Fertilisation: Because two distinct fertilisation events (syngamy and triple fusion) take place simultaneously in the same female embryo sac, the phenomenon is called Double Fertilisation (unique to angiosperms).
