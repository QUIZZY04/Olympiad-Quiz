Question 1
Why should curd and sour substances not be kept in brass and copper vessels?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Chemical Explanation and Balanced Equations:

When curd, lemon juice, or other sour substances (which contain organic acids like lactic acid, citric acid) are kept in brass and copper vessels, the acids react with the active copper and zinc metals present in the alloy to produce toxic, poisonous metal salts and hydrogen gas:

$$\text{Acid} + \text{Metal} \longrightarrow \text{Toxic Metal Salt} + \text{H}_2(g)\uparrow$$

These metal compounds make the food poisonous and unfit for human consumption, leading to severe food poisoning and gastrointestinal distress. Hence, curd and sour foodstuffs must never be stored in brass and copper containers; instead, glass, stainless steel, or tin-coated (kalai) containers are recommended.

Question 2
Why does an aqueous solution of an acid conduct electricity, whereas solutions of compounds like alcohol and glucose do not?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Mechanism of Electrical Conduction:

Electric current in solutions is carried by freely moving mobile ions.

  1. Aqueous Acid Solution ($HCl, H_2SO_4$):
    When an acid like hydrochloric acid dissolves in water, it undergoes complete ionisation into hydronium ions ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$) and chloride ions ($\text{Cl}^-$): $$\text{HCl}(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \longrightarrow \text{H}_3\text{O}^+(aq) + \text{Cl}^-(aq)$$ The presence of these mobile ions enables the aqueous acid solution to conduct electricity.
  2. Glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$) and Alcohol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$):
    Although glucose and alcohol molecules contain hydrogen atoms, their hydrogen atoms are bonded by strong covalent bonds. When dissolved in water, they do not dissociate to form hydrogen ($\text{H}^+$) or hydronium ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$) ions.
  3. Conclusion: In the absence of free mobile ions, solutions of glucose and alcohol do not show acidic character and do not conduct electricity.

Question 3
Why is it recommended that acid should be added to water and not water to the acid?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Safety Protocol and Thermodynamic Principle:

The dilution of a concentrated acid (especially concentrated sulphuric acid $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) is an extremely exothermic process that releases a large amount of heat energy.

Why Acid must be added to Water:

  1. If Water is added to Concentrated Acid:
    The initial drops of water react with an enormous excess of acid. The huge localized heat generated instantly boils the water into steam, causing the corrosive hot acid to splash violently out of the container onto the face, hands, or clothes of the operator, causing severe chemical burns. Furthermore, the localized thermal shock may crack and break the glass beaker.
  2. Safe Method (Acid added slowly to Water):
    When concentrated acid is added dropwise down the side of the beaker into a large volume of water with constant stirring, the water has a very high specific heat capacity. It absorbs and dissipates the released heat evenly throughout the mixture, preventing boiling and acid splashing.

Question 4
Tooth enamel is one of the hardest substances in our body. How does it undergo damage due to eating sweets? What should be done to prevent it?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Biochemical Mechanism of Tooth Decay:

  1. Normal Enamel: Tooth enamel is composed of calcium hydroxyapatite (a crystalline form of calcium phosphate $\text{Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$), which is the hardest substance in the human body. It is insoluble in water.
  2. Acid Attack (Threshold $\text{pH} < 5.5$):
    When we consume sweet, sugary, or starchy foods, bacteria present in the mouth degrade the residual carbohydrates and produce organic acids (like lactic acid). When the oral cavity pH falls below 5.5, the acid corrodes and dissolves the calcium phosphate enamel: $$\text{Enamel corrodes at pH } < 5.5$$
  3. Prevention:
    Cleaning the mouth with toothpaste after meals prevents decay because toothpastes are generally basic/alkaline in nature ($\text{pH} \approx 8-9$). They neutralize the excess acids produced by bacteria and protect enamel from corrosion.

Question 5
Plaster of Paris should be stored in a moisture-proof container. Explain why. Write the chemical equation for its reaction with water.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Chemical Properties and Storage of Plaster of Paris (POP):

  1. Formula of POP: Calcium sulphate hemihydrate, $\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
  2. Reason for Moisture-proof Storage:
    Plaster of Paris is a hygroscopic white powder. When exposed to even traces of atmospheric moisture or water, it rapidly hydrates and sets into a hard, solid mass called Gypsum, losing its pliable plastering properties: $$\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}(s) + 1\frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \longrightarrow \text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(s) \quad \text{(Gypsum - Hard solid mass)}$$
  3. Therefore, to prevent premature setting and spoilage, Plaster of Paris must strictly be stored in air-tight, moisture-proof containers.

Question 6
Give the chemical name, chemical formula, method of preparation and two important uses of: (i) Bleaching powder, (ii) Baking soda, (iii) Washing soda.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Industrial Salts: Preparation, Chemical Formulae & Key Applications

1. Bleaching Powder:

  • Chemical Name & Formula: Calcium oxychloride, $\text{CaOCl}_2$.
  • Preparation: Prepared by the action of dry chlorine gas on dry slaked lime ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$): $$\text{Ca(OH)}_2(s) + \text{Cl}_2(g) \longrightarrow \text{CaOCl}_2(s) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$
  • Key Uses:
    (a) For bleaching cotton and linen in textile industry and wood pulp in paper factories.
    (b) As a disinfectant for sterilizing drinking water to make it germ-free.
    (c) As an oxidising agent in chemical laboratories.

2. Baking Soda:

  • Chemical Name & Formula: Sodium hydrogen carbonate (Sodium bicarbonate), $\text{NaHCO}_3$.
  • Preparation (Solvay Process): $$\text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 + \text{NH}_3 \longrightarrow \text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{NaHCO}_3\downarrow$$
  • Key Uses:
    (a) As an ingredient in baking powder (mixture of $\text{NaHCO}_3$ and mild edible tartaric acid) to make cakes and bread soft and spongy by releasing $\text{CO}_2$: $$\text{NaHCO}_3 + \text{H}^+ \longrightarrow \text{CO}_2\uparrow + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Sodium salt of acid}$$
    (b) As an active antacid to neutralize excess stomach acidity.
    (c) In soda-acid fire extinguishers.

3. Washing Soda:

  • Chemical Name & Formula: Sodium carbonate decahydrate, $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
  • Preparation: Recrystallisation of anhydrous sodium carbonate (soda ash) from water: $$\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3(s) + 10\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \longrightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}(s)$$
  • Key Uses:
    (a) In glass, soap, and paper industries.
    (b) For removing permanent hardness of water (precipitating $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions).
    (c) As a domestic cleansing agent for laundry.

Case Study Question • pH in Human Digestion & Antacids
A patient visited a doctor complaining of acute burning sensation and sharp pain in the upper abdomen after having a spicy meal. The doctor diagnosed hyperacidity and prescribed a milk of magnesia suspension. (i) Name the acid produced naturally in our stomach and state its biological role. (ii) What is milk of magnesia? Write its chemical formula. (iii) Write the neutralization reaction that takes place in the patient's stomach.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Natural Stomach Acid: Hydrochloric acid ($\text{HCl}$). It maintains a strongly acidic pH ($\approx 1.2 - 2.0$) necessary to activate the gastric enzyme propepsin into active pepsin for protein digestion, and kills harmful bacteria ingested with food.
  2. (ii) Milk of Magnesia: It is a mild, non-corrosive suspension of Magnesium hydroxide in water. Chemical Formula: $\text{Mg(OH)}_2$.
  3. (iii) Neutralization Reaction:
    $$\text{Mg(OH)}_2(aq) + 2\text{HCl}(aq) \longrightarrow \text{MgCl}_2(aq) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$ It safely neutralizes the excess stomach acid without damaging the delicate mucous membrane lining.
