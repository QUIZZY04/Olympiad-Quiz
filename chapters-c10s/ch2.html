You have been provided with three test tubes. One of them contains distilled water and the other two contain an acidic solution and a basic solution, respectively. If you are given only red litmus paper, how will you identify the contents of each test tube?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Identification Procedure using Red Litmus Paper only:

  1. Dip a strip of red litmus paper into each of the three test tubes one by one.
  2. The test tube in which the red litmus turns blue contains the basic solution.
  3. Now take this newly turned blue litmus paper and dip it into the remaining two test tubes.
  4. The solution that turns this blue litmus paper back to red is the acidic solution.
  5. The remaining test tube which causes no colour change with either red litmus or blue litmus contains distilled water (neutral).

In-Text Page 22 • Q1
Why should curd and sour substances not be kept in brass and copper vessels?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Chemical Reason:

Curd and other sour food substances contain organic acids (such as lactic acid). These acids react chemically with the metals copper and zinc (present in brass) to form toxic, poisonous metal salts and liberate hydrogen gas:

$$\text{Acid} + \text{Metal (Cu, Zn)} \longrightarrow \text{Toxic Metal Salt} + \text{H}_2(g)$$

Consuming food contaminated with these toxic copper/zinc compounds causes severe food poisoning and damage to health. Therefore, sour substances should be stored in glass, ceramic, or stainless steel containers.

In-Text Page 25 • Q1
Why do $\text{HCl}$, $\text{HNO}_3$, etc., show acidic characters in aqueous solutions while solutions of compounds like alcohol and glucose do not show acidic character?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Explanation:

  1. Acidic behaviour is solely due to the presence of free hydrogen ions $\text{H}^+(aq)$ or hydronium ions $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ in aqueous solution.
  2. Compounds like $\text{HCl}$ and $\text{HNO}_3$ are polar covalent molecules. In water, they ionise completely to produce hydronium ions: $$\text{HCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + \text{Cl}^-$$
  3. Compounds like glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$) and ethyl alcohol ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$) also contain hydrogen, but their hydrogen atoms are bonded by non-ionisable covalent bonds. They do not dissociate in water to produce $\text{H}^+$ or $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ ions. Hence, they do not show acidic character or turn blue litmus red.

In-Text Page 25 • Q2
Why does an aqueous solution of an acid conduct electricity?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Mechanism of Electrical Conduction:

Electrical conductivity in liquids requires the presence of mobile, charge-carrying particles (ions). When an acid dissolves in water, it undergoes ionisation to produce freely moving cations (hydronium ions, $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$) and anions (such as $\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$). Under an electric potential difference, these mobile ions migrate towards oppositely charged electrodes, thereby conducting electric current.

In-Text Page 25 • Q3
Why does dry $\text{HCl}$ gas not change the colour of the dry litmus paper?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Reason:

Dry $\text{HCl}$ gas contains covalent hydrogen chloride molecules and exists in the molecular form without any moisture. Acids exhibit acidic properties and ionise to release hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$) only in the presence of water:

$$\text{HCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + \text{Cl}^-$$

In the total absence of moisture (dry gas and dry litmus), $\text{HCl}$ cannot dissociate to form $\text{H}^+(aq)$ or $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ ions. Since hydrogen ions are necessary to effect the colour change of litmus indicator, dry $\text{HCl}$ gas cannot change the colour of dry blue litmus paper.

In-Text Page 25 • Q4
While diluting an acid, why is it recommended that the acid should be added to water and not water to the acid?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Thermodynamic and Safety Rationale:

The dilution of a concentrated mineral acid (such as concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) is an extremely exothermic process that liberates a large quantity of heat energy.

  • If water is added to concentrated acid: The small drop of water reacts with an immense excess of concentrated acid. The enormous localized heat instantaneously vaporizes the water into steam, causing the corrosive hot acid to splash violently onto the face and hands of the operator, causing severe chemical burns. The thermal shock can also shatter the glass container.
  • If acid is added slowly to water: Water has a very high specific heat capacity. Adding the concentrated acid dropwise down the sides of the container into a large volume of water with constant stirring allows the heat generated to be absorbed and dissipated safely throughout the water without boiling or splashing.

In-Text Page 25 • Q5
How is the concentration of hydronium ions ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$) affected when a solution of an acid is diluted?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Effect of Dilution on $[\text{H}_3\text{O}^+]$:

Dilution means adding more water to an existing acid solution without changing the amount of acid solute. As water is added, the total volume of the solution increases, but the total number of hydronium ions remains essentially the same. Therefore, the concentration of hydronium ions ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$) per unit volume decreases, resulting in a decrease in acidity and an increase in pH.

In-Text Page 25 • Q6
How is the concentration of hydroxide ions ($\text{OH}^-$) affected when excess base is dissolved in a solution of sodium hydroxide?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Effect of Dissolving Excess Base:

When excess sodium hydroxide (a strong base) is dissolved in an existing $\text{NaOH}$ solution, it dissociates completely into additional sodium ions ($\text{Na}^+$) and hydroxide ions ($\text{OH}^-$):

$$\text{NaOH}(s) \xrightarrow{\text{water}} \text{Na}^+(aq) + \text{OH}^-(aq)$$

Since more $\text{OH}^-$ ions are added to the existing volume of solution, the concentration of hydroxide ions ($\text{OH}^-$) per unit volume increases, making the solution more basic and raising its pH.

In-Text Page 28 • Q3
Do basic solutions also have $\text{H}^+(aq)$ ions? If yes, then why are these basic?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Presence of $\text{H}^+$ in Basic Solutions:

Yes, basic solutions also contain $\text{H}^+(aq)$ ions. In any aqueous solution, water self-ionises according to the ionic product of water ($K_w = [\text{H}^+][\text{OH}^-] = 10^{-14}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$):

$$\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+ + \text{OH}^-$$

A solution is basic because the concentration of hydroxide ions ($\text{OH}^-$) is much greater than the concentration of hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$):

$$[\text{OH}^-] \gg [\text{H}^+]$$

Because hydroxide ions vastly predominate over hydrogen ions, the solution exhibits alkaline/basic properties.

NCERT Exercise • Q8
Why do acids not show acidic behaviour in the absence of water?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Fundamental Chemical Principle:

Acidic behaviour (such as sour taste, turning blue litmus red, reacting with metals to liberate $\text{H}_2$) is strictly caused by the presence of free hydrogen ions $\text{H}^+(aq)$ or hydronium ions $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$.

An acid molecule consists of polar covalent bonds. It can only ionise and dissociate to release $\text{H}^+$ ions when water molecules are present to hydrate and pull apart the ions via dipole-dipole attractions:

$$\text{HX} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + \text{X}^-$$

In the complete absence of water, acids exist entirely as neutral covalent molecules without free hydronium ions, and therefore cannot exhibit acidic behaviour.

  1. Observation: Fizzing (effervescence due to the rapid liberation of hydrogen gas) will occur much more vigorously in test tube A containing hydrochloric acid.

    • $\text{HCl}$ is a strong mineral acid that undergoes almost $100\%$ complete dissociation in aqueous solution into $\text{H}^+$ and $\text{Cl}^-$ ions: $$\text{HCl} \longrightarrow \text{H}^+ + \text{Cl}^-$$ Hence, test tube A contains a very high concentration of $\text{H}^+$ ions, causing a very rapid reaction with magnesium.
    • $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ (acetic acid) is a weak organic acid that undergoes only partial dissociation in water: $$\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{COO}^- + \text{H}^+$$ Because $[\text{H}^+]$ is substantially lower in test tube B, the rate of hydrogen gas evolution is much slower.

NCERT Exercise • Q11
Fresh milk has a pH of 6. How do you think the pH will change as it turns into curd? Explain your answer.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Change in pH during Curdling:

As fresh milk turns into curd, its pH will decrease below 6 (becoming more acidic, typically around pH 4.5 – 5.0).

Explanation: During curd formation, the bacterium Lactobacillus ferments the natural milk sugar (lactose) into lactic acid. The production of lactic acid increases the concentration of hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$) in the milk. Because pH is inversely related to $[\text{H}^+]$, the increase in lactic acid concentration lowers the pH of the milk.

NCERT Exercise • Q12
A milkman adds a very small amount of baking soda to fresh milk. (a) Why does he shift the pH of the fresh milk from 6 to slightly alkaline? (b) Why does this milk take a long time to set as curd?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Detailed Answers:

  1. (a) Reason for shifting pH to slightly alkaline:
    Baking soda ($\text{NaHCO}_3$) is a mild base. Adding it shifts the pH of fresh milk from 6 to around 7.4–8.0 (slightly alkaline). In an alkaline medium, lactic acid bacteria cannot grow rapidly, and any small amount of lactic acid formed naturally by souring is immediately neutralised. This prevents the milk from spoiling or curdling quickly during warm weather.
  2. (b) Why it takes a longer time to set as curd:
    For milk to coagulate into curd, its pH must drop significantly to acidic levels (around pH 4.5) to precipitate the casein protein. Because the milk has been made alkaline, the lactic acid produced initially is consumed in neutralising the added baking soda. Only after the alkaline base is completely neutralised can the pH drop below 6 to form curd. Hence, it takes a much longer time to set.

NCERT Exercise • Q13
Plaster of Paris should be stored in a moisture-proof container. Explain why.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Reason for Moisture-proof Storage:

Plaster of Paris ($\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}$) is a hygroscopic powder. When it comes into contact with even atmospheric moisture, it absorbs water and undergoes hydration to form Gypsum ($\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$), setting into a hard, solid insoluble mass:

$$\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} + 1\frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$

Once it sets into gypsum, it loses its setting ability, plasticity, and usefulness for medical casts or decorative moulding. To prevent this spoilage, it must strictly be sealed in moisture-proof containers.

NCERT Exercise • Q14
What is a neutralisation reaction? Give two examples.
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Definition:

A chemical reaction in which an acid reacts with a base to form salt and water with the evolution of heat is called a neutralisation reaction:

$$\text{Acid} + \text{Base} \longrightarrow \text{Salt} + \text{Water} + \text{Heat}$$

At the ionic level, it involves combination of hydronium ions with hydroxide ions to form water: $\text{H}^+(aq) + \text{OH}^-(aq) \longrightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$.

Two Standard Examples:

  1. $$\text{NaOH}(aq) + \text{HCl}(aq) \longrightarrow \text{NaCl}(aq) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$
  2. $$\text{Mg(OH)}_2(aq) + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4(aq) \longrightarrow \text{MgSO}_4(aq) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)$$

Case Study Question • Chlor-Alkali Process & Industrial By-Products
During the electrolysis of an aqueous solution of sodium chloride (brine), three economically crucial industrial chemicals are co-produced. (i) Name the process and write the balanced overall chemical equation. (ii) Name the gas collected at the anode and the cathode respectively. (iii) Give one major commercial application for each of the three products obtained.

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Process Name & Overall Equation: Chlor-Alkali Process (named because 'chlor' stands for chlorine and 'alkali' stands for sodium hydroxide). $$2\text{NaCl}(aq) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \xrightarrow{\text{Electricity}} 2\text{NaOH}(aq) + \text{Cl}_2(g)\uparrow + \text{H}_2(g)\uparrow$$
  2. (ii) Gases Liberated at Electrodes:
    • Anode (+): Chlorine gas ($\text{Cl}_2$) is liberated.
    • Cathode (-): Hydrogen gas ($\text{H}_2$) is liberated. (Sodium hydroxide solution forms near the cathode).
  3. (iii) Industrial Applications:
    • $\text{Cl}_2$: Water treatment (disinfectant), manufacture of PVC, CFCs, pesticides, and bleaching powder.
    • $\text{H}_2$: Fuel for rockets, hydrogenation of vegetable oils to form vanaspati ghee, production of ammonia for fertilisers.
    • $\text{NaOH}$: De-greasing metals, manufacture of soaps, detergents, paper making, and artificial textile fibres (rayon).
