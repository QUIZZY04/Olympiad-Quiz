Question 1
Why do we prefer a convex mirror as a rear-view mirror in vehicles?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Optical Reasons for Using Convex Mirrors as Rear-View Mirrors in Vehicles:

  1. Always Produces an Erect Image: A convex mirror always forms an erect (upright) and virtual image of objects placed anywhere in front of it, allowing the driver to see vehicles right-side up.
  2. Diminished Image Size: The image produced is always diminished (smaller in size), which allows distant and approaching vehicles to be seen clearly within the mirror's frame.
  3. Much Wider Field of View: Because convex mirrors are curved outwards, they have a vastly wider field of view compared to a plane mirror or concave mirror. This enables the driver to monitor a significantly larger area of traffic behind the vehicle, dramatically enhancing road safety during reversing and lane changes.

Question 2
An object, 4.0 cm in size, is placed at 25.0 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15.0 cm. At what distance from the mirror should a screen be placed in order to obtain a sharp image? Find the nature and the size of the image.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Numerical Solution:

Step 1: Given Parameters (New Cartesian Sign Convention)

  • Object height: $h = +4.0\text{ cm}$
  • Object distance: $u = -25.0\text{ cm}$ (in front of mirror, negative)
  • Focal length of concave mirror: $f = -15.0\text{ cm}$ (concave mirror focal point is in front, negative)

Step 2: Calculate Image Distance ($v$) using Mirror Formula

$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} \implies \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u}$$ $$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{-15} - \frac{1}{-25} = -\frac{1}{15} + \frac{1}{25}$$

LCM of 15 and 25 is 75:

$$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{-5 + 3}{75} = -\frac{2}{75}$$ $$v = -\frac{75}{2} = -37.5\text{ cm}$$

The screen should be placed at a distance of $37.5\text{ cm}$ in front of the mirror.

Step 3: Calculate Image Size ($h'$) and Nature using Magnification Formula

$$m = -\frac{v}{u} = \frac{h'}{h}$$ $$h' = -\frac{v}{u} \times h = -\left(\frac{-37.5}{-25}\right) \times 4.0 = -(1.5) \times 4.0 = -6.0\text{ cm}$$
Figure: Ray Diagram of Concave Mirror with Object between C and F Forming Image Beyond C

Characteristics of the Image:

  • Position: At $37.5\text{ cm}$ in front of the mirror (on the same side as the object).
  • Nature: Real and Inverted (negative sign of $v$ and $h'$).
  • Size: Enlarged / Magnified (height $= 6.0\text{ cm}$, $|m| = 1.5 > 1$).

Question 3
State the laws of refraction of light. Light enters from air to glass having refractive index 1.50. What is the speed of light in the glass? (Speed of light in vacuum is $3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$).
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Laws of Refraction of Light:

  1. First Law: The incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal to the interface of two transparent media at the point of incidence, all lie in the same plane.
  2. Second Law (Snell's Law of Refraction):
    The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is a constant for the light of a given colour and for the given pair of media: $$\frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \text{constant} = n_{21} = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$$

Numerical Calculation of Speed of Light in Glass:

  • Absolute refractive index of glass: $n_g = 1.50$
  • Speed of light in vacuum: $c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$
$$n_g = \frac{c}{v_g} \implies v_g = \frac{c}{n_g}$$ $$v_g = \frac{3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}}{1.50} = 2.0 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$$

Final Answer: The speed of light in glass is $2 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$.

Question 4
A concave lens has focal length of 15 cm. At what distance should the object from the lens be placed so that it forms an image at 10 cm from the lens? Also, find the magnification produced by the lens and define power of a lens.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Numerical Solution:

Step 1: Given Parameters

  • Focal length of concave lens: $f = -15\text{ cm}$ (concave lens has virtual focus, negative)
  • Image distance: $v = -10\text{ cm}$ (concave lens always forms a virtual image on the same side as the object, negative)

Step 2: Calculate Object Distance ($u$) using Lens Formula

$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} \implies \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{f}$$ $$\frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{-10} - \frac{1}{-15} = -\frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{15}$$

LCM of 10 and 15 is 30:

$$\frac{1}{u} = \frac{-3 + 2}{30} = -\frac{1}{30} \implies u = -30\text{ cm}$$

The object should be placed at a distance of $30\text{ cm}$ in front of the lens.

Step 3: Calculate Magnification ($m$)

$$m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{-10\text{ cm}}{-30\text{ cm}} = +\frac{1}{3} \approx +0.33$$

The positive sign indicates an erect and virtual image, diminished to one-third of the object's height.

Step 4: Power of a Lens Definition & Formula:

The power of a lens ($P$) is defined as the reciprocal of its focal length expressed in metres. It measures the degree of convergence or divergence of light rays:

$$P = \frac{1}{f\text{ (in metres)}}$$

The SI unit of power of a lens is Dioptre (D). $1\text{ D} = 1\text{ m}^{-1}$.

Question 5
Explain the New Cartesian Sign Convention for reflection and refraction. Compare mirror formula with lens formula, and find the net power and focal length of a combination of lenses of powers +3.5 D and -1.5 D placed in contact.
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Comparison of Optical Instruments: Spherical Mirrors vs Spherical Lenses

1. Cartesian Sign Conventions:

  • Object is always placed to the left of the optical centre/pole: $u$ is always negative.
  • All distances measured in the direction of incident light are positive; opposite to incident light are negative.
  • Distances measured upward perpendicular to the principal axis are positive; downward are negative.
  • Focal Lengths:
    • Concave mirror & Concave lens: $f$ is negative ($-$).
    • Convex mirror & Convex lens: $f$ is positive ($+$).

2. Formula Comparison:

Parameter Spherical Mirrors Spherical Lenses
Fundamental Formula $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}$ $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u}$
Magnification ($m$) $m = -\frac{v}{u} = \frac{h'}{h}$ $m = +\frac{v}{u} = \frac{h'}{h}$
Converging Action Concave Mirror converges light rays. Convex Lens converges light rays.
Diverging Action Convex Mirror diverges light rays. Concave Lens diverges light rays.

3. Numerical on Power of Combination:

Find the focal length and nature of a lens combination consisting of a convex lens of $+3.5\text{ D}$ and a concave lens of $-1.5\text{ D}$ in contact:

$$P_{\text{net}} = P_1 + P_2 = +3.5\text{ D} + (-1.5\text{ D}) = +2.0\text{ D}$$ $$f_{\text{net}} = \frac{1}{P_{\text{net}}} = \frac{1}{+2.0\text{ D}} = +0.5\text{ m} = +50\text{ cm}$$

Since the net power and focal length are positive, the combination behaves as a converging (convex) lens of focal length $50\text{ cm}$.

Case Study Question • Solar Concentrators & Dental Mirrors
A dentist uses a small mirror to inspect cavities in the teeth of patients. Large curved mirrors are also used in solar furnaces to produce high temperatures. (i) Which type of mirror is used by dentists and why? (ii) Where should the patient's tooth be placed relative to this mirror? (iii) Which type of mirror is used in solar furnaces and where is the solar crucible placed?

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Dentist Mirror: A Concave mirror. When an object is placed between the pole ($P$) and principal focus ($F$) of a concave mirror, it forms an erect, virtual, and highly magnified image, enabling the dentist to inspect tiny dental fissures and tooth decay clearly.
  2. (ii) Position of Tooth: Between the pole ($P$) and the principal focus ($F$) of the mirror (within the focal length).
  3. (iii) Solar Furnace Mirror: Large Concave mirrors are used. The solar crucible or vessel containing materials to be melted is placed at the Principal Focus ($F$) of the mirror. Incoming parallel solar rays from the sun converge intensely at the focus, generating temperatures over $3000^\circ\text{C}$ without fossil fuels.
