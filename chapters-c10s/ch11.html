Therefore, $6.25 \times 10^{18}$ electrons constitute one coulomb of negative charge.

In-Text Page 198 • Q1
On what factors does the resistance of a conductor depend?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Factors Determining Electrical Resistance ($R$):

The resistance of a uniform metallic conductor depends on:

  1. Length ($l$): Resistance is directly proportional to conductor length ($R \propto l$). Doubling the length doubles resistance.
  2. Area of Cross-section ($A$): Resistance is inversely proportional to cross-sectional area ($R \propto 1/A$). Thicker wires have less resistance.
  3. Nature of Material (Resistivity, $\rho$): Characterized by the specific electrical resistivity $\rho$ of the material.
  4. Temperature ($T$): For pure metallic conductors, resistance increases linearly with rising temperature.
$$R = \rho \frac{l}{A}$$

Will current flow more easily through a thick wire or a thin wire of the same material, when connected to the same source? Why?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Answer & Scientific Explanation:

Current will flow more easily through the thick wire.

Reason: Resistance of a conductor is inversely proportional to its cross-sectional area ($R \propto 1/A$). A thick wire possesses a larger cross-sectional area ($A_{\text{thick}} > A_{\text{thin}}$), and therefore has significantly less electrical resistance ($R_{\text{thick}} < R_{\text{thin}}$). By Ohm's law ($I = V/R$), for the same applied potential difference, smaller resistance permits a larger current to flow easily with less internal collision.

In-Text Page 198 • Q3
Let the resistance of an electrical component remains constant while the potential difference across the two ends of the component decreases to half of its former value. What change will occur in the current through it?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Calculation:

According to Ohm's law, electric current is directly proportional to potential difference across a constant resistance:

$$I = \frac{V}{R}$$

If the new potential difference is $V' = \frac{V}{2}$ and resistance $R$ remains constant:

$$I' = \frac{V'}{R} = \frac{V/2}{R} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{V}{R}\right) = \frac{I}{2}$$

Conclusion: The current flowing through the component will also decrease to half of its initial value.

In-Text Page 206 • Q3
What are the advantages of connecting electrical devices in parallel with the battery instead of connecting them in series?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Advantages of Parallel Circuit in Domestic Wiring:

  1. Independent Operation: Each appliance gets its own independent on/off switch. If one appliance faults or is turned off, the other appliances continue to operate normally without interruption. In a series circuit, any break stops current in all devices.
  2. Full Mains Voltage across Every Device: In parallel, each appliance receives the full rated line voltage ($220\text{ V}$), allowing devices to operate at full intended power. In series, voltage divides among devices, causing bulbs to glow dimly.
  3. Different Current Requirements Satisfied: Devices with widely different power ratings (e.g., $1000\text{ W}$ heater requiring $4.5\text{ A}$ vs $40\text{ W}$ lamp requiring $0.18\text{ A}$) draw their required current according to their individual resistance.
  4. Low Total Circuit Resistance: The overall equivalent resistance of the domestic wiring is low, preventing excessive overall power dissipation.

In-Text Page 209 • Q1
Why does the cord of an electric heater not glow while the heating element does?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Application of Joule's Heating Law ($H = I^2 R t$):

  1. The connecting cord and the heating element are connected in series; therefore, the exact same electric current ($I$) flows through both.
  2. Connecting Cord: Made of thick copper or aluminium with very low electrical resistivity, hence its resistance ($R_{\text{cord}}$) is negligible. Negligible heat is dissipated ($H \propto R$), so it remains cool and does not glow.
  3. Heating Element: Made of high-resistance alloy (Nichrome) with high resistivity. Because its resistance ($R_{\text{element}}$) is very large, an enormous quantity of Joule heat is developed, causing the element to heat up to glowing incandescence (red-hot, $>800^\circ\text{C}$).

  1. Let $n$ resistors of resistance $R = 176\ \Omega$ be connected in parallel.
  2. The equivalent resistance of $n$ identical parallel resistors is $R_p = \frac{R}{n} = \frac{176}{n}$.
  3. By Ohm's law for $V = 220\text{ V}$ and $I = 5\text{ A}$: $$R_p = \frac{V}{I} = \frac{220\text{ V}}{5\text{ A}} = 44\ \Omega$$
  4. Equating both expressions: $$\frac{176}{n} = 44 \implies n = \frac{176}{44} = \mathbf{4}$$

Therefore, exactly 4 resistors must be connected in parallel.

NCERT Exercise • Q16
An electric heater of resistance 8 ohm draws 15 A from the service mains 2 hours. Calculate the rate at which heat is developed in the heater.
[2 Marks]

The rate at which heat is developed is $1800\text{ Joules per second}$ ($1800\text{ W}$).

NCERT Exercise • Q17
Explain the following: (a) Why is tungsten used almost exclusively for filament of electric lamps? (b) Why are conductors of electric heating devices made of an alloy rather than a pure metal?
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Reasons:

  1. (a) Tungsten for Lamp Filaments: Tungsten has an exceptionally high melting point ($3380^\circ\text{C}$) and very high electrical resistivity. It can be heated to high incandescence where it emits bright white light without melting, evaporating, or burning in the presence of inert argon/nitrogen filling gas.
  2. (b) Alloys for Heating Devices: Alloys (e.g., Nichrome) have much higher resistivity than their constituent pure metals and do not oxidise (burn) at red-hot temperatures ($>800^\circ\text{C}$), generating intense Joule heat.

NCERT Exercise • Q18
Explain the following: (a) Why is the series arrangement not used for domestic circuits? (b) How does the resistance of a wire vary with its area of cross-section? (c) Why are copper and aluminium wires usually employed for electricity transmission?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Explanations:

  1. (a) Series Arrangement in Domestic Circuits: In series, if any one appliance burns out or is switched off, the entire circuit is broken and all other appliances turn off. Furthermore, the voltage divides among appliances ($V = V_1 + V_2 + \dots$), so each device receives inadequate voltage and cannot operate at its rated power.
  2. (b) Resistance vs Area of Cross-section: Resistance of a wire is inversely proportional to its cross-sectional area: $$R \propto \frac{1}{A}$$ Doubling the cross-sectional area halves the resistance, while halving the area doubles resistance.
  3. (c) Copper and Aluminium for Transmission: They possess extremely low electrical resistivity, which minimizes $I^2 R$ transmission line power losses over long distances. They are also ductile, lightweight, and cost-effective.

  3. Total Monthly Bill: $$\text{Cost} = 157.2 \times ₹\ 6.50 = \mathbf{₹\ 1021.80}$$
