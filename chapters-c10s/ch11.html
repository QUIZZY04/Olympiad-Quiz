Question 1
State Ohm's Law. Write its mathematical formula and define the SI unit of resistance (1 Ohm).
[2 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Ohm's Law:

Statement: The electric current ($I$) flowing through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference ($V$) applied across its ends, provided its temperature and other physical conditions remain constant.

Mathematical Formulation:

$$V \propto I \implies V = IR \implies R = \frac{V}{I}$$
  • $V$ = Potential difference across conductor (in Volts, $\text{V}$)
  • $I$ = Current flowing through conductor (in Amperes, $\text{A}$)
  • $R$ = Resistance of conductor (in Ohms, $\Omega$)

Definition of 1 Ohm ($1\ \Omega$):

If a potential difference of $1\text{ Volt}$ across the ends of a conductor causes a current of $1\text{ Ampere}$ to flow through it, then the resistance of the conductor is said to be $1\text{ Ohm}$ ($1\ \Omega = 1\text{ V} / 1\text{ A}$).

Question 2
On what factors does the resistance of a conductor depend? Write the formula relating resistance and resistivity, and state the SI unit of resistivity.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Factors on which the Resistance of a Conductor Depends:

The electrical resistance ($R$) of a uniform cylindrical conductor depends on four factors:

  1. Length of the Conductor ($l$): Resistance is directly proportional to its length: $$R \propto l$$ Doubling the wire's length doubles its resistance.
  2. Area of Cross-section ($A$): Resistance is inversely proportional to its cross-sectional area: $$R \propto \frac{1}{A}$$ A thick wire offers much less resistance than a thin wire of the same material.
  3. Nature of the Material ($\rho$): Characterized by the specific resistivity ($\rho$) of the material: $$R = \rho \frac{l}{A}$$
  4. Temperature: For metallic conductors, electrical resistance increases with increase in temperature.

Electrical Resistivity ($\rho$):

$$\rho = \frac{R \cdot A}{l}$$

SI unit of resistivity is $\mathbf{\Omega \cdot \text{m}}$ (Ohm-metre). It is an intrinsic characteristic property of the material that does not depend on length or thickness.

Question 3
What are the advantages of connecting electrical devices in parallel with the battery rather than in series in domestic circuits?
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Why Domestic Electrical Appliances are Connected in Parallel, NOT in Series:

  1. 1. Independent Operation (No Global Failure):
    In a series circuit, if one appliance burns out, gets damaged, or is turned off, the single continuous circuit is broken, and all other appliances in the house turn off immediately. In a parallel circuit, each branch is independent; if one bulb fuses, other rooms remain fully illuminated.
  2. 2. Independent Switches:
    In series, all appliances share a single switch. In parallel circuits, every individual device (fan, light, AC, TV) can have its own independent switch.
  3. 3. Constant Voltage for Each Appliance:
    In parallel, each appliance receives the full domestic supply voltage ($220\text{ V}$ in India) across its terminals, operating at its full rated efficiency. In series, the $220\text{ V}$ divides across all devices, causing lights to dim and appliances to underperform.
  4. 4. Different Current Requirements:
    Different electrical appliances have vastly different power ratings and current requirements (e.g. an electric heater draws $10\text{ A}$, while an LED bulb requires only $0.05\text{ A}$). In series, current is identical through all components ($I = \text{constant}$), which is completely incompatible. Parallel circuits provide separate current paths matching each appliance's impedance.

Question 4
Three resistors of $5\ \Omega, 10\ \Omega$ and $30\ \Omega$ are connected (i) in series, (ii) in parallel to a 12 V battery. Find the equivalent resistance and total current drawn in each case.
[3 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Step-by-step Circuit Numerical Solution:

1. Resistors in Series:

When resistors $R_1 = 5\ \Omega, R_2 = 10\ \Omega, R_3 = 30\ \Omega$ are connected in series:

$$R_s = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 = 5 + 10 + 30 = \mathbf{45\ \Omega}$$

The equivalent resistance is maximum in series.

2. Resistors in Parallel:

When connected in parallel across a $12\text{ V}$ battery:

$$\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3} = \frac{1}{5} + \frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{30}$$

LCM of 5, 10, 30 is 30:

$$\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{6 + 3 + 1}{30} = \frac{10}{30} = \frac{1}{3} \implies R_p = \mathbf{3\ \Omega}$$
Figure: Parallel Electrical Circuit with Three Resistors and a 12 V Battery Source

3. Total Current from 12 V Battery in Parallel:

$$I_{\text{total}} = \frac{V}{R_p} = \frac{12\text{ V}}{3\ \Omega} = \mathbf{4\text{ A}}$$

4. Branch Currents:

  • $I_1 = \frac{V}{R_1} = \frac{12}{5} = 2.4\text{ A}$
  • $I_2 = \frac{V}{R_2} = \frac{12}{10} = 1.2\text{ A}$
  • $I_3 = \frac{V}{R_3} = \frac{12}{30} = 0.4\text{ A}$
  • Check: $I_1 + I_2 + I_3 = 2.4 + 1.2 + 0.4 = 4.0\text{ A}$. (Verified)

Question 5
State Joule's Law of Heating and describe three practical applications. An electric refrigerator rated 400 W operates 8 hours/day and an iron of 1000 W operates 2 hours/day. Calculate the total electricity bill for 30 days at Rs 5.00 per unit (kWh).
[5 Marks]
✅ CBSE Board Standard Step-by-Step Solution

Joule's Law of Heating and Electric Power Calculations:

1. Joule's Law of Heating:

When an electric current $I$ flows through a resistor $R$ for time $t$, the electrical energy consumed is dissipated entirely as heat energy ($H$).

$$\mathbf{H = I^2 R t}$$

Three Factors: Heat produced in a resistor is:

  1. Directly proportional to the square of current ($H \propto I^2$).
  2. Directly proportional to the resistance for a given current ($H \propto R$).
  3. Directly proportional to the time for which current flows ($H \propto t$).

2. Practical Applications:

  • Heating Appliances: Electric irons, toasters, and geysers use heating elements made of Nichrome (alloy of Ni, Cr, Mn, Fe) because nichrome has very high resistivity and does not oxidise (burn) even at red-hot temperatures ($800^\circ\text{C}$).
  • Electric Bulbs: Filaments are made of Tungsten due to its extraordinarily high melting point ($3422^\circ\text{C}$).
  • Electric Fuse: Made of a lead-tin alloy with low melting point to melt and break circuit during overcurrent.

3. Electrical Energy Commercial Unit Problem:

An electric refrigerator rated $400\text{ W}$ operates 8 hours/day. An electric iron rated $1000\text{ W}$ operates 2 hours/day. What is the cost of energy to operate them for 30 days at Rs 5.00 per kWh?

  1. Energy consumed by refrigerator in 30 days: $$E_1 = P_1 \times t_1 \times 30 = 400\text{ W} \times 8\text{ h} \times 30 = 96,000\text{ Wh} = 96\text{ kWh}$$
  2. Energy consumed by electric iron in 30 days: $$E_2 = P_2 \times t_2 \times 30 = 1000\text{ W} \times 2\text{ h} \times 30 = 60,000\text{ Wh} = 60\text{ kWh}$$
  3. Total energy consumed $= 96 + 60 = 156\text{ kWh (units)}$.
  4. Total cost $= 156\text{ units} \times \text{Rs } 5.00/\text{unit} = \mathbf{\text{Rs } 780.00}$.

Case Study Question • Short Circuit & Earthing Protection
A resident plugged a high-power immersion heater into a domestic socket. Suddenly a spark occurred, the fuse wire melted, and the house lights went off. (i) Differentiate between short-circuiting and overloading. (ii) What is the function of an earth wire in metallic appliances? (iii) Why is fuse always connected in the live wire?

Step-by-step Solution:

  1. (i) Short-circuiting vs Overloading:
    • Short-circuiting: Occurs when the live wire and neutral wire come in direct physical contact due to faulty insulation. The resistance of the circuit drops near zero, and an enormous, dangerous surge of current flows instantly.
    • Overloading: Occurs when too many electrical appliances of high power ratings are switched on simultaneously across a single socket, drawing current beyond the safe current-carrying capacity of domestic wires.
  2. (ii) Function of Earth Wire: The green/yellow earth wire is connected to a metal plate buried deep in the ground. It connects to the metallic casing of heavy appliances (refrigerator, toaster, iron). If an insulation fault makes the metal body live, the current safely drains into the earth through the low-resistance path, tripping the fuse and protecting the user from a lethal electric shock.
  3. (iii) Fuse in Live Wire: If the fuse is placed in the neutral wire and blows, the appliance remains connected to the high-voltage live wire ($220\text{ V}$), posing a fatal shock risk if touched. Placing it in the live wire completely isolates the appliance from the electrical mains upon blowing.
