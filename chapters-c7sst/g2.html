🌍 Our Environment (Chapter 2) — Concentric interior layers, rocks, the rock cycle, and valuable mineral resources

Chapter Summary & Key Historical/Geographical Concepts
The Earth is a dynamic planet composed of several concentric layers, much like an onion. The uppermost layer is the Crust, the thinnest of all layers (averaging 35 km on continental masses and just 5 km on oceanic floors). The continental crust consists primarily of silica and alumina ('sial'), while the oceanic crust consists of silica and magnesium ('sima'). Below the crust lies the Mantle, extending up to a depth of 2,900 km, composed of dense, semi-molten magma. The innermost layer is the Core, with a radius of approximately 3,500 km, composed of heavy metals nickel and iron ('nife') under colossal temperature and pressure. The Earth's crust is made of rocks—natural masses of mineral matter. Rocks are classified into three major types: 1) Igneous rocks (primary rocks formed by cooling of molten magma, either intrusive like granite or extrusive like basalt of the Deccan Traps); 2) Sedimentary rocks (formed by deposition, compression, and hardening of rock fragments called sediments, often encasing plant and animal fossils, e.g., sandstone); 3) Metamorphic rocks (igneous and sedimentary rocks transformed under intense heat and pressure without melting, e.g., clay changing into slate and limestone into marble). Through the geological Rock Cycle, rocks continuously transform from one type to another over millions of years.
🎯 Central Theme: The dynamic internal composition of the Earth and the continuous metamorphic recycling of lithospheric materials.

Exercises — Q1 (i to vi)
Answer the following questions:
(i) What are the three layers of the earth?
(ii) What is a rock?
(iii) Name three types of rocks.
(iv) How are extrusive and intrusive rocks formed?
(v) What do you mean by a rock cycle?
(vi) What are the uses of rocks?
(vii) What are metamorphic rocks?
7 Marks
Detailed Solution
(i) Three Layers of the Earth: The Crust (outermost thin shell), the Mantle (middle layer up to 2,900 km), and the Core (innermost metallic layer).

(ii) Definition of a Rock: Any natural mass of mineral matter that makes up the Earth's crust is called a rock. Rocks can be of different color, size, and texture.

(iii) Three Types of Rocks: Igneous rocks, Sedimentary rocks, and Metamorphic rocks.

(iv) Formation of Extrusive and Intrusive Rocks:
  • Extrusive Igneous Rocks: Formed when molten lava erupts onto the Earth's surface and cools down rapidly. It develops a fine-grained crystalline structure (e.g., Basalt, which forms the Deccan Plateau).
  • Intrusive Igneous Rocks: Formed when molten magma cools down slowly deep inside the Earth's crust. Because it cools slowly, it forms large crystals and grains (e.g., Granite).
(v) Rock Cycle: The continuous cyclical transformation of one type of rock into another under changing geological conditions of melting, cooling, weathering, sedimentation, heat, and pressure over millions of years.

(vi) Uses of Rocks:
  • Construction of roads, houses, bridges, and monuments (e.g., Red Fort made of red sandstone, Taj Mahal made of white marble).
  • Grinding stones (granite) for spices and grains.
  • Source of valuable minerals and fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas, iron, aluminum, gold).
(vii) Metamorphic Rocks: Rocks that are formed when existing igneous or sedimentary rocks undergo profound physical and chemical transformations under the influence of extreme heat and pressure inside the Earth (e.g., limestone transforms into marble; clay into slate).

Exercises — Q4 (Give Reasons)
Give reasons:
(i) We cannot go to the centre of the earth.
(ii) Sedimentary rocks are formed from sediments.
(iii) Limestone is changed into marble.
4.5 Marks
Detailed Solution
(i) We cannot go to the centre of the earth because:
The center of the Earth lies approximately 6,000 km below the ocean floor. At this extreme depth, temperature and pressure are unimaginably high (exceeding 5,000°C), where solid rocks turn molten and no human being or drilling machine can survive or operate.

(ii) Sedimentary rocks are formed from sediments because:
Rocks roll down, crack, hit one another, and are broken down into small fragments called sediments. These loose sediments are transported and deposited by wind, rivers, and glaciers into riverbeds and sea basins. Over millions of years, layers of sediments accumulate, are compressed under heavy overburden weight, and are cemented together by minerals into solid layered rocks.

(iii) Limestone is changed into marble because:
When sedimentary limestone is subjected to tremendous heat and crushing pressure deep within the Earth's crust (due to tectonic movements or volcanic magma intrusions), its calcite crystals recrystallize without melting, transforming into metamorphic marble.

Competency-Based Case Study: The Rock Cycle in Earth Dynamics
Trace how a single grain of quartz can journey through all three rock families across millions of years via the Rock Cycle.

The Rock Cycle demonstrates the conservation and continuous recycling of matter in Earth's lithosphere:

  1. Igneous Phase: Magma cools slowly underground, crystallizing into an igneous rock such as Granite, which contains quartz crystals.
  2. Sedimentary Phase: Uplift and weathering expose the granite to wind, rain, and frost. The rock breaks down, and river currents transport the durable quartz sand grain to an ocean basin. Compressed under thousands of sedimentary layers, it turns into sedimentary Sandstone.
  3. Metamorphic Phase: Tectonic plate collision buries the sandstone deep under mountains where intense heat and pressure recrystallize the quartz into metamorphic Quartzite.
  4. Return to Magma: Subduction eventually drags the quartzite down into the mantle where it melts back into magma, completing the infinite cycle.
