Human-Environment Interactions – The Tropical and the Subtropical Region

🌍 Our Environment (Chapter 6) — Life in the Amazon Basin (Equatorial) and the Ganga-Brahmaputra Basin (Subtropical)

Chapter Summary & Key Historical/Geographical Concepts
Human lives are shaped by local geographical and climatic realities, exemplified by two distinct river basins: 1) The Amazon Basin (Equatorial Region): Located between 10°N and 10°S in South America, drained by the mighty Amazon River. The climate is hot and wet throughout the year with daily torrential rains. The basin is covered by dense tropical rainforests (selvas) whose thick canopy blocks sunlight from reaching the ground, leaving the forest floor dark and damp. Epiphytes like orchids and bromeliads thrive. Fauna includes toucans, hummingbirds, sloths, tapirs, anacondas, and flesh-eating piranha fish. Indigenous people practice 'slash and burn' agriculture, growing manioc (cassava), tapioca, sweet potatoes, and maize, living in thatched houses called 'malocas'. The construction of the Trans-Amazon Highway (1970) opened the interior, causing catastrophic deforestation; 2) The Ganga-Brahmaputra Basin (Subtropical Region): Located between 10°N and 30°N in the Indian subcontinent. Drained by the Ganga, Brahmaputra, and their tributaries (Yamuna, Ghaghra, Kosi, Teesta). Dominated by a monsoon climate with hot summers and torrential monsoon rains (June to September). The fertile alluvial floodplains support intensive agriculture, with paddy (rice) as the main crop. It has one of the highest population densities in the world. Natural vegetation consists of tropical deciduous forests (teak, sal, bamboo) and mangrove forests (Sundarbans). Fauna includes the Bengal tiger, one-horned rhinoceros, elephants, and the blind river dolphin ('Susu'). Major cities like Varanasi, Patna, and Kolkata thrive on its banks.
🎯 Central Theme: Comparative human geography of equatorial rainforests versus subtropical riverine floodplains.

Intext Question — Page 41
What is 'Susu' (Blind Dolphin), and why is its presence an indicator of water health?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
Susu (also known as the Ganges River Dolphin) is a blind freshwater dolphin found in the Ganga and Brahmaputra river systems. It navigates and hunts prey using echolocation (ultrasonic clicks).
Indicator of health: Susu can survive only in clean, unpolluted freshwater with balanced oxygen levels. Its presence signifies a healthy, living river ecosystem; its disappearance indicates toxic industrial and chemical pollution.

Exercises — Q4 (Give Reasons)
Give reasons:
(i) The rainforests are depleting.
(ii) Paddy is grown in the Ganga-Brahmaputra plains.
4 Marks
Detailed Solution
(i) The rainforests of the Amazon are depleting because:
Large-scale developmental activities, modern highway construction (e.g., Trans-Amazon Highway), cattle ranching, commercial logging for timber, and expanding mining operations are clearing vast swathes of rainforest every year. As the topsoil is washed away by torrential rains, the lush rainforest transforms into barren wasteland.

(ii) Paddy is grown in the Ganga-Brahmaputra plains because:
Paddy (rice) requires deep, fertile alluvial soil and an abundance of stagnant water. The flat terrain of the Ganga-Brahmaputra basin provides deep, silt-rich alluvial soil deposited by seasonal river floods, alongside high annual monsoon rainfall (over 100 cm) and perennial canal irrigation systems necessary for water-intensive rice cultivation.

Competency-Based Comparative Human Geography
Contrast the population density of the Amazon Basin with the Ganga-Brahmaputra Basin. Why does geography dictate such divergent demographic patterns?

Geographical factors fundamentally dictate settlement capacity and carrying capacity:

  • Amazon Basin (Extremely Low Density): Oppressive equatorial heat, relentless humidity, leaching of soil nutrients by daily downpours, impassable thick jungle canopy, and disease-carrying insects prevent large-scale urban infrastructure. Settlements remain sparse and tribal.
  • Ganga-Brahmaputra Basin (Extremely High Density): Mild subtropical climate, flat topography facilitating road and railway construction, and perennial rivers depositing nutrient-rich alluvium enable intensive double-cropping of food grains. This exceptional agricultural productivity supports colossal urban populations and mega-cities (over 1,000 persons per sq. km).
