How the State Government Works

⚖️ Social and Political Life – II (Chapter 3) — MLAs, constituencies, ruling party vs opposition, the Legislative Assembly debate, and public accountability

Chapter Summary & Key Historical/Geographical Concepts
In India, federal governance operates at three tiers: national (Union), state, and local. Every state in India has a Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha). For electoral purposes, each state is demarcated into distinct geographic territories called Constituencies (e.g., Himachal Pradesh has 68 constituencies, Uttar Pradesh has 403). From each constituency, the adult electorate votes to elect one representative known as a Member of Legislative Assembly (MLA). The political party whose elected MLAs win more than half the total number of constituencies in a state secures a simple majority and becomes the Ruling Party, while all other elected MLAs constitute the Opposition. The Governor (the constitutional head of state appointed by the President of India) formally invites the leader of the majority party to form the government as the Chief Minister. The Chief Minister then selects other MLAs to head various executive ministries (Health, Education, Agriculture, Public Works). The Legislative Assembly serves as the supreme democratic forum where MLAs from both ruling and opposition parties debate urgent state crises (e.g., water scarcity, acute hospital shortages during an epidemic), question ministers, demand policy accountability, and approve state budgets. Executive power resides with government departments headed by civil servants (Secretaries), overseen by elected Ministers. The government is also held accountable through press conferences, investigative journalists, and public demonstrations.

Intext Question — Page 33
What is the difference between an MLA and a Minister?
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
  • MLA (Member of Legislative Assembly): An elected representative chosen by the voters of a specific constituency to sit in the state legislature, raise constituency issues, and participate in making laws. Every minister is an MLA, but not every MLA is a minister.
  • Minister: An MLA from the ruling party (or ruling coalition) who is specifically appointed by the Governor on the Chief Minister's recommendation to head a specific executive government department (such as Health, Education, Finance) and administer executive state policies.

Exercises — Q1
Use the terms 'constituency' and 'represent' to explain who an MLA is and how is the person elected?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
An MLA (Member of Legislative Assembly) is an elected representative of the citizens in the state legislature:
  1. Demarcation of Constituencies: For legislative elections, the entire state is divided into specific geographical areas called constituencies based on population.
  2. Election Process: Every adult citizen (aged 18 and above) residing in that constituency has the right to cast one vote. Candidates from different political parties (as well as independents) contest the election.
  3. Representation: The candidate who secures the highest number of votes in that constituency wins the election and becomes the MLA to represent the voices, grievances, and developmental needs of the people of that constituency in the State Legislative Assembly.

Exercises — Q2
How did some MLAs become Ministers? Explain.
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
MLAs become Ministers through the following sequential constitutional process:
  1. Securing Legislative Majority: In assembly elections, the political party whose candidates win more than half the total number of constituencies achieves a majority and becomes the ruling party.
  2. Election of Chief Minister: The elected MLAs of the majority party meet and formally elect their leader, whom the Governor invites and swears in as the Chief Minister.
  3. Appointment of Council of Ministers: The Chief Minister selects a team of capable MLAs from the ruling party to head various government departments (Finance, Home, Education, Health, Transport). The Governor officially administers the oath of office to them, appointing them as Cabinet Ministers.

Exercises — Q3
Why should decisions taken by the Chief Minister and other ministers be debated in the Legislative Assembly?
3 Marks
Detailed Solution
Decisions taken by the Chief Minister and the Cabinet must be debated in the Legislative Assembly because:
  • Democratic Accountability: In a representative democracy, the executive is answerable to the people through the legislature. Debating decisions ensures policies are scrutinized for public welfare rather than private or partisan benefit.
  • Voice of the Opposition: Opposition MLAs represent millions of citizens who did not vote for the ruling party. Through debate, they highlight regional grievances, point out financial irregularities, and expose neglected issues (such as hospital negligence or rural water crises).
  • Approval of Public Funds: Ministers cannot spend public taxpayer money arbitrarily; the state budget and developmental expenditures require official scrutiny and legislative sanction from the Assembly.

Exercises — Q4
What was the problem in Patalpuram? What discussion/action was taken by the following: (a) Public meeting (b) Legislative Assembly (c) Press conference (d) Chief Minister?
4 Marks
Detailed Solution
The Problem in Patalpuram: Patalpuram district faced acute drinking water shortages, contaminated water supply, and widespread outbreaks of diarrhea resulting in the deaths of over 15 people, while the local district hospital was overwhelmed with patients.

Actions Taken by Various Forums:
  • (a) Public Meeting / Protests: Angry citizens, women with empty pitchers, and political activists organized a protest rally, gheraoed the local MLA's residence, and demanded immediate government action to sanitize water supplies and bring health crises under control.
  • (b) Legislative Assembly: Opposition MLAs passionately attacked the Health Minister for negligence, while ruling party MLAs defended their administration; the Assembly fiercely debated water tanker deployment, doctor shortages, and sanitation funding.
  • (c) Press Conference: The Health Minister held a press briefing with journalists, detailing emergency measures (water tankers, oral rehydration packets, mobile medical teams), and answered investigative questions from reporters.
  • (d) Chief Minister: Visited the grieving families in Patalpuram, announced monetary compensation for victims' families, and ordered the Public Works Department (PWD) and Health Department to supply clean water and repair healthcare sanitation infrastructure immediately.

Competency-Based Legislative Analysis: Separation of Powers
In state governance, how does the dual role of the Chief Minister and Cabinet Ministers (who belong to both the Legislative wing and the Executive wing) demonstrate the parliamentary system of checks and balances?

In India's parliamentary democracy, the Executive is drawn directly from the Legislature, creating institutional synergy balanced by checks:

  • Executive Action: As heads of executive departments, ministers formulate policies, supervise civil servants, deploy budgets, and enforce laws.
  • Legislative Accountability: Because ministers remain sitting MLAs, they must sit on the floor of the Legislative Assembly and face daily questioning, adjournment motions, and budgetary debates from opposition MLAs.
  • Check on Absolute Power: If the Chief Minister or ministers lose the confidence of the majority of MLAs in the Assembly, a 'No-Confidence Motion' can be passed, immediately bringing down the entire state government. This ensures the executive never becomes despotic.
