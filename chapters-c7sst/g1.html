Environment

🌍 Our Environment (Chapter 1) — Components of environment, biotic and abiotic realms, domains of the Earth, and ecosystems

Chapter Summary & Key Historical/Geographical Concepts
Environment is our basic life support system, providing the air we breathe, water we drink, food we eat, and land where we live. It consists of three broad components: 1) Natural components (land, air, water, living things); 2) Human-made components (buildings, parks, bridges, roads, industries); 3) Human components (individuals, families, communities, religion, education, economy). Natural environment comprises biotic factors (living organisms like plants and animals) and abiotic factors (non-living elements like land and minerals). The Earth has four interconnected domains: the Lithosphere (the solid crust or top layer of rock and soil), the Hydrosphere (all water bodies including oceans, rivers, lakes), the Atmosphere (the thin blanket of air held by Earth's gravity, protecting us from harmful solar radiation), and the Biosphere (the narrow zone where land, water, and air interact to support life). An ecosystem is a functional community formed by the interaction of all living organisms with each other and with their physical and chemical surroundings, linked by transfers of energy and matter. Humans modify the environment through technological interventions (industrialization, transport, agriculture), necessitating a sustainable, harmonious balance between natural ecosystems and human development.
🎯 Central Theme: The holistic relationship between natural domains (lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere, biosphere) and human activities.
📖 Intext Questions & Activities
Intext Question — Page 3
What is an ecosystem? Give two examples of natural ecosystems.
2 Marks
Detailed Solution
An ecosystem is a dynamic, self-sustaining community formed by the complex interaction of all living organisms (plants, animals, microorganisms) with each other and with the physical and chemical factors of their surrounding non-living environment (soil, water, sunlight, air), linked through nutrient cycles and energy flow.
Examples: A tropical rainforest, a freshwater pond, a desert, or a coral reef.

Exercises — Q1 (i to vii)
Answer the following questions:
(i) What is an ecosystem?
(ii) What do you mean by natural environment?
(iii) Which are the major components of the environment?
(iv) Give four examples of human-made environment.
(v) What is lithosphere?
(vi) Which are the two major components of biotic environment?
(vii) What is biosphere?
7 Marks
Detailed Solution
(i) Ecosystem: A biological system formed by the interaction of all living organisms with each other and with the physical and chemical components of their environment, united by transfers of energy and material.

(ii) Natural Environment: The natural environment encompasses all living and non-living things that occur naturally on Earth without human creation, comprising land (lithosphere), water (hydrosphere), air (atmosphere), and life (biosphere).

(iii) Major Components of Environment:
  • Natural Components: Air, water, land, and living organisms.
  • Human-Made Components: Buildings, bridges, roads, monuments, factories.
  • Human Components: Individuals, families, communities, religious, educational, and political institutions.
(iv) Four Examples of Human-Made Environment: Buildings, bridges, roads, and industrial parks / dams.

(v) Lithosphere: The solid crust or the hard top layer of the Earth composed of rocks and minerals, covered by a thin layer of soil. It is an irregular surface with various landforms such as mountains, plateaus, plains, and valleys.

(vi) Two Major Components of Biotic Environment: Plants (flora) and Animals (fauna).

(vii) Biosphere: The narrow contact zone of the Earth where land (lithosphere), water (hydrosphere), and air (atmosphere) interact with each other to support life.

Exercises — Q4 (Give Reasons)
Give reasons:
(i) Man modifies his environment.
(ii) Plants and animals depend on each other.
4 Marks
Detailed Solution
(i) Man modifies his environment because:
  • Humans possess intellectual creativity and evolving needs to secure food, shelter, comfort, and economic prosperity.
  • To meet these expanding demands, humans clear forests for agriculture and settlements, dam rivers for hydroelectricity, extract minerals from the lithosphere, and construct transportation networks, modifying natural landscapes to suit modern civilizational requirements.
(ii) Plants and animals depend on each other because:
  • Gaseous Exchange: Plants produce oxygen during photosynthesis, which animals require for respiration; in return, animals exhale carbon dioxide, which plants need for photosynthesis.
  • Food & Nutrition: Herbivores and omnivores feed directly on plants for energy, while carnivores depend on herbivores.
  • Seed Dispersal & Pollination: Animals, insects, and birds pollinate flowers and disperse seeds, while animal excreta and decaying animal bodies provide organic humus and fertilizers to nourish plant roots.

💡 Competency-Based Case Study & HOTS (CBSE 2026-27 Pattern) Applied Social Science
Competency-Based Environmental Case Study
Analyze the environmental adage: 'Nature provides enough to satisfy every man's needs, but not every man's greed.' How does unbridled human modification of the abiotic environment trigger ecological crises?

This principle underscores the vital boundary between sustainable utilization and ecological collapse:

  • Sustainable Needs: Early humans lived in ecological balance, foraging and cultivating without degrading the regenerative capacity of forests and water cycles.
  • Greed & Abiotic Exploitation: Industrialized greed strips the lithosphere through open-cast mining, saturates the atmosphere with greenhouse gases (causing global warming), and dumps toxic industrial waste into the hydrosphere.
  • Consequences: Modifying the abiotic foundation destroys the delicate biosphere—triggering species extinction, rising sea levels, and climate anomalies that ultimately threaten human survival itself.
