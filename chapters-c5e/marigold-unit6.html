Chapter 6: Class Discussion & The Talkative Barber

Observational school poem by Gervase Phinn & Tale of chatter from Arabian Nights | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Poem 'Class Discussion': During a lively classroom debate where everyone loudly airs opinions, Jane sits silently staring around; when asked by teacher Ms. Jane explains that the world is filled with naturally quiet people.
  • Prose 'The Talkative Barber': The Sultan of Cashgar summons a barber to shave his head; the barber begins an endless monologue boasting of his six disabled brothers instead of doing his job.
  • Comedic Climax: The desperate Sultan promises all the day's royal feast food to the barber just to get his head shaved; the barber inspects each dish leisurely while the Sultan waits half-shaved in agony.
  • Core Values: Respecting introverted personalities, active listening, conversational restraint, and professional focus.
  • Grammar: Prefixes of negation (im-, dis-, un-), adverbs ending in -ly, descriptive character dialogue.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Reading is Fun • Poem (Class Discussion)
What activity was going on in the classroom? Who was the quiet student, and how did she respond when the teacher questioned her silence?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
A lively class discussion was underway where students and the teacher were actively voicing their opinions. Jane was the quiet student who sat serenely without uttering a word. When the teacher gently asked if anything was wrong, Jane looked up and replied with quiet poise: 'There are many people in this world who are rather quiet you know!'
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Class discussion / debate taking place 1 Mark
Jane identified as the calm, quiet student 1 Mark
Jane's response that many people in the world are quiet 1 Mark
Q2 Reading is Fun • Story (The Talkative Barber)
Why was the Sultan of Cashgar in such an urgent hurry, and why did the Barber take so dreadfully long to shave him?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The Sultan was in a great hurry because he had invited a group of esteemed friends to a grand midday feast at his palace. The Barber took forever because instead of shaving the royal head, he constantly put down his razor to deliver long, unsolicited speeches about astrology, his six talkative brothers, and his self-proclaimed brilliance.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Sultan's urgency: hosting friends for midday feast 1.5 Marks
Barber's delay: putting down razor to gossip endlessly 1.5 Marks
Q3 Reading is Fun • Story (The Talkative Barber)
How many brothers did the Barber have, and what peculiar physical disability did each brother possess?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The Barber had six brothers, each with a unique physical handicap:
  1. The first was humpbacked (Bacbouc).
  2. The second was toothless (El Haddar).
  3. The third was half-blind (Bequir).
  4. The fourth was totally blind (El Kouz).
  5. The fifth had his ears cropped (Alnaschar).
  6. The sixth had a cleft palate (Schacabac).
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Total 6 brothers identified 1 Mark
Correct physical traits described 2 Marks
Q4 Reading is Fun • Story (The Talkative Barber)
Why did the Sultan finally agree to give all the prepared palace food to the Barber?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The Sultan was trapped in extreme distress with only half of his head shaved while the Barber lingered on. To get rid of the annoying chatterbox and secure the completion of his shave before his luncheon guests arrived, the desperate Sultan surrendered all the lavish feast dishes.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Distress of remaining half-shaved 1 Mark
Surrendering food to get the shave finished quickly 1 Mark
Q5 Let us Learn Grammar • Opposites with Prefixes
Form the opposites of the following words by adding appropriate prefixes (un-, im-, dis-):
(a) patient, (b) obedient, (c) believe, (d) polite, (e) perfect, (f) pure.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
  • (a) patient → impatient
  • (b) obedient → disobedient
  • (c) believe → disbelieve
  • (d) polite → impolite
  • (e) perfect → imperfect
  • (f) pure → impure
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct prefixes for (a), (b), (c) 1.5 Marks
Correct prefixes for (d), (e), (f) 1.5 Marks
Q6 Reading is Fun • Thematic Inquiry & Character Study
In 'Class Discussion & The Talkative Barber', analyze how the central characters face and resolve their dilemma. What core moral value or life perspective does this convey to young readers about empathy, teamwork, or patience?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Thematic Analysis & Character Values:
1. Core Dilemma: In Class Discussion & The Talkative Barber, the characters encounter unexpected difficulties that challenge their confidence and understanding.
2. Resolution Strategy: Instead of acting impulsively or giving up, they listen carefully to each other, remain calm, and apply thoughtful cooperation.
3. Moral Insight: The story highlights that genuine empathy, perseverance, and honesty turn challenging situations into enriching growth opportunities.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of central dilemma and resolution 1.5 M
Expression of core moral value and life lesson 1.5 M
Q7 Vocabulary & Word Play • Idioms, Antonyms & Synonyms
Enhance your vocabulary through language skills based on 'Class Discussion & The Talkative Barber':
(a) Provide one synonym and one antonym for each word: courageous, generous, ancient.
(b) Choose one idiom connected to the theme of 'Class Discussion & The Talkative Barber', define it, and frame a meaningful sentence.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Vocabulary Analysis:
• (a) Synonyms & Antonyms:
- Courageous: Synonym: brave | Antonym: timid.
- Generous: Synonym: kind-hearted | Antonym: selfish.
- Ancient: Synonym: antique / historic | Antonym: modern.
• (b) Idiom: 'Turn over a new leaf' (to start behaving in a better and more responsible way).
Sentence: Inspired by the lessons of Class Discussion & The Talkative Barber, Amit decided to turn over a new leaf and complete his homework promptly every day.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate synonyms and antonyms for all 3 words 1.5 M
Correct idiom definition and well-structured context sentence 1.5 M
Q8 Applied Grammar • Tenses, Prepositions & Conjunctions
Apply grammar rules to complete the following exercises:
(a) Fill in the blanks with suitable prepositions (in, across, through, beneath): The brave traveller walked _______ the dense forest and found shelter _______ a giant banyan tree.
(b) Combine the sentences using an appropriate conjunction (although, because, so): The weather was rainy. We completed our football tournament.
(c) Convert into Past Continuous Tense: She reads an adventure tale.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Grammar Solutions:
• (a) The brave traveller walked through the dense forest and found shelter beneath a giant banyan tree.
• (b) Although the weather was rainy, we completed our football tournament.
• (c) Past Continuous: She was reading an adventure tale.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate prepositions: 'through' and 'beneath' 1.0 M
Appropriate subordinating conjunction 'Although' 1.0 M
Correct Past Continuous verb form 'was reading' 1.0 M
Q9 Talk Time • Experiential Sharing & Discussion
Imagine you experienced an unexpected challenge similar to the one depicted in 'Class Discussion & The Talkative Barber'. Describe how you felt, what decision you took, and what you learned from the experience.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Experiential Reflection:
• Context: While working on an inter-school science project, our model malfunctioned on the final morning before evaluation.
• Action: Remembering the teamwork and composure demonstrated in Class Discussion & The Talkative Barber, my partners and I divided the troubleshooting tasks calmly. We identified the loose wiring and repaired the connections together.
• Takeaway: Composure, active listening, and collective trust achieve far better results than panic during stressful moments.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Personal reflection grounded in chapter values 1.5 M
Clarity of learning takeaway and emotional maturity 1.5 M
Q10 Sentence Mechanics • Punctuation & Direct Speech
Punctuate and rewrite the following conversational passage correctly:
neha whispered to her teacher madam may i borrow the illustrated atlas from the school library i need it for my geography project
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Corrected Dialogue with Punctuation:
Neha whispered to her teacher, "Madam, may I borrow the illustrated atlas from the school library? I need it for my geography project."

Explanation: Added proper capitalization at the beginning of speech and proper nouns, comma before opening quotation marks, question mark at the end of the interrogative sentence, and closing quotation marks outside the final full stop.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate opening quotation marks, capitalization, and comma 1.5 M
Correct question mark and closing quotation mark placement 1.5 M
Q11 Creative Writing • Diary Entry & Short Composition
Write a short diary entry (50–60 words) reflecting on the most inspiring thought presented in 'Class Discussion & The Talkative Barber'. Express how you will practice this value in your school or neighborhood.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Sample Diary Entry:

Dear Diary,
Today our teacher discussed 'Class Discussion & The Talkative Barber'. It made me realize how powerful small gestures of kindness and patience are. Starting tomorrow, I have decided to share my extra stationery with classmates in need and spend 15 minutes helping my younger sibling with math reading. When we uplift others, our own world becomes brighter.
— Aarav

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Inclusivity in Classrooms: Valuing Introverts & Active Listeners

In many schools, oral participation marks are awarded solely to children who speak loudly and often, causing naturally quiet, contemplative students like Jane to feel undervalued or pressured.

Analytical Task: Drawing on Gervase Phinn's 'Class Discussion', justify why quiet observation is an asset in group discussions, and propose two classroom evaluation methods that reward thoughtful listening.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
The Power of the Quiet Observer in Modern Classrooms:
  1. Why Silence is Not Lack of Knowledge: Quiet students often process complex ideas with deeper analytical reflection, avoiding impulsive or redundant chatter. A productive society requires deep thinkers and attentive listeners just as much as vocal orators.
  2. Method 1 — Written Reflection Journals: Allow students the option to submit a brief 3-sentence written synthesis of the class debate at the end of the lesson, rewarding insight over mere volume.
  3. Method 2 — Socratic Peer-Listening Badges: Award marks when a student accurately summarizes what another classmate just stated before adding a new point, cultivating genuine listening.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for defending contemplative introversion + 3.5 Marks for 2 concrete evaluation rubrics (written reflection, active listening badges) = 5 Marks
