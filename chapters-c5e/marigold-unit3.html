Chapter 3: My Shadow & Robinson Crusoe

Playful optical poem by Robert Louis Stevenson & Survival adventure by Daniel Defoe | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Poem 'My Shadow': A child observes his shadow behaving unpredictably; growing tall like an Indian-rubber ball, shrinking to nothing, and staying asleep in bed before dawn dew.
  • Prose 'Robinson Crusoe': Marooned alone on an uninhabited desert island for years, Crusoe encounters a single human footprint on the beach sand.
  • Psychology of Fear: The sensory shock of the unknown; mistaking every distant bush and tree for a hostile human figure; retreating into his fortified cave castle.
  • Reasoning & Faith: Crusoe's progression from panic to rational deduction; recognizing the foot was larger than his own and praying for spiritual strength.
  • Vocabulary & Grammar: Describing words, degrees of comparison, compound sentences with and/but/because.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Reading is Fun • Poem (My Shadow)
Why does the child say that his shadow 'jumps into bed before me' and 'stays asleep' in the morning?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
When the child walks towards his bed in the evening with a light behind him, his cast shadow falls ahead into the bed before he climbs in. In the early morning before sunrise, because there is no bright directional sunlight to cast a silhouette, the child playfully imagines his lazy shadow stayed fast asleep in bed.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Shadow falling ahead of him onto bed in evening light 1.5 Marks
Absence of sunlight before dawn treated as 'sleeping' 1.5 Marks
Q2 Reading is Fun • Poem (My Shadow)
What time of the day is your shadow the tallest, and when is it the shortest? Explain the scientific reason.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
A shadow is tallest in the early morning and late afternoon when the sun is low on the horizon, creating a long projection. It is shortest at midday (noon) because the sun is positioned directly overhead, casting light vertically downwards.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Tallest in morning & late afternoon (low sun angle) 1.5 Marks
Shortest at noon (overhead vertical sun) 1.5 Marks
Q3 Reading is Fun • Story (Robinson Crusoe)
What convinced Robinson Crusoe that the mark on the sand was truly the footprint of a human being?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Crusoe examined the mark closely and found the clear, unmistakable impression of every part of a human foot: heel, sole, arch, and all five distinct toes. There was no mistaken identity—it was an authentic human footprint.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear impression of heel, toes, and sole 1 Mark
Unmistakable human foot pattern confirmed 1 Mark
Q4 Reading is Fun • Story (Robinson Crusoe)
Why was Robinson Crusoe terrified when looking at the trees and bushes around him as he ran home?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Crusoe's mind was seized by extreme panic and wild imagination. Every rustling shadow, distant tree trunk, and swaying bush appeared to his frightened eyes like a savage or cannibal lying in ambush waiting to kill him.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Extreme fear and heightened imagination 1.5 Marks
Mistaking bushes and tree stumps for ambushers 1.5 Marks
Q5 Reading is Fun • Story (Robinson Crusoe)
Why was Robinson Crusoe sure that the footprint could not have been made by his own foot?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
He was sure for two logical reasons: First, he had not visited that particular stretch of the shore for many months. Second, when he placed his own foot beside the impression in the sand, he discovered that the footprint was noticeably larger than his own foot.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Had not visited that shore location in a long time 1 Mark
Measurement proved footprint was larger than his own 1 Mark
Q6 Let us Learn Grammar • Conjunctions
Join the following pairs of sentences using suitable conjunctions (and, but, because, so):
(a) Robinson ran as fast as he could. He was terrified.
(b) I wanted to go outside. It was raining heavily.
(c) She studied diligently for the exam. She scored first division.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
  • (a) Robinson ran as fast as he could because he was terrified.
  • (b) I wanted to go outside, but it was raining heavily.
  • (c) She studied diligently for the exam, so she scored first division.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
(a) Joined with 'because' 1 Mark
(b) Joined with 'but' 1 Mark
(c) Joined with 'so' 1 Mark
Q7 Reading is Fun • Thematic Inquiry & Character Study
In 'My Shadow & Robinson Crusoe', analyze how the central characters face and resolve their dilemma. What core moral value or life perspective does this convey to young readers about empathy, teamwork, or patience?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Thematic Analysis & Character Values:
1. Core Dilemma: In My Shadow & Robinson Crusoe, the characters encounter unexpected difficulties that challenge their confidence and understanding.
2. Resolution Strategy: Instead of acting impulsively or giving up, they listen carefully to each other, remain calm, and apply thoughtful cooperation.
3. Moral Insight: The story highlights that genuine empathy, perseverance, and honesty turn challenging situations into enriching growth opportunities.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of central dilemma and resolution 1.5 M
Expression of core moral value and life lesson 1.5 M
Q8 Vocabulary & Word Play • Idioms, Antonyms & Synonyms
Enhance your vocabulary through language skills based on 'My Shadow & Robinson Crusoe':
(a) Provide one synonym and one antonym for each word: courageous, generous, ancient.
(b) Choose one idiom connected to the theme of 'My Shadow & Robinson Crusoe', define it, and frame a meaningful sentence.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Vocabulary Analysis:
• (a) Synonyms & Antonyms:
- Courageous: Synonym: brave | Antonym: timid.
- Generous: Synonym: kind-hearted | Antonym: selfish.
- Ancient: Synonym: antique / historic | Antonym: modern.
• (b) Idiom: 'Turn over a new leaf' (to start behaving in a better and more responsible way).
Sentence: Inspired by the lessons of My Shadow & Robinson Crusoe, Amit decided to turn over a new leaf and complete his homework promptly every day.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate synonyms and antonyms for all 3 words 1.5 M
Correct idiom definition and well-structured context sentence 1.5 M
Q9 Applied Grammar • Tenses, Prepositions & Conjunctions
Apply grammar rules to complete the following exercises:
(a) Fill in the blanks with suitable prepositions (in, across, through, beneath): The brave traveller walked _______ the dense forest and found shelter _______ a giant banyan tree.
(b) Combine the sentences using an appropriate conjunction (although, because, so): The weather was rainy. We completed our football tournament.
(c) Convert into Past Continuous Tense: She reads an adventure tale.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Grammar Solutions:
• (a) The brave traveller walked through the dense forest and found shelter beneath a giant banyan tree.
• (b) Although the weather was rainy, we completed our football tournament.
• (c) Past Continuous: She was reading an adventure tale.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate prepositions: 'through' and 'beneath' 1.0 M
Appropriate subordinating conjunction 'Although' 1.0 M
Correct Past Continuous verb form 'was reading' 1.0 M
Q10 Talk Time • Experiential Sharing & Discussion
Imagine you experienced an unexpected challenge similar to the one depicted in 'My Shadow & Robinson Crusoe'. Describe how you felt, what decision you took, and what you learned from the experience.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Experiential Reflection:
• Context: While working on an inter-school science project, our model malfunctioned on the final morning before evaluation.
• Action: Remembering the teamwork and composure demonstrated in My Shadow & Robinson Crusoe, my partners and I divided the troubleshooting tasks calmly. We identified the loose wiring and repaired the connections together.
• Takeaway: Composure, active listening, and collective trust achieve far better results than panic during stressful moments.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Personal reflection grounded in chapter values 1.5 M
Clarity of learning takeaway and emotional maturity 1.5 M
Q11 Sentence Mechanics • Punctuation & Direct Speech
Punctuate and rewrite the following conversational passage correctly:
neha whispered to her teacher madam may i borrow the illustrated atlas from the school library i need it for my geography project
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Corrected Dialogue with Punctuation:
Neha whispered to her teacher, "Madam, may I borrow the illustrated atlas from the school library? I need it for my geography project."

Explanation: Added proper capitalization at the beginning of speech and proper nouns, comma before opening quotation marks, question mark at the end of the interrogative sentence, and closing quotation marks outside the final full stop.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate opening quotation marks, capitalization, and comma 1.5 M
Correct question mark and closing quotation mark placement 1.5 M
Q12 Creative Writing • Diary Entry & Short Composition
Write a short diary entry (50–60 words) reflecting on the most inspiring thought presented in 'My Shadow & Robinson Crusoe'. Express how you will practice this value in your school or neighborhood.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Sample Diary Entry:
Date: 24th October 2026
Dear Diary,
Today our teacher discussed 'My Shadow & Robinson Crusoe'. It made me realize how powerful small gestures of kindness and patience are. Starting tomorrow, I have decided to share my extra stationery with classmates in need and spend 15 minutes helping my younger sibling with math reading. When we uplift others, our own world becomes brighter.
— Aarav
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Format adherence (date, salutation, tone) 1.0 M
Thematic relevance and actionable resolution 2.0 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Psychological Resilience & Overcoming Irrational Fears

When 10-year-old Kabir heard strange scratching noises outside his bedroom window during a night storm, his mind raced with fears of burglars or monsters, paralyzing him under his blanket until morning, only to discover an overgrown tree branch tapping against the glass pane.

Analytical Task: Connecting Kabir's experience to Robinson Crusoe's fear of the footprint, explain how fear magnifies harmless objects, and propose two steps children can take to rationally assess scary situations.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Overcoming Fear through Rational Inquiry:
  1. How Fear Distorts Reality: When humans experience acute anxiety, the brain activates a primitive fight-or-flight response that interprets neutral stimuli (tree branches, wind rustling bushes) as mortal threats, just as Crusoe mistook innocent shrubbery for hostile savages.
  2. Step 1 — Calm Breathing & Evidence Gathering: Instead of hiding in blind panic, stop, take three deep belly breaths, and look for tangible evidence (listen for rhythmic mechanical patterns vs live footsteps; check light sources).
  3. Step 2 — Communicate with Trusted Adults: Immediately wake an elder or parent to investigate together rather than enduring solitary, agonizing fear throughout the night.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for explaining psychological amplification of fear + 3.5 Marks for 2 practical investigative and communication steps = 5 Marks
