Chapter 9: Vocation

Rabindranath Tagore's poetic masterpiece exploring childhood imagination, routine & dignity of labour | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Themes of childhood innocence, the allure of freedom, occupational empathy, and the dignity of labour.
  • The child's daily schedule contrasts with the unrestricted freedom of the hawker, the gardener, and the night watchman.
  • Vivid poetic imagery: the gong striking ten, the giant street lamp with a red eye, chasing shadows with a lantern.
  • Dignity of labour: honoring honest everyday community workers without bias or condescension.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Let us Think • Comprehension
Who is the speaker in Rabindranath Tagore's poem 'Vocation'?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The speaker is a young, observant schoolboy who shares his thoughts and longings as he follows his daily routine from morning until bedtime.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of the young schoolboy 1 Mark
Child perspective navigating daily routine 1 Mark
Q2 Let us Think • Comprehension
Why does the speaker yearn to become a bangle-selling hawker?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The speaker is captivated by the hawker's absolute freedom: the hawker has no fixed road he must travel, no compulsory destination, no strict clock telling him when to go home, and nobody scolding or hurrying him.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Absence of fixed routes and strict schedules 1 Mark
Freedom from being hurried or scolded 1 Mark
Q3 Let us Think • Comprehension
What specific activities of the gardener appeal deeply to the child?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The child is fascinated that the gardener can dig the damp soil with his spade freely, soil his clothes with mud and dust, and get soaked in the rain or baked under the hot sun without any adult intervening, scolding him, or stopping him.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Freedom to dig soil with spade 1 Mark
Getting clothes muddy without scolding 1 Mark
Enduring sun and rain without restriction 1 Mark
Q4 Let us Think • Comprehension
What striking visual metaphor does the poet use to describe the street lamp at night?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The poet compares the solitary glowing street lamp to an enormous giant standing tall in the dark lane with a single shining red eye on its head.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Metaphor of a giant 1 Mark
With one red eye on its head 1 Mark
Q5 Let us Think • Comprehension
Chart the chronological daily schedule of the child speaker as described in the poem:
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
  • 10:00 AM: The temple/clock tower gong strikes ten, and the child walks down the narrow lane to school, encountering the hawker crying "Bangles, crystal bangles!"
  • 4:00 PM: The child returns home from school and peers through the open garden gate, watching the gardener digging the earth.
  • Late Evening (Bedtime): Mother sends the child to bed; gazing through his bedroom window, he watches the lonely night watchman walking up and down with his lantern.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
10 AM morning encounter with hawker 1 Mark
4 PM afternoon return watching gardener 1 Mark
Bedtime night view of watchman 1 Mark
Q6 Let us Think • Think & Discuss
Why does the child romanticize the vocations of the hawker, gardener, and watchman, while overlooking their hardships?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The child feels constrained by strict adult-imposed rules (fixed school hours, homework, staying clean, mandatory bedtimes). In his innocent yearning for independence, he sees only their freedom of movement, unaware of the hawker's financial insecurity, the gardener's exhausting physical fatigue, or the watchman's chilly, perilous night shifts.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Contrast between child's rigid routine and perceived freedom 1.5 Marks
Insight into underlying economic and physical hardships 1.5 Marks
Q7 Let us Think • Think & Discuss
What occupational workers and community helpers do you observe on your own daily journey to school?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
On my way to school, I observe street sanitation workers sweeping avenues, traffic police officers directing morning vehicles, milk delivery personnel on bicycles, vegetable vendors arranging fresh carts, bus drivers, and security guards saluting at gates. Each performs vital labor that keeps our community functioning smoothly.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Listing diverse community helpers observed 1.5 Marks
Appreciation of their vital role in community life 1.5 Marks

Q10 Let us Write • Paragraph Writing
Write a short composition (60–80 words) on 'My Dream Vocation: How I Aspire to Contribute to Society':
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
My Dream Vocation:
When I grow up, my dream vocation is to become a dedicated paediatrician. I want to treat children in rural and underserved areas who lack access to modern hospitals. Providing healing medicines, educating families on nutrition, and soothing anxious parents with warmth will give my life profound purpose. Every vocation that serves humanity with integrity is sacred, and I hope to carry out my work with deep empathy, patience, and lifelong devotion.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Clear vocational aspiration articulated with social purpose 2 Marks
Grammar, emotional sincerity, and thematic maturity 2 Marks
Q11 Let us Explore • Social Studies
Explain Mahatma Gandhi's philosophy of 'Bread Labour' and why every occupation deserves equal dignity and respect.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Dignity of Labour & Bread Labour:
Mahatma Gandhi championed the principle that every human being should perform honest physical labor to earn their livelihood. He emphasized that no work is 'lowly' or 'inferior'—whether one is a philosopher, engineer, farmer, or sanitation worker, all work that supports life is sacred and honorable. A civilized nation treats manual workers with the same dignity, fair wages, and courtesy as office professionals.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Concept of Bread Labour explained accurately 1.5 Marks
Universal equality and dignity of manual/mental work 1.5 Marks
Q12 Reading is Fun • Thematic Inquiry & Character Study
In 'Vocation', analyze how the central characters face and resolve their dilemma. What core moral value or life perspective does this convey to young readers about empathy, teamwork, or patience?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Thematic Analysis & Character Values:
1. Core Dilemma: In Vocation, the characters encounter unexpected difficulties that challenge their confidence and understanding.
2. Resolution Strategy: Instead of acting impulsively or giving up, they listen carefully to each other, remain calm, and apply thoughtful cooperation.
3. Moral Insight: The story highlights that genuine empathy, perseverance, and honesty turn challenging situations into enriching growth opportunities.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of central dilemma and resolution 1.5 M
Expression of core moral value and life lesson 1.5 M

Q14 Applied Grammar • Tenses, Prepositions & Conjunctions
Apply grammar rules to complete the following exercises:
(a) Fill in the blanks with suitable prepositions (in, across, through, beneath): The brave traveller walked _______ the dense forest and found shelter _______ a giant banyan tree.
(b) Combine the sentences using an appropriate conjunction (although, because, so): The weather was rainy. We completed our football tournament.
(c) Convert into Past Continuous Tense: She reads an adventure tale.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Grammar Solutions:
• (a) The brave traveller walked through the dense forest and found shelter beneath a giant banyan tree.
• (b) Although the weather was rainy, we completed our football tournament.
• (c) Past Continuous: She was reading an adventure tale.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate prepositions: 'through' and 'beneath' 1.0 M
Appropriate subordinating conjunction 'Although' 1.0 M
Correct Past Continuous verb form 'was reading' 1.0 M

Q16 Sentence Mechanics • Punctuation & Direct Speech
Punctuate and rewrite the following conversational passage correctly:
neha whispered to her teacher madam may i borrow the illustrated atlas from the school library i need it for my geography project
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Corrected Dialogue with Punctuation:
Neha whispered to her teacher, "Madam, may I borrow the illustrated atlas from the school library? I need it for my geography project."

Explanation: Added proper capitalization at the beginning of speech and proper nouns, comma before opening quotation marks, question mark at the end of the interrogative sentence, and closing quotation marks outside the final full stop.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate opening quotation marks, capitalization, and comma 1.5 M
Correct question mark and closing quotation mark placement 1.5 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Workplace Empathy, Child Labor Awareness & Social Responsibility

While walking back from school, 10-year-old Meera sees a young boy her own age working at a roadside tea stall, washing greasy tumblers and serving hot glasses in the afternoon heat. Unlike Tagore's poetic speaker who romantically wished to be a hawker, Meera realizes the boy is missing school due to family poverty.

Analytical Task: Contrast the child speaker's romanticized viewpoint in 'Vocation' with Meera's real-world realization regarding child labor. Propose three civic actions to uphold children's Right to Education (RTE).
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Critical Contrast & Child Rights Action:
  1. Poetic Fantasy vs. Grim Reality: Tagore's speaker views street vocations through an innocent prism of freedom from homework and bedtimes. Meera observes the painful socio-economic reality of child labor, where young children are robbed of their fundamental right to play, learn, and grow safely.
  2. Report to Childline (1098): Citizens should report child labor instances to the national toll-free helpline 1098 or District Child Protection Units for immediate rehabilitation.
  3. Enrollment in RTE Government Schools: Connect the child's family with local Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan centers that provide free education, midday meals, textbooks, and uniforms under the Right to Education Act.
  4. Ethical Consumer Boycott: Refuse to patronize commercial establishments that illegally employ underage children.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for contrasting romanticized freedom with socio-economic reality + 3.5 Marks for 3 actionable child-rights protection measures = 5 Marks
