Chapter 9: Sing a Song of People & Around the World

Rhythmic urban poem by Lois Lenski & Jules Verne's transcontinental railway adventure | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Poem 'Sing a Song of People': The relentless kinetic rush of modern city life; people hurrying through subways, revolving doors, taxis, and sidewalks, holding umbrellas in rain yet remaining isolated in crowds.
  • Prose 'Around the World in Eighty Days': Phileas Fogg, Passepartout, and Aouda journey across the American continent on the Union Pacific Railroad.
  • The Medicine Bow Bridge Crisis: The suspension bridge over the torrential river is structurally damaged; the train driver backs up a mile and dashes at top velocity (100 mph) across the creaking bridge just before it collapses into the chasm.
  • The Sioux Attack & Sledge Ride: Defending the train against Native American arrows, detaching the locomotive, and racing over frozen snow on a sail-propelled sledge to Omaha.
  • Themes & Grammar: Courage under technological crisis, urban vs rural pace, sequence of narrative events.

All Textbook Exercises & In-Text Tasks (20 Questions)

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Reading is Fun • Poem (Sing a Song of People)
Which modes of transport and urban settings are mentioned in the poem 'Sing a Song of People'?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The poem mentions city transit including subway trains beneath the ground, taxicabs, buses, tall building elevators (lifts), and revolving doors, through which endless crowds hurry back and forth along sidewalks.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Subways, taxis, and buses listed 1 Mark
Elevators, revolving doors & sidewalks listed 1 Mark
Q2 Reading is Fun • Story (Around the World in Eighty Days)
Why was the train halted near Medicine Bow station, and what bold strategy did the engine driver adopt to cross the suspension bridge?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The train was halted because a red signal warned that the suspension bridge over Medicine Bow river had broken wires and was condemned to collapse under heavy load.
  • The Bold Plan: The driver backed the train up a full mile along the track.
  • Maximum Acceleration: He opened the throttle to full steam, accelerating the locomotive to an astonishing 100 miles per hour.
  • The Leap: The train crossed the chasm so swiftly that its weight had barely touched the rails before the entire bridge plunged into the churning torrent behind them, landing all passengers safely on the opposite bank!
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Reason: Damaged suspension bridge with broken wires 1 Mark
Driver backed up 1 mile for runway 1 Mark
Accelerated to 100 mph to cross before collapse 2 Marks
Q3 Reading is Fun • Story (Around the World in Eighty Days)
How did Passepartout display extraordinary bravery during the attack on the train?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
During the chaotic attack when warriors jumped onto the train and overpowered the driver, the uncontrolled engine was speeding directly towards a fortified fort. Passepartout bravely crawled underneath the jolting carriages, uncoupled the raging locomotive engine from the passenger cars, and brought the runaway train to a safe halt near Fort Kearney.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Crawling beneath moving railcars 1.5 Marks
Uncoupling locomotive to halt runaway train safely 1.5 Marks
Q4 Let us Learn Grammar • Sequence of Events
Arrange the following events from 'Around the World' in correct chronological sequence (1 to 4):
(a) The bridge collapsed into the river.
(b) The signalman warned that the bridge was weak.
(c) The train sped across the gorge at maximum velocity.
(d) The driver reversed the engine one mile back.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
  1. (b) The signalman warned that the bridge was weak.
  2. (d) The driver reversed the engine one mile back.
  3. (c) The train sped across the gorge at maximum velocity.
  4. (a) The bridge collapsed into the river.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
First two steps correct: (b) -> (d) 1 Mark
Final two steps correct: (c) -> (a) 1 Mark
Q5 Reading is Fun • Poem (Sing a Song of People)
Contrast how people move and behave in the city when it rains versus when the sun shines bright, as depicted in the poem.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
When it pours with rain, city pedestrians hurry along sidewalks under dark, dripping umbrellas held tightly over their heads, faces lowered to avoid the cold spray. When the bright sun shines, the crowds pour out of skyscrapers, shops, and doors onto sunny sidewalks, yet still rush past at top speed without stopping or looking at their fellow citizens.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Rainy scene: hurrying with umbrellas held low 1.5 Marks
Sunny scene: pouring from doors, still rushing past hurriedly 1.5 Marks
Q6 Word Building & Matching Pairs
Match the urban transport terms from the poem with their correct operational descriptions:
(1) Subway • (2) Revolving door • (3) Elevator • (4) Sidewalk
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
  • (1) Subway: Underground electric railway network running beneath city streets.
  • (2) Revolving door: Circular rotating glass door that permits continuous pedestrian entry while preventing wind drafts.
  • (3) Elevator (Lift): Mechanical enclosed platform carrying passengers vertically between floors of skyscrapers.
  • (4) Sidewalk (Footpath): Paved designated walkway along the side of streets for pedestrians.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Subway & Revolving door accurately matched 2 Marks
Elevator & Sidewalk accurately matched 2 Marks
Q7 Reading is Fun • Thematic Inquiry & Character Study
In 'Sing a Song of People & Around the World', analyze how the central characters face and resolve their dilemma. What core moral value or life perspective does this convey to young readers about empathy, teamwork, or patience?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Thematic Analysis & Character Values:
1. Core Dilemma: In Sing a Song of People & Around the World, the characters encounter unexpected difficulties that challenge their confidence and understanding.
2. Resolution Strategy: Instead of acting impulsively or giving up, they listen carefully to each other, remain calm, and apply thoughtful cooperation.
3. Moral Insight: The story highlights that genuine empathy, perseverance, and honesty turn challenging situations into enriching growth opportunities.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Identification of central dilemma and resolution 1.5 M
Expression of core moral value and life lesson 1.5 M
Q8 Vocabulary & Word Play • Idioms, Antonyms & Synonyms
Enhance your vocabulary through language skills based on 'Sing a Song of People & Around the World':
(a) Provide one synonym and one antonym for each word: courageous, generous, ancient.
(b) Choose one idiom connected to the theme of 'Sing a Song of People & Around the World', define it, and frame a meaningful sentence.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Vocabulary Analysis:
• (a) Synonyms & Antonyms:
- Courageous: Synonym: brave | Antonym: timid.
- Generous: Synonym: kind-hearted | Antonym: selfish.
- Ancient: Synonym: antique / historic | Antonym: modern.
• (b) Idiom: 'Turn over a new leaf' (to start behaving in a better and more responsible way).
Sentence: Inspired by the lessons of Sing a Song of People & Around the World, Amit decided to turn over a new leaf and complete his homework promptly every day.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate synonyms and antonyms for all 3 words 1.5 M
Correct idiom definition and well-structured context sentence 1.5 M
Q9 Applied Grammar • Tenses, Prepositions & Conjunctions
Apply grammar rules to complete the following exercises:
(a) Fill in the blanks with suitable prepositions (in, across, through, beneath): The brave traveller walked _______ the dense forest and found shelter _______ a giant banyan tree.
(b) Combine the sentences using an appropriate conjunction (although, because, so): The weather was rainy. We completed our football tournament.
(c) Convert into Past Continuous Tense: She reads an adventure tale.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Grammar Solutions:
• (a) The brave traveller walked through the dense forest and found shelter beneath a giant banyan tree.
• (b) Although the weather was rainy, we completed our football tournament.
• (c) Past Continuous: She was reading an adventure tale.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate prepositions: 'through' and 'beneath' 1.0 M
Appropriate subordinating conjunction 'Although' 1.0 M
Correct Past Continuous verb form 'was reading' 1.0 M
Q10 Talk Time • Experiential Sharing & Discussion
Imagine you experienced an unexpected challenge similar to the one depicted in 'Sing a Song of People & Around the World'. Describe how you felt, what decision you took, and what you learned from the experience.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Experiential Reflection:
• Context: While working on an inter-school science project, our model malfunctioned on the final morning before evaluation.
• Action: Remembering the teamwork and composure demonstrated in Sing a Song of People & Around the World, my partners and I divided the troubleshooting tasks calmly. We identified the loose wiring and repaired the connections together.
• Takeaway: Composure, active listening, and collective trust achieve far better results than panic during stressful moments.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Personal reflection grounded in chapter values 1.5 M
Clarity of learning takeaway and emotional maturity 1.5 M
Q11 Sentence Mechanics • Punctuation & Direct Speech
Punctuate and rewrite the following conversational passage correctly:
neha whispered to her teacher madam may i borrow the illustrated atlas from the school library i need it for my geography project
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Corrected Dialogue with Punctuation:
Neha whispered to her teacher, "Madam, may I borrow the illustrated atlas from the school library? I need it for my geography project."

Explanation: Added proper capitalization at the beginning of speech and proper nouns, comma before opening quotation marks, question mark at the end of the interrogative sentence, and closing quotation marks outside the final full stop.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Accurate opening quotation marks, capitalization, and comma 1.5 M
Correct question mark and closing quotation mark placement 1.5 M
Q12 Creative Writing • Diary Entry & Short Composition
Write a short diary entry (50–60 words) reflecting on the most inspiring thought presented in 'Sing a Song of People & Around the World'. Express how you will practice this value in your school or neighborhood.
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Sample Diary Entry:
Date: 24th October 2026
Dear Diary,
Today our teacher discussed 'Sing a Song of People & Around the World'. It made me realize how powerful small gestures of kindness and patience are. Starting tomorrow, I have decided to share my extra stationery with classmates in need and spend 15 minutes helping my younger sibling with math reading. When we uplift others, our own world becomes brighter.
— Aarav
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Format adherence (date, salutation, tone) 1.0 M
Thematic relevance and actionable resolution 2.0 M

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Urban Loneliness vs Connected Communities

Lois Lenski describes city crowds where millions of people walk shoulder-to-shoulder on busy sidewalks and subways, yet never look at or smile at each other, creating an environment of deep urban isolation despite intense physical proximity.

Analytical Task: Contrast the social connectedness of traditional village life with modern city routines. Suggest two daily practices urban residents can adopt to build warm neighborhood communities.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Bridging Urban Isolation with Human Connection:
  1. Urban Proximity vs Village Bonding: In rural communities, citizens share interdependent relationships, knowing neighbors by name and celebrating festivals together. In high-density cities, sensory overload causes people to put up emotional walls, staring at phone screens in elevators rather than acknowledging fellow humans.
  2. Practice 1 — The 'Warm Micro-Greeting': Make it a daily rule to offer a cheerful 'Good Morning' and warm eye contact to building security guards, delivery personnel, and floor neighbors in elevators.
  3. Practice 2 — Neighborhood Balcony Gardens & Book Exchanges: Establish small community book sharing shelves or common herb planter boxes in apartment corridors to spark natural, informal doorstep conversations.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for contrasting urban isolation with rural bonding + 3.5 Marks for 2 practical community-building practices = 5 Marks
