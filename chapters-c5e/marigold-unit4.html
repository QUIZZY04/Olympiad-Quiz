Chapter 4: Crying & My Elder Brother

Cathartic poem by Galway Kinnell & Munshi Premchand's classic 'Bade Bhai Sahab' | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Poem 'Crying': The philosophy of emotional catharsis; crying wholeheartedly until the pillow is soaked so that one can emerge genuinely joyful and jump into a refreshing shower.
  • Prose 'My Elder Brother': The poignant relationship between scholarly, disciplined elder brother Bhaiya and playful, carefree younger brother Munna.
  • Academic Irony: Bhaiya studies endlessly, filling notebooks with repeated phrases, yet fails his grades; Munna flies kites and plays marbles, yet tops his class.
  • Life Wisdom vs Rote Learning: Bhaiya's mature realization that real-world life experience, parental sacrifice, and emotional maturity outweigh mere textbook memorization.
  • Grammar & Composition: Letter writing format, timetable planning, idioms, character contrast.

100% Complete Ingestion
Q1 Reading is Fun • Poem (Crying)
According to the poet Galway Kinnell, should you cry just a little bit or cry a lot? What can you do after crying completely?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
The poet advises that one should not cry just a little bit; rather, one must cry wholeheartedly until the pillow is completely soaked. After purging all pent-up sorrow, one can jump merrily into a splashing shower and laugh joyfully without any lingering gloom.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Cry wholeheartedly until pillow is soaked 1 Mark
Jump into shower and emerge laughing joyfully 1 Mark
Q2 Reading is Fun • Story (My Elder Brother)
What did Munna love to do all day, and how did his daily activities contrast with Bhaiya's routine?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Munna loved playing outdoors: running in green fields, tossing pebbles, flying paper kites, and playing marbles with friends. In stark contrast, Bhaiya sat at his desk for hours with open books, constantly reading, writing notes, and copying words repeatedly to build a strong educational foundation.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Munna: flying kites, marbles, running in green fields 1.5 Marks
Bhaiya: sitting for hours studying and writing 1.5 Marks
Q3 Reading is Fun • Story (My Elder Brother)
Why was Munna unhappy with the strict timetable that Bhaiya prepared for him?
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Munna was unhappy because Bhaiya's rigid study schedule packed every single hour with subjects like English, Maths, History, and Grammar from 6 a.m. to night, leaving zero time for playing outdoors in the green fields.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Rigid schedule packed with back-to-back subjects 1 Mark
Zero time allocated for sports or outdoor play 1 Mark
Q4 Reading is Fun • Story (My Elder Brother)
What happened when the yearly exam results were announced? How did each brother react?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
When the results arrived, Bhaiya failed his class once again, while Munna stood first in his class. Bhaiya felt heartbroken and wept quietly, whereas Munna felt proud of his ease. However, seeing his elder brother's genuine sorrow filled Munna with remorse and empathy.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Bhaiya failed while Munna stood first 1.5 Marks
Bhaiya's sadness and Munna's empathy & guilt 1.5 Marks
Q5 Character Analysis & Themes
Explain Bhaiya's philosophy on 'Bookish Knowledge versus Real Life Experience'. Why does he cite their parents and Headmaster's mother?
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Bhaiya explains that passing examinations is not the sole benchmark of wisdom:
  • Pride Destroys: He warns Munna using the example of King Ravana, whose immense knowledge was undone by arrogant pride.
  • Parents' Real Wisdom: Their parents never attended school or passed degrees, yet they possess profound life experience to manage money, family health, and crises far better than educated youth.
  • Elder Care Duty: Even if Munna surpasses Bhaiya academically, Bhaiya will always remain elder by five years, holding the natural responsibility and duty to guide, supervise, and protect his younger sibling.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Warning against arrogant pride (example of Ravana) 1 Mark
Parents' life wisdom vs theoretical degrees 1.5 Marks
Elder sibling's moral right to guide and counsel 1.5 Marks
Q6 Let us Learn Grammar • Suffixes
Add suffixes (-ness, -ly, -ful) to form new words from the root words: (a) happy, (b) serious, (c) care, (d) bright.
[2 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
  • (a) happy + ness = happiness / happy + ly = happily
  • (b) serious + ly = seriously
  • (c) care + ful = careful
  • (d) bright + ness = brightness / bright + ly = brightly
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Correct suffix transformation for (a) & (b) 1 Mark
Correct suffix transformation for (c) & (d) 1 Mark

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Balanced Learning: Cognitive Academics vs Co-Curricular Play

Many modern primary school students are subjected to relentless after-school coaching classes, leaving them zero unstructured time for physical games, imaginative hobbies, or family conversations, leading to burnout.

Analytical Task: Synthesizing the core conflict in 'My Elder Brother', draft a healthy weekly schedule and set of principles that balances academic diligence with physical playtime.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Guiding Principles for Balanced Holistic Child Development:
  1. The 60-40 Academic-Play Balance: For every 60 minutes of rigorous academic focus, allocate 40 minutes for active physical outdoor play (football, cycling, badminton) which re-energizes brain synapses and enhances memory retention.
  2. Experiential Application over Mechanical Rote: Replace passive memorization with real-world application (measuring room perimeters, maintaining a pocket expense journal, discussing news).
  3. Nurturing Sibling Empathy: Academic marks should never be used to humiliate or compare siblings at home; mutual respect and emotional security must remain the core family priority.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for identifying burnout from rote schedules + 3.5 Marks for 3 balanced development principles = 5 Marks
