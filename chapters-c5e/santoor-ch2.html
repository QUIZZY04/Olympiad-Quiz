Chapter 2: Gone with the Scooter

Lively neighborhood story about friendship, honesty, outdoor sports & an unexpected twist | CBSE Session 2026–27

Core Pedagogical Competencies & Concepts
  • Themes of honesty, ownership, cooperative neighborhood play, and resolving disputes calmly.
  • Gopi finds a lost ball in his garden and invites friends to play rather than selfishly hoarding it.
  • Friends (Deepak, Manoj, Ramani) argue over whose ball it might be before deciding to play together.
  • The surprise ending: a stray shot lands in a passing scooter's basket, carrying the ball away amidst universal laughter.

Q10 Let us Speak & Listen
Discussion Prompt: Why is outdoor neighborhood play vital for children, and how does it teach conflict resolution?
[3 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working
Key Discussion Points:
  • Physical Fitness: Running, bending, and striking improve cardiovascular stamina, agility, and motor reflexes far more than video games.
  • Conflict Resolution: In the story, rather than shouting over who owns the ball, the boys channel their energy into playing together. Outdoor play teaches negotiation, compromise, and rule-following.
  • Social Bonding: Playing on the street creates enduring friendships, teaches gracious winning and losing, and nurtures empathy.
CBSE Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Physical health benefits highlighted 1 Mark
Conflict resolution & negotiation skills explained 1 Mark
Social development & sportsmanship described 1 Mark

Create a Sports Inventory Chart: List 5 ball games, stating the ball type, number of players, and core rule for each.
[4 Marks]
✅ Step-by-Step Solution & Working

  • 2. Football: Stitched leather sphere; 11 players per side; players kick the ball into opponent's goal without using hands (except goalkeeper).
  • 3. Field Hockey: Hard plastic/composite dimpled ball; 11 players per side; curved sticks maneuver the ball into goal nets.
  • 4. Basketball: Large inflated rubber/leather ball; 5 players on court; bouncing/dribbling ball to shoot through an elevated ring.
  • 5. Tennis: Hollow rubber felt-coated ball; 1 (singles) or 2 (doubles); rackets volley the ball over a center net within boundary lines.

🌟 NEP 2020 Competency-Based Case Study

Integrity, Sharing & Peaceful Conflict Management

During recess, Kabir finds an expensive imported badminton racket lying unattended on the bleachers. Two classmates, Aman and Tushar, immediately rush over. Aman claims his cousin owns an identical one, while Tushar insists whoever picked it up has the right to keep it or trade it.

Analytical Task: Drawing inspiration from Gopi's ethical handling in 'Gone with the Scooter', outline the steps Kabir should take to ensure honesty and fairness prevail.
Evaluation & Model Answer:
Action Plan for Ethical Conflict Resolution:
  1. Reject 'Finders Keepers': Kabir must firmly explain that finding an unattended object does not confer legal or moral ownership. Keeping it would amount to dishonesty.
  2. Deposit with Authority: Hand the racket over immediately to the Physical Education teacher or school Lost and Found desk, noting the time and location found.
  3. Public Announcement: Request the teacher to make an announcement asking the rightful owner to identify the racket by describing specific marks or brand details.
  4. Promote Cooperative Sportsmanship: Suggest that while awaiting the owner, the friends can borrow standard school equipment to play doubles together, avoiding personal disputes.
Rubric: 1.5 Marks for ethical rejection of false ownership + 3.5 Marks for 4 structured conflict-resolution steps = 5 Marks
